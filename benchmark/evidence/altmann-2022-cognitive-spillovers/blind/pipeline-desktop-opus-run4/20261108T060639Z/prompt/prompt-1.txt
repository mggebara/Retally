Methodology Summary: - **Design:** A lab experiment (BonnEconLab, University of Bonn; oTree; recruited via hroot) with a 3x2 between-subjects design. 564 subjects took part: 8 sessions for each decision environment (Baseline, Directed Attention, Active Choice), about 96 subjects per treatment cell. The second dimension is cognitive resources, Scarce vs Ample. Within each session, subjects were randomised individually to Scarce or Ample to remove session effects. Tasks, numbers, defaults and their order were the same for all subjects and treatments.
- **Tasks and manipulation:** Each round has a background task: memorise a number shown for 10 s and recall it 30 s later (EUR 0.40 if correct). The number has 7 digits in Scarce and 2 digits in Ample. During the 30 s there is also a decision task: pick which of three sums of six addends is largest, each sum between 20 and 34 (EUR 0.10 if correct). One option is a randomly preselected default, and it was the correct option in 6 of the 20 rounds. In Baseline the decision task is visible only while a key is held down, so whether and how long subjects attend is recorded. Attention spans in seconds were logged in 50% of Baseline sessions, balanced across Scarce and Ample. Directed Attention shows the task all the time but keeps the default. Active Choice shows it all the time with no default.
- **Session structure:** Part 1 has 10 rounds of the background task alone (5–9 digits). Part 2 has 10 rounds of the decision task alone, 30 s each. Part 3 is the main part: 20 rounds of both tasks at once. A post-experimental questionnaire follows, including a 10-item Raven's Progressive Matrices test given to a random 50% of Baseline subjects. Parts 1–2 and the sociodemographics serve as balance checks (Table C.1).
- **Outcomes:** (i) Passivity or default adherence: the share of rounds where the default is the final choice, overall and split by whether the default is correct. (ii) Attention: total seconds the decision task was viewed per round. The extensive margin is any attention (key pressed at least once); the intensive margin is seconds conditional on any attention. (iii) Decision quality: share of correct answers in the decision task, and in the background task as the test for spillovers. (iv) Overall payoffs. In Active Choice, a round with no choice earns 0 for the decision task.
- **Main comparisons and tests:** Baseline-Scarce vs Baseline-Ample (Hypotheses 1–2); Directed and Active vs Baseline within each resource condition (Hypotheses 3–4); default option in Directed-Scarce vs the same option in Active-Scarce. Non-parametric tests use subject-level averages over the 20 main rounds unless stated otherwise: Mann-Whitney U between treatments, Wilcoxon signed-rank for within-subject Part 2 vs Part 3 comparisons, and Fisher exact tests for the share of zero-attention rounds. Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests compare attention distributions at the subject-round level, with CDFs of subject mean attention used to check first-order stochastic dominance. t-tests compare mean attention and accuracy. All p-values are two-sided.
- **Regression specification:** Parametric tests use individual subject-round observations (linear models), with standard errors clustered at the subject level. For heterogeneity, the outcome (passive choice) is regressed on a treatment dummy (Scarce), a dummy for a Raven score above the sample median, and their interaction. The diff-in-diff test is on the interaction. Further splits use the top quartile of Raven scores.
- **Decomposition:** An Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition (Appendix C.3) splits the Scarce–Ample gap in passivity into an extensive-margin part (rounds with complete inattention) and the remainder.
- **No ML components:** There is no train/test split, no cross-validation and no tuned hyperparameters. The design parameters are digit lengths (2 vs 7), display and retention times (10 s / 30 s), payoffs (EUR 0.40 / 0.10) and 20 main rounds.
