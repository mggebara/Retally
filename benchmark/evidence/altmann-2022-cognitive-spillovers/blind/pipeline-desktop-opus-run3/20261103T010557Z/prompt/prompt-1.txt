Methodology Summary: - **Design:** Lab experiment (BonnEconLab, University of Bonn; oTree; ORSEE-type recruitment by Bock et al. 2014), 3x2 between-subjects design, 564 subjects, 8 sessions per decision environment (Baseline, Directed Attention, Active Choice), about 96 subjects per cell. Cognitive-resource condition (Scarce = memorise 7-digit numbers vs Ample = 2-digit numbers) is randomised across individuals *within* sessions; the decision environment varies across sessions. Tasks, numbers, defaults and their order are identical for all subjects and treatments.
- **Tasks and procedure:** Four parts: (1) 10 rounds of background task only (5–9 digit numbers; shown for 10 s, recalled after 30 s; €0.40 if correct); (2) 10 rounds of decision task only (30 s each; €0.10 if correct); (3) main part, 20 rounds of both tasks at once (background number shown for 10 s, then a 30 s window with the decision task: choose which of three 6-addend sums is largest, each sum between 20 and 34; recall at the end); (4) a post-experimental questionnaire. Parts 1–2 serve as ability and balance checks (Table C.1). Feedback is given only after part 3.
- **Treatment environments:** Baseline: the decision task is visible only while a key is held down, and a randomly preselected default applies if the subject is passive. Directed Attention: the task is always on screen, still with a random default. Active Choice: always on screen, no default. The random default is correct in 6 of the 20 main rounds (30%).
- **Outcome measures:** passivity = sticking to the default (also split by whether the default is correct or incorrect); for Baseline, entering the decision task at all (key pressed at least once: extensive margin) and attention span in seconds per round (intensive margin; recorded only in 50% of Baseline sessions, balanced across Scarce and Ample); decision-task accuracy (overall and conditional on entering the task); background-task accuracy (cognitive spillover); overall payoffs. For Active-Choice vs Directed-Scarce, the comparison is the share choosing the option that is the default in Directed against the share choosing the same option in Active.
- **Statistical tests:** Non-parametric tests on subject-level averages over the 20 main rounds: Mann-Whitney U between treatments, Wilcoxon signed-rank within subjects (part 2 vs part 3 accuracy), Fisher exact test for shares of fully inattentive choices, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on attention spans at the subject-round level. t-tests and regressions use subject-round observations with standard errors clustered at the subject level. All p-values are two-sided.
- **Heterogeneity regression:** Uses a 10-item Raven's Progressive Matrices score from the questionnaire, collected for a random 50% of Baseline-Ample and Baseline-Scarce participants. Specification: subject-round default-adherence indicator regressed on a Scarce-treatment dummy, an above-median-Raven dummy and their interaction (difference-in-differences). Standard errors are clustered by subject. Top-quartile subjects are also compared with Mann-Whitney U.
- **Decomposition:** An Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition (Appendix C.3) splits the Baseline-Scarce vs Baseline-Ample difference in passivity into an extensive-margin part (complete inattention) and the remainder. First-order stochastic dominance is checked with CDFs of subject mean attention (and of decision-level data, Figure C.1).
- **Prediction or ML methods:** None. There is no train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning. Treatment effects are pure between-treatment comparisons, with no control variables beyond those described above.
