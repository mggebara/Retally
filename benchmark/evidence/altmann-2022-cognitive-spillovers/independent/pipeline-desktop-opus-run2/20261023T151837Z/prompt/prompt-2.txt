Title: DISCUSSION PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design / data-generating process:** Lab experiment (BonnEconLab, oTree, ORSEE-type recruitment via Bock et al. 2014) with a 3x2 between-subjects design: decision environment (Baseline / Directed Attention / Active Choice) x cognitive load (Ample = memorize 2-digit numbers vs. Scarce = memorize 7-digit numbers in the background task). N = 564 subjects, 8 sessions per environment (~96 subjects per cell). Scarce vs. Ample was randomized across individuals within a session, which removes session effects. Tasks, numbers, defaults and their order were identical across all subjects and treatments. There is no ML-style train/test split or cross-validation; identification comes from random assignment to treatment.
- **Tasks and parameters:** Main part (Part 3) has 20 rounds. In each round a number is shown for 10 s and recalled after 30 s (EUR 0.40 if correct). During those 30 s subjects do the decision task: pick which of 3 sums of six addends is largest, with each sum between 20 and 34 (EUR 0.10 if correct). In Baseline and Directed, one option is a randomly preselected default, and it was correct in 6 of 20 rounds (30%). In Baseline the decision task is visible only while a key is held down, so the experiment records whether subjects entered the task at all (extensive margin). Attention spans in seconds (intensive margin) were recorded in 50% of Baseline sessions. Directed shows the task permanently and keeps the default. Active shows it permanently with no default; 5.35% of Active cases had no choice and paid 0. Part 1 (10 recall rounds, 5–9 digits) and Part 2 (10 decision-task-only rounds) measure baseline ability and are used for balance checks (Table C.1).
- **Outcome variables:** (i) Passivity / default adherence: 1 if the final choice equals the default, at the subject-round level. It is also split by whether the default was correct or incorrect. (ii) Inattention: never entered the decision task in a round (Baseline). (iii) Attention span: total seconds the decision task was viewed per round. (iv) Decision-task accuracy, background-task accuracy, and total payoffs, used to measure cognitive spillovers. (v) Choice share of the default option in Directed vs. the same option in Active.
- **Main statistical tests:** Non-parametric tests use subject-level averages over the 20 rounds unless noted, and all tests are two-sided. Treatment comparisons use Mann-Whitney U tests. Part 2 vs. Part 3 accuracy within Baseline-Ample uses a Wilcoxon signed-rank test (83.6% vs. 86%, p = 0.308). Attention distributions are compared with a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test at the subject-round level. Complete inattention (32.1% vs. 2.5%) uses a Fisher exact test. Mean attention (25.76 s vs. 13.15 s; conditional on entering, 26.4 vs. 17.8 s) and decision accuracy conditional on entry (85.0% in Ample, 12.1 pp lower in Scarce) use t-tests. Subject-level CDFs check first-order stochastic dominance.
- **Parametric / regression specification:** Parametric tests use subject-round observations, and standard errors are clustered at the subject level. The heterogeneity analysis regresses default adherence on a Scarce treatment dummy, a dummy for an above-median Raven score, and their interaction (diff-in-diff p = 0.022). Coefficients are raw percentage-point differences in rates, not standardised. The Raven score comes from a 10-item Raven's Progressive Matrices test given to a random 50% of Baseline subjects in the post-experimental questionnaire. The top quartile of the Raven score is also compared separately (37.3% vs. 33.6%, MWU p = 0.866).
- **Decomposition:** An Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition (Appendix C.3) splits the Baseline-Scarce vs. Baseline-Ample passivity gap into an extensive-margin part (inattention) and the remainder. The extensive margin accounts for about 70% of the gap.
- **Headline results and sample restrictions:** Default adherence is 31.8% in Baseline-Ample vs. 60.0% in Baseline-Scarce (MWU p < 0.01). When the default is correct it is 84.0% vs. 87.9% (p = 0.069); when it is incorrect it is 9.4% vs. 48.1% (p < 0.01). Directed-Ample is 30.2% vs. 31.8% in Baseline-Ample (p = 0.243), and Directed-Scarce is about 41% vs. 60%. Baseline-environment analyses use the Baseline sessions only. Attention-span analyses use the 50% subsample with recorded spans; in that subsample, inattention is 26.3% vs. 2.5%. Intensive-margin accuracy is computed only on rounds in which the subject entered the decision task. Directed and Active are compared with Baseline on decision-task accuracy, background-task accuracy and total payoffs to test for negative cognitive spillovers.
