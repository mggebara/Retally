Title: DISCUSSION PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design / estimation approach:** This is a controlled lab experiment (BonnEconLab, oTree, recruited with hroot), not a predictive ML study, so there is no train/test split, no cross-validation and no hyperparameter tuning. It uses a 3x2 between-subjects design. One dimension is cognitive resources: Ample (memorize 2-digit numbers) vs Scarce (7-digit numbers) in the background task. The other is the decision environment: Baseline (a blank screen, and the subject must press and hold a key to see the decision task), Directed Attention (task shown permanently, one option randomly preselected as the default) and Active Choice (task shown permanently, no default). N = 564 subjects in 24 sessions (8 per environment), about 96 per cell (Table 1, Table C.1). Ample vs Scarce was randomized across individuals within each session, to remove session effects.
- **Task / procedure:** There are four parts. Part 1 is 10 rounds of the background task alone, with 5 to 9 digit numbers. Part 2 is 10 rounds of the decision task alone, 30 s each. Part 3, the main part, is 20 rounds with both tasks at once. Part 4 is a questionnaire, which for a random 50% of Baseline subjects included a 10-item Raven's Progressive Matrices test. In each round a number is shown for 10 s and must be recalled after 30 s (EUR 0.40 if correct). Meanwhile the decision task asks which of three six-addend sums is largest, with every sum between 20 and 34 (EUR 0.10 if correct). The default was random, and it happened to be correct in 6 of the 20 rounds (30%). Tasks, numbers, defaults and their order were the same for all subjects. Parts 1 and 2 serve as balance checks on baseline ability (Table C.1).
- **Outcome variables:** The passivity rate is the default adherence rate, i.e. how often the subject chose or kept the default, overall and split by whether the default was correct. Attention is measured two ways: the extensive margin (whether the subject pressed the key at least once in a round) and the attention span (total seconds the decision task was visible). Attention spans were recorded in only 50% of Baseline sessions, balanced across Ample and Scarce. Decision quality is the share of correct answers in the decision task and in the background task, plus total payoffs. The 5.35% of Active Choice cases with no option chosen count as incorrect and earn 0 for the decision task.
- **Preprocessing / unit of analysis:** Unless stated otherwise, non-parametric tests use subject-level averages over the 20 main rounds. Parametric tests use subject-round observations and allow for clustering at the subject level. All p-values are two-sided. The results on intensive-margin decision quality and conditional attention keep only cases where the subject entered the decision task in that round.
- **Statistical tests:**
  - Mann-Whitney U tests compare default adherence across treatments, e.g. Baseline-Ample 31.8% vs Baseline-Scarce 60.0%. When the default was correct the rates are 84.0% vs 87.9% (p = 0.069); when it was incorrect, 9.4% vs 48.1%. Directed-Ample is at 30.2% (p = 0.243 vs Baseline-Ample).
  - A Wilcoxon signed-rank test compares Part 2 and Part 3 decision accuracy in Baseline-Ample (86% vs 83.6%, p = 0.308).
  - A Kolmogorov-Smirnov test at the subject-round level compares attention distributions.
  - t-tests compare mean attention (25.76 vs 13.15 s), conditional attention (26.4 vs 17.8 s) and conditional accuracy (85.0%, versus 12.1 pp lower under Scarce).
  - A Fisher exact test compares complete inattention (2.5% vs 32.1%, or 26.3% in the attention subsample).
  - First-order stochastic dominance is checked with CDFs of subject mean attention (Figure 5, Figure C.1).
- **Regression specification (heterogeneity):** This is a linear probability / OLS regression of default adherence (a 0/1 indicator per subject-round) on a Scarce treatment dummy, a dummy for an above-median Raven score, and their interaction. Coefficients are in raw percentage-point units and are not standardised. Standard errors are clustered at the subject level. The difference-in-differences of 31.4 pp vs 11.7 pp has p = 0.022. A top-quartile comparison (37.3% vs 33.6%) uses Mann-Whitney U (p = 0.866). The sample is restricted to Baseline subjects who took the Raven test. The excerpt does not report any further fixed effects or controls.
- **Decomposition:** An Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition (Appendix C.3) splits the Baseline-Scarce vs Baseline-Ample gap in passivity. The extensive margin (complete inattention) accounts for about 70% of the gap.
- **Later comparisons (Hypotheses 3 and 4):** The analysis compares Directed and Active Choice with Baseline within each resource condition. It covers passivity, how often the option preselected as default in Directed is chosen compared with the same option in Active Choice (about 10 pp higher), decision-task accuracy, background-task accuracy (cognitive spillovers) and total payoffs.
