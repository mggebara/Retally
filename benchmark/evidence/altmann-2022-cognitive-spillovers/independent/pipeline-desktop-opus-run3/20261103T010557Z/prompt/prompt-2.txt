Title: DISCUSSION PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design:** Laboratory experiment (BonnEconLab, oTree, recruited via hroot), 3x2 between-subjects design, N = 564 subjects, 8 sessions per decision environment, about 96 subjects per cell. First dimension is cognitive load in a background memory task: **Scarce** (memorise a 7-digit number) vs **Ample** (2-digit number), randomised across individuals *within* a session. Second dimension is the decision environment, varied across sessions: **Baseline** (the decision task is visible only while a key is held down, with a random default), **Directed Attention** (task shown all the time, random default preselected) and **Active Choice** (task shown all the time, no default). Cells: Baseline-, Directed-, Active- × Ample/Scarce.
- **Task structure / procedures:** Four parts. Part 1 has 10 rounds of the memory task only (5 to 9 digits; €0.40 per correct recall). Part 2 has 10 rounds of the decision task only (30 s; €0.10 per correct answer). Part 3 is the main part: 20 rounds of both tasks at once. In each round the number is shown for 10 s, recall comes 30 s later, and in those 30 s the subject picks which of three 6-addend sums is largest (each sum between 20 and 34). Part 4 is a questionnaire, including a 10-item Raven's matrices test for a random 50% of Baseline subjects. Tasks, numbers, defaults and their order are the same for every subject; the default was correct in 6 of the 20 rounds (30%). No feedback is given until part 3 ends. Parts 1 and 2 also serve as balance checks (Table C.1).
- **Outcome variables:** (i) Passivity / default adherence: the share of rounds in which the default is the outcome, overall and split by whether the default was correct. (ii) Attention: in Baseline, whether the subject entered the task at all (extensive margin) and the total seconds the key was held (intensive margin). Seconds were recorded in 50% of Baseline sessions, balanced across Ample and Scarce. (iii) Decision quality: share of decision tasks answered correctly. (iv) Background-task accuracy and total payoffs, to measure cognitive spillovers.
- **Unit of analysis / inference:** Non-parametric tests use subject-level averages over the 20 main rounds. These are mainly Mann-Whitney U tests between treatments, plus a Wilcoxon signed-rank test comparing part 2 with part 3 accuracy in Baseline-Ample (83.6% vs 86%, p = 0.308). Parametric tests (t-tests and regressions) use subject-round observations with standard errors clustered at the subject level. Other tests: Fisher's exact test for the share of fully inattentive rounds, a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on the subject-round distribution of attention spans, and CDFs of subject-mean attention to check first-order stochastic dominance. All p-values are two-sided.
- **Headline estimates (Baseline):** Default adherence is 31.8% in Ample vs 60.0% in Scarce (MWU p < 0.01). When the default is correct: 84.0% vs 87.9% (p = 0.069). When it is incorrect: 9.4% vs 48.1% (p < 0.01). Mean attention falls from 25.76 s to 13.15 s (t-test p < 0.01). Full inattention is 2.5% vs 32.1% of rounds (26.3% in the timing subsample; Fisher p < 0.01). Conditional on entering the task, attention is 26.4 s vs 17.8 s, and accuracy is 85.0% in Ample and 12.1 pp lower in Scarce (p < 0.01).
- **Heterogeneity regression:** A linear model at the subject-round level of the passive-choice dummy on a Scarce treatment dummy, an above-median Raven score dummy and their interaction, with SEs clustered by subject. The treatment effect is 31.4 pp below the median vs 11.7 pp above it; the difference-in-differences has p = 0.022. In the top quartile: 37.3% vs 33.6% (MWU p = 0.866).
- **Decomposition:** An Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition (Appendix C.3) splits the Baseline Scarce–Ample gap in passivity into an extensive-margin part (full inattention), which accounts for about 70%, and an intensive-margin part.
- **Intervention comparisons:** Directed-Ample vs Baseline-Ample passivity is 30.2% vs 31.8% (MWU p = 0.243). In the Scarce condition, passivity falls from 60% in Baseline to about 41% in Directed. The default option is chosen about 10 pp more often in Directed-Scarce than the same option in Active-Scarce. In Active Choice, 5.35% of rounds had no choice and earned €0. Decision- and background-task accuracy and total payoffs are compared across environments within each load condition; the text supplied ends before the full results on these comparisons.
