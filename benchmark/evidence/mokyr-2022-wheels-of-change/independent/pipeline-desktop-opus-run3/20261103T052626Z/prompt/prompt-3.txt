Methodology: - **Data / unit of observation**: Cross-section of England's government-area districts, 1710–50 (326 districts; Isles of Scilly dropped for missing HYDE population → 325). Apprentice counts come from the Apprenticeship Stamp Tax registers (IR 1). Masters' locations are matched to TownsList (apprentices found in 10,201 of 36,144 locations; all other locations set to zero) and summed to the district. Domesday Book (1086) mills are geolocated to districts. The Domesday survey leaves out London, Winchester, Bristol, Tamworth, Durham and Northumberland, and covers Cumberland, Westmorland and Lancashire only partly, so every regression using mills has **N = 298 districts**.
- **Variable construction (units)**: The dependent variable w_i is wright apprentices (millwrights, wheelwrights and "wrights") per capita. The key regressor is Domesday watermills per capita. Both are normalised per 10,000 people using mean district population in 1710–50 (HYDE). These are raw per-10,000 counts, not standardised. Headline coefficient: 0.15 unconditional, 0.14 with geographic, agricultural and climatic controls, 0.12 in the full specification. That is, one more 1086 mill per 10,000 people goes with about 0.12–0.14 more wright apprentices per 10,000 in 1710–50. Drapers/clothiers apprentices (4,359 people) and weaver apprentices per capita proxy for textile activity in later regressions.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 1, OLS, levels-on-levels)**: w_i = β·mills_i + X′γ + ε_i, with a constant. Table 2 adds controls step by step: (1) no controls; (2) latitude, longitude, mean ruggedness, mean elevation; (3) adds agricultural suitability, wheat suitability and mean pasture suitability; (4) adds mean precipitation and temperature; (5) is the preferred full specification. Column (5) adds mean population 1710–50, minimum distance to an important harbour, distance to London, minimum distance to a Roman road, minimum distance to a navigable river, and district area. No fixed effects. Adjusted R² runs from 0.57 to 0.61. Figure 1 plots the partial correlation for column (5).
- **IV / 2SLS (Table 3)**: Domesday mills are instrumented with a grinding-mill suitability measure. This is the length of rivers in the district running through moderate terrain ruggedness (TRI between 2 and 6), interacted with an indicator for high wheat suitability (mean GAEZ wheat-suitability category ≤ 5, where lower is more suitable). The main effects "River suitability" and "Wheat suitability" enter as controls. There are three paired OLS/IV specifications: no controls (cols 1–2), main geographic + agricultural + climatic controls (cols 3–4), and those plus other geographic controls (cols 5–6). IV coefficients are 0.16, 0.15 and 0.16. First-stage F-statistics are 19.90, 16.96 and 16.17. First stage and reduced form are in Online Appendix Table E.4.
- **Black Death extension (Eq. 2, OLS)**: w_i = δ1·mills_i + δ2·dpop1300–1400_i + δ3·(dpop1300–1400_i × mills_i) + X′γ + ω_i. Here dpop is the (negative) HYDE population growth from 1300 to 1400, and δ3 (expected positive) is the coefficient of interest. A Winchester-district (Hampshire) case study compares the River Meon with the River Itchen, using Domesday mills, Taylor's 1759 map, and wrights within 5 miles of each river.
- **Inference**: Heteroscedasticity-robust standard errors **clustered at the (historical) county level** in all regressions. Significance is two-sided at 10/5/1%.
- **Further analyses / robustness**: Domesday mills are used to predict drapers, weavers and iron-making apprentices, with placebo regressions for industries that did not use water power. Other checks: a horserace between mills and wrights; a mediation analysis attributing 30–70% of the mills→industrialisation effect to wrights; worsted-weaver relocation between 1710–50 and 1750–1800; and tests for bias from unobservables (Section 5.4). This is a cross-sectional regression design with no train/test split or cross-validation, and no ML hyperparameters.
