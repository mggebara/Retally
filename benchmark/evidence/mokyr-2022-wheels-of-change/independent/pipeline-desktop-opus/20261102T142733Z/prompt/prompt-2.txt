Methodology: - **Design / estimator:** Cross-sectional district-level OLS (with an IV/2SLS check) across English government-area districts. Benchmark equation (1): w_i = β·Mills_i + X′γ + ε_i, where w_i = number of wright apprentices (millwrights, wheelwrights, "wrights") per capita in 1710–50 and Mills_i = number of Domesday Book (1086) watermills per capita. The form is linear in levels (not logs). There is no machine-learning train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning.
- **Units (not standardised):** Both variables are per capita, meaning per 10,000 people, using the district's mean population in 1710–50 (HYDE). The headline coefficient is in raw units: wright apprentices per 10,000 people per additional DB mill per 10,000 people. It is 0.15 unconditional (Table 2 col 1), 0.14 with geographic, agricultural and climatic controls (cols 2–4), and 0.12 in the full specification (Table 2 col 5; significant at 1%, SE 0.03).
- **Data construction / preprocessing:** Masters' locations in the Apprenticeship Stamp Tax registers (IR 1, 1710–50) were matched to TownsList places. Apprentices were found in 10,201 of 36,144 places, and all other places were set to zero. Counts were then summed to district level and divided by mean 1710–50 population (×10,000). Draper/clothier apprentices (4,359) proxy for textiles, with weavers as an alternative proxy. DB mills (about 5,600 mills in more than 3,000 places) were geolocated to districts and normalised the same way.
- **Sample restrictions:** England only. There are 326 districts; the Isles of Scilly are dropped for missing HYDE population, leaving N = 325. Domesday Book does not cover London, Winchester, Bristol, Tamworth, Durham or Northumberland, and covers Cumberland, Westmorland and Lancashire only partly. This limits mill regressions to N = 298 (all columns of Tables 2–3).
- **Control set (preferred spec, Table 2 col 5 / Table 3 cols 5–6):**
  - Main geographic: latitude and longitude of the district centroid, mean ruggedness (TRI), mean elevation.
  - Agricultural: agricultural suitability (Ramankutty), wheat suitability (GAEZ), mean pasture suitability.
  - Climatic: mean precipitation, mean temperature.
  - Other geographic/trade: distance to London; minimum distances to an important harbour, a navigable river and a Roman road; district area.
  - Mean population 1710–50.
  - A constant. There are no fixed effects.
- **IV strategy (Table 3):** DB mills are instrumented with a grinding-mill suitability instrument. This is the length of rivers in the district with moderate ruggedness (TRI between 2 and 6), interacted with high wheat suitability (mean GAEZ wheat category ≤ 5). River suitability and wheat suitability also enter as separate controls. The IV coefficients are 0.16 (col 2, no controls), 0.15 (col 4) and 0.16 (col 6, full controls). First-stage F is 19.90, 16.96 and 16.17.
- **Standard errors:** Heteroscedasticity-robust and clustered at the historical county level in all regressions.
- **Extensions / robustness:** Equation (2) adds the 1300–1400 population change (Black Death), interacted with DB mills, and δ3 is the coefficient on the interaction. Further checks cover:
  - placebo industries (textiles and iron versus non-mill industries);
  - a worsted relocation test for 1710–50 versus 1750–1800;
  - a horserace between mills and wrights;
  - mediation analysis, in which wrights account for 30–70% of the effect of mills on industrialisation;
  - a test for bias from unobservables (Section 5.4).

  Evaluation rests on coefficient size and significance (*/**/*** at 10/5/1%, two-sided), adjusted R² (0.57–0.62) and first-stage F.
