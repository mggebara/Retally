Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis:** A cross-section of England's government area districts for 1710–50. There are 326 districts; the Isles of Scilly are dropped because HYDE has no population figure, which leaves 325. Regressions that use Domesday Book (1086) mills are limited to 298 districts. Domesday leaves out London, Winchester, Bristol and Tamworth, omits Durham and Northumberland, and covers Cumberland, Westmorland and Lancashire only partly. Variables come from 10,201 locations, geolocated into districts.
- **Variable construction:** Occupation counts come from the Apprenticeship Stamp Tax registers (IR 1, starting in 1710). Each master's location is matched to TownsList; locations with no apprentices (out of 36,144) are set to zero. Counts are summed by district and divided by mean district population for 1710–50 (HYDE), giving apprentices per 10,000 people.
  - Dependent variable: "wrights", meaning apprentices to millwrights, wheelwrights and plain "wrights".
  - Key regressor: Domesday watermills per capita, normalised by the same 1710–50 population.
  - Textile proxies used later: drapers/clothiers (4,359 apprentices) and weavers.
- **Estimating equation (eq. 1):** Linear OLS in levels, not logs: w_i = β·Mills_i + X′γ + ε_i. Variables are in raw per-capita units, not standardised, and every regression has a constant. Table 2 adds controls step by step:
  - Col (1): no controls.
  - Col (2): latitude, longitude, mean ruggedness and mean elevation of the district centroid.
  - Col (3): adds agricultural suitability, wheat suitability and mean pasture suitability.
  - Col (4): adds mean precipitation and temperature.
  - Col (5), the preferred specification: adds mean population 1710–50, minimal distance to an important harbour, distance to London, minimal distance to a Roman road, minimal distance to a navigable river, and district area.
  - There are no fixed effects. N = 298.
- **Standard errors:** Heteroscedasticity-robust and clustered at the historical county level in all regressions.
- **IV strategy (Table 3, cols 2, 4 and 6; 2SLS against OLS in cols 1, 3 and 5):** Domesday mills are instrumented with "suitability for grain-grinding mills". This is the length of rivers in the district with moderate ruggedness (TRI between 2 and 6), interacted with a dummy for high wheat suitability (mean GAEZ wheat suitability category ≤ 5, where lower means more suitable).
  - River suitability and wheat suitability also enter as separate controls.
  - Control blocks: none in cols 1–2; main geographic, agricultural and climatic controls in cols 3–4; all of those plus the other geographic controls in cols 5–6.
  - The first-stage F-statistic is reported. First stage and reduced form are in Online Appendix Table E.4.
- **Black Death extension (eq. 2):** w_i = δ1·mills_i + δ2·dpop1300–1400_i + δ3·(dpop1300–1400_i × mills_i) + X′γ + ω_i.
  - dpop is the (negative) population growth from 1300 to 1400, from HYDE 3.1. δ3 is the coefficient of interest and is expected to be positive.
  - Estimated by OLS with the same controls and county clustering (Table 5).
  - Supported by a descriptive case study of the Rivers Meon and Itchen in Winchester district, comparing Domesday mills with Taylor's 1759 map of Hampshire and counting wrights within 5 miles of each river.
- **Further analyses and robustness:**
  - Placebo regressions of other industries on Domesday mills, against textiles and iron.
  - A horserace between mills and wrights.
  - A mediation analysis splitting geography from other sources of persistence.
  - Tests of bias from unobservables (Subsection 5.4).
  - Worsted versus woollen weavers in 1710–50 and 1750–1800.
  - Coal and carboniferous strata checks (Online Appendix D).
  - Evaluation rests on coefficient significance in two-sided tests, adjusted R², and the first-stage F for instrument strength. There is no train/test split or cross-validation.
