Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis:** A cross-section of English government-area districts. There are 326 districts; the Isles of Scilly are dropped because the HYDE project has no population data for them, which leaves 325. Specifications that use Domesday Book (1086) mills have 298 districts, because the survey leaves out London, Winchester, Bristol and Tamworth, omits Durham and Northumberland, and covers Cumberland, Westmorland and Lancashire only partly. Occupational counts come from the Apprenticeship Stamp Tax registers for 1710–50. Masters' locations are matched to TownsList: apprentices are found in 10,201 of its 36,144 English locations, and every other location is set to zero.
- **Preprocessing / variable construction:** Apprentice counts are summed to the district level and divided by the district's mean population in 1710–50 (HYDE), giving apprentices per 10,000 people. Domesday mills are normalised to per-capita terms with the same 1710–50 population. "Wrights" means apprentices to millwrights, wheelwrights and plain "wrights". "Drapers" means apprentices to drapers and/or clothiers (4,359 people) and proxies textile activity; apprentices to weavers serve as an alternative proxy. Ruggedness is the Terrain Ruggedness Index (Riley et al., 1999). Wheat suitability comes from FAO-GAEZ on a 5×5 arc-minute grid. Agricultural suitability comes from Ramankutty et al. (2002).
- **Baseline estimating equation (Eq. 1, Table 2):** OLS, levels on levels (not logs): w_i = β·Mills_i + X′γ + ε_i. Here w_i is wright apprentices per capita in 1710–50 and Mills_i is Domesday watermills per capita in 1086. The headline coefficients are unstandardised, read as additional wright apprentices per 10,000 people for one more mill per 10,000 people. All regressions include a constant, and there are no fixed effects.
- **Control sets (built up across Table 2, cols 1–5):**
  - Col 1: no controls.
  - Col 2 adds district-centroid latitude and longitude, mean ruggedness and mean elevation.
  - Col 3 adds agricultural suitability, wheat suitability and mean pasture suitability.
  - Col 4 adds mean precipitation and mean temperature.
  - Col 5, the preferred full specification, also adds mean population 1710–50, minimal distance to an important (18th-century) harbour, distance to London, minimal distance to a Roman road, minimal distance to a navigable river, and district area.
  - Table 3 groups these as main geographic, agricultural, climatic and other geographic controls.
- **IV strategy (Table 3, cols 2/4/6):** Domesday mills per capita are instrumented with a grinding-mill suitability measure: the length of rivers in the district with moderate ruggedness (TRI between 2 and 6), interacted with an indicator for high wheat suitability (mean GAEZ wheat-suitability category ≤ 5, where lower means more suitable). The two components, river suitability and wheat suitability, also enter as controls. IV columns are compared with OLS at three control levels: none, main geographic + agricultural + climatic, and all controls. The first-stage F-statistic is reported, and first stages and reduced forms are in Online Appendix Table E.4.
- **Black Death heterogeneity test (Eq. 2, Table 5):** OLS of w_i on Mills_i, dpop_1300–1400,i (the negative population growth between 1300 and 1400, from HYDE) and their interaction, plus controls X. The coefficient of interest is δ3 on dpop × Mills. A district-level case study of Winchester (Hampshire) compares the Meon and Itchen rivers. It counts mills in 1086 against Taylor's 1759 map, and counts locations with wrights within 5 miles of each river in 1710–60.
- **Other analyses described:**
  - Placebo regressions of other industries' apprentices on Domesday mills, against textile and iron industries.
  - A horserace between mills and wrights in predicting industrialisation.
  - A mediation analysis separating the geographic effect from the wright channel.
  - Worsted versus woollen weavers in 1710–50 and 1750–1800, as a test of relocation.
  - A test for bias from unobservables (Section 5.4).
- **Inference:** Standard errors are heteroscedasticity-robust and clustered at the historical county level in all regressions. Significance is assessed with two-sided tests at the 1%, 5% and 10% levels, and adjusted R² is reported. There is no train/test split or cross-validation, since this is an econometric cross-section.
