Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis:** A cross-section of England's government-area districts. There are 326 districts; the Isles of Scilly are dropped because the HYDE project has no population data for them, leaving 325. Any specification that uses Domesday Book (1086) mills is limited to 298 districts. The reason is gaps in Domesday coverage: London, Winchester, Bristol and Tamworth are missing, Durham and Northumberland are omitted, and Cumberland, Westmorland and Lancashire are only partly covered. There is no train/test split or cross-validation; all models are in-sample regressions.
- **Outcome construction (preprocessing):** The source is the Apprenticeship Stamp Tax registers (IR 1) for 1710–50. Each master's location is matched to TownsList places (10,201 of 36,144 locations have apprentices; all other locations are set to zero). Counts are summed to the district and divided by mean district population in 1710–50 (HYDE), giving apprentices per 10,000 people. The dependent variable "wrights" counts apprentices to millwrights, wheelwrights and plain "wrights". Textile proxies, used in later sections, are apprentices to drapers/clothiers ("drapers", 4,359 people) and, as an alternative, weavers.
- **Key regressor:** Domesday Book (1086) watermills per capita, measured as the number of DB mills in the district per 10,000 people, using the same 1710–50 mean population as the denominator.
- **Benchmark specification, Eq. (1), Table 2:** OLS in levels, w_i = β·Mills_i + X′γ + ε_i, with a constant. The headline estimates are unstandardised: wright apprentices per 10,000 people per additional DB mill per 10,000 people. Controls are added stepwise across columns (1)–(5):
  - Column 1: no controls.
  - Columns 2–4 add, in order: latitude, longitude, mean ruggedness (TRI) and mean elevation; agricultural suitability, wheat suitability (FAO-GAEZ) and mean pasture suitability; mean precipitation and mean temperature.
  - Column 5, the preferred column, adds mean population 1710–50 plus the "other geographic" controls: distance to London, minimal distance to an important harbour, to a Roman road and to a navigable river, and district area.
  - There are no fixed effects. N = 298.
- **Standard errors:** Robust to heteroskedasticity and clustered at the historical county level in every regression.
- **IV strategy, Table 3:** DB mills per capita are instrumented with a grinding-mill suitability measure. It equals the length of rivers in the district with ruggedness between 2 and 6, interacted with an indicator for high wheat suitability (mean GAEZ wheat category ≤ 5; lower means more suitable). Wheat suitability and "river suitability" also enter as controls. The models are estimated by 2SLS, each paired with an OLS column:
  - Columns 1–2: no controls.
  - Columns 3–4: main geographic, agricultural and climatic controls.
  - Columns 5–6: those controls plus the other geographic controls.
  - The paper reports first-stage F-statistics; first stages and reduced forms are in Online Appendix Table E.4.
- **Black Death extension, Eq. (2), Table 5:** OLS of wrights per capita on DB mills per capita, the population change 1300–1400 (HYDE), and their interaction (δ3 is the coefficient of interest), plus the same control matrix X and county-clustered SEs. This is backed by a case study of Winchester district: DB mill locations on the Meon and Itchen rivers are compared with mills on Taylor's 1759 map of Hampshire, and wrights are counted within 5 miles of each river.
- **Other checks described in the paper:** Placebo regressions of other industries on DB mills. A "horserace" between mills and wrights in explaining industrialisation (drapers/textiles, iron). A mediation analysis that separates the geographic channel from the wright channel. A test for omitted-variable bias from unobservables (Section 5.4). The worsted-weaver location used as a falsification test for 1710–50 versus 1750–1800.
