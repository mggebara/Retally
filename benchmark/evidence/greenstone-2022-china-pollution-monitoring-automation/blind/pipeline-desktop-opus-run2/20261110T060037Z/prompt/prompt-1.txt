## Paper Information
Title: NBER WORKING PAPER SERIES
Abstract (first 500 chars): We examine the introduction of automatic air pollution monitoring, which is a central feature of 
China’s “war on pollution.” Exploiting 654 regression discontinuity designs based on city-level 
variation in the day that monitoring was automated, we find that reported PM10 concentrations 
increased by 35% immediately post–automation and were sustained. City-level variation 
in underreporting is negatively correlated with income per capita and positively correlated with 
true pre-automation PM10 
Methodology Summary: - **Main design: station-level sharp RD in time (eq. 1).** The outcome P_{i,c,t} is reported PM10 (µg/m³, in levels) at station i in city c on day t. The treatment indicator is I(t ≥ Auto_{i,c,t}), and the running variable is days since the city's automation date (months for AOD and monthly data). A control function f(t − Auto) is allowed to differ before and after the cutoff. β1 is the discontinuity of interest. The preferred estimator is non-parametric local linear RD with a triangular kernel and Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik (2014) MSE-optimal bandwidth selection. Selected bandwidths are 109 days without covariates (col 1) and 263 days with covariates (col 2), and 6–10 months in the monthly AOD specifications. Parametric RD and alternative kernels are robustness checks (App. B3), as is the covariate-adjusted RD of Calonico et al. (2019).
- **Covariate adjustment by residualizing (Lee & Lemieux 2010).** In the adjusted columns, PM10 is first regressed by OLS on station fixed effects (α_i), month-of-year fixed effects, and weather controls: daily temperature, precipitation, relative humidity and wind speed, taken from the nearest of 403 weather stations. The RD is then run on the residuals. Column 1 of Table 1 includes no fixed effects or controls. Standard errors are clustered at the city level.
- **Data and sample.** The panel is station-daily data from 2011–2016 from the Ministry of Ecology and Environment and local bureaus. It covers 123 cities (60 in Wave 1, deadline 1 Jan 2013; 63 in Wave 2, deadline 1 Jan 2014) with 654 stations that have PM10 data both before and after automation. Wave 3 cities have no pre-period data and are excluded. Automation dates come from news reports, and all stations in a city automate on the same day. Table 1A subsamples are: all cities (1,049,325 daily observations before bandwidth selection), Wave 1, Wave 2, and the 84 "deadline" cities that automated exactly on their wave deadline, which addresses strategic timing. Placebo and validity checks are: the same RD on monthly MODIS AOD (TERRA/AQUA); weather-continuity tests (App. B2); monthly PM10; SO2 and NO2; US consulate data; bunching at AQI thresholds; and the variance of PM10.
- **Medium-run event study / DiD (eq. 2; Table 1 Panel B).** Daily PM10, or log(PM10) in column 5, is regressed on period dummies: 7–12, 5–6 and 3–4 months before, and 1–2, 3–4, 5–6 and 7–12 months after automation. The omitted reference period is 1–2 months before automation. Controls are weather, station FE, and either month FE (col 1) or year-by-month FE (cols 2–5). The sample is restricted to deadline cities, 1 Jan 2012–31 Dec 2013. The treatment group is 242 Wave-1 deadline stations automated on 1 Jan 2013, and the never-treated control group is 123 Wave-2 deadline stations. In columns 3–5, each Wave-1 station is matched to its geographically nearest Wave-2 station, with replacement, and the model is re-estimated on the paired sample. SEs are clustered by city.
- **City-specific RDs and cross-city correlates (Table 2).** Eq. 1 is estimated non-parametrically city by city on monitor-day data. There are 74 cities without reporting gaps. 49 cities lack PM10 reports for more than 2 months before automation, and 5 of these, with gaps over 6 months, are dropped, leaving 44 used in robustness checks. There are three manipulation measures:
  - (A) a dummy for an RD estimate that is positive and significant at 5%, for the 74 cities;
  - (B) the same dummy for 118 cities, where the 44 gap cities are coded 1 if their mean PM10 rose by at least 35 µg/m³ from Jan–Jun 2013 to Jan–Jun 2014 (with 20/30/40 thresholds as a robustness check) or if their RD is positive and significant;
  - (C) the city RD coefficient itself, with weights equal to 1/SE.

  The cross-sectional OLS regressors are ln(2012 GDP per capita), ANN-corrected 2012 PM10, and the Nie et al. (2018) corruption index. Each is entered alone and then all together (col 4), each is expressed per 1 SD, and standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust.
- **Correcting pre-automation PM10 with an ANN (App. B10).** An artificial neural network (Stata BRAIN module) is trained on post-automation data to predict PM10 from AOD and weather. It is then applied to pre-automation periods, assuming the post-automation relationship carries over. Fit is assessed by the share of post-automation PM10 variance explained.
- **Behavioural responses (Table 3).** Outcomes are monthly city-level Baidu PC search indices for "anti-haze face masks" and "air filters", in levels and as log(1+searches); mobile data are excluded because they are unavailable before May 2013. Panel A runs the same non-parametric triangular-kernel RD on all 123 cities (8,661 mask and 8,590 filter observations), with and without city FE, month FE and weather controls (residualized). It is then split into "normal" and "manipulating" cities, using definition B. Panel B estimates the eq. 2 DiD with city FE, year-month FE and optional weather controls, using 39 Wave-1 deadline cities as treated and 32 Wave-2 deadline cities as controls. SEs are clustered by city.
