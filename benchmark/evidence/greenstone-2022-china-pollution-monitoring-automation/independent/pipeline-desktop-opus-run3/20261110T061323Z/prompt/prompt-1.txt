## Paper Information
Title: NBER WORKING PAPER SERIES
Abstract (first 500 chars): We examine the introduction of automatic air pollution monitoring, which is a central feature of 
China’s “war on pollution.” Exploiting 654 regression discontinuity designs based on city-level 
variation in the day that monitoring was automated, we find that reported PM10 concentrations 
increased by 35% immediately post–automation and were sustained. City-level variation 
in underreporting is negatively correlated with income per capita and positively correlated with 
true pre-automation PM10 
Methodology Summary: - **Design and estimating equation (eq. 1, Table 1 Panel A):** Station-level sharp regression discontinuity in time. P_ict (reported daily PM10 in µg/m3, in levels, not standardised) = β1·I(t ≥ Auto_ic) + f(t − Auto_ic), with separate slopes before and after automation, + weather W + station FE α_i + month FE + u. The running variable is days relative to the city's automation date, and β1 is the headline discontinuity. The sample covers 123 cities and 654 stations, 2011–2016: 60 Wave-1 cities (deadline Jan 1, 2013) and 63 Wave-2 cities (deadline Jan 1, 2014). Wave 3 is excluded because no pre-automation data exist. The full sample has 1,049,325 station-day observations before bandwidth selection.
- **Estimation method:** The preferred estimator is non-parametric local linear RD with a triangular kernel and the Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik (2014) MSE-optimal bandwidth. Col (1) has no covariates (bandwidth 109 days, N = 91,470). Col (2) uses residualised PM10 (bandwidth 263 days, N = 232,326). For col (2), PM10 is first regressed by OLS on station FE, month FE and weather (temperature, precipitation, relative humidity, wind speed), and the RD is run on the residuals (Lee & Lemieux 2010). Headline estimates are 34.7 (SE 10.7) and 34.9 (SE 5.8) µg/m3, about 35% of the post-automation mean of 99.5. Robustness checks: covariate-adjusted RD following Calonico et al. (2019), other kernels, and a parametric polynomial RD (App. B3).
- **Subsamples and placebo outcomes:** Wave 1 only: 27.5 (bandwidth 140). Wave 2 only: 64.7 (bandwidth 234). Cities that automated exactly on their deadline (84 cities): 57.1 (bandwidth 184); this guards against strategic timing. The same RD is run on monthly MODIS AOD, with the running variable in months (bandwidths of 6–10 months), and finds no discontinuity. Weather variables are tested for continuity at the cutoff. NO2 and SO2 are also tested (App. B6). Each station is matched to its nearest weather station, and stations are geocoded with Google Maps.
- **Event-study / DiD (eq. 2, Table 1 Panel B):** Daily station PM10 is regressed on period dummies for 7–12, 5–6 and 3–4 months before automation and for 1–2, 3–4, 5–6 and 7–12 months after. The reference period is 1–2 months before. Controls are weather and station FE, plus either month FE (col 1) or year-month FE (cols 2–5). The sample is restricted to deadline cities over Jan 1, 2012 – Dec 31, 2013: Wave-1 deadline stations (242) are treated on Jan 1, 2013, and Wave-2 deadline stations (123) serve as never-treated controls (N = 176,426). Cols (3)–(5) match each Wave-1 station to its geographically nearest Wave-2 station, with replacement (N = 186,499). Col (5) uses log(PM10) and finds post-automation effects of 0.24–0.32.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the city level in all RD and DiD specifications. Table 2's cross-city regressions use heteroskedasticity-robust SEs.
- **City-specific heterogeneity (Fig. 2, Table 2):** The local linear RD is estimated city by city on monitor-day data. 49 cities with more than 2 months of missing pre-automation PM10 are set aside, and 5 cities with more than 6 months missing are dropped entirely. The main sample is 74 cities. Manipulation is then regressed on per-1-SD standardised regressors: ln(2012 GDP per capita), ANN-corrected 2012 PM10, and a corruption index (Nie et al. 2018). Each regressor enters alone (cols 1–3) and all three enter together (col 4). Manipulation is defined three ways:
  - (A) a dummy for an RD estimate that is positive and significant at 5%, N = 74;
  - (B) a dummy that also uses the 44 cities with missing data, flagged if their Jan–Jun 2014 vs Jan–Jun 2013 PM10 increase is at least 35 µg/m3, N = 118;
  - (C) the continuous RD coefficient, in a regression weighted by 1/SE, N = 74.
- **ANN correction of pre-automation PM10 (App. B10):** An artificial neural network (Stata BRAIN module) is trained on post-automation data to predict PM10 from AOD and weather. It explains 81% of post-automation PM10 variation and is then used to predict pre-automation PM10. The corrected pre-period mean is 24.4 µg/m3 (29%) higher than the reported one.
- **Welfare outcomes (Table 3):** The outcomes are monthly city-level Baidu PC search indices for "anti-haze face masks" and "air filters", in levels and as log(1+x). Panel A uses the same triangular-kernel RD, with and without residualising on city FE, month FE and weather. It is run on the full sample (8,661 mask and 8,590 filter observations) and separately for "normal" and "manipulating" cities. Panel B is a DiD with city FE and year-month FE, plus weather in some columns, on 39 Wave-1 deadline (treated) cities and 32 Wave-2 deadline (control) cities. SEs are clustered by city.
