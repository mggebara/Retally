## Paper
Title: American Political Science Review (2022) 116, 1, 249–264
Methodology: - **Design / data (main analysis):** Natural experiment using an Unexpected Event during Survey Design (UESD). The data are British Election Study (BES) internet panel Wave 12 (YouGov, rolling daily fieldwork May 5–June 7, 2017). Respondents surveyed before the May 22, 2017 Manchester Arena bombing are the control group (n = 16,377) and those surveyed after it are the treated group (n = 16,972). Treatment = "Surveyed after Manchester attack" (0/1). Balance check: a regression of treatment assignment on demographics (App. B, Table B1). Only Labour Party membership predicts assignment, so every individual-level model controls for Labour membership.
- **Outcomes:** (1) *Like May*: "How much do you like or dislike…" on an 11-point scale, 0 = strongly dislike to 10 = strongly like, in raw scale points (not standardised). (2) *May best PM*: binary, 1 = May and 0 = Corbyn. The same like/dislike scale is also used for Corbyn, Farron and Sturgeon (Table 3).
- **Table 1 specifications:** Favorability is estimated by OLS and May-best-PM by logit. Each outcome is run (a) with only the Labour-membership control and (b) with full controls: ethnically British identity, gender, Labour party membership, other party membership, income, and ideology. Missing income and ideology are recoded to the scale midpoint. Standard errors are clustered on the day of the survey. Headline coefficients on post-attack: favorability −0.332 (SE 0.054; N = 32,642) and −0.351 (0.043; N = 26,506, R² = 0.442). Best PM logit: −0.204 (0.038; N = 34,394) and −0.333 (0.042; N = 27,844, pseudo-R² = 0.329). The full-control logit implies a drop of about 5 percentage points in the probability of preferring May as PM. Table 3 runs OLS with survey weights, full controls and day-clustered SEs for each leader: May −0.351, Corbyn +0.398, Farron 0.059 (not significant), Sturgeon +0.115.
- **Difference-in-differences (Table 2):** Uses the BES panel across waves. The model includes the treatment indicator (surveyed after the attack), a Time indicator (0 = waves before the attack, 1 = wave 12 onward), their interaction, wave fixed effects and full controls. The key coefficient is Manchester attack × time: −0.1274 (SE 0.0187) for likeability by OLS (N = 143,499) and −0.0501 (0.0044) for best PM (N = 97,155). The note calls the best-PM model logistic, but the coefficients are on a probability scale.
- **Mechanism:** A "negative views of women" index built from 5 items asked in earlier waves (traditional gender roles, opposition to equality / more women in office, women's unfair advantage at work, discrimination in favor of women). Each item is rescaled to 0–1 with higher values meaning more negative, and the items are averaged (α = 0.71; mean 0.39, SD 0.2). The index is interacted with the post-attack indicator, and predicted marginal effects are plotted in Fig. 1 (App. C, Table C1). Robustness adds party × attack and ideology × attack interactions plus triple interactions with the index. Subgroup models: Conservatives vs. Labour (Fig. 2), gender, and ideology.
- **Robustness / falsification:** Models with no controls, OLS versions, and controls added one by one (Tables A1–A3). Placebo outcome: keeping nuclear submarines. Pre- and post-wave views of May by assignment group. A split of the control group at its median date. Extra controls for the daily GBP/EUR exchange rate and Brexit "Leave" vote intention. Narrowed bandwidths of ±10 days and ±4 days around the attack (Fig. 3, Table E2). Multilevel model with survey day as level 2, ARIMA models, and the June 3 London Bridge attack as an alternative event (Table A6).
- **Constituency-level vote analysis:** OLS on English constituencies only. DV = change in Conservative vote share 2015→2017; the placebo DV is the 2010→2015 change. Key regressor: standardised distance from Manchester to the constituency midpoint. Controls: population, Brexit vote share, and shares ethnically British, aged 65+, UK-born, Christian, and unemployed (App. G, Table G1). The distance coefficient is positive with a one-tailed p = 0.079; the placebo is not significant.
- **Cross-national test:** TSCS panel of quarterly executive approval (Carlin, Carreras & Love 2020) for 66 countries, 1975–2017 (4,359 country-quarters; 20 countries had a woman leader). Attack dummy = an international terrorist attack (GTD definition) with more than 15 deaths, giving 60 country-quarters, 9 of them under women leaders. DV = percent approving of the executive in the quarter *following* the attack. The attack dummy is interacted with a woman-executive indicator. Country fixed effects are included, with controls for GDP growth, log inflation, executive left-right ideology, and an election-year dummy. Results: about +3.1 points of approval for men leaders and about −4.3 points for women leaders (Table 4).
- **Supplementary experiment (2012, N = 992 UK adults):** Respondents were randomly assigned to a terror-threat news story or a control condition and then rated leaders on a 0–100 feeling thermometer. May, then Home Secretary, scored about 7 points higher under threat (p = 0.003), a boost comparable to the one other leaders received.
