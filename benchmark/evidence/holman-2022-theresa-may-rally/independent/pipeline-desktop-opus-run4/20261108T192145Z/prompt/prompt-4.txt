Treatment = surveyed after the May 22, 2017 Manchester Arena bombing (n = 16,972) vs. before (control, n = 16,377). Balance checks regress assignment on demographics (Appendix B, Table B1); only Labour Party membership is imbalanced, so a Labour-member control is always included. There is no train/test split or cross-validation; it is causal inference from survey regressions.

Full controls: ethnically British identity, gender, Labour Party membership, other party membership, income, ideology. Missing ideology and income are recoded to the scale midpoint (results reported as unchanged if those cases are dropped). Table 3 uses survey weights.

The paper reports a predicted drop of about 5 percentage points in the probability of choosing May as best PM. Standard errors are clustered on day of survey in all Wave-12-only analyses.

Outcome ~ Manchester attack (treatment: surveyed after the attack) × Time (0 for waves before the attack, 1 for wave 12 onward) + main effects + full controls + wave fixed effects. The index is interacted with the attack dummy, and marginal effects are plotted, about −0.4 at the modal value. Robustness checks add party and ideology interactions and triple interactions (Figure C2).

The distance coefficient is positive, with a one-tailed p = 0.079; the placebo is null.
