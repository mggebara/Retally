Methodology Summary: - **Design and data (headline):** The paper uses an Unexpected Event during Survey Design (UESD). The Manchester Arena bombing on 22 May 2017 is treated as an as-if-random treatment within British Election Study (BES) internet panel Wave 12, which YouGov fielded on a rolling daily basis from 5 May to 7 June 2017. There are about 16,377 pre-attack respondents (control) and 16,972 post-attack respondents (treatment). The treatment dummy is "Surveyed after Manchester attack" (0 = before, 1 = after). Balance checks regress treatment assignment on the same demographics later used as controls (Appendix Table B1). Only Labour Party membership is imbalanced, so every individual-level model controls for it. A placebo outcome (keeping nuclear submarines) shows no difference.
- **Outcomes and estimation (Table 1, preferred specification):**
  - "Like May" is an 11-point scale (0 = strongly dislike, 10 = strongly like), estimated by OLS. The coefficients are unstandardised, in scale points.
  - "May best PM" is binary (1 = May, 0 = Corbyn), estimated by logistic regression. Coefficients are log-odds; the paper converts the logit to a predicted effect of about 5 percentage points.
  - Each outcome has two columns: Labour control only, and full controls.
  - Results:

    | Outcome | Labour control only | Full controls |
    |---|---|---|
    | Like May (OLS) | −0.332 (SE 0.054), N=32,642, R² 0.143 | −0.351 (SE 0.043), N=26,506, R² 0.442 |
    | May best PM (logit) | −0.204 (SE 0.038), N=34,394, pseudo-R² 0.090 | −0.333 (SE 0.042), N=27,844, pseudo-R² 0.329 |

  - Tables 1 and 3 use survey weights.
- **Controls, missing data and standard errors:**
  - Full controls: identifies as ethnically British, gender, Labour Party membership, other party membership, income, and ideology.
  - Missing ideology and income are recoded to the scale midpoint rather than dropped. The paper says results hold when those cases are dropped, with no controls, and with OLS (Appendix Table A1).
  - Standard errors in all Wave-12-only analyses are clustered on the day of the survey.
  - The estimation sample is BES Wave 12 respondents with non-missing outcome and controls, which explains the smaller N in the full-control columns.
- **Panel difference-in-differences (Table 2):**
  - Uses the BES panel across waves. Regressors: treatment group (surveyed before/after the attack within Wave 12), Time (0 = waves before the attack, 1 = Wave 12 onward), and their interaction. The interaction "Manchester attack × time" is the key term.
  - Includes wave fixed effects and the full controls. The DV is May likeability (OLS) or May best PM (the note says logistic).
  - Like May: interaction −0.1274 (SE 0.0187); Manchester attack 0.0178; Time −0.5007; constant 1.9443; N=143,499.
  - May best PM: interaction −0.0501 (SE 0.0044); attack −0.0002; Time 0.2098; constant 0.1505; N=97,155.
  - The best-PM magnitudes look like probability-scale (LPM or marginal-effect) coefficients.
  - An average-treatment-effect version is in Appendix Table A5.
- **Mechanism (gender attitudes, Fig. 1 / Table C1):** Five pre-attack items are each rescaled to 0–1, coded so higher means more negative views of women, and averaged into an index (alpha 0.71, mean 0.39, SD 0.2). They cover traditional gender roles, opposition to equality or more women in office, women's unfair workplace advantage, and discrimination in favour of women. The index is interacted with the post-attack dummy in an OLS model of Like May. Marginal effects are plotted across the index; at the modal value the effect is about −0.4. Robustness checks add party × attack and ideology × attack interactions, plus triple interactions with the index (Fig. C2). Separate models are run for Conservatives and Labour (Fig. 2, Table C2), and by gender and ideology (Tables C3–C4).
- **Alternative explanations and robustness:**
  - Same full-control OLS specification with Corbyn, Farron and Sturgeon likeability as DVs (Table 3). Effects: Corbyn +0.398, Farron +0.059 (n.s.), Sturgeon +0.115, versus May −0.351.
  - Adds a daily GBP/EUR exchange-rate control (economy) and a Brexit "Leave" vote-intention control.
  - Narrows the bandwidth to ±10 days and to ±4 days around 22 May (Fig. 3, Table E2).
  - Splits the control group at its median date to check stability (Table E1).
  - Other time specifications: time controls, attack × time, a multilevel model with survey day as level 2, and ARIMA (Tables E4–E5).
  - London Bridge attack (3 June) as a second event (Table A6).
  - A 2012 UK online survey experiment (N=992) with a terror-threat vs control news story, scored on a 0–100 thermometer, finds May rated about 7 points higher under threat (p=0.003).
- **Constituency analysis (Fig. 4, Table G1):**
  - OLS on English constituencies only. DV is the change in Conservative vote share 2015→2017; the placebo DV is the change 2010→2015.
  - Key regressor: standardised distance from Manchester to each constituency's midpoint.
  - Controls: constituency population, Brexit (Leave) vote share, and shares ethnically British, over 65, UK-born, Christian, and unemployed.
  - Distance has a positive effect for 2015–17 (one-tailed p=0.079) and no effect in the placebo.
- **Cross-national analysis (Table 4):**
  - Time-series cross-section of quarterly executive approval (Carlin, Carreras and Love 2020) for 66 countries, 1975–2017 (4,359 country-quarters; 20 countries ever led by a woman).
  - The attack dummy is a Global Terrorism Database (GTD) international attack with more than 15 deaths (60 country-quarters, 9 under women leaders).
  - DV is percent approving in the quarter after the attack. The attack dummy is interacted with a woman-executive dummy.
  - Country fixed effects. Controls: GDP growth, log inflation, the executive's left-right placement, and an election-year dummy.
  - Effects: men about +3.1 points, women about −4.3 points.
