Methodology Summary: - **Design / data (headline analysis):** Unexpected Event during Survey Design (UESD) natural experiment using British Election Study (BES) internet panel Wave 12 (YouGov, rolling daily fieldwork May 5–June 7, 2017). Treatment = respondent surveyed after the May 22, 2017 Manchester Arena bombing (1) vs. before (0); roughly 16,377 pre and 16,972 post respondents. Balance checks regress treatment assignment on demographics (Appendix B, Table B1); placebo outcome (keep nuclear submarines) and prior/later-wave views of May also checked.
- **Outcomes:** (i) "Like May" — 0–10 like/dislike scale for party leaders (0 = strongly dislike, 10 = strongly like), in raw scale units (not standardised); (ii) "May best PM" — binary, May = 1 vs. Corbyn = 0. Same like-scale used for Corbyn, Farron, Sturgeon in placebo-leader comparisons (Table 3, OLS with survey weights).
- **Estimating equations (Table 1):** Like May = α + β·PostAttack + controls (OLS); May best PM via logistic regression with the same RHS (predicted probabilities computed post hoc). Two specifications each: Labour-party-membership control only (always included because of imbalance), and full controls = ethnically British identity, gender, Labour party membership, other party membership, income, ideology. Missing ideology and income are recoded to the scale midpoint (results robust to dropping them). Appendix: no controls and OLS (linear probability) for the binary outcome as robustness.
- **Standard errors:** clustered by day of survey for all Wave-12-only analyses; robustness with multilevel model (day as level 2), time controls, attack × time interactions, ARIMA models (Appendix E).
- **Panel difference-in-differences (Table 2):** pooled BES panel waves; regressors = treatment group (surveyed after attack in W12), Time (0 for waves before W12, 1 for W12 onward), their interaction (key coefficient), full controls, and wave fixed effects; OLS for likeability, logit for best PM. ATE-model version in Appendix A5.
- **Mechanism:** Gender Attitudes index = mean of five pre-W12 items (traditional gender roles, opposition to women's equality/more women in office, women's unfair workplace advantage, discrimination in favor of women), each rescaled 0–1 and coded so higher = more negative views of women (alpha reported); interacted with PostAttack, full controls; also triple interactions with party and ideology. Subgroup models separately for Conservative and Labour supporters, by gender and ideology.
- **Robustness to rival explanations (Figure 3):** add daily GBP/EUR exchange rate (economy) control; add Brexit "Leave" vote-intention control; restrict bandwidth to ±10 days and ±4 days around May 22; split control group at median date; London Bridge (June 3) attack analysis.
- **Additional tests:** (a) Constituency-level OLS (England only): DV = Conservative vote share 2017 minus 2015 (placebo: 2015 minus 2010), key regressor = standardized distance from Manchester to constituency midpoint; controls for population, Brexit vote share, shares ethnically British, over 65, UK-born, Christian, unemployed; one-tailed test. (b) 2012 UK survey experiment (n = 992), terror-threat news vs. control, 0–100 feeling thermometers. (c) Cross-national TSCS: 66 countries, 1975–2017, 4,359 country-quarters; DV = % approving executive in the quarter after the attack; attack dummy = international (GTD definition) attack with >15 deaths, interacted with woman executive; country fixed effects; controls GDP growth, logged inflation, executive left-right placement, election year.
