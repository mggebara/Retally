Methodology Summary: - **Design / data (individual level):** Natural experiment using the British Election Study (BES) online internet panel (YouGov), Wave 12 (fielded May 5 – June 7, 2017, rolling daily random-subsample invitations). Treatment = being surveyed after the May 22, 2017 Manchester Arena bombing (1) vs. before (0) (pre n = 16,377; post n = 16,972). Framed as an Unexpected Event during Survey Design (UESD; Muñoz, Falcó-Gimeno & Hernández 2020). Balance checks regress treatment assignment on the demographic controls (Appendix B, Table B1); placebo/falsification outcomes (views of May in earlier/later waves; "Britain should keep its submarines with nuclear weapons") in Table B2.
- **Outcomes and estimators (Table 1):** (i) *Like May*: "How much do you like or dislike…" 0–10 scale (0 = strongly dislike, 10 = strongly like), estimated by OLS in raw scale units (not standardised); (ii) *May best PM*: binary, Theresa May = 1 vs. Jeremy Corbyn = 0, estimated by logistic regression (effects also converted to predicted-probability changes). Each outcome is estimated twice: with only a Labour-party-membership control (always included because Labour membership was imbalanced across groups), and with full controls. Table 3 repeats the full-control OLS for Like Corbyn, Like Farron (Lib Dem) and Like Sturgeon (SNP); Table 3 notes OLS with survey weights.
- **Controls / preprocessing:** Full controls = identifies as ethnically British, gender, Labour party membership, other party membership, income, and left-right ideology (same variables as in the balance checks). Missing ideology and income are recoded to the scale midpoint (robustness: dropping them). Robustness: no controls, OLS for the binary outcome (Table A1), controls added one at a time (Tables A2–A3).
- **Standard errors:** Clustered on the day of the survey for all Wave-12-only analyses. Additional time robustness (Appendix E): time controls, attack×time interaction, multilevel model with survey day as level 2, ARIMA models; daily GBP/EUR exchange rate control (economy); Brexit "Leave" vote-intention control; restricted bandwidths of ±10 days and ±4 days around the attack (4 days = campaign pause); median-date split of the control group as a temporal-stability check; London Bridge (June 3, 2017) attack as a secondary event (Table A6).
- **Panel difference-in-differences (Table 2):** Pooled BES panel waves; regressors = treatment (surveyed after attack within wave 12), time indicator (0 = waves before the attack, 1 = waves from wave 12 onward), and their interaction (key coefficient), plus full controls and wave fixed effects; OLS for May likeability, logistic for May best PM. Also an average-treatment-effect DiD version (Table A5) and DiD for other leaders (Table D4); lagged previous-wave leader evaluation as a control (Table D5).
- **Mechanism (gender attitudes):** Five pre-Wave-12 items (traditional gender roles, opposition to women's equality / more women in office, women's unfair workplace advantage, discrimination in favor of women), each rescaled to 0–1, coded so higher = more negative views of women, averaged into a single 0–1 index (Cronbach's alpha reported). Index interacted with the post-attack indicator (Like May, full controls; Appendix C, Table C1), with marginal effects plotted across the index (Fig. 1); robustness adds attack×party and attack×ideology interactions and triple interactions with gender attitudes (Fig. C2); subgroup models for Conservative vs. Labour supporters (Table C2/Fig. 2), by respondent gender and ideology (Tables C3–C4).
- **Constituency-level party analysis (Fig. 4, Table G1):** OLS on English constituencies only; DV = Conservative vote share 2017 minus 2015 (placebo DV: 2015 minus 2010), positive = Conservative gain; key regressor = standardized distance from Manchester to each constituency's midpoint; controls for constituency population, Brexit (Leave) vote share, and shares ethnically British, aged over 65, UK-born, Christian, and unemployed; one-tailed test reported. Data from Kollman et al. (2019) constituency returns.
- **Cross-national TSCS (Table 4) and 2012 experiment:** Quarterly executive approval (Carlin, Carreras & Love 2020) for 66 countries, 1975–2017 (4,359 country-quarters; 20 countries with a woman executive). Major attack dummy = any GTD international attack with more than 15 deaths (60 country-quarters, 9 under women leaders); DV = percent approving in the quarter following the attack; attack dummy interacted with woman-executive indicator; country fixed effects; controls for GDP growth, logged inflation, executive's left-right ideology, and election year. Separately, a 2012 online survey experiment (N = 992 UK residents) randomly assigned a terrorism-threat news story vs. control and compared 0–100 feeling-thermometer ratings of May (then Home Secretary) and other leaders (difference in means; Appendix F).
