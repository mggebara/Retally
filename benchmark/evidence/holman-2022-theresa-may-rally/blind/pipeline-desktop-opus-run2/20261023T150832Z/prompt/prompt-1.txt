Methodology Summary: - **Design and data (main case):** The paper treats the May 22, 2017 Manchester Arena bombing as a natural experiment, using an Unexpected Event during Survey Design (UESD) on British Election Study (BES) internet panel Wave 12 (YouGov; fielded May 5 to June 7, 2017, rolling daily random subsamples). Treatment = 1 if the respondent took the survey after the attack and 0 if before. Balance checks regress assignment on demographics (Appendix B, Table B1); placebo outcomes (e.g., keeping nuclear submarines) and May evaluations in earlier and later waves are also checked (Table B2).
- **Outcomes and headline specifications (Table 1):** (i) "Like May": 0–10 like/dislike scale, estimated by OLS; (ii) "May best PM": May = 1 vs. Corbyn = 0, estimated by logistic regression. Each outcome has two columns: one with only a Labour-party-membership control (always included because Labour members are unbalanced across groups), and one with full controls. Full controls are ethnically British identity, gender, Labour party membership, other party membership, income, and ideology (the same variables used in the balance checks). Missing ideology and income are coded to the scale midpoint. Coefficients are in raw (unstandardised) units: scale points for OLS and log-odds for logit, with predicted-probability effects computed after estimation. Table 3 (leader comparisons: May, Corbyn, Farron, Sturgeon) uses OLS with survey weights and full controls. Robustness checks: no controls and OLS for the binary outcome (A1), controls added one at a time (A2–A3), and lagged leader evaluations (D5).
- **Standard errors:** For Wave-12-only analyses, errors are clustered by day of survey. Alternatives include clustering plus time controls, an attack × time interaction, a multilevel model with survey day as level 2, and ARIMA models (Appendix E).
- **Difference-in-differences / interrupted time series (Table 2):** This uses the BES panel across waves. The model includes the treatment (surveyed after the attack), a time indicator (0 for waves before the attack, 1 for wave 12 onward), and treatment × time (the key term), plus wave fixed effects and the full controls. It is estimated by OLS for likeability and logit for best PM, with an average-treatment-effect version in Table A5.
- **Mechanism (gender attitudes):** Five pre-attack items (traditional gender roles, opposition to equality and to more women in office, women's unfair workplace advantage, discrimination favoring women) are each rescaled to 0–1, coded so that higher means more negative toward women, and combined into one index. The index is interacted with the attack indicator, and the predicted pre/post difference is plotted across the index (Fig. 1, Table C1). Robustness adds attack × party and attack × ideology interactions and their triple interactions with gender attitudes (C2). Subgroup models are run separately for Conservatives and Labour (C2), by gender and by ideology (C3–C4).
- **Alternative-explanation tests (Fig. 3, Table E2):** These add a daily GBP/EUR exchange-rate control and a current Brexit "Leave" vote-intention control. They also narrow the bandwidth to respondents surveyed within 10 days, and within 4 days (the campaign pause), on either side of the attack. A temporal-stability check splits the control group at its median date (E1). A supplementary analysis covers the June 3 London Bridge attack (A6). A separate 2012 UK online survey experiment (N = 992) randomly assigned a terror-threat news story vs. control and compared 0–100 feeling thermometers for May and other leaders (Appendix F).
- **Constituency-level analysis (Fig. 4, Table G1):** OLS on English constituencies only. The dependent variable is the change in Conservative vote share from 2015 to 2017, with the 2010–2015 change as a placebo. The key regressor is the standardised distance from Manchester to each constituency's midpoint. Controls are constituency population, Brexit (Leave) vote share, and the shares of the population that are ethnically British, over 65, UK-born, Christian, and unemployed. Inference uses a one-tailed test.
- **Cross-national time-series cross-section (Table 4):** Quarterly executive approval data (Carlin, Carreras and Love 2020) for 66 countries, 1975–2017 (4,359 country-quarters, 20 countries with a woman leader). A major attack is defined as an international attack in the Global Terrorism Database with more than 15 deaths (60 country-quarters, 9 under women leaders). The dependent variable is the percentage approving of the executive in the quarter after the attack. The attack dummy is interacted with a woman-executive indicator. The model includes country fixed effects and controls for GDP growth, logged inflation, the executive's left-right placement, and an election-year indicator.
