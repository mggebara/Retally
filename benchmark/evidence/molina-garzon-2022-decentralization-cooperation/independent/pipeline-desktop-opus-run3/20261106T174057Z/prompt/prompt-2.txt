Title: Decentralization Can Increase Cooperation among
Methodology: - **Design / sample**: Quasi-experiment on the staggered Honduran health-sector decentralization. Propensity score matching on all available pre-decentralization municipal data (iterative propensity score specification following Imbens & Rubin 2015; details in Zarychta et al. 2019b, Table A4) selected 31 municipalities: 21 decentralized (treated) and 10 matched centrally-administered (control). Mean propensity of being targeted is 0.64 for treated and 0.58 for controls. Robustness uses a reduced sample of 24 municipalities that drops six treated municipalities with weaker matches (propensities 0.47 vs 0.49; SI Table SA1).
- **Data collection**: In 2017, 31 daylong lab-in-the-field workshops ran with 232 public servants (about 8 per workshop). Roles were regional health authority (R), administrative intermediary (AI), 4 health-center doctors/nurses (HC) and a mayor's-office official (M). Each workshop had a pre-survey, a 10-round public goods game (PGG) and a post-survey with social-network prompts. Seven late arrivals are excluded from the cooperation data, leaving 225 players × 10 rounds = 2,250 observations. The Table 2 regressions use N = 1,980; the lagged group contribution regressor drops round 1, and covariates are missing for some players.
- **Outcome (cooperation)**: Individual contribution to the group fund in each round, from 0 to 10 lempiras (unstandardised, in lempiras). Game design follows Isaac & Walker (1988): each player gets a 10-lempira endowment per round, and the group fund is doubled and split equally. Decisions are private, and the group total is revealed after each round. Rounds 1–5 have no communication; rounds 6–10 allow communication before decisions.
- **Main estimating equation (Table 2)**: Weighted OLS with weights from the municipal-level propensity scores. Contribution_it is regressed on Decentralized (1 = decentralized, 0 = centrally administered). Model (1) is main effects only. The preferred Model (2) adds Decentralized × Communication; its interaction is 0.76 (SE 0.38, p<.05) and its Decentralized main effect is 0.41 (SE 0.30). Model (1) gives Decentralized = 0.83 (SE 0.23, p<.01). The controls are the same in both models:
  - Communication dummy (rounds 6–10)
  - Player-type dummies HC, AI and R (reference M)
  - Round (1–10), as a linear trend
  - Lagged group contribution
  - Number of players in the session
  - People known in the room
  - Female, Age, Education and Years in health sector
  - General trust

  There are no fixed effects. Fit statistics are R² 0.26 / 0.27 and AIC 9483 / 9476.
- **Standard errors**: The Table 2 note says "robust standard errors". The text says the OLS accounts "for the clustered characteristics of the observations", meaning repeated rounds within individuals within municipalities. Table 1 balance tests use weighted OLS with SEs clustered by municipality. Robustness uses a multilevel Tobit model (censoring at 0/10, with random variance at the individual and municipality levels; SI Table SA3).
- **Descriptives (Table 1)**: Propensity-score-weighted means (SD) by administration type for 77 control and 148 treated individuals. It reports p-values for differences from weighted OLS with municipality-clustered SEs, plus standardized mean differences.
- **Mechanism analysis (Table 3)**: Weighted Poisson regression of the number of realized cross-level ties per individual, with an offset for the log of total possible cross-level ties with the other workshop participants. There are two outcomes: "all ties" (knew the person as a colleague) and "strong ties" (friend). Each is run as a base model (Decentralized only, N = 231) and a full model (N = 226). The full model adds Female, Education, Age, Years in health sector, Generalized trust and player-type dummies HC/AI/R (reference M). SEs are clustered by municipality, and models are compared by BIC. The key estimates for Decentralized are:

  | Model | All ties | Strong ties |
  |---|---|---|
  | Base | 0.22† | 0.61* |
  | Full | 0.24† | 1.07** |

  For the strong-ties full model, this is a predicted rise from about 0.07 to 0.21 in the proportion of strong cross-level ties.
- **Evaluation / inference**: Significance is judged by conventional p-value thresholds († p<.1, * p<.05, ** p<.01). Substantive effects are shown as predicted contributions by round and administration type (Fig. 3). Raw mean contributions in the communication rounds are 7.9 (decentralized) vs 6.7 (centralized) lempiras, and 7.9 vs 6.6 in the last round. A supplementary pairwise correlation links average PGG contribution with perceived health-system improvement (0.37, p = .04). There is no train/test split or cross-validation.
