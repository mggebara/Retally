Methodology Summary: - **Design and sample selection:** This is a quasi-experiment built on the staggered rollout of health-sector decentralization in Honduras. Propensity score matching on all available pre-decentralization municipal data selected 31 municipalities: 21 decentralized (treated) and 10 matched centrally-administered (control). The propensity model was specified iteratively, following Imbens & Rubin (2015), and no more than 3 municipalities were taken from any one state. All analyses are **weighted by the municipal-level propensity scores**. A robustness check uses a reduced sample of 24 municipalities, dropping the 6 treated municipalities with weaker matches (SI Appendix C, Table SA1).
- **Data collection:** Daylong lab-in-the-field workshops were held in 2017 (232 participants; about 8 per workshop). Each workshop included a regional health authority representative (R), at least one administrative-intermediary representative (AI), four health-centre doctors or nurses (HC) and a mayor's-office official (M). Participants took a pre-survey, played a public goods game (PGG, the first activity) and took a post-survey with social-network prompts. Seven late arrivals are excluded from the game data, leaving 225 individuals × 10 rounds = 2,250 person-rounds.
- **PGG protocol:** The game follows Isaac & Walker (1988). Each player receives a 10-lempira endowment per round and decides privately how much to contribute. The group fund is doubled and split equally. There are 10 rounds; rounds 1–5 have no communication and rounds 6–10 allow communication before decisions. The group total is announced after each round.
- **Main cooperation model (Table 2):** Pooled OLS on the person-round panel, weighted by the propensity scores.
  - **Dependent variable:** individual contribution in the round, in lempiras (0–10, levels, not standardised).
  - **Key regressor:** a Decentralized dummy (1 = decentralized, 0 = centrally administered).
  - **Column 1** adds a Communication dummy (rounds 6–10).
  - **Column 2 (preferred)** adds Decentralized × Communication.
  - **Controls:** player-type dummies for HC, AI and R (reference category is M), round number (1–10), lagged group contribution (the previous round's group total), number of players in the session, number of people known in the room, female, age, education, years in the health sector and general trust (a survey dummy).
  - **Fixed effects:** none.
  - **Standard errors:** described as robust and accounting for clustering. The text says "accounting for the clustered characteristics"; the Table 1 tests cluster by municipality.
  - **Estimation sample:** 1,980 observations, because round 1 has no lagged group contribution and observations with missing covariates are lost.
- **Robustness for cooperation:** A multilevel Tobit model handles the censoring of contributions at 0 and 10, with random effects (grouped variance) at the individual and municipality levels (SI Appendix F, Table SA3). Other alternative specifications are in SI Appendix F.
- **Mechanism model (Table 3):** Weighted Poisson regressions at the individual level.
  - **Outcome:** the count of cross-level ties the person reports with other workshop participants, with an offset for log(total possible cross-level ties), so the model is effectively for the proportion of ties realised. Two versions are used: "All ties" (knew the person as a colleague, i.e. known) and "Strong ties" (friend).
  - **Specifications:** a base model with only the Decentralized dummy, and a full model that adds female, education, age, years in the health sector, generalized trust and player-type dummies (HC, AI and R versus M).
  - **Standard errors:** clustered by municipality.
  - **Sample:** N = 231 for the base model and 226 for the full model.
  - **Robustness (SI Appendix H):** an hours-together valued tie measure, alternative controls, and collapsing administrative levels.
- **Balance and descriptive checks (Table 1):** Weighted means by administration type, with difference-in-means tests via weighted OLS with standard errors clustered by municipality, plus standardized mean differences (SMD). Fit is reported as R² and AIC for the OLS models and BIC for the Poisson models.
- **Supplementary check:** A pairwise correlation between municipal average PGG contribution and perceived health-system improvement over 5 years, taken from a separate nationwide health-worker survey (SI Table SA16). There is no train/test split or cross-validation; this is purely inferential regression analysis.
