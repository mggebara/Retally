Methodology Summary: - **Design (Study 1):** Pre-registered (EGAP Design ID 20190905AA) choice-based conjoint experiment fielded by Dynata on nationally representative online samples of 1086 U.S. and 1074 German respondents (2160 total), giving 18,966 (U.S.) and 18,862 (Germany) unique choice tasks. After a pre-treatment sociodemographic battery (age, gender, education, religiosity, trust in government, political interest, ideology, partisanship, foreign policy orientation, empathy), each respondent saw nine pairs of randomized migrant profiles (one pair per screen), rated each profile, and chose which migrant to admit for settlement in their state.
- **Attributes and baselines (Table 1):** Seven fully randomized attributes; baselines in parentheses: Reason for Migration (Economic Opportunity; others Drought, Flooding, Wildfires, Political/Religious/Ethnic Persecution) — the focal attribute; Language Fluency (None; Broken, Fluent); Gender (Female; Male); Occupation (Unemployed; Cleaner, Teacher, Doctor); Religion (Atheist/Agnostic; Christian, Muslim — "agnostic" replaced by "atheist" in Germany); Origin (Another region in your country; Afghanistan, Ethiopia, Myanmar, Ukraine); Vulnerability (None; PTSD, Food insecurity, No surviving family members, Physically handicapped).
- **Estimator / specification:** Average marginal component effects (AMCEs; Hainmueller, Hopkins & Yamamoto 2014), estimated as a linear probability model of the binary outcome "profile chosen/preferred" (1/0, profile-level) regressed on dummy indicators for every non-baseline level of all seven attributes. Coefficients are unstandardised changes in the probability that a profile is preferred relative to the attribute's baseline level (percentage-point scale); no fixed effects or respondent covariates in the main model. Estimated separately for the U.S. and German samples.
- **Standard errors:** Robust standard errors clustered by respondent; figures show 90% confidence intervals; significance stars at p<0.1, 0.05, 0.01.
- **Samples / subsets:** Full model uses all profile pairs (Figure 1 left panel; Table 2). Subset models restrict to internal-migrant (origin = another region in your country) profile pairs (middle panel) and to international-migrant (foreign origin) profile pairs (right panel). Robustness also excludes internal migrant profiles that may seem implausible.
- **Supplementary analyses (appendix):** Conjoint diagnostic tests; marginal means (Leeper, Hobolt & Tilley 2020) as an alternative to AMCEs; heterogeneous effects by interacting Origin × Reason for Migration and by respondent subgroups (employed, college educated); a separate validation survey on perceived deservingness/self-responsibility of migrant types.
- **Study 2:** Between-subjects factorial experiment on representative U.S. and German samples: after the same pre-test, respondents were randomly assigned a mock article priming migration, climate change, or climate migration (in the respondent's country or worldwide), with a control article about soccer. Outcomes are multiple-item importance indices of support for policies to mitigate climate change and to aid (climate) migrants; treatment effects compared against control, plus correlations with respondent empathy and subgroup analyses.
