Methodology Summary: **Study 1: Conjoint Experiment**

- **Design**: Choice-based conjoint experiment embedded in nationally representative online surveys (fielded via Dynata) in the U.S. (n=1,086; 18,966 choice tasks) and Germany (n=1,074; 18,862 choice tasks). Respondents evaluated 9 pairs of randomized migrant profiles, rating each and selecting one for settlement.

- **Attributes (7)**: Reason for migration (5 levels: economic opportunity [baseline], drought, flooding, wildfires, political/religious/ethnic persecution), language fluency, gender, occupation, religion, origin (internal or Afghanistan/Ethiopia/Myanmar/Ukraine), and vulnerability. Attribute levels fully randomized.

- **Pre-test battery**: Prior to the conjoint, respondents answered sociodemographic questions (age, gender, education, religiosity, trust in government, political interest, ideology, partisanship, foreign policy orientation, empathy).

- **Estimator**: Average Marginal Component Effects (AMCEs) from linear regression of binary profile choice on attribute-level dummies (baseline levels italicized in Table 1). Marginal means also computed. Standard errors are robust and clustered by respondent. Reported with 90% confidence intervals.

- **Subsample analyses**: AMCEs re-estimated separately on internal-migrant profile pairs and international-migrant profile pairs; robustness checks include diagnostic tests, heterogeneous treatment effects (Leeper, Hobolt, and Tilley 2020), interactions of origin × reason for migration, exclusions of implausible internal-migrant profiles, and subgroup analyses by employment and college education.

**Study 2: Factorial Experiment**

- **Design**: Between-subjects factorial experiment on separate nationally representative U.S. and German samples. After the same pre-test, respondents were randomly assigned to read a mock article priming migration, climate change, or climate migration (at national or worldwide scale); control group read a soccer article.

- **Outcomes**: Multi-item indices measuring importance/support for policies addressing migration, climate change mitigation, and aiding climate migrants. Analysis examines treatment effects on these indices overall and across subgroups, and the correlation of empathy with the outcomes.

- **Pre-registration**: All hypotheses pre-registered with EGAP (Design ID: 20190905AA); IRB-approved.
