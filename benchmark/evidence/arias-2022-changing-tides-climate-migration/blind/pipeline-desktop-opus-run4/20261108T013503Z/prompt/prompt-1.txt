Methodology Summary: - **Design (Study 1):** Pre-registered (EGAP Design ID 20190905AA) choice-based conjoint experiment fielded by Dynata on nationally representative online samples: 1086 U.S. and 1074 German respondents (2160 total), giving 18,966 (U.S.) and 18,862 (Germany) unique profile observations from choice tasks. Each respondent saw 9 pairs of randomized migrant profiles, each on a new screen, after a pre-treatment sociodemographic battery (age, gender, education, religiosity, trust in government, political interest, ideology, partisanship, foreign policy orientation, empathy).
- **Randomized attributes (Table 1; baseline levels in parentheses):** Reason for migration (Economic Opportunity; also Drought, Flooding, Wildfires, Political/Religious/Ethnic Persecution) as the focal attribute; Language Fluency (None; Broken, Fluent); Gender (Female; Male); Occupation (Unemployed; Cleaner, Teacher, Doctor); Religion (Atheist/Agnostic; Christian, Muslim; "agnostic" became "atheist" in Germany); Origin (Another region in your country; Afghanistan, Ethiopia, Myanmar, Ukraine); Vulnerability (None; PTSD, Food insecurity, No surviving family members, Physically handicapped).
- **Outcome:** Binary forced choice: whether the profile is the one the respondent prefers to admit for settlement in their state (respondents also rated each profile). Unit of analysis is the profile within a choice task.
- **Estimator:** Average marginal component effects (AMCEs; Hainmueller, Hopkins & Yamamoto 2014), estimated as a linear probability model of the chosen-profile indicator on dummy indicators for every non-baseline level of all seven attributes (full model, all attributes entered together). Coefficients are unstandardised changes in the probability a profile is chosen relative to the baseline level. No fixed effects or respondent covariates in the main specification. Estimated separately for the U.S. and German samples.
- **Standard errors / inference:** Robust standard errors clustered by respondent; Figure 1 shows 90% confidence intervals; Table 2 stars at p<0.1, 0.05, 0.01.
- **Samples / subsets:** Main results (Table 2; left panel of Figure 1) use all profile pairs. Subset analyses limit to internal-migrant (within-country origin) profile pairs (middle panel) and international-migrant profile pairs (right panel). Robustness checks in the appendix: marginal means (Leeper, Hobolt & Tilley 2020), diagnostic tests, origin x reason interactions, excluding implausible internal-migrant profiles, and heterogeneity by respondent employment and college education.
- **Study 2:** Between-subjects factorial experiment on representative U.S. and German samples: after the same pre-test, respondents read a mock article priming migration, climate change, or climate migration (in the respondent's country or worldwide), with a control article about soccer. Outcomes are multiple-item importance indices of support for policies addressing each issue (e.g., climate change mitigation, aiding climate migrants); treatment effects are compared with control, and empathy is examined as a correlate and across subgroups.
- **No ML components:** no train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning; evaluation is by AMCE point estimates and their statistical significance against the economic-migrant baseline (H1: climate > economic; H2: climate < persecuted).
