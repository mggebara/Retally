Methodology Summary: - **Design / data (Study 1):** Choice-based (forced-choice, paired-profile) conjoint experiment fielded by Dynata on nationally representative online samples: 1,086 U.S. and 1,074 German respondents (2,160 total), each shown 9 pairs of randomized migrant profiles, giving 18,966 (U.S.) and 18,862 (Germany) profile-level observations ("unique choice tasks"). Pre-registered with EGAP (Design ID 20190905AA). A pre-treatment battery measured age, gender, education, religiosity, trust in government, political interest, ideology, partisanship, foreign-policy orientation and empathy (used for subgroup analyses, not as controls in the main AMCE models).
- **Attributes and baselines (Table 1; baseline listed first):** Reason for migration (Economic opportunity [baseline], Drought, Flooding, Wildfires, Political/Religious/Ethnic persecution — focal attribute); Language fluency (None, Broken, Fluent); Gender (Female, Male); Occupation (Unemployed, Cleaner, Teacher, Doctor); Religion (Atheist/Agnostic, Christian, Muslim); Origin (Another region in your country, Afghanistan, Ethiopia, Myanmar, Ukraine); Vulnerability (None, PTSD, Food insecurity, No surviving family members, Physically handicapped). All attributes fully and independently randomized.
- **Estimand / estimating equation:** Average Marginal Component Effect (AMCE; Hainmueller, Hopkins & Yamamoto 2014), estimated as a linear probability model (OLS) of a binary outcome — whether the profile was chosen/preferred for settlement in the respondent's state (1 = chosen, 0 = not) — on dummy indicators for all non-baseline levels of all seven attributes (no interactions in the main model, no respondent covariates or fixed effects). Coefficients are unstandardised changes in the probability (percentage points/100) that a profile is preferred relative to the baseline level.
- **Samples / estimation:** Models estimated separately for the U.S. and German samples. Main ("full") model uses all profiles (Figure 1 left panel; Table 2). Subset models: internal-migrant profile pairs only (origin = "another region in your country") and international-migrant profile pairs only (Figure 1 middle/right panels). No train/test split or cross-validation (experimental inference).
- **Standard errors / inference:** Robust standard errors clustered by respondent; Table 2 stars at *** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.1; Figure 1 shows 90% confidence intervals.
- **Headline results (Table 2, U.S. / Germany AMCE vs. economic opportunity):** Drought 0.035 (SE 0.012) / 0.081 (0.012); Flooding 0.037 (0.012) / 0.086 (0.012); Wildfires 0.040 (0.011) / 0.060 (0.012); Persecution 0.076 (0.012) / 0.162 (0.013). Other attributes e.g. Fluent 0.104/0.140, Male −0.044/−0.050, Doctor 0.187/0.179, Muslim −0.047/−0.085.
- **Robustness (appendix):** marginal means (P(prefer economic) ≈ 0.45–0.48; climate ≈ 0.48–0.53; refugee ≈ 0.53–0.60), conjoint diagnostics, origin × reason interactions, subgroup/heterogeneous effects (Leeper, Hobolt & Tilley 2020; e.g., employed, college-educated), and excluding implausible internal-migrant profiles.
- **Study 2:** Between-subjects factorial survey experiment on representative U.S. and German samples; respondents randomly assigned to a mock article priming migration, climate change, or climate migration (in-country or worldwide) vs. a control article about soccer; outcomes are multi-item importance indices of support for climate-change mitigation and aiding climate migrants; treatment effects (difference in means/regression vs. control) found null, with empathy positively correlated with outcomes.
