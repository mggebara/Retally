## Paper Information
Title: American Economic Review 2022, 112(3): 1–36
Abstract (first 500 chars): 1 Local Elites as State Capacity: How City Chiefs Use Local Information to Increase Tax Compliance in the Democratic Republic of the Congo† By Pablo Balán, Augustin Bergeron, Gabriel Tourek, and Jonathan L. Weigel*
Methodology Summary: - **Design / identification:** A cluster-randomized policy experiment run within Kasaï-Central's 2018 property tax campaign in Kananga (DRC). The unit of randomization is the neighborhood, defined from a satellite map to approximate the *localité*. There are 364 neighborhoods; 8 were excluded because they hosted a logistics pilot, and the commune of Nganza was excluded because of conflict. That leaves 356 neighborhoods and 45,162 registered properties. Neighborhoods were assigned to five arms (Table 2): Central (110, state tax-ministry collectors), Local (111, city chiefs), CLI (80, state collectors after a half-day consultation with the chief), CXL (50, a state collector and a chief together) and pure Control (5, the old declarative system). The randomization is blocked and stratified on (i) geographic location, (ii) treatment status in the 2016 campaign and (iii) the chief's past tax-collection experience. Following Banerjee et al. (2017), the full randomization was run 100 times and the run with the minimum t-statistics on balance checks of 8 neighborhood-level baseline variables was kept. The main comparison is Local vs. Central. Analysis uses de facto assignment: one CXL neighborhood was implemented as Local.
- **Data / outcomes:** The main outcomes come from administrative data. Receipts from the handheld printers, plus payments made at the ministry, are matched by tax ID to the property register covering the universe of registered properties. Outcomes are household-level tax compliance (a paid/did-not-pay indicator) and revenue in Congolese francs per property. Secondary outcomes come from three surveys: a baseline of 4,246 households (Jul–Dec 2017, about 12 per neighborhood, every Xth house), a midline at every property 2–4 weeks after collection (36,130 completed) and an endline (Mar–Sep 2019) that re-tracked 3,893 baseline respondents.
- **Estimating equation (eq. 1), OLS:** y_ijkt = β0 + β·Local_jkt + X_ijk·Γ + α_k + θ_t + ε_ijkt, where i indexes households/properties, j neighborhoods, k randomization strata and t campaign periods. The dependent variable enters in levels (a linear probability model for compliance, CF for revenue). The key regressor is the Local treatment dummy, with Central as the omitted category. The coefficients are in raw units (percentage points for compliance, CF for revenue) and are not standardized.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Every specification includes randomization-stratum fixed effects (α_k). The main analyses' covariate vector X contains only dummies for house type, i.e. the low- vs. high-value property band. The preferred specification for tax outcomes (Table 4, column 2) also includes time fixed effects (θ_t) for the waves of the staggered tax campaign, to net out time trends and time imbalance across arms. Column 1 is the same regression without the wave fixed effects. Endline-survey outcomes drop both the wave fixed effects and the house-type dummies.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the neighborhood level, the unit of randomization (356 clusters).
- **Sample restrictions:** Pure Control neighborhoods are excluded from the treatment comparisons. About 285 very-high-value "villa" properties fall outside the campaign and the evaluation. The main estimation sample is all registered properties in the compared arms, including exempt properties. Exempt properties (state-owned, schools/churches, owners who are elderly (55+), widowed or disabled, and houses under construction) are dropped only in a robustness column.
- **Robustness (Table 4 and online appendix):** (col. 3) data collapsed to the neighborhood level; (col. 4) property-value-band fixed effects added; (col. 5) exempt properties excluded. Online Appendix Table A5 uses alternative adjustments for time imbalance. Table A6 controls for, and interacts treatment with, the cross-randomized collector-wage scheme (a 30% piece rate vs. a flat 750 CF) and the household-level tax-rate abatements. Table A7 adds pre-analysis-plan covariate sets, such as controlling for the imbalanced variable years of education, and drops the misassigned neighborhood. Table A8 controls for collector characteristics.
- **Balance and attrition checks:** Table 3 regresses baseline, midline, registration and neighborhood characteristics on treatment indicators, with stratum fixed effects and neighborhood-clustered SEs and Central omitted. Online Appendix Table A2 adds omnibus parametric F-tests for each bilateral treatment comparison, run separately by data source. Attrition (baseline to endline, replacement at endline, registration to midline) is tested the same way.
- **Mechanisms:** The analysis compares CLI to Central to test whether the chief's local information explains the effect; in CLI the chief rated each household's ability and willingness to pay on 3-point scales. It examines collector visits on the extensive and intensive margins and compares the characteristics of visited households across arms. It also tests whether chiefs outperform during the registration stage, when route-based visiting neutralized targeting. Heterogeneity is examined by chief knowledge (from a quiz module), baseline trust in and power of the chief, and the cross-randomized tax-notice messages.
