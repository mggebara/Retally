## Paper Information
Title: EVALUATING DELIBERATIVE COMPETENCE:
Abstract (first 500 chars): We examine methods for evaluating opportunity-neutral interventions designed to improve the 
quality of decision making in settings where people imperfectly comprehend consequences. In an 
experiment involving financial education, conventional outcome metrics (financial literacy and 
directional changes in behavior) imply that two interventions, one with practice and feedback, 
one without, are equally beneficial even though only the first reduces average bias. We trace 
these evaluative failure
Methodology Summary: - **Design / data:** Two online randomized experiments run on Amazon Mechanical Turk (US residents aged 18+; Experiment B also required at least 500 completed HITs and an approval rating of at least 98%). Experiment A had 504 subjects in 8 sessions (Apr–May 2014) and four arms: Control (index-funds video), Treatment (compound-interest video based on Malkiel & Ellis 2013, including the rule of 72), Substance-Only and Rhetoric-Only. Experiment B had 401 subjects in 2 sessions (Oct 2018) and two arms: Treatment (the same video split into 3 parts, with 6 practice problems and individualized feedback) and Control (a portfolio-diversification module with practice and feedback). Each subject took part in exactly one arm of one experiment. The two experiments are analysed separately and never compared statistically with each other.
- **Valuation tasks (Stage 2):** Each subject completed 10 pairs of tasks. In each, the subject's immediate dollar equivalent of a future reward R ∈ {$20, 18, 16, 14, 12} paid after t ∈ {36, 72} days was elicited with an iterated multiple price list (resolution $0.20). Each reward appeared once in a simple frame ("We will pay you $R in t days") and once in a complex frame (investing $a at X% daily interest, compounded daily; parameters in Table 2). Task order was randomized at the subject level. Valuations are interval-coded and the interval midpoint is used. Each valuation is normalized as r = r̃/R and expressed in percentage points (0–100).
- **Knowledge outcome (Stage 3):** An incentivized test with 5 compound-interest questions (Table 3) and 5 questions on the control-module material. Scores are counted out of 5 for each block.
- **Sample restriction:** Subjects who switched two or more times in any price list are dropped (49 in Experiment A, 53 in Experiment B), leaving 803 subjects who respected monotonicity. Estimation samples: Experiment A Treatment vs. Control has 215 subjects (2,150 valuation observations; 1,075 per delay). Experiment A with all four arms has 455 subjects (4,550 observations). Experiment B has 348 subjects (3,480 observations).
- **Estimating equations (conventional metrics):** OLS regressions of an outcome on a full set of treatment-arm indicators with no constant, so each coefficient is an arm mean, plus a "Difference" (Treatment − Control) and p-values for each arm against Control. Outcomes are: (a) the subject-level test score out of 5 (Table 4, columns 1–4; Table 6, columns 1–2); (b) the normalized complex-frame valuation r^c at the task level, pooled over delays and separately for t = 72 and t = 36 (Table 5, columns 1–6); (c) the complex- and simple-frame valuations for all four Experiment A arms (Table 6, columns 3–4). The headline specifications have no additional controls. Robustness checks with controls are in Appendix D. Table 2 notes that the analysis controls for the gap between the rule-of-72 and exact future values.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered by subject for regressions with multiple valuation observations per subject (Tables 5 and 6). Table 4's subject-level test-score regressions report conventional OLS standard errors. Randomization balance is checked with F-tests on 68 demographic characteristics, and differential attrition is reported.
- **Proposed metric (deliberative competence):** For each subject i and task pair (R, t), d = −|r^c − r^s|, the negative absolute price-metric bias in percentage points, so that higher values mean better decisions. It is averaged over each subject's tasks, then over subjects, and compared across arms. The distributions of the valuation difference r^c − r^s are shown as empirical CDFs by arm (Figure 3).
- **Evaluation protocol:** Interventions are assessed with three metrics: (1) test scores, (2) directional shifts in complex-frame valuations, and (3) deliberative competence. Rhetoric-Only and Substance-Only are used to separate the effects of substance from rhetoric on knowledge and on behaviour. There is no train/test split or cross-validation, since this is a randomized-experiment design.
