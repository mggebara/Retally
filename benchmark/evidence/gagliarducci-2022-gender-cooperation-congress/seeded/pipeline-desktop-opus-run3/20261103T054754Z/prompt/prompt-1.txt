Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis**: The unit is the bill. The sample covers all House public bills (simple, concurrent and joint resolutions and private bills are excluded) from the 101st Congress (elected 1988) to the 111th (elected 2008). Bills come from THOMAS and are cross-checked against Adler–Wilkerson's Congressional Bills Project and Fowler's cosponsorship data, which also supply the topic and passage outcomes. The bills are linked to sponsor biographies (ICPSR up to the 104th Congress, then the Biographical Directory), to House Clerk election returns and to Census district characteristics.
- **Outcome variables**: There are two cooperation measures. (a) Coalition building: the total number of cosponsors on a bill, in levels; the paper does not say it is logged or standardised. (b) Bipartisanship: the percent of cosponsors from the opposite party, out of the number of cosponsors plus 1 (the sponsor), so that bills with zero cosponsors are kept. The paper says results are robust to dividing by the number of cosponsors alone, or to using the count of opposite-party cosponsors with the number of cosponsors as a control.
- **Baseline OLS specification (eq. 4, Table 3)**: Y_ijkt = α + β·Female_i + c_t + Bill_j + Spons_it + Distr_kt + ε_ijkt. It is estimated for the pooled sample and separately for Democrats and Republicans. The columns build up step by step: (1) Congress (time) fixed effects only; (2) adds bill-category dummies, i.e. 226 minor-topic dummies (Adler–Wilkerson classification) and dummies for the 33 committees of referral; (3) adds sponsor controls instead: tenure, a rookie dummy, age, a committee chair or ranking member dummy, an Ivy League dummy, 5 previous-occupation dummies and the total number of bills sponsored; (4) adds district controls only: 3 macro-region dummies, % black, % urban, % foreign-born, % over 65, log median income and log population density; (5) the preferred specification, with all bill, individual and district controls. Coefficients are unstandardised: a difference in the number of cosponsors, or in percentage points of opposite-party cosponsors.
- **Supporting regressions**: Table 1 regresses the probability that a bill passes the House on the number of cosponsors, the % of opposite-party cosponsors and their interaction. Column 2 adds interactions with a female dummy; the joint test that all three interactions are zero gives p = 0.12. Figure 1's predicted Republican vote share comes from an OLS regression of the actual Republican vote share on the district controls. Specifications with district fixed effects were tried but gave imprecise estimates because gender varies little within districts. The paper also reports that 10 more cosponsors raise the probability of passing the House by 0.67 percentage points and of becoming law by 0.19 percentage points.
- **Regression discontinuity (eq. 5)**: The RD sample is limited to representatives elected in mixed-gender (male vs female) races. The specification is Y = α + β·Female + Female×f(MV) + f(MV) + c_t + ε, where MV is the female candidate's margin of victory over the male candidate, with separate polynomials on each side of the cutoff at 0. It is run pooled and by party. A McCrary density test does not reject continuity in the pooled sample, but there is a strong discontinuity for Republicans, with male winners much more common, so the authors caution that the party-specific RD rests on stronger assumptions.
- **Matching**: Matching is also used as a robustness approach to selection of women into districts. Its details (bandwidth or polynomial order for the RD, the matching covariates, the inference method) are not in the text provided.
- **Inference and standard errors**: The text provided does not say how standard errors are computed (robust or clustered, and on what). Significance is reported at conventional levels. Since there are many bills per sponsor, clustering by Congress member is likely but is not stated.
- **Additional analyses** (described in the introduction): gender of the cosponsors (women attract more female cosponsors); seniority, status and influence of the cosponsors; heterogeneity by sponsor seniority; and heterogeneity by bill topic (women's-issues bills).

* women_friend2: identify minor topics where the fraction of female *cosponsors* is above some threshold (which threshold?)

* women_friend2: identify minor topics where the fraction of female *cosponsors* is above the 75th percentile
* Important: the 75th percentile means the 75th percentile of the distribution of minor topics (227 observations overall)
egen tag_minor = tag(minor)

egen x = mean(pct_cosponsors_fem) if tag_bill==1, by(minor)

sum pct_sponsors_fem_min if tag_minor==1, d

label var women_friend3 "1 if topic where fraction of F *sponsors* > 75th pctile"
drop x

* identify cosponsored women-friendly bills by the opposite party, and by women
forvalues j = 1/3 {
   * identify the number of women-friendly bills cosponsored
   egen x = count(HRnumber) if women_friend`j'==1, by(v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served)
   bysort v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served: egen nwfbills_cosponsored`j'=max(x)
   replace nwfbills_cosponsored`j'=0 if nwfbills_cosponsored`j'==.
   drop x

   * calculate the percentage of women_friendly bills cosponsored that were sponsored by the opposite party, 
   * out of the total number of women-friendly bills cosponsored
   egen x = sum(cosponsor_opposite_party) if women_friend`j'==1, by(v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served)
   bysort v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served: egen nwfbills_cosponsored_opposite`j'=max(x)
   replace nwfbills_cosponsored_opposite`j'=0 if nwfbills_cosponsored_opposite`j'==.
   drop x
   gen pctwfbills_cosponsored_opposite`j' = nwfbills_cosponsored_opposite`j'/(nwfbills_cosponsored`j')

   * calculate the percentage of women_friendly bills cosponsored that were sponsored by a female, 
   * out of the total number of women-friendly bills cosponsored
   egen x = sum(sponsor_female) if women_friend`j'==1, by(v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served)
   bysort v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served: egen nwfbills_cosponsored_female`j'=max(x)
   replace nwfbills_cosponsored_female`j'=0 if nwfbills_cosponsored_female`j'==.
   drop x
   gen pctwfbills_cosponsored_female`j' = nwfbills_cosponsored_female`j'/(nwfbills_cosponsored`j')

   * calculate the percentage of women_friendly bills cosponsored that were sponsored by a female opposite, 
   * out of the total number of women-friendly bills cosponsored
   egen x = sum(sponsor_female_opp) if women_friend`j'==1, by(v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served)
   bysort v2 cosponsor_state_abbrev cosponsor_district cosponsor_term_served: egen nwfbills_cosponsored_femopp`j'=max(x)
   replace nwfbills_cosponsored_femopp`j'=0 if nwfbills_cosponsored_femopp`j'==.
   drop x
   gen pctwfbills_cosponsored_femopp`j' = nwfbills_cosponsored_femopp`j'/(nwfbills_cosponsored`j')
}

	version 15.1
	syntax [, nobs(integer 10000) beta_a(real 1.0) beta_b(real 1.0) rho_x(real 0.7) /*
	*/        zbar_R(real 0.5) zbar_D(real -0.5) /*
	*/        alpha_R(real 0.5) alpha_D(real 0.5) /*
	*/        kappa_ksi_R(real 0.0) beta_ksi_R(real 10.0) kappa_ksi_D(real 0.0) beta_ksi_D(real 10.0) /*
	*/        phi0_R(real -1.0) phi1_R(real -1.0) phi0_D(real -1.0) phi1_D(real -1.0)   /*
	*/        kappa_u(real 0.0) beta_u(real 1.0)   /*
	*/        gamma0(real 0.0)  gamma1(real -5.0) gamma2(real 0.0) gamma3(real 0.0)/*
	*/        tau0(real 0.3) tau1(real -1.0)  tau2(real 0.0)]
	drop _all
	set obs `nobs'
	
	* Overall district ideology
    gen z = 2*(rbeta(`beta_a',`beta_b')-0.5)
	gen x = z + (sqrt((1-`rho_x'^2)/`rho_x'^2))*(2*(rbeta(`beta_a',`beta_b')-0.5))
	
	* Ideology of R and candidates: weighted average of national party and local ideology, plus noise	
	gen z_R = `alpha_R'*`zbar_R' + (1-`alpha_R')*z + `kappa_ksi_R'*(rbeta(`beta_ksi_R',`beta_ksi_R')-0.5)
	gen z_D = `alpha_D'*`zbar_D' + (1-`alpha_D')*z + `kappa_ksi_D'*(rbeta(`beta_ksi_D',`beta_ksi_D')-0.5)

	* Gender is correlated with candidate ideology
	gen byte female_D = rnormal(`phi0_D' + `phi1_D'*z_D)>0
	gen byte female_R = rnormal(`phi0_R' + `phi1_R'*z_R)>0
	
	* Voteshare depends on ideology of the candidates plus noise
	gen u = `kappa_u'*(rbeta(`beta_u', `beta_u')-0.5)
	gen voteshare_D = (exp(`gamma0' + `gamma1'*(z - (z_D+z_R)/2) + `gamma2'*female_D - `gamma3'*female_R + u)/    /*
	*/                (1+ exp(`gamma0' + `gamma1'*(z - (z_D+z_R)/2) + `gamma2'*female_D  - `gamma3'*female_R + u )))

	replace voteshare_female = (1-voteshare_D) if female_D==0 & female_R==1
