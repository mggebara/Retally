Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample**: Bill-level panel of all House public bills (simple and concurrent resolutions, and joint resolutions, are excluded) introduced from the 101st Congress (elected 1988) through the 111th Congress (elected 2008). Bills come from THOMAS and are cross-validated against the Congressional Bills Project (Adler & Wilkerson) and the Fowler et al. cosponsorship data. They are linked to sponsor biographical data (ICPSR up to the 104th Congress, then the Biographical Directory), to House election returns (Clerk of the House) and to district census characteristics (U.S. Census Summary Files via ICPSR). The unit of observation is bill j sponsored by member i, elected at time t in district k.
- **Outcome variables (unstandardised levels)**: (a) the total number of cosponsors on the bill, a measure of coalition building; (b) the percent of cosponsors from the opposite party, out of the total number of bill supporters (cosponsors + 1 for the sponsor), so that bills with zero cosponsors are kept. Robustness checks divide by the number of cosponsors only, or use the number of opposite-party cosponsors as the outcome with the number of cosponsors as a control.
- **Baseline OLS specification (eq. 4, Table 3)**: Y_ijkt = α + β·Female_i + c_t + Bill_j + Spons_it + Distr_kt + ε_ijkt, estimated in levels with a female-sponsor dummy as the key regressor. Each specification is run for all members pooled and then separately for Democrats and Republicans. Columns add controls step by step: (1) Congress (time) fixed effects only; (2) + bill category dummies (226 mutually exclusive minor topics from congressionalbills.org and 33 committee-of-referral dummies); (3) + individual controls: tenure, rookie dummy, age, a dummy for committee chair or ranking member, Ivy League dummy, five previous-occupation dummies, and total number of bills sponsored; (4) Congress FE + district controls only: three macro-region dummies, % black, % urban, % foreign-born, % over 65, log median income, log population density; (5) the preferred, full specification with Congress FE plus all bill, individual and district controls. District fixed effects were tried but dropped as uninformative because there is little within-district variation in gender.
- **Model-assumption checks (Table 1)**: A regression of a dummy for the bill passing the House on the number of cosponsors, the % of opposite-party cosponsors and their interaction (column 1). Column 2 interacts all three with a female-sponsor dummy and tests whether the three interaction terms are jointly zero (F-test). Auxiliary measures are a predicted Republican vote share, from an OLS regression of the actual Republican share on the district controls listed above (used in Figure 1), the first dimension of DW-NOMINATE, and the Party Unity Score.
- **Regression discontinuity (eq. 5)**: The sample is restricted to members elected in mixed-gender (male vs. female) races. The specification is Y = α + Female + Female·f(MV) + f(MV) + c_t + ε, where MV is the female candidate's margin of victory over the male candidate (threshold at 0) and f(·) is a flexible polynomial allowed to differ on each side of the cutoff, with Congress fixed effects. It is run pooled and by party. A McCrary density test of the running variable is run for the pooled sample and separately by party. The authors warn that the by-party RD comparisons rest on stronger assumptions.
- **Matching**: Used as an additional approach to selection of women into districts, matching female-sponsored to male-sponsored bills/members on observed district and individual characteristics. It is reported alongside OLS and RD as a robustness check.
- **Inference and evaluation**: There is no train/test split or cross-validation. Evaluation compares the sign, size and significance of the female coefficient across specifications and methods, and across parties. Standard errors are not described in detail in the text available; given the bill-level data with repeated sponsors, they are presumably clustered at the sponsor (Congress member) level.
- **Heterogeneity analyses**: Female cosponsor recruitment, the seniority/status/influence of recruited cosponsors, interactions with sponsor seniority, and splits by whether a bill concerns women's issues.
