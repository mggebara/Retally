Title: 1
Methodology: - **Data and sample:** Spanish Household Budget Survey (HBS, INE) microdata. The sample is limited to households interviewed between August 2011 and April 2014, so that no other major events (such as the April 2014 price change) fall inside the window. This gives a rotating two-year household panel of 25,775 observations. Each household is interviewed twice, in the same two-week period of consecutive years. Sampling is stratified into 26 two-week subsamples per year. Electricity consumption (kWh) and expenditure (€) come from the last paid bill (the month before the interview), scaled up to annual by the number of bills per year. The household price is p = expenditure/consumption in €/kWh, including all taxes. The estimation sample in Table 2 has 20,872 observations from 10,729 households.
- **Identification (difference-in-differences):** The reform took effect on 1 Aug 2013: the fixed charge F rose 112% and the regulated volumetric access rate f fell about 36%. Households are grouped by interview dates. In the control group (D=0) both interviews fell before the reform. In the treated group (D=1) the first interview was before the reform and the second after it. The treatment variable is T = 1 for post-reform interviews (only treated households' second interview), and its coefficient δ is the DiD estimator. The paper treats the interview date as exogenous, because the survey design makes the subsamples balanced on observables. Balance is checked with Table 1 and pre-reform QQ-plots of covariates (Fig. 4).
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 4, log-log demand):** ln(q_it) = α + β ln(p_it) + β ln(y_it) + γX_it + δT_it + θ_i + φ_t + ε_it.
  - Dependent variable: ln electricity consumption (kWh) in models 1–4, and ln electricity expenditure in models 5–8, with the same specification.
  - Regressors: ln(price per kWh), ln(income) and ln(income)², and "months since reform".
  - Headline coefficients are unstandardised log points, read as approximate percentage changes.
- **Controls, fixed effects and standard errors:**
  - X includes household size, number of rooms, surface (m²), elderly dummy, education of the household head (4 levels), economic situation (number of active members, 3 levels), housing tenure (renting), building older than 25 years, municipality size dummies (5), urban dummy, province-capital dummy and regional dummies.
  - Quarterly dummies capture seasonality and weather. Weather is controlled through regional × time (quarter) dummies.
  - Year fixed effects are in all models. Household fixed effects are added in columns 3, 4, 7 and 8, estimated by LSDV.
  - Standard errors are robust and clustered at the household level.
- **Estimators (Table 2):**
  - OLS: columns 1 and 5.
  - IV-LIML: columns 2 and 6.
  - Household-FE LSDV: columns 3 and 7.
  - FE + IV-LIML: columns 4 and 8. These are the preferred specifications.
  - ln(price) is instrumented with household effective prices (expenditure/quantity) of potatoes, water and fuel. This gives 3 instruments for 1 endogenous regressor, so the model is overidentified.
  - Diagnostics: first-stage F, Kleibergen-Paap rk LM (df 3), Cragg-Donald Wald F against the Stock-Yogo LIML 10% critical value of 6.46, Montiel Olea–Pflueger effective F, and Sargan/Hansen J (df 2).
  - Headline estimates of the Reform (T) effect on consumption: −0.178 (OLS), −0.123 (IV), −0.154 (FE), −0.123 (FE-IV, SE 0.054).
  - Headline estimates on expenditure: −0.156, −0.090, −0.098, −0.089.
- **Robustness on a cross-section (Table 3):** the sample is restricted to the reform year 2013 only. Households treated in 2014 are excluded.
  - Models are run without and with controls, on 18,770 and 14,894 observations respectively.
  - Standard errors are clustered at the two-week interview period.
  - Reform (T) estimates: −0.124 and −0.096 for consumption, −0.082 and −0.078 for expenditure.
- **Distributional analysis:**
  - Quantile regressions (Koenker–Bassett) are run on the 2013 cross-section with controls (N = 14,894), at deciles q10–q90. Table 4 covers consumption and Table 5 covers expenditure. Standard errors are bootstrapped with 50 replications.
  - Progressivity is measured with the Suits and Kakwani indices, using Gini indices and concentration curves of electricity expenditure against total household expenditure, for treated and control households.
  - Electricity's share of total expenditure is shown by income decile.
- **Not applicable:** no train/test split or cross-validation is used. Evaluation rests on the significance and magnitude of δ (stars: *** p<0.01, ** p<0.05, * p<0.1 in Table 2; *** p<0.001, ** p<0.01, * p<0.05 in Tables 3–5) and on the IV diagnostics above.

liml <- function(dep, fes) {
  vars <- c(dep, "lnp", exog, insts)
  M <- as.matrix(d[, ..vars])
  storage.mode(M) <- "double"
  fdf <- as.data.frame(d[, ..fes])
  M <- as.matrix(fixest::demean(M, fdf))
  colnames(M) <- vars
  y <- M[, dep]
  ex <- exog
  # drop exogenous columns that vanished or are collinear after demeaning
  ex <- ex[apply(M[, ex, drop = FALSE], 2, function(x) var(x) > 1e-10)]
  q0 <- qr(M[, ex, drop = FALSE]); ex <- ex[q0$pivot[seq_len(q0$rank)]]
  if (!("Tr" %in% ex)) ex <- unique(c("Tr", ex))
  X <- M[, c("lnp", ex), drop = FALSE]
  W <- M[, ex, drop = FALSE]
  Z <- M[, c(ex, insts), drop = FALSE]
  Y0 <- cbind(y, M[, "lnp"])
  qW <- qr(W); qZ <- qr(Z)
  MW <- qr.resid(qW, Y0); MZ <- qr.resid(qZ, Y0)
  A <- crossprod(MW); B <- crossprod(MZ)
  k <- min(Re(eigen(solve(B, A))$values))
  MZX <- qr.resid(qZ, X)
  XX <- crossprod(X) - k * crossprod(MZX, X)
  Xy <- crossprod(X, y) - k * crossprod(MZX, y)
  b <- drop(solve(XX, Xy)); names(b) <- colnames(X)
  u <- y - drop(X %*% b)
  Xh <- X - k * MZX
  bread <- solve(crossprod(Xh, X))
  sc <- rowsum(Xh * u, d$id)
  V <- bread %*% crossprod(sc) %*% t(bread)
  G <- nrow(sc); n <- nrow(X)
  V <- V * G / (G - 1)
  se <- sqrt(diag(V)); names(se) <- colnames(X)
  list(coef = b, se = se, kappa = k)
}
