Title: 1
Methodology: - **Design / identification:** A quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design built on the August 2013 Spanish electricity tariff reform. The fixed charge F rose 112% and the regulated volumetric charge f fell about 36%. Data come from the Spanish Household Budget Survey (HBS, INE). The HBS interviews 26 independently stratified two-week subsamples each year, and each household is interviewed twice, in the same two-week period of consecutive years. Treated households (D=1) have their first interview before the reform date R (1 Aug 2013) and their second after it (i1 < R < i2). Control households (D=0) have both interviews before R. The treatment variable T = 1 for interviews held after the reform. The estimated coefficient on T is δ, the DiD estimate of the average treatment effect. Because bills arrive with a lag, the effective response is counted from the first reformed bill (about one month after the reform).
- **Sample:** Households interviewed between August 2011 and April 2014. The sample stops in April 2014 because a later regulatory change cut prices, and the window avoids other confounding events. The panel has 25,775 observations. The Table 2 estimation sample is 20,872 observations on 10,729 households. Electricity consumption (kWh) and expenditure (€) come from the last paid bill, covering the month before the interview, multiplied by the number of bills per year. The price is the household's effective price, ln(expenditure/kWh), including all taxes. Instrument prices can only be built for households that bought those goods in the period.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 4, log-log structural demand model):** ln(q_it) = α + β ln(p_it) + β ln(y_it) + γX_it + δT_it + θ_i + φ_t + ε_it. The dependent variable is ln(kWh); the same specification is re-run with ln(electricity expenditure) as the dependent variable. Income enters as ln(income) and ln(income)². The table also includes a "months since reform" variable. The coefficient on T reads as an approximate percentage change. Coefficients are in natural units (log points / elasticities). Nothing in the paper says they are standardised.
- **Controls / fixed effects:** X contains household size; number of rooms and surface (m²); an elderly dummy; head's education (4 levels); household economic situation (number of active members, 3 levels); housing tenure (renting); building age >25 years; municipality size dummies (5 levels); an urban-area dummy; a province-capital dummy; and regional dummies. Quarterly dummies absorb seasonality and weather, together with the regional dummies. Year fixed effects are included in all columns. Household fixed effects are added in the LSDV/within columns (3, 4, 7, 8).
- **Estimators (Table 2):** Columns 1 and 5 are OLS. Columns 2 and 6 are IV. Columns 3 and 7 are LSDV with household FE. Columns 4 and 8 are household-FE IV and are the preferred specifications. The IV is LIML, instrumenting ln(electricity price) with the effective unit prices (expenditure/quantity) of potatoes, water and fuel. With 3 instruments the model is over-identified with 2 degrees of freedom. Reported diagnostics are the first-stage F, the Kleibergen-Paap rk LM test, the Cragg-Donald Wald F (against the Stock-Yogo LIML 10% critical value), the Montiel Olea-Pflueger effective F, and the Sargan and Hansen J tests. Headline results: the reform coefficient on consumption is −0.154 (col 3) and −0.123 (col 4, SE 0.054); on expenditure it is −0.089 (col 8).
- **Standard errors:** Robust and clustered at the household level for Table 2.
- **Robustness (Table 3):** A cross-section restricted to 2013 only, which excludes households treated in 2014. It is estimated by OLS with and without controls, with N = 18,770 without controls and 14,894 with controls. SEs are clustered at the two-week interview period. Headline estimates: T = −0.096 for consumption and −0.078 for expenditure (with controls). Appendix Tables A2 and A3 report interactions of T with income and other variables.
- **Distributional analysis:** Quantile regressions (Koenker-Bassett) on the 2013 sample (N = 14,894, controls included) at deciles q10–q90, for ln consumption (Table 4) and ln expenditure (Table 5). SEs are bootstrapped with 50 replications. Incidence is assessed with Suits and Kakwani progressivity indices, computed from Gini indices and concentration curves of electricity expenditure versus total expenditure for treated and control households. There is no train/test split or cross-validation; the paper uses in-sample regression inference only.

liml <- function(y, x, fe) {
  res1 <- function(var) {
    m <- feols(as.formula(paste0(var, " ~ ", rhs, " | ", fe)), data = S, notes = FALSE, warn = FALSE,
               fixef.rm = "none")
    resid(m, na.rm = FALSE)
  }
  yt <- res1(y); xt <- res1(x)
  Zt <- sapply(zuse, res1)
  okr <- complete.cases(cbind(yt, xt, Zt))
  yt <- yt[okr]; xt <- xt[okr]; Zt <- as.matrix(Zt)[okr, , drop = FALSE]
  Mz <- function(u) lm.fit(Zt, u)$residuals
  Y <- cbind(yt, xt); MY <- apply(Y, 2, Mz)
  A <- crossprod(Y); B <- crossprod(MY)
  kappa <- min(Re(eigen(solve(B, A))$values))
  beta <- (sum(xt * yt) - kappa * sum(MY[, 2] * yt)) / (sum(xt * xt) - kappa * sum(MY[, 2] * xt))
  fs_r <- lm.fit(Zt, xt)
  rss1 <- sum(fs_r$residuals^2); rss0 <- sum(xt^2)
  Fst <- ((rss0 - rss1) / ncol(Zt)) / (rss1 / (length(xt) - ncol(Zt)))
  S2 <- S
  S2$yadj__ <- S2[[y]] - beta * S2[[x]]
  m2 <- feols(as.formula(paste0("yadj__ ~ ", rhs, " | ", fe)), data = S2, cluster = ~id,
              fixef.rm = "none")
  list(beta = beta, kappa = kappa, delta = unname(coef(m2)["Tr"]), F = Fst)
}
