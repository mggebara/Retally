Abstract (first 500 chars): QUASI-EXPERIMENT Xavier Labandeira, José M. Labeaga, Jordi J. Teixidó* August 5, 2021 The global energy mix and cost structure of the power industry are experiencing a redefinition. Many countries are revamping electricity-pricing systems to guarantee fixed-cost recovery, often by raising the fixed charge of two-part tariff schemes.
Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample**: Spanish Household Budget Survey (HBS, INE), restricted to households interviewed between August 2011 and April 2014. The window stops in April 2014 because a regulatory change cut electricity prices that month. This gives a two-year rotating panel of 25,775 observations. Each household is interviewed twice, in the same two-week period (of 26 per year) in consecutive years. Electricity kWh and € come from the last paid bill (the month before the interview), annualised by multiplying by the number of bills per year. Price is effective price p = expenditure/consumption in €/kWh, including taxes. Estimation sample in Table 2: 20,872 observations, 10,729 households.
- **Treatment definition (difference-in-differences)**: reform date R = 1 August 2013 (the fixed charge F rose 112% and the regulated volumetric charge f fell about 36% for households under 10 kW). D = 1 if the first interview was before R and the second after R. D = 0 if both interviews were before R. T (the post-reform treatment dummy, the key regressor) = 1 if the interview took place after R. Households only respond after receiving a reformed bill, so treated households are those that receive a reformed bill for the second time (a one-month lag). A "months since reform" variable is also included.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 4, log-log)**: ln(q_it) = α + β ln(p_it) + β ln(y_it) + γX_it + δT_it + θ_i + φ_t + ε_it. The outcome q is annualised household kWh. The same specification is run with ln(electricity expenditure) as the outcome. δ (coefficient on T) is the ATE, read as an approximate percentage change. Income enters as ln(income) and ln(income)². Coefficients are unstandardised.
- **Controls / fixed effects**: household size, number of rooms, surface (m²), elderly dummy, education of household head (4 levels), economic situation (number of active members: 2/1/0), renting (tenure), building older than 25 years, municipality-size dummies (5), urban-area dummy, province-capital dummy, regional dummies, and quarterly dummies (for weather and seasonality). Year FE in all models. Household FE (LSDV/within) in the preferred columns 3, 4, 7 and 8.
- **Estimators (Table 2)**: (1)/(5) pooled OLS; (2)/(6) IV; (3)/(7) LSDV with household FE; (4)/(8) household-FE IV. Columns 1–4 are for consumption and 5–8 for expenditure. Columns 4 and 8 are preferred. In the IV models, ln(price) is instrumented by households' effective prices (expenditure/quantity) of potatoes, water and fuel: 3 instruments, 2 overidentifying restrictions. IV models are estimated by LIML. Diagnostics: first-stage F, Kleibergen-Paap rk LM, Cragg-Donald Wald F against the Stock-Yogo LIML 10% critical value, Montiel Olea-Pflueger effective F, Sargan and Hansen J.
- **Standard errors**: robust, clustered at the household level (Table 2).
- **Robustness (Table 3)**: cross-section restricted to the 2013 reform year, excluding households treated in 2014. Models run with and without controls, for consumption and expenditure, estimated by OLS. Standard errors are robust and clustered at the two-week interview period. Samples are 18,770 observations without controls and 14,894 with controls.
- **Distributional analysis (Tables 4–5)**: quantile regression (Koenker-Bassett) of ln consumption and ln expenditure on T, ln price, ln income, ln income² and controls, using the 2013 sample (N = 14,894). Estimated at deciles q10–q90, with bootstrapped standard errors (50 replications). Incidence is assessed with Suits and Kakwani progressivity indices, computed from Gini indices and concentration curves of electricity expenditure against total household expenditure, separately for treated and control households.
