## Paper Information
Title: 1
Abstract (first 500 chars): QUASI-EXPERIMENT Xavier Labandeira, José M. Labeaga, Jordi J. Teixidó* August 5, 2021 The global energy mix and cost structure of the power industry are experiencing a redefinition. Many countries are revamping electricity-pricing systems to guarantee fixed-cost recovery, often by raising the fixed charge of two-part tariff schemes.
Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample restrictions**: Household-level microdata from the Spanish Household Budget Survey (HBS, INE). The window is limited to households interviewed between August 2011 and April 2014, so that no other major events fall in it: the reform took effect on 1 August 2013, and a regulatory price cut followed in April 2014. The HBS is a rotating two-year panel in which each household is re-interviewed in the same two-week period of the following year. The pooled panel has 25,775 observations. The Table 2 estimation sample is 20,872 observations from 10,729 households. Electricity consumption (kWh) and expenditure (€) come from the last paid bill, which covers the month before the interview, multiplied by the number of bills per year. Each year's sample is stratified into 26 two-week interview subsamples.
- **Treatment definition (difference-in-differences)**: D = 1 for households whose first interview was before the reform and whose second was after it (i1 < R < i2). D = 0 for households with both interviews before the reform. T is the post-reform dummy (interview after R) and is the key regressor; its coefficient δ is the DiD/ATE estimate. Identification rests on the interview date being exogenous and on the 26 stratified subsamples being identical by design. Balance is checked with QQ-plots of pre-reform covariates for D = 0 versus D = 1 (Fig. 4) and with Table 1 summary statistics.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 4, log-log structural demand)**: ln(q_it) = α + β ln(p_it) + β ln(y_it) + γX_it + δT_it + θ_i + φ_t + ε_it.
  - q is annualised kWh.
  - p is the household's effective price (expenditure/consumption, €/kWh, including all taxes).
  - y is household income. Table 2 also includes ln(income)² and "months since reform".
  - The same specification is estimated with ln(electricity expenditure) as the dependent variable.
  - Coefficients are unstandardised; δ is read as an approximate percentage change.
- **Controls and fixed effects**:
  - Controls: household size; number of rooms; surface (m²); an elderly-member dummy; head's education (4 levels); household economic situation (number of economically active members, 3 levels); housing tenure (renting); building older than 25 years; municipality-size dummies (5 levels); urban-area dummy; provincial-capital dummy; regional dummies; quarterly dummies for seasonality and weather. Table 1 also lists a retirement-income dummy.
  - Fixed effects: year FE in all columns, plus household FE estimated by LSDV/within in the preferred columns 3, 4, 7 and 8.
- **Estimators (Table 2)**: Columns 1 and 5 are pooled OLS. Columns 2 and 6 are IV. Columns 3 and 7 are household-FE (LSDV). Columns 4 and 8 are household-FE IV, the preferred specification. Columns 1–4 use log consumption and columns 5–8 log expenditure.
  - In the IV columns, ln(electricity price) is treated as endogenous and instrumented with the household's effective prices (expenditure/quantity) for potatoes, water and fuel. That gives 3 instruments for 1 endogenous regressor (2 overidentifying restrictions), estimated by LIML.
  - Diagnostics: first-stage F; Kleibergen-Paap rk LM underidentification test; Cragg-Donald Wald F against Stock-Yogo LIML 10% critical values; Montiel Olea-Pflueger effective F; Sargan and Hansen J overidentification tests.
- **Standard errors**: In Table 2, heteroskedasticity-robust and clustered at the household level.
- **Robustness: 2013 cross-section (Table 3)**: Restricted to 2013 interviews only, so households treated in 2014 are excluded. Columns 1 and 3 have no controls (N = 18,770). Columns 2 and 4 include ln price, ln income, ln income² and controls (N = 14,894). Estimated by OLS with robust SEs clustered by two-week interview period.
- **Distributional analysis (Tables 4–5)**: Quantile regressions (Koenker-Bassett) of ln consumption and ln expenditure on T, ln price, ln income, ln income² and controls, run on the 2013 sample (N = 14,894) at the deciles q10–q90. Standard errors are bootstrapped with 50 replications. Regressivity is then assessed with Suits and Kakwani progressivity indices, computed from Gini indices and concentration curves of electricity expenditure against total household expenditure for treated and control households. Relative burden is also compared by income decile (median electricity share of total expenditure).
