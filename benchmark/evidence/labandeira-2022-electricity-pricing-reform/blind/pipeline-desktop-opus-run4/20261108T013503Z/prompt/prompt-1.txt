## Paper Information
Title: 1
Abstract (first 500 chars): QUASI-EXPERIMENT Xavier Labandeira, José M. Labeaga, Jordi J. Teixidó* August 5, 2021 The global energy mix and cost structure of the power industry are experiencing a redefinition. Many countries are revamping electricity-pricing systems to guarantee fixed-cost recovery, often by raising the fixed charge of two-part tariff schemes.
Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample restrictions:** Spanish Household Budget Survey (HBS, INE). The sample is limited to households interviewed between August 2011 and April 2014, chosen to avoid other regulatory events (a price change in April 2014). It is an unbalanced two-year rotating panel: each household is interviewed in the same two-week period (one of 26 per year) in two consecutive years, and only half of each cohort is invited back. The full panel has 25,775 observations; the main estimation sample is 20,872 observations on 10,729 households (Table 2). Electricity consumption (kWh) and expenditure (€) come from the last paid bill (the previous month) and are annualised by the number of bills per year. The household price is p = expenditure/consumption in €/kWh, including all taxes.
- **Treatment definition (DiD via interview date):** The reform date is R = 1 August 2013 (fixed charge F up 112%, regulated volumetric charge f down about 36%). D = 1 if the first interview is before R and the second after R (treated). D = 0 if both interviews are before R (control). The treatment variable T = 1 for interviews held after R, so it switches on only at a treated household's post-reform interview. Identification rests on the HBS stratified design, under which the 26 two-week subsamples are comparable except for interview date. Pre-reform balance is checked with Table 1 summary statistics, QQ-plots (Fig. 4) and kernel densities.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 4, log-log structural demand):** ln(q_it) = α + β ln(p_it) + β ln(y_it) [+ ln(y)²] + γX_it + δT_it + θ_i + φ_t + ε_it. The dependent variable is ln electricity consumption (kWh), with ln electricity expenditure as a second outcome (Table 2 cols 5–8). δ is the DiD effect of the reform, read as an approximate percentage change. Coefficients are unstandardised and the price and income coefficients are elasticities. Table 2 also includes a "months since reform" term.
- **Controls / fixed effects:** ln income and ln income squared. X contains household size, number of rooms, surface (m²), an elderly dummy, head's education (4 levels), household economic situation (number of active members, 3 levels), housing tenure (renting), building older than 25 years, municipality size dummies (5), urban area dummy, province capital dummy and regional dummies. Quarterly dummies absorb seasonality and weather, together with the regional dummies. Year fixed effects appear in all Table 2 models. Household fixed effects are added via LSDV in cols 3, 4, 7 and 8. The preferred specifications are col 4 (consumption) and col 8 (expenditure): household FE + year FE + controls + IV.
- **Estimators / IV:** OLS (cols 1 and 5), IV without household FE (cols 2 and 6), LSDV household FE (cols 3 and 7), and FE-IV (cols 4 and 8). In the IV models, ln(electricity price) is the single endogenous regressor. It is instrumented with households' effective unit prices (expenditure/quantity) for three other goods: potatoes, water and fuel. That gives 2 overidentifying restrictions, and only households purchasing these goods can be used. All IV models are estimated by LIML. Diagnostics reported: first-stage F, Kleibergen-Paap rk LM underidentification test (df 3), Cragg-Donald Wald F against Stock-Yogo LIML 10% critical values, Montiel Olea–Pflueger effective F (LIML 10%/20% thresholds), and Sargan and Hansen J overidentification tests (df 2).
- **Standard errors:** Panel models (Table 2) use robust standard errors clustered at the household level. The 2013 cross-section robustness models (Table 3) use robust standard errors clustered at the two-week interview period.
- **Robustness / cross-section:** Table 3 restricts the sample to the reform year 2013 and compares households interviewed before and after August 2013 within the same year, so 2014-treated households are excluded. It reports the specification without controls (18,770 observations) and with controls (14,894 observations), for ln consumption and ln expenditure. Appendix tables add interactions of T, including with income groups.
- **Distributional analysis:** Quantile regressions (Koenker–Bassett, Eq. 5–6) of ln consumption (Table 4) and ln expenditure (Table 5) on T, ln price, ln income, ln income squared and controls. They use the 2013 cross-section (14,894 observations) at the deciles q10–q90 and report bootstrapped standard errors with 50 replications. Incidence by income uses median electricity budget shares by income decile (2012–2014) and the Suits and Kakwani progressivity indices, computed from Gini indices and concentration curves of electricity expenditure versus total household expenditure for treated and control households.
