**Cross-national observational analysis (motivating evidence):**
- Replicates Chenoweth and Lewis (2013) using NAVCO 2.0 merged with EPR data (Vogt et al. 2015, via Thurber 2018): 220 groups, 110 countries, 1946–2006
- Dependent variable: campaign success (achieved all stated goals within one year of peak activity)
- Key specification interacts campaign tactic (violent vs. nonviolent) with ethnic group identity, measured two ways: political status (included vs. excluded) and group size (majority vs. minority)

**Study 1 — Factorial survey experiment (US and Israel, Nov 2018–Mar 2019):**
- Samples: 2,269 US respondents; 3,063 Israeli respondents
- 2×3 design (US) and 3×3 design (Israel): protester identity (US: white/Black; Israel: white Jewish/Ethiopian/Arab) × tactic (march in streets / shut down traffic / destroy property)
- Identity signaled via distinctive names + photos (US) or article title (Israel); gender held roughly constant across images (~7% women US, ~13% Israel)
- Balance tests reported in SI Section 3.4

**Study 2 — Extended factorial experiment (June 2020):**
- Samples: 3,013 US; 3,465 Israel
- US: 2×2×2 design; Israel: 3×2×2 design with 2 unrealistic cells omitted (10 conditions total)
- Factors: protester identity × protest goal (general/layoffs vs. group-based/racism-police brutality) × explicit commitment to nonviolence (yes/no)

**Outcome measures:**
- Perceived violence (0–10 scale)
- Recall of violence (binary; coded 1 if respondent checked damaging police cars, looting, or throwing rocks from a tactic list)
- Police action required (0–10 scale)
- Approval of protest + open-ended explanation

**Estimation:**
- OLS regressions of each DV on treatment indicators; baseline = white protesters marching in streets (Study 1) or white protesters/generic goal/no commitment (Study 2)
- Headline coefficients are unstandardized (raw scale units); percent changes computed relative to intercept
- Robustness checks adding pretreatment covariates reported in SI Section 3.5
- Standard errors appear conventional (not explicitly described as robust/clustered)
- Subgroup analyses by respondent ethnicity (white vs. Black in US; Jewish vs. Arab in Israel; Ethiopian respondents not sampled due to <2% population share)

**Mechanism analysis:**
- Qualitative coding + Structural Topic Model (STM) with 10 topics on open-ended responses, using protester identity as document-level covariate to test whether topic prevalence differs by treatment condition
