Effective for Whom? Ethnic Identity and Nonviolent Resistance DEVORAH MANEKIN
Methodology Summary: - **Motivating observational analysis (Figure 1):** Replicates the Chenoweth and Lewis (2013) campaign-outcome model using NAVCO 2.0 merged with the Ethnic Power Relations (EPR) dataset (combined by Thurber 2018). It adds an ethnic-identity indicator and interacts it with campaign tactic (violent vs. nonviolent). Ethnic identity is measured in two alternative ways: political status (included/dominant vs. excluded/disadvantaged, Panel A) and group size (majority vs. minority, Panel B). The outcome is campaign success, defined as achieving all stated goals within a year of the campaign's peak. Coverage is 220 groups in 110 countries, 1946–2006. Results are shown as predicted Pr(success); the full specification is in SI Section 2.
- **Study 1 design (randomized survey experiment):** Online vignette experiment using simulated news articles. US: fielded in 3 waves, Nov 2018–Jan 2019, N = 2,269, a 2×3 factorial design. Protester identity is white vs. Black, signalled by the names Charlie vs. Tyrone plus a crowd photo. Tactic has three levels: march in streets / shut down traffic / destroy police cars. Israel: fielded in 2 waves, Feb–Mar 2019, N = 3,063, a 3×3 design. Identity is white Jews / Ethiopian Jews / Arab Israelis, named in the article title. Tactics are march / shut down traffic / destroy garbage cans. Balance on demographic covariates is checked (SI 3.4).
- **Study 2 design:** Fielded June 2020, during the George Floyd protests, with nondisruptive tactics only (marching). US: N = 3,013, a 2×2×2 factorial design: identity (white/Black) × goal (general = protesting layoffs vs. minority-group goal = racism/police brutality) × explicit commitment to nonviolence (yes/no). Israel: N = 3,465, a 3×2×2 design with 2 cells dropped (white Jews protesting for minority goals, with and without commitment), giving 10 conditions.
- **Outcomes (identical in both studies):** (1) Perceived degree of violence, on a 0–10 scale (0 = completely nonviolent, 10 = extremely violent). (2) Recall violence, a binary indicator asked after the other outcomes. It is coded 1 if the respondent checked any of: damaging police cars, looting local stores, throwing rocks at police. It is coded 0 if they checked only marching and/or blocking traffic. (3) Police action required, on a 0–10 scale. All are used in raw units in Tables 1–2. The interaction and conditional-effect figures (Figs 3, 6, 7) report coefficients in standard-deviation units.
- **Estimating equations (Tables 1 and 2):** OLS regression of each outcome on treatment dummies only, with no covariates in the headline models. Pretreatment covariates are added only as a robustness check (SI 3.5). Table 1 regresses each outcome on the minority-identity dummies (Black; or Ethiopian and Arab) plus tactic dummies (shut down traffic; destroy property). The baseline is white protesters marching in the streets. Table 2 regresses each outcome on the identity dummies plus a minority-group-goal dummy and a commitment-to-nonviolence dummy. The baseline is white protesters with the generic goal and no commitment. No fixed effects. Effects are also expressed as a percentage change relative to the intercept (coefficient divided by intercept). Conventional OLS standard errors are reported; no robust or clustered SEs are mentioned. Significance thresholds are 0.10, 0.05 and 0.01.
- **Heterogeneity/interaction analyses:** Identity × tactic interactions in Study 1 (Fig 3), with the dominant group as baseline. Subgroup analysis by respondent ethnicity within the march-in-streets condition: white vs. Black respondents in the US, white Jewish vs. Arab respondents in Israel; Ethiopian respondents were too few to sample (Fig 4). In Study 2: identity × goal (US only, Fig 6) and identity × commitment (Fig 7).
- **Samples:** Full samples, with no restrictions noted, apart from minor missingness in one US Study 2 outcome (police action, N = 3,008).
- **Mechanism / text analysis:** Respondents gave an open-ended explanation of their approval or disapproval of the protesters. Responses to nonviolent-protest conditions from both studies are pooled. These are analysed qualitatively and with a structural topic model (STM) using 10 topics. Protester ethnicity in the vignette (minority vs. majority) is the prevalence covariate. The reported quantity is the change in topic proportion from moving from white to Black protesters (US), together with each topic's top words.
