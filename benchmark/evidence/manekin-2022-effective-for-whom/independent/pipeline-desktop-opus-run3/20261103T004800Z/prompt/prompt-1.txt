Effective for Whom? Ethnic Identity and Nonviolent Resistance DEVORAH MANEKIN
Methodology Summary: - **Motivating observational analysis (Figure 1):** The authors replicate Chenoweth and Lewis (2013) using NAVCO 2.0 merged with Ethnic Power Relations (EPR), as combined by Thurber (2018). The data cover 220 groups in 110 countries, 1946–2006. The outcome is campaign success, coded 1 if the campaign achieved all of its stated goals within a year of its peak. They add ethnic group identity and interact it with tactic (violent vs. nonviolent). Ethnic identity is measured two ways: political status (included/dominant vs. excluded/disadvantaged, Panel A) and group size (majority vs. minority, Panel B). Predicted Pr(success) is plotted by tactic and group. The full specification is in SI Section 2 and is not reported in the main text.
- **Study 1 design (survey experiment):** Respondents read simulated news-article vignettes.
  - US: 2×3 factorial, N = 2,269, three waves from Nov 2018 to Jan 2019. Protester identity is white or Black, signalled by the names Charlie/Tyrone plus a crowd photo.
  - Israel: 3×3 factorial, N = 3,063, two waves in Feb–Mar 2019. Protesters are white Jews, Ethiopian Jews or Arab Israelis, with identity given in the article title.
  - Tactic has three levels: march in streets (base), shut down traffic, and destroy property (police cars in the US, garbage cans in Israel).
  - Balance tests are in SI 3.4.
- **Study 2 design (June 2020, during the George Floyd protests):** Protests are marching only.
  - US: 2×2×2 factorial, N = 3,013. Factors are identity (white/Black), goal (general: layoffs vs. group-based: racism and police brutality) and explicit commitment to nonviolence (no/yes).
  - Israel: 3×2×2 factorial with the two "white Jews protesting minority-group goals" cells dropped, leaving 10 conditions, N = 3,465.
- **Outcomes (identical in both studies):**
  1. Perceived degree of violence, on a 0–10 scale (0 = completely nonviolent, 10 = extremely violent).
  2. Recall violence: a binary taken from a later check-all-that-apply list of tactics. It is 1 if the respondent recalled damaging police cars, looting stores or throwing rocks, and 0 if they recalled only marching and/or blocking traffic.
  3. Police action required, on a 0–10 scale.
- **Estimation:** OLS of each outcome on treatment dummies, main effects only, with no covariates in the headline models. Coefficients are in raw scale units, not standardised. Adding pretreatment covariates does not change the results (SI 3.5).
  - Table 1 base category: white protesters marching in streets. Table 2 base category: white protesters, generic (layoffs) goal, no commitment. The intercept is the baseline mean.
  - Effects are reported as percent changes relative to the intercept. Examples: US Black protesters on perceived violence, 0.235/2.774 = 8.5%; recall violence, 0.039/0.052 = 75%.
  - Standard errors are conventional OLS standard errors. No clustering or robust correction is mentioned.
  - Significance stars: * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01.
  - Table 1: N = 2,269 (US) and 3,063 (Israel) for all outcomes.
  - Table 2: N = 3,013 for US violence and recall, 3,008 for US police action, and 3,465 for every Israel outcome.
- **Headline estimates:**
  - Table 1 US, Black protesters: 0.235 (SE 0.117) perceived violence, 0.039 (0.018) recall, 0.268 (0.127) police. R² = 0.235, 0.322 and 0.160.
  - Table 1 Israel, Ethiopian protesters: 0.425 (0.107) perceived violence, 0.085 (0.023) recall, 0.181 (0.118, not significant) police.
  - Table 1 Israel, Arab protesters: 0.623 (0.108), 0.047 (0.023) and 0.964 (0.119).
  - Table 2 US, Black protesters: 0.347 (0.115), 0.044 (0.028) and 0.429 (0.114). Minority goal: 0.628, 0.168 and 0.547. Commitment: −0.243, −0.021 and −0.212.
  - Table 2 Israel, Ethiopian protesters: 0.950, 0.285 and 0.732. Arab protesters: 0.587, 0.062 and 0.802. Minority goal: 0.628, 0.119 and 0.574. Commitment: −0.309, 0.004 and −0.151.
- **Heterogeneity and interaction analyses:**
  - Figure 3: conditional effects of identity by tactic, from identity × tactic interactions.
  - Figure 6: identity × goal (US).
  - Figure 7: identity × commitment.
  - Figures 3, 6 and 7 report coefficients in standard-deviation units.
  - Figure 4: subgroup means for the "march in streets" condition, by respondent group (white vs. Black in the US; Jewish vs. Arab in Israel; there were too few Ethiopian respondents to include).
- **Mechanism (text analysis):** Open-ended explanations of approval or disapproval of nonviolent protests are pooled across both studies. They are analysed qualitatively and with a structural topic model (STM). The STM uses 10 topics, with protester identity (minority vs. majority) as a topic-prevalence covariate. Results are reported as the change in topic proportion from white to Black protesters, about 0.01 against a base proportion of about 0.1, i.e. roughly 10%. Approval rates for nonviolent protests: US 59% (white) vs. 55% (Black); Israel 87% (white) vs. 83% (Ethiopian) and 48% (Arab).
