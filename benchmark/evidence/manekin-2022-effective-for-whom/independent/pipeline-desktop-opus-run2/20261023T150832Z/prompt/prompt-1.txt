Effective for Whom? Ethnic Identity and Nonviolent Resistance DEVORAH MANEKIN
Methodology Summary: - **Motivating cross-national analysis (Figure 1):** Replicates Chenoweth and Lewis (2013) using NAVCO 2.0 merged with Ethnic Power Relations (EPR) (Thurber 2018). The sample covers 220 groups in violent and nonviolent campaigns in 110 countries, 1946–2006. The outcome is campaign success, defined as achieving all stated goals within a year of the campaign's peak. Campaign tactic (violent vs. nonviolent) is interacted with ethnic group identity, measured in two ways: political status (included/dominant vs. excluded) from EPR (Panel A) and group size (majority vs. minority) (Panel B). Results are plotted as predicted Pr(success). The full specification is in SI Section 2.
- **Study 1 design (survey experiments):** Respondents read simulated news-article vignettes. US: 2×3 factorial, protester identity (white vs. Black, signalled by the names Charlie/Tyrone and a crowd photo) × tactic (march in streets / shut down traffic / destroy police cars); N = 2,269, three waves, Nov 2018–Jan 2019. Israel: 3×3 factorial, identity (white Jews / Ethiopian Jews / Arab Israelis, named in the headline) × tactic (march / block traffic / destroy garbage cans); N = 3,063, two waves, Feb–Mar 2019. Balance tests are in SI 3.4.
- **Study 2 design:** June 2020, during the George Floyd protests. All vignettes show non-disruptive marching only. Three randomised factors: identity; protest goal (generic: layoffs vs. minority group-based: racial prejudice/police brutality); and an explicit statement of commitment to nonviolence (yes/no). US: 2×2×2, N = 3,013. Israel: 3×2×2 with two cells dropped (white Jews protesting minority goals), giving 10 conditions, N = 3,465.
- **Dependent variables (identical in both studies):** (1) perceived degree of violence, on a 0–10 scale (0 = completely nonviolent, 10 = extremely violent); (2) recall violence, a binary coded 1 if the respondent's post-outcome checklist recalls damaging police cars, looting, or throwing rocks, and 0 if they recall only marching or blocking traffic; (3) police action required, on a 0–10 scale.
- **Estimation (Tables 1 and 2):** Separate OLS regression of each DV on treatment dummies only, run separately by country, with no covariates and no fixed effects. Headline coefficients are in raw DV units (scale points, or probability for recall); they are not standardised.
  - Table 1: DV ~ minority-identity dummies (Black; or Ethiopian and Arab) + tactic dummies (Shut down traffic; Destroy police cars / garbage cans). Baseline is white protesters marching in the streets. Example US estimates: Black = 0.235 (SE 0.117) on perceived violence, 0.039 (0.018) on recall, 0.268 (0.127) on police action; intercepts 2.774, 0.052 and 3.837.
  - Table 2: DV ~ identity + Minority group goal + Commitment to nonviolence. Baseline is white protesters, generic goal, no commitment. Example US estimate: Black = 0.347 (0.115) on perceived violence.
  - Effect sizes are reported as percentage changes relative to the intercept (e.g., 0.235/2.774 = 8.5%).
- **Heterogeneity and interactions:** Identity × tactic (Fig. 3), identity × goal (Fig. 6, US only) and identity × commitment (Fig. 7) conditional effects are plotted in standard-deviation units. Subgroup analysis by respondent ethnicity (Fig. 4) uses the "march in streets" condition only and compares white vs. Black respondents in the US and Jewish vs. Arab respondents in Israel (too few Ethiopian respondents to include them).
- **Inference and robustness:** Conventional OLS standard errors are reported in parentheses; the paper mentions no clustering or robust correction. Significance stars: * p<0.10, ** p<0.05, *** p<0.01. Adding pretreatment covariates does not change the results (SI 3.5). Observations: all respondents per sample, except that US Study 2 police action has N = 3,008.
- **Mechanism analysis:** Open-ended explanations of approval or disapproval, restricted to nonviolent-protest conditions and pooled across both studies, are analysed qualitatively and with a Structural Topic Model (STM). The STM uses K = 10 topics, with protester identity (minority vs. majority) as the topic-prevalence covariate. Reported coefficients are the change in topic proportion from white to minority protesters.
