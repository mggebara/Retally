Methodology Summary: - **Design / data**: Pre-registered (AEARCTR-0003252) online survey experiment run with pureprofile in two waves (Wave A: Aug 31–Oct 9 2018; Wave B: Nov 21 2018–Jan 2 2019); final sample N = 4,065, representative of the US population aged 18–65. Respondents are randomized into two information arms – T74 ("high wage gap": ACS 2016 says women earn $74 per $100 for 45-year-old full-time employees with a Bachelor's degree) and T94 ("low wage gap": CPS Oct 2017 says $94) – or a pure control group (no information). Prior beliefs are elicited before treatment (incentivized for a random ~half: $2 bonus if within $2 of the ACS/CPS value); posterior beliefs are elicited on one of five randomly assigned related wage statistics. An obfuscated follow-up ~2 weeks later reaches only T74/T94 respondents (N = 1,105, 36% recall).

- **Outcomes**: Self-reported categorical views (gap is large / is a problem / government should do more) and five-point support for specific policies (gender quotas, affirmative action, equal pay legislation, wage transparency [Wave A only], reporting website [Wave B only], public child-care subsidies); behavioral outcomes: signing a progressive (Petition I) or conservative (Petition II) White House petition (observed at group level via duplicated petition URLs), and donation of $0–$300 from a $300 lottery prize to AAUW (each $ topped up by $0.50); also mechanism items (perceived drivers of the gap, fairness, government effectiveness). Summary indices are constructed per outcome family (concern; specific policy demand; impersonal drivers).

- **Preprocessing / units**: Qualitative outcomes are z-scored using the mean and SD of the pure control group (so headline treatment effects on these outcomes are in control-group SD units); in the follow-up sample (T74/T94 only) outcomes are z-scored on the full follow-up sample. Beliefs/posteriors are in dollars per $100 (also expressed in SDs). For the correlational analysis (Tables 3–4) observations below the 5th and above the 95th percentile of the prior-belief distribution are dropped (deviation from PAP; robustness with 3rd/97th cut-offs).

- **Main specification (eq. 1)**: OLS on the sample restricted to the two treatment arms (control group excluded): Y_i = β0 + β1·T74_i + Θ'X_i + u_i, where T74_i = 1 if assigned the $74 (ACS) signal, 0 if T94. β1 is the causal effect of the high-gap signal. Linear (levels) specification, no fixed effects beyond the dummy controls.

- **Controls X_i (pre-specified)**: survey wave, gender, prior belief, census region of residence, five age categories, having ≥1 child, log household income, at least a 2-year college degree, employment status dummies (full-time, part-time, self-employed, unemployed, student, out of labor force incl. retired), and political orientation (Democrat incl. leaning Democrat, Republican incl. leaning Republican, Independent, "other").

- **Inference / weights**: Heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors; probability weights applied to all regressions (correcting accidental oversampling of young women). Multiple-testing: FWER-adjusted p-values for the three summary indices and Anderson (2008) sharpened FDR q-values for individual outcomes within families.

- **Extensions**: (i) 2SLS scaling exercise instrumenting posterior beliefs with T74 assignment (e.g., 1 SD lower belief → +0.17 SD affirmative action, +0.18 SD equal pay demand; Table 5 Panel C) and back-of-envelope share of partisan/gender policy gaps explained by belief gaps (≤6% / ≤7%); (ii) heterogeneity via eq. 2: Y_i = β0 + β1H_i + β2T74_i + β3T74_i·H_i + Θ'X_i + u_i (H = gender, partisanship, etc.); (iii) prior-belief correlates (Table 2) by OLS of prior beliefs on gender, partisanship, employment, education; (iv) robustness: persistence in follow-up (Table 6), compliant-subpopulation analysis by recent news consumption, specifications including the pure control group with signal-minus-prior as regressor (Appendix D.6), results by wave (Appendix G.3).

- **Evaluation**: No train/test split or cross-validation (experimental econometrics). Key estimates: posterior beliefs differ by ~$13 (≈2/3 SD) between arms; T74 raises "gap is large" 0.6 SD, "problem" 0.4 SD, "government should do more" 0.2 SD; ~0.1 SD effects on affirmative action and equal pay legislation; Petition I +2 pp, Petition II −1 pp; donation effect small/insignificant.
