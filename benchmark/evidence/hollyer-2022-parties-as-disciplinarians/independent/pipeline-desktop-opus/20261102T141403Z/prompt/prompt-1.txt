Methodology Summary: - **Design overview:** A formal OLG game-theoretic model (symmetric subgame perfect equilibria in pure strategies; uncommitted, committed and loyal equilibria) produces Hypotheses 1–4. These are tested with (i) a cross-national, cross-sectional OLS analysis of parties and (ii) a regression discontinuity (RD) case study of Brazilian mayoral elections, 1996–2012. Two qualitative case studies (Austria ÖVP, Spain PSOE) are in Supplemental Appendix F. There is no train/test split and no cross-validation. Inference is purely in-sample.
- **Cross-national data and outcomes:** Party-level data from the 2009 Democratic Accountability and Linkages Project (DALP; Kitschelt 2013) expert survey. *Programmaticness* is the average of two 4-point items (1 = "not at all", 4 = "very strongly"): emphasis on policy positions in campaigning, and appeal to voters' partisan identification. *Personalism* is the single 4-point item on how much the party campaigns on the leader's charismatic personality. Both are in raw 1–4 scale units, not standardised.
- **Key predictors:**
  - *Volatility*: country-level Pedersen Index (average change in party vote-share percentages between consecutive elections), averaged over the four election pairs before 2009 (or as many as are available).
  - *Group attachment*, measured two ways:
    - *Ethnic party*: a binary DALP indicator of links with ethnic or linguistic organizations, coded 1 when a majority of experts report such ties.
    - *Ideological extremism*: the absolute distance of the party's 10-point left-right placement from the country's average party placement. It is continuous, from 0 to about 6.
- **Estimating equations (OLS):**
  - H1: Outcome_{i,p} = μ + β·Volatility_i + X_{i,p}γ + Z_iθ + u_{i,p}, for country i and party p.
  - H2/H3: the same equation plus Group Attachment_{i,p} and Volatility_i × Group Attachment_{i,p}.
  - Party-level controls X: party size, and links to unions, businesses and religious organizations.
  - Country-level controls Z: electoral system (plurality / PR / mixed), ballot rules (open / closed / mixed), average district magnitude, political openness, length of democracy, separation-of-powers rules, economic development, inequality, and ethnolinguistic cleavages (details in Table C2).
  - There are no fixed effects.
  - Standard errors are clustered by country.
- **Cross-national sample and reporting:**
  - The sample is 431 party observations from 75 countries. Countries and parties with missing data are dropped (Figure C1).
  - Table 1 reports predicted outcomes at low volatility (25th percentile) and high volatility (75th percentile), plus their differences. These are shown overall, by ethnic vs. nonethnic party, and by moderate (distance 0) vs. extremist (distance 3, about the 75th percentile) party.
  - Coefficient estimates are in Table D1.
  - Headline result: Personalism is +0.30 higher at high volatility (SE 0.06, p<0.01) and Programmaticness is −0.11 lower (SE 0.05, p<0.05).
- **Cross-national robustness checks:** commodity terms of trade as a more exogenous volatility measure (Table D6); 2D/3D extremism measures; a group-attachment index that includes union ties; more detailed institutional controls (Supplemental Appendix D/E).
- **Brazil RD design:**
  - Unit: party-municipality, re-using the data of Klašnja and Titiunik (2017).
  - Running variable: the party's margin of victory at t (its vote share minus that of its strongest opponent), with the cutoff at 0.
  - Treatment: an incumbent-party mayoral win, interpreted as a positive shock to λ.
  - Outcomes: whether the party nominates a charismatic candidate at t+1, proxied by indicators for "Young" and "Young and outsider".
- **RD estimation and results:**
  - Method: local linear polynomial with MSE-optimal main bandwidth h and bias bandwidth b, robust bias-corrected inference, using rdrobust (Calonico et al. 2017).
  - Reported quantities: the conventional point estimate τ_RD, the robust bias-corrected 95% CI, and the robust CI with h = b.
  - Young: τ = −0.084, p = 0.040, CI [−0.185, −0.005], h = 13.491, b = 24.643, N+ = 527, N− = 559.
  - Young and outsider: τ = −0.092, p = 0.015, CI [−0.185, −0.020], h = 12.280, b = 22.333, N+ = 501, N− = 523.
  - Figure 6 uses binned means with a second-order polynomial fit for visual display only.
