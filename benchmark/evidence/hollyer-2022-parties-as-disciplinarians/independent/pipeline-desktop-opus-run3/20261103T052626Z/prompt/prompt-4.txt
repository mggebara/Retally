## Paper
Title: Parties as Disciplinarians: Charisma and
Methodology: - **Design and data (cross-national part):** A cross-sectional, party-level OLS analysis that tests predictions from a formal overlapping-generations model. Outcomes come from the 2009 Democratic Accountability and Linkages Project (DALP, Kitschelt 2013) expert survey. Because of missing data, the estimation sample is 431 parties in 75 countries (Table 1 note), fewer than DALP's 80+ countries (Figure C1).
- **Variables built from the survey (no standardisation; outcomes stay on the raw 1–4 DALP scale):**
  - *Programmaticness* is the average of two 4-point items: how much the party emphasises policy positions in campaigning, and how much it appeals to voters' partisan identification.
  - *Personalism* is the 4-point item on how much the party campaigns on its leader's charismatic personality.
  - *Volatility* is the country-level Pedersen Index (average change in party vote-share percentages between consecutive elections), averaged over the four election pairs before 2009, or as many as are available.
  - Group-attachment measures:
    - *Ethnic party*: binary, coded 1 when a majority of experts report ethnic or linguistic organisational ties.
    - *Ideological extremism*: the absolute distance of the party's DALP 10-point left-right placement from the country's mean party placement. It is continuous, roughly 0–6.
- **Estimating equations:**
  - H1: Outcome_{i,p} = μ + β·Volatility_i + X_{i,p}γ + Z_iθ + u_{i,p}, for country i and party p, in levels (linear).
  - H2/H3: the same model plus Group Attachments_{i,p} and Volatility_i × Group Attachments_{i,p}. This is run separately with the ethnic measure and with the extremism measure.
  - There are no fixed effects.
- **Controls:**
  - Party level (X): party size; links to unions, businesses and religious organisations.
  - Country level (Z): electoral system (plurality / PR / mixed); ballot rules (open / closed / mixed); average district magnitude; degree of political openness; length of democracy; separation-of-powers rules; economic development; inequality; ethnolinguistic cleavages. Omitted categories are listed in Table C2.
- **Inference:** Standard errors are clustered by country (footnote 17; Table 1). Significance levels are ** p<0.01, * p<0.05, † p<0.1.
- **Reporting protocol (Table 1):** Coefficients are in Appendix Table D1. The main text reports predicted Personalism and Programmaticness at low volatility (25th percentile) and high volatility (75th percentile), plus their differences:
  - Overall: Personalism 2.67 → 2.97, difference 0.30**; Programmaticness 3.08 → 2.97, difference −0.11*.
  - By ethnic vs. nonethnic party: nonethnic Personalism difference 0.35**, ethnic 0.04.
  - By moderate party (distance 0) vs. extremist party (distance 3, about the 75th percentile): moderate Personalism difference 0.53** and Programmaticness difference −0.30**; extremist 0.05 and 0.08.
  - Differences across party types at each volatility level: ethnic minus nonethnic Personalism at low volatility 0.31*, Programmaticness at high volatility 0.20**; extremist minus moderate 0.15 and 0.44**.
- **Robustness checks (appendix):**
  - Commodity terms of trade as a more exogenous volatility measure (Table D6).
  - Two- and three-dimensional extremism measures.
  - A group-attachment index that includes union ties.
  - More detailed institutional controls.
  - Raw scatterplots with linear fits (Figures 3–5).
- **Brazil case study (regression discontinuity, RD):**
  - Data: mayoral elections 1996–2012, reusing the Klašnja and Titiunik (2017) data.
  - Running variable: the incumbent party's margin of victory at t (its vote share minus its strongest opponent's), with cutoff 0.
  - Outcomes: whether the party nominates a charismatic candidate at t+1, measured as "Young" and as "Young and outsider".
  - Estimation: local linear polynomial using rdrobust, with the MSE-optimal main bandwidth h and bias bandwidth b.
  - Inference: robust bias-corrected 95% confidence intervals, also reported with h = b.
