## Paper
Title: Parties as Disciplinarians: Charisma and
Methodology: - **Data and outcomes (cross-national analysis):** Party-level expert-survey data from the Democratic Accountability and Linkages Project (DALP, Kitschelt 2013; fielded 2009). *Programmaticness* is the average of two DALP items: how much the party stresses policy positions in campaigns, and how much it appeals to voters' partisan identification. *Personalism* is the single item on how much the party campaigns on its leader's charismatic personality. All items are on a 1–4 scale (1 = "not at all", 4 = "very strongly"). Outcomes stay on this raw 1–4 scale; the paper does not standardise them.
- **Key regressors:** *Volatility* is the Pedersen Index (average change in party vote-share percentages between two elections), averaged over the four election pairs before 2009, or as many as exist, for each country. Group attachments are measured two ways. *Ethnic party* is binary: a majority of experts report ties to ethnic or linguistic organisations. *Ideological extremism* is continuous (about 0–6): the absolute distance of the party's DALP placement on the 10-point left–right scale from the country mean placement of all parties.
- **Estimating equations (OLS, cross-section, party p in country i):** For H1, Eq. (4): Outcome_{i,p} = μ + β·Volatility_i + X_{i,p}γ + Z_iθ + u_{i,p}. For H2 and H3, add Group Attachments_{i,p} and Volatility_i × Group Attachments_{i,p}, estimating one model for ethnicity and one for extremism. The models are linear in levels, with no logs.
- **Controls (preferred specification):** Party-level controls (X) are party size and links to unions, businesses and religious organisations. Country-level controls (Z) are electoral system (plurality / PR / mixed), ballot rules (open / closed / mixed), average district magnitude, political openness, length of democracy, separation of powers, economic development, inequality and ethnolinguistic cleavages (details in Tables C2–C3). There are no fixed effects, since the design is a cross-section.
- **Inference, sample and reporting:** Standard errors are clustered by country. The estimation sample has 431 party observations from 75 countries, after dropping parties and countries with missing DALP or covariate data (the survey covers 80+ countries). Table 1 reports predicted Personalism and Programmaticness (1–4 scale) at low volatility (25th percentile) and high volatility (75th percentile), plus the differences. Coefficients are in Table D1. Headline results:
  - Overall: Personalism 2.67 vs 2.97, difference 0.30 (SE 0.06, p<0.01); Programmaticness 3.08 vs 2.97, difference −0.11 (SE 0.05, p<0.05).
  - Ethnicity, nonethnic vs ethnic parties: Personalism difference 0.35** vs 0.04; Programmaticness difference −0.15* vs 0.03.
  - Ideology, moderate (distance = 0) vs extremist (distance = 3, about the 75th percentile): Personalism difference 0.53** vs 0.05; Programmaticness difference −0.30** vs 0.08.
  - Ethnic-minus-nonethnic and extremist-minus-moderate gaps are also reported: Personalism at low volatility 0.31* and 0.15; Programmaticness at high volatility 0.20** and 0.44**.
- **Robustness (cross-national):** Two- and three-dimensional extremism measures, a group-attachment index that adds union ties, more detailed institutional controls, and commodity terms of trade as a more exogenous volatility proxy (Table D6). These are in Supplemental Appendix Section D; the main text also shows raw scatterplots with linear fits (Figures 3–5).
- **Brazil RD case study:** Sharp regression discontinuity on Brazilian mayoral elections, 1996–2012, reusing the Klašnja and Titiunik (2017) data. The running variable is the incumbent party's margin of victory at t (its vote share minus its strongest opponent's), with the cutoff at 0. Outcomes at t+1 are whether the party nominates a *Young* candidate, or a *Young and outsider* candidate (candidate-level charisma proxies). Estimation uses local linear polynomials with MSE-optimal bandwidths and robust bias-corrected inference via `rdrobust` (Calonico et al. 2017), defaults (triangular kernel).
- **RD results (Table 2):**

  - Young and outsider: τ = −0.092, p = 0.015, CI [−0.185, −0.020], h = 12.280, b = 22.333, N+ = 501, N− = 523.
  - Robust CIs with h = b: [−0.172, 0.043] for Young and [−0.194, −0.001] for Young and outsider.
