Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis (cross-national):** Party-level, cross-sectional data from the 2009 Democratic Accountability and Linkages Project (DALP, Kitschelt 2013) expert survey, merged with country-level data. Parties or countries with missing data are dropped (fewer than the 80+ DALP countries remain; see Fig. C1). The estimation sample is 431 party observations from 75 countries (Table 1 note).
- **Outcomes (both on DALP's 1–4 scale, 1 = "not at all", 4 = "very strongly"):** *Programmaticness* is the average of two DALP items: how much a party stresses policy positions in campaigning, and how much it draws on voters' partisan identification. *Personalism* is the single DALP item on how much a party's campaign relies on the leader's charismatic personality. Both are used in raw units, with no standardisation.
- **Key regressors:** *Volatility* is the country average of the Pedersen index (average change in party vote-share percentages between two elections) over the four election pairs before 2009, or as many as are available. There are two group-attachment measures. (i) *Ethnic party* is a binary dummy, coded 1 if most experts report DALP ties to ethnic or linguistic organisations. (ii) *Ideological extremism* is continuous, roughly 0–6: the absolute distance of the party's DALP 10-point left-right placement from the country's ideological centre (the mean placement of all parties in that country).
- **Estimating equations (OLS, linear levels):** For H1: Outcome_{i,p} = μ + β·Volatility_i + X_{i,p}γ + Z_iθ + u_{i,p} (country i, party p). For H2/H3, a group-attachment measure and its interaction with Volatility are added: β1·Volatility + β2·GroupAttach + β3·Volatility×GroupAttach + controls. Each group measure (ethnic, extremism) goes in its own model. There are no fixed effects (cross-section).
- **Controls:** Party level (X): party size, and links to unions, businesses and religious organisations. Country level (Z): electoral system (plurality / PR / mixed), ballot rules (open / closed / mixed), average district magnitude, political openness, length of democracy, separation-of-powers rules, economic development, inequality, and ethnolinguistic cleavages. Definitions and omitted categories are in Table C2. Standard errors are **clustered by country**.
- **Reporting / evaluation (Table 1):** The headline numbers are predicted values of each outcome on the 1–4 scale, not raw coefficients (coefficients are in Table D1). Low volatility = 25th percentile of the sample, high volatility = 75th percentile; differences come with clustered SEs. For ideology, a "moderate" party sits at distance 0 from the country mean and an "extremist" party at distance 3 (about the 75th percentile). Predictions are compared across party types at low and at high volatility. Robustness checks: commodity terms of trade as an alternative, more exogenous volatility measure (Table D6); two- or three-dimensional extremism measures; a group-attachment index that includes union ties; more detailed institutional controls (Section D).
- **Brazil RD case study:** Mayoral elections, 1996–2012, reanalysing the Klašnja and Titiunik (2017) data. The running variable is the incumbent party's margin of victory at t (its vote share minus that of its strongest opponent), with cutoff 0. Outcomes at t+1 are whether the party nominates a charismatic candidate, proxied by indicators for (a) a young candidate and (b) a young outsider. Estimation is local linear with MSE-optimal main bandwidth h and bias bandwidth b, and robust bias-corrected 95% CIs, via `rdrobust` (Calonico et al. 2017). The table also reports a robust CI with h = b and effective sample sizes N+ and N− inside the bandwidth. Figure 6 shows binned means with a second-order polynomial fit.
- **No ML components:** There is no train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning. Inference comes from cluster-robust OLS (cross-national) and RD robust inference (Brazil).
