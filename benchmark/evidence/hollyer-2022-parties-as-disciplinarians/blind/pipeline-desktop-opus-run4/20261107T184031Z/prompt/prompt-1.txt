Methodology Summary: - **Design overview:** A formal overlapping-generations model (symmetric subgame perfect equilibria in pure strategies; committed, uncommitted and loyal equilibria) followed by two empirical illustrations: (i) a cross-national, cross-sectional party-level OLS analysis using the 2009 Democratic Accountability and Linkages Project (DALP) expert survey, and (ii) a regression discontinuity (RD) case study of Brazilian mayoral elections, 1996–2012. Qualitative case studies (Austria ÖVP, Spain PSOE) are in Supplemental Appendix F. There is no train/test split or cross-validation.
- **Cross-national outcomes (DALP, 4-point scales, 1 = "not at all" to 4 = "very strongly"; unstandardised):** *Programmaticness* = average of two items (emphasis on policy positions in campaigning; appeal to voters' partisan identification). *Personalism* = item on the party's campaign focus on the leader's charismatic personality.
- **Cross-national key regressors:** *Volatility* = country-level Pedersen Index, averaged over the four election pairs (or as many as are available) before 2009. Group attachment is measured two ways: (a) a binary *ethnic party* indicator (coded ethnic if a majority of experts report ethnic/linguistic organisational ties); (b) continuous *ideological extremism* = absolute distance of the party's DALP 10-point left–right placement from the country's average party placement (range about 0–6).
- **Estimating equations (OLS, linear, levels):** H1: Outcome_{i,p} = μ + β·Volatility_i + X_{i,p}γ + Z_iθ + u_{i,p} (country i, party p). H2/H3 add Group Attachment_{i,p} and Volatility_i × Group Attachment_{i,p}. Party-level controls X: party size and links to unions, businesses and religious organisations. Country-level controls Z: electoral system (plurality / PR / mixed), ballot rules (open / closed / mixed), average district magnitude, political openness, length of democracy, separation-of-powers rules, economic development, inequality and ethnolinguistic cleavages (definitions and omitted categories in Table C2). There are no fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered by country.
- **Sample and reporting:** The estimation sample is the 431 party observations from 75 countries with non-missing data (DALP covers more than 80 countries; parties and countries are dropped for missing data, see Figure C1). Table 1 reports predicted outcomes rather than coefficients (coefficients are in Table D1): low volatility (25th percentile) vs. high volatility (75th percentile), plus their difference, overall and by group type. A moderate party is defined as distance 0 and an extremist party as distance 3 (about the 75th percentile). Robustness checks: commodity terms of trade as an alternative, more exogenous, volatility measure (Table D6); 2- and 3-dimensional ideology measures; a group-attachment index that includes union ties; more detailed institutional controls (Appendix D/E).
- **Brazil RD design:** The data are Brazilian mayoral elections, 1996–2012, from Klašnja and Titiunik (2017). The running variable is the incumbent party's margin of victory at t (party vote share minus that of its strongest opponent), with a cutoff at 0. Treatment = the party wins the mayoralty. Outcomes are whether the party nominates a charismatic candidate at t+1, proxied by binary indicators: *Young*, and *Young and outsider*.
- **RD estimation and inference:** Local linear polynomial estimation with MSE-optimal main bandwidth h and a separate MSE-optimal bias bandwidth b, run with `rdrobust` (Calonico et al. 2017). The paper reports the conventional point estimate τ_RD with robust bias-corrected 95% CIs and p-values, plus robust bias-corrected CIs when h = b. N+ and N− are the effective sample sizes within h on each side of the cutoff. The paper relies on the falsification/validity tests in Klašnja and Titiunik (2017). Figure 6 shows binned means with a second-order polynomial fit on each side.
