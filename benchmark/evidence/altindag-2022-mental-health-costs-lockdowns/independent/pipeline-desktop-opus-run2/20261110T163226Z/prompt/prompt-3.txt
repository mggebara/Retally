Title: DISCUSSION PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design / estimator:** Sharp reduced-form regression discontinuity (RD) using Turkey's age-specific COVID-19 curfew for people aged 65+ (imposed March 21, 2020). The paper estimates Eq. (1) by OLS: y_i = α + β z_i + f(x_i) + ε_i for x_i ∈ (c−h, c+h). Here z_i = 1 if the respondent was born before January 1956 (i.e., in or before December 1955), and the running variable x_i is the number of months the respondent is older than the threshold birth month. f(x_i) is a local linear function fitted separately on each side of the cutoff, with a uniform kernel; results are described as "highly similar" with a triangular kernel. Robustness checks use a quadratic control function (Appendix Table A8). The paper also gives 2SLS/Wald-type scalings in footnote 38, using self-reported curfew status (first stage ≈0.7) or days outside (≈1.1) as the endogenous variable, but the headline estimates are reduced-form.
- **Data / sample:** A phone survey run by KONDA from May 29 to July 4, 2020 among urban residents of 26 regions, drawn from KONDA's existing respondent database. It targets people born 1950–1961 (ages 59–70, with 6 treated and 6 control birth cohorts). The response rate was 88%, and the analysis sample has at most 1,909 individuals. The cutoff (Dec 1955 vs Jan 1956) was chosen by simulating every birth year-month as a threshold and picking the largest jump in self-reported curfew exposure (85 pp; Figure A2). The estimation sample is restricted to observations within bandwidth h of the cutoff.
- **Bandwidth:** The main bandwidth is the Imbens–Kalyanaraman (2012) optimal bandwidth of 45 months, computed for days spent outside in the previous week. Each main table also reports the Calonico–Cattaneo–Titiunik (2014) optimal bandwidth (17 months) and fixed bandwidths of 30 and 60 months.
- **Controls / fixed effects (preferred specification):** Month-of-birth fixed effects, province fixed effects and surveyor fixed effects, plus indicators for education level, ethnicity (non-Turkish origin) and gender (female).
- **Standard errors / inference:** Clustered by month-year of birth (Lee and Card 2008). For multiple testing, the paper reports FDR-adjusted q-values (Benjamini, Krieger and Yekutieli 2006, as applied in Anderson 2008). Adjustment is done within the outcome family of each table, or each panel (for example, all mobility outcomes in Table 3, or the labor outcomes in Table 5 Panel A).
- **Outcomes / variable construction:**
  - Mobility (Table 3): days outside in the previous week, a self-reported subject-to-curfew dummy, and a never-left-home dummy.
  - Mental health (Table 4): built from the 20 yes/no items of the WHO SRQ-20. Following Anderson (2008), there are three indices (each an average of item z-scores, rescaled to mean 0 and SD 1, so coefficients are in SD units): an overall mental distress index (20 items), a somatic index (4 items) and a nonsomatic index (16 items). There is also an unstandardized count of "yes" answers (0–20). Higher values mean more distress.
  - Channels (Table 5): labor and income dummies, limited social interaction and limited physical activity dummies (extremely or very limited versus pre-COVID), current household size, and conflict with a household member. Table 6 covers support for the curfew and satisfaction with the government.
- **Validity checks:** A McCrary (2008) density test at h = 45 gives p = 0.8. Covariate balance is tested for predetermined covariates (high school, illiterate, female, married, widowed/separated, non-Turkish ethnicity, ever had psychological support, chronic disease, pre-COVID household size), with no significant jumps (Table A2); SUR joint-test p-values are 0.25–0.75. The paper also checks the nonresponse rate at the threshold (Figure A3). Figures show binned monthly means with local linear fits, residualized on the fixed effects and controls.

anderson_index <- function(d, its) {
  zs <- sapply(its, function(v) {
    m <- mean(d[[v]][ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
    s <- sd(d[[v]][ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
    (d[[v]] - m) / s
  })
  zs <- as.matrix(zs)
  idx <- rowMeans(zs, na.rm = TRUE)

  m <- mean(idx[ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
  s <- sd(idx[ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
  (idx - m) / s
}
