Methodology Summary: - **Design and data:** The study is a sharp reduced-form regression discontinuity (RD) design built around Turkey's age-specific COVID-19 curfew for people aged 65+, which started on March 21, 2020. The data come from an original KONDA phone survey run May 29 to July 4, 2020. Respondents were drawn from KONDA's existing respondent database and limited to urban residents in 26 regions, aged 59–70 (born 1950–1961). That gives 6 treated and 6 control birth-year cohorts. The analysis sample has at most 1,909 individuals. There is no train/test split or cross-validation; this is a causal-inference design.
- **Treatment and running variable:** The treatment indicator z_i = 1 if the respondent was born before January 1956, i.e. in or before December 1955. This cutoff was chosen by a simulation over every birth year-month threshold, keeping the one with the largest jump in self-reported curfew exposure (Fig. A2). The forcing variable x_i is the number of months the respondent is older than the threshold index month. Birth dates were read from national ID cards.
- **Estimating equation (Eq. 1):** y_i = α + β z_i + f(x_i) + ε_i for x_i ∈ (c − h, c + h). f(·) is a local linear function fit separately on each side of the cutoff. The paper uses a uniform kernel; a triangular kernel and a quadratic control function are robustness checks (Table A8). β is reported as a reduced-form / first-stage level effect. Footnote 38 also gives Wald/2SLS-style rescalings by curfew status and by days outside.
- **Controls and fixed effects:** All specifications add month-of-birth fixed effects, province fixed effects and surveyor (interviewer) fixed effects. They also include indicator variables for education level, ethnicity and gender.
- **Bandwidth:** The headline bandwidth is chosen with the Imbens–Kalyanaraman (2012) optimal-bandwidth procedure. For days spent outside last week it is 45 months, and that bandwidth is used to interpret magnitudes. Main tables also report the Calonico–Cattaneo–Titiunik (2014) optimal bandwidth (17 months) and fixed bandwidths of 30 and 60 months.
- **Outcomes:**
  - *Mobility (first stage, Table 3):* days went outside in the previous week; self-reported being subject to the curfew; never left home.
  - *Mental health (Table 4):* items from the WHO SRQ-20 (20 binary items). Following Anderson (2008), three indices are built by averaging z-scores of their items and then standardising each index to mean 0, SD 1, with higher values meaning more distress:
    - a mental distress index (all 20 items);
    - a somatic index (4 items);
    - a nonsomatic index (16 items).
    A fourth measure is the raw SRQ-20 count of "yes" answers, with no clinical cutoff applied.
  - *Channels (Table 5):*
    - Panel A, labor/income: worked for pay; worked paid or unpaid; has a job they could not attend; enough money for usual needs; worried about spending.
    - Panel B, isolation: limited social interaction and limited physical activity, each a dummy for "extremely/very limited" in the last month versus pre-COVID.
    - Panel C, household: current household size; conflict with a household member.
  - *Beliefs (Table 6 / A7):* curfew support, satisfaction with the government's COVID policy, and religiosity.
- **Inference:** Standard errors are clustered by month-year of birth (Lee and Card 2008). To correct for multiple testing, Anderson (2008) / Benjamini–Krieger–Yekutieli (2006) sharpened FDR q-values are computed within domains: each table, or each panel within a table (following Field et al. 2021).
- **Validity checks:**
  - McCrary (2008) density test at the 45-month bandwidth (Fig. A4).
  - Covariate balance on predetermined characteristics: high school completion, illiteracy, female, married, widowed/separated, non-Turkish ethnicity, ever received psychological support, chronic disease, pre-COVID household size. Shown in Fig. 1 and Table A2, with a SUR joint test across bandwidths.
  - Test for a break in survey nonresponse at the cutoff (Fig. A3).
  - Graphs plot one-month-bin local averages with local linear fits; mental-health outcomes are residualized on the fixed effects and controls (Fig. 3; unresidualized versions in Fig. A6).
