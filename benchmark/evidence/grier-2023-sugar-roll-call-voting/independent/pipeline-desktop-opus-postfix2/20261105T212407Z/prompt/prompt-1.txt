Letter Campaign Contributions and Roll-Call Voting in the U.S. House
Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimator:** Two-period panel of U.S. House roll-call votes on two near-identical anti-sugar-subsidy amendments: the 2013 Pitts amendment (H.Amdt. 227 to H.R. 1947, failed 206–221 on 6/20/2013) and the 2018 Foxx amendment (H.Amdt. 605 to H.R. 2, failed 137–278 on 5/17/2018). Estimation is a fixed-effects (conditional) logit. The preferred model (Table 1, col. 1) uses district fixed effects. Col. 2 uses incumbent (member) fixed effects as a robustness check. The authors used Stata, with marginal-effect plots from the Marhis package at 50 equally spaced values of each variable.
- **Specification:** The dependent variable is 1 for an "aye" vote on the reform amendment (a vote against sugar interests) and 0 otherwise. The key regressor is inflation-adjusted (real) sugar-industry contributions to the incumbent, in levels (raw dollars, not logs and not standardised). Headline coefficient: −0.0006 (p = 0.001) with district FE and −0.0004 (p = 0.001) with incumbent FE. The other coefficients are in natural units: ACU 0.20 / 0.06, Tenure −0.30 / −1.43, Poverty 2.19 / 2.24, %BA 1.20 / 0.59, Median income 0.0001 / 0.0006, %>65 −1.88 / −0.38, Ag committee −36.3 / −39.3, Constant −20.9 / −63.1. Pseudo R² is 0.745 / 0.721.
- **Controls (full set):**
  - ACU rating, 1–100, where higher means more conservative; DW-NOMINATE is used as a robustness alternative.
  - Tenure (seniority, in terms).
  - District poverty rate.
  - % of population with a bachelor's degree.
  - Median income.
  - % of population over 65 (Census data).
  - A dummy for membership on the House Agriculture Committee.

  A Republican dummy was tried; it becomes insignificant once ideology is included, so it is not in the main specification. Unemployment and % rural were tried and do not change the results. Anti-sugar contributions are not included.
- **Contribution measure (preprocessing):** Built from Center for Responsive Politics raw data. It sums contributions to House incumbents (113th and 115th Congresses) from all sugar-industry PACs plus individual contributions over $200 from sugar-industry employees and their families. Amounts are deflated to real dollars. Baseline window: from one year before the vote through the end of the vote's calendar year (about 6/20/2012–12/31/2013 and 5/17/2017–12/31/2018). Leadership PACs and single-candidate Super PACs were checked and contained essentially no sugar money.
- **Estimation sample:** With fixed effects, only units whose vote changed between 2013 and 2018 contribute.
  - District FE: 93 vote-switching districts × 2 votes = 186 observations.
  - Incumbent FE: incumbents who voted both times and switched, 53 members (47 Aye→No, 6 No→Aye). Table 1 reports N = 106.
  - Districts that voted the same way both times drop out.
  - Of the 40 districts that switched under a new incumbent, 32 went Aye→No (contributions +348%) and 8 went No→Aye (−38%), per Table 2.
- **Inference:** p-values come from robust (heteroskedasticity-robust) standard errors, with no clustering stated. Significance stars: * p < 0.10, ** p < 0.05, *** p < 0.01.
- **Robustness (Table 3; full models in Appendix Table A.2):**
  - Calendar-year windows (1/1–12/31/2013 and 2018): coefficient −0.0006*** with district FE, −0.0005*** with incumbent FE.
  - Pre-vote 12-month windows (6/20/12–6/20/13 and 5/17/17–5/17/18): −0.0006*** with district FE, −0.0003*** (p = 0.003) with incumbent FE.
  - Appendix Table A.3 gives the original results using contributions aggregated by election cycle.
- **Evaluation:** There is no train/test split or cross-validation. The outputs are coefficient signs and significance, pseudo R², and marginal-effect plots of predicted P(vote for reform) with 95% CIs:
  - Contributions: about 0.75 at $0, falling to about 0.20 at $15,500.
  - ACU score: about 0.25 at 20 and about 0.65 at 70.
  - Tenure: about 0.35 for a nine-term member.
