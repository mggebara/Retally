Methodology Summary: ## Methodology Summary

- **Estimating equation / specification**: Fixed effects logit model where the dependent variable = 1 if the House member voted "aye" (in favor of reform, against sugar interests) on the 2013 Pitts amendment (H. Amdt. 227) or 2018 Foxx amendment (H. Amdt. 605), and 0 otherwise. Key regressor is inflation-adjusted sugar industry contributions (in dollars, unstandardized). Two specifications: (1) district fixed effects, (2) incumbent fixed effects.

- **Control set (preferred specification)**: ACU ideology score (1–100), tenure (seniority), poverty rate, % with bachelor's degree, median income, % population over 65, and a dummy for House Agriculture Committee membership. DW-Nominate used as a robustness alternative for ideology.

- **Sample restrictions**: Because of logit fixed effects, estimation uses only districts (or incumbents) whose vote changed between the two amendments — 93 vote-switching districts (186 observations) for district FE; 53 vote-switching incumbents (106 observations) for incumbent FE. Observations where the vote did not change are mechanically dropped.

- **Data construction / preprocessing**: Vote data from GovTrack; contributions from the Center for Responsive Politics, aggregating all sugar PAC contributions plus individual contributions >$200 from sugar-industry-affiliated donors (and family), covering the 113th and 115th Congresses. Contributions inflation-adjusted. Demographic controls from U.S. Census.

- **Contribution time windows (headline + robustness)**: Headline window = one year before each vote through end of the calendar year of each vote. Robustness windows: (a) calendar years 2013 and 2018; (b) 12 months prior to the vote up to vote date (6/20/12–6/20/13 and 5/17/17–5/17/18).

- **Standard errors**: Robust standard errors; p-values reported in Tables 1 and 3.

- **Evaluation / reporting**: Logit coefficients with p-values, pseudo-R², and marginal effects of sugar contributions, ideology, and tenure plotted at 50 equally spaced values using the Marhis Stata package, with 95% confidence intervals.

- **No train/test split or cross-validation** is used; this is an observational fixed-effects regression study, not a predictive modeling exercise.
