Letter Campaign Contributions and Roll-Call Voting in the U.S. House
Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimator:** Two-period panel of U.S. House roll-call votes on two nearly identical antisugar-subsidy amendments: the 2013 Pitts amendment (H.Amdt. 227 to H.R. 1947, 113th Congress, vote of June 20, 2013) and the 2018 Foxx amendment (H.Amdt. 605 to H.R. 2, 115th Congress, vote of May 17, 2018). They are treated as repeated votes on the same issue and estimated with a conditional (fixed effects) logit. Table 1 col. 1 uses district fixed effects (preferred). Table 1 col. 2 uses incumbent (member) fixed effects as a robustness check.
- **Estimating equation:** Pr(Aye_it = 1) = Λ(α_i + β·SugarContrib_it + γ·X_it), where i is a district (or incumbent) and t ∈ {2013, 2018}. The dependent variable is 1 for an "aye" vote on the reform amendment (against sugar interests) and 0 otherwise. The key regressor is inflation-adjusted (real dollars, levels, not logged) total sugar-industry contributions to the incumbent. Coefficients are raw logit coefficients and are not standardised.
- **Controls (preferred specification):** ACU ideology rating (American Conservative Union, 1–100, higher = more conservative); tenure/seniority (terms in office); district poverty rate; % of population with a bachelor's degree; median income; % of population over 65 (Census Bureau); and a dummy for House Agriculture Committee membership. There is no party dummy in the headline model, because party is insignificant once ideology is included. DW-Nominate is used in place of ACU as a robustness check. Unemployment and % rural were also tried as controls. Antisugar contributions are not included.
- **Contribution measure / preprocessing:** Raw Center for Responsive Politics data. Contributions from all sugar-industry PACs are summed together with individual contributions over $200 from sugar-industry employees (and their family members), then deflated to real terms. Main window: one year before the vote through the end of the vote's calendar year. Robustness windows (Table 3; full models in Appendix A.2): (a) calendar years 1/1/13–12/31/13 and 1/1/18–12/31/18; (b) the 12 months before each vote up to the vote date (6/20/12–6/20/13 and 5/17/17–5/17/18), which excludes money given after the vote. Appendix A.3 uses the CRP election-cycle aggregates. Leadership PAC and single-candidate Super PAC money was checked but is not part of the measure.
- **Estimation sample:** House incumbents in the 113th and 115th Congresses. With district FE, only districts whose vote outcome switched between 2013 and 2018 contribute identifying variation: 93 districts, 186 observations. The incumbent-FE sample is restricted to the same member voting in both years and switching their vote: 53 members, 106 observations. Districts that voted the same way both times drop out of the conditional logit.
- **Inference:** p-values are based on robust (heteroskedasticity-robust) standard errors. No clustering is reported. Pseudo-R² is reported.
- **Post-estimation:** Marginal effects of sugar contributions, ACU ideology and tenure on the predicted probability of voting for reform. These are computed with the Stata Marhis package at 50 equally spaced values of each variable, with the other covariates at their observed values, and plotted with 95% CIs and a background histogram (Figures 1–3).
- **Descriptive analysis:** Table 2 tabulates the 93 vote-switching districts by switch direction (Aye→No vs. No→Aye) and by same vs. different incumbent (53 vs. 40), and reports the percent change in sugar contributions for each group. There is no train/test split or cross-validation; this is in-sample econometric inference.
