Letter Campaign Contributions and Roll-Call Voting in the U.S. House
Methodology Summary: - **Design and estimator:** The authors treat two near-identical anti-sugar-subsidy House amendments as repeated votes on the same issue in a two-period panel.
  - The 2013 vote is H.Amdt. 227 (Pitts) to H.R. 1947, held 6/20/2013, 113th Congress.
  - The 2018 vote is H.Amdt. 605 (Foxx) to H.R. 2, held 5/17/2018, 115th Congress.
  - The main model is a district fixed-effects (conditional) logit.
  - A second specification uses incumbent fixed effects. This follows the approach of Stratmann (2002).

- **Estimating equation:**
  - **Dependent variable:** 1 if the member voted "aye" on the reform amendment (against sugar interests), 0 otherwise.
  - **Key regressor:** inflation-adjusted total sugar-industry contributions to the incumbent, in real dollars. It enters linearly and is not standardised.
  - **Controls:**
    - American Conservative Union (ACU) ideology score (1–100, higher means more conservative)
    - Tenure/seniority
    - District poverty rate
    - % with a bachelor's degree
    - Median income
    - % of the population over 65
    - Dummy for House Agriculture Committee membership
  - **Fixed effects:** district (Table 1, col. 1) or incumbent (col. 2). Identification comes only from within-unit changes over time.

- **Data sources and construction:**
  - Votes come from GovTrack.us.
  - Contributions come from Center for Responsive Politics individual-level data. The authors sum all sugar-industry PAC contributions plus individual contributions over $200 from sugar-industry employees or their family members.
  - They checked Leadership PACs and single-candidate Super PACs for sugar money but did not add them to the measure.
  - Ideology and seniority come from the ACU.
  - Demographic and economic controls come from the U.S. Census Bureau.

- **Contribution time window (main specification):** contributions are summed from one year before each vote through the end of that vote's calendar year, so post-vote money is included.

- **Estimation sample:**
  - Conditional logit drops units whose vote did not change. The district FE model therefore uses the 93 vote-switching districts, giving 186 observations.
  - The incumbent FE robustness sample keeps only members who voted on both amendments and switched, giving 53 members. The table reports 106 observations.

- **Inference:** p-values are based on robust (heteroskedasticity-robust) standard errors. No clustering is reported.

- **Post-estimation:**
  - Marginal effects on the probability of voting for reform are plotted with 95% CIs for contributions, ideology, and tenure (Figs. 1–3).
  - Each effect is computed at 50 equally spaced values of the variable, with other covariates at their observed values, using the Stata Marhis package.
  - The model's reported fit statistic is the pseudo R².

- **Robustness checks:**
  - Two alternative contribution windows are tested in both FE models (Table 3; full regressions in Appendix A.2):
    - Calendar years of the votes: 1/1–12/31/2013 and 1/1–12/31/2018.
    - The 12 months up to each vote date, excluding post-vote money: 6/20/12–6/20/13 and 5/17/17–5/17/18.
  - DW-Nominate replaces the ACU score as the ideology measure.
  - A Republican party dummy is added.
  - Other controls (unemployment, % rural) are added.
  - Contributions aggregated by election cycle are used (Appendix A.3).
