## Paper Information
Title: Hate Crimes and Gender Imbalances: Fears over Mate
Abstract (first 500 chars): As the number of refugees rises across the world, anti-refugee violence has become a pressing concern. What ex-
plains the incidence and support of such hate crime? We argue that fears among native men that refugees pose a threat in
the competition for female partners are a critical but understudied factor driving hate crime. Employing a comprehensive
data set on the incidence of hate crime across Germany, we first demonstrate that hate crime rises where men face disad-
vantages in local mating 
Methodology Summary: - **Ecological (municipality-level) analysis — estimation:** Logistic regression of a binary hate-crime indicator on *Excess Males*, with standard errors clustered at the county level (Figure 1; full results in SI Table C.1). Two specifications: (i) **Sum** — cross-section of municipalities, DV = 1 if the municipality recorded ≥1 anti-refugee hate crime over 2015–2017 combined (17% of municipalities), 0 otherwise; (ii) **Annual** — municipality-year panel, DV = 1 if ≥1 hate crime in that year (5.6% in 2015, 12.1% in 2016, 6.8% in 2017, out of 11,168 municipalities). County fixed effects and year fixed effects included. Robustness: linear probability model (SI C.3), negative binomial on incident counts (SI C.5), physical attacks only (SI C.4), East–West interactions (SI C.6), alternative age ranges (SI C.2), placebo (SI C.9).
- **Key regressor and data:** *Excess Males* = number of men / number of women aged 15–44 in the municipality (a raw ratio, not standardised; 1 = balance, 1.2 ≈ 80th percentile). Hate-crime DV from the "Mut gegen rechte Gewalt" chronicle (Amadeu Antonio Foundation & PRO ASYL): arson on refugee housing, other attacks on refugee housing, physical attacks on refugees. Alternative mate-competition measure: *Male Disadvantage* = male / female unemployment rate (r = −0.048 with Excess Males; SI Figure C.8).
- **Controls (preferred spec):** population, population density, population change, unemployment rate, ratio of male to female unemployment rate, number of refugees (stock, county level), change in refugee population (flow), percent highly educated, percent manufacturing, change in percent manufacturing, AfD vote share, violent crime per capita; plus county and year FE.
- **Sample restriction:** municipalities with extreme Excess Males values (top and bottom 2.5 percentiles) are dropped — mostly very small municipalities (median population of excluded = 247); results stated to be insensitive to this.
- **Ecological evaluation/quantity of interest:** predicted probability of ≥1 hate crime across the range of Excess Males, and first difference from Excess Males = 1 to 1.2: +2.60 percentage points (95% CI [0.88, 4.19]) and +1.71 points (95% CI [0.76, 2.55]) for the two models; also 20th→80th percentile comparisons vs. unemployment rate and share highly educated.
- **Individual-level survey analysis:** Respondi online survey, 4 waves (Sept 2016–Dec 2017), each ~3,000 respondents, quota-representative on age (18+), gender, state. *Mate Competition* item (Wave 4, 1–4 scale). Figure 2: proportion agreeing (agree somewhat/strongly) by terciles of municipal Excess Males, for all respondents, men 18–44, men 30–40, with 95% CIs and p-values.
- **Hate-crime support regressions (Table 1):** OLS, DV = *Only Means* (1 = disagree strongly … 4 = agree strongly), key regressor = *Mate Competition* (1–4, unstandardised); conventional OLS SEs (ordered logit gives similar results). Six nested columns: (1) Mate Competition only (coef 0.437, SE 0.016, N = 3,019); (2) + Job Competition (1–4) and Life Satisfaction (0–10) (0.263, N = 3,019); (3) + sociodemographics — age, gender, education, income, main economic activity, employment status, German citizenship, household size, self-assessed social status, religious affiliation, state of residence (0.236, N = 3,008); (4) + national attitudes toward refugees (0.206); (5) + local attitudes toward refugees (0.185); (6) + additional controls — public contact with refugees, closeness to refugee shelters, estimated number of refugees in town, views of/closeness to AfD, left-right placement, attitudes toward Muslims (0.155, SE 0.019, N = 3,008, R² = 0.459, adj. R² = 0.431). Figure 4 repeats column (6) for DVs Only Means, Justified, Message, Prevent, Condemn (reverse-coded), reporting one-unit effects with 95% CIs.
- **List experiment (social-desirability check):** Wave 2 (also Wave 1 and pilot); 50% of sample split into control (3 non-sensitive items) and treatment (3 items + Only Means); difference in mean counts (2.09 − 1.94 ≈ 15%) compared with direct question (18%, among the 75% asked directly); difference insignificant. No train/test split or cross-validation is used (inferential, not predictive, analysis).
