Title: Hate Crimes and Gender Imbalances: Fears over Mate
Methodology: - **Ecological design (municipality level, Germany, 2015–2017):** Logistic regression of anti-refugee hate crime incidence on *Excess Males* (number of men ÷ number of women aged 15–44 in the municipality; a raw ratio, not standardised). Hate crime data come from "Mut gegen rechte Gewalt" (Amadeu Antonio Foundation & PRO ASYL) and cover arson against refugee housing, other attacks on refugee housing, and physical attacks on refugees. The DV is binary in two versions: (a) **Sum**: 1 if the municipality had ≥1 hate crime at any point in 2015–2017 (17% of municipalities; unit = municipality); (b) **Annual**: 1 if ≥1 hate crime in a given year (unit = municipality-year panel; 5.6%/12.1%/6.8% of 11,168 municipalities in 2015/2016/2017).
- **Preferred ecological specification:** logit with **county and year fixed effects** and controls for population, population density, population change, unemployment rate, ratio of male to female unemployment rate (Male Disadvantage), number of refugees (stock, county level), change in refugee population (flow), % highly educated, % manufacturing, change in % manufacturing, AfD vote share, and violent crime per capita (SI Table C.1/C.11). **SEs clustered at the county level.** Headline quantity: change in predicted probability of ≥1 hate crime when Excess Males goes from 1.0 to 1.2 (≈80th percentile): +2.60 pp (95% CI [0.88, 4.19]) and +1.71 pp (95% CI [0.76, 2.55]) across the Sum/Annual models (Figure 1, right panel).
- **Sample restriction (ecological):** observations with extreme Excess Males values are dropped (top and bottom 2.5 percentiles; mostly very small municipalities, median population 247). No train/test split or cross-validation; this is inferential regression.
- **Ecological robustness checks:** linear probability model (SI C.3); negative binomial on incident counts (SI Table C.5); physical attacks only (SI Table C.4); other age ranges for the sex ratio (SI Table C.2); East–West interactions (SI Table C.6); the alternative mate-competition measure Male Disadvantage = male ÷ female unemployment rate (r = −0.048 with Excess Males; SI Fig. C.8); a placebo analysis (SI C.9); interactions with county refugee inflows (results mixed); 20th→80th percentile effect comparisons with unemployment and education.
- **Individual-level survey data:** Respondi online panel, 4 waves (Sept 2016–Dec 2017), each ≈3,000 respondents, quota-representative on age (18+), gender and Bundesland. *Mate Competition* ("The inflow of refugees makes it more difficult for native men to find female partners", 1–4 scale, from strongly disagree to strongly agree) was asked in Wave 4. Figure 2 shows the proportion agreeing (with 95% CIs and p-values) across terciles of municipal Excess Males, for all respondents, men aged 18–44 and men aged 30–40. Validity checks: single-male status and Women's Role predict Mate Competition, controlling for 17 other refugee-related attitudes (SI Fig. D.3).

Prevalence = difference in mean item counts (2.09 − 1.94 ≈ 15%), compared with direct questioning of the remaining 75% (18%); the difference is not significant, so direct items are used afterwards.
