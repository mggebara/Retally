## Paper Information
Title: Hate Crimes and Gender Imbalances: Fears over Mate
Abstract (first 500 chars): As the number of refugees rises across the world, anti-refugee violence has become a pressing concern. What ex-
plains the incidence and support of such hate crime? We argue that fears among native men that refugees pose a threat in
the competition for female partners are a critical but understudied factor driving hate crime. Employing a comprehensive
data set on the incidence of hate crime across Germany, we first demonstrate that hate crime rises where men face disad-
vantages in local mating 
Methodology Summary: - **Ecological analysis (municipality level, Germany, 2015–2017):** Dependent variable is a binary indicator of anti-refugee hate crime from the "Mut gegen rechte Gewalt" chronicle (Amadeu Antonio Foundation / PRO ASYL), covering arson attacks on refugee housing, other attacks on refugee housing (e.g., vandalism) and physical attacks on refugees. Two codings: (a) "Sum": municipality = 1 if it had at least one hate crime over the whole 3-year period 2015–2017, 0 otherwise (unit = municipality; ~11,168 municipalities); (b) "Annual": municipality-year = 1 if at least one hate crime that year (panel of municipality-years).
- **Key regressor and specification:** Excess Males = number of men / number of women aged 15–44 in the municipality (a raw ratio, entered in levels, not standardised). Estimated by logistic regression of the hate-crime indicator on Excess Males plus controls, with county fixed effects (and year fixed effects in the annual panel). Controls (fn. 24): population, population density, population change, unemployment rate, ratio of male to female unemployment rate, number of refugees (stock, county level), change in refugee population (flow), percent highly educated, percent manufacturing, change in percent manufacturing, AfD vote share, and violent crime per capita. Standard errors clustered at the county level.
- **Sample restriction and quantities of interest:** Municipalities with extreme Excess Males values (top and bottom 2.5 percentiles) are dropped. The headline quantity is the change in predicted probability of at least one hate crime when Excess Males moves from 1.0 (balanced) to 1.2 (≈80th percentile), shown with 95% CIs (Figure 1). Comparisons of 20th→80th percentile changes for Excess Males vs. unemployment rate and share highly educated.
- **Ecological robustness checks (SI):** linear probability model (SI C.3); negative binomial model for incident counts (SI C.5); physical attacks only (SI C.4); alternative age ranges for the sex ratio (SI C.2); East–West interactions (SI C.6); an alternative mate-competition measure, Male Disadvantage = male unemployment rate / female unemployment rate (SI Fig. C.8); interactions with county refugee inflows; placebo analysis (SI C.9).
- **Survey data:** Four-wave online Respondi panel (Sept 2016–Dec 2017), each wave ~3,000 respondents, quota-representative on age (18+), gender and state. Mate Competition item (Wave 4): "The inflow of refugees makes it more difficult for native men to find female partners," 4-point scale (1 = disagree strongly … 4 = agree strongly). Descriptives: proportion agreeing (agree somewhat/strongly) by terciles of municipality Excess Males, for all respondents, men aged 18–44 and men aged 30–40, with 95% CIs and p-values (Figure 2). Discriminant-validity check: regress Mate Competition and 17 other refugee-related items on being a single male and on Women's Role, controlling for the other refugee views.
- **List experiment (Wave 2; also pilot and Wave 1 with Message item):** 50% of the sample split into control (3 unrelated statements) and treatment (same 3 + Only Means); the difference in mean number of items agreed with estimates the share supporting hate crime. This is compared with the direct-question share among the remaining 75% (non-list respondents plus the control group).
- **Individual-level regressions (Table 1):** OLS of Only Means ("violence is sometimes the only means…", 1–4) on Mate Competition (1–4, unstandardised), with nested specifications: (1) Mate Competition only; (2) + Job Competition (4-point) and Life Satisfaction (0–10); (3) + sociodemographics (age, gender, education, income, main economic activity, employment status, German citizenship, household size, self-assessed social status, religious affiliation, state of residence); (4) + national attitudes toward refugees; (5) + local attitudes toward refugees; (6, preferred) + additional controls (self-reported contact with refugees, estimated closeness to refugee shelters, estimated number of refugees in town, views about and closeness to the AfD, left–right self-placement, attitudes toward Muslims). Conventional OLS standard errors; N ≈ 3,000 (Wave 4). Ordered logit used as a robustness check.
- **Other outcomes (Figure 4, SI D.5.1):** the column (6) specification re-estimated for the hate-crime-support items Justified, Message, Prevent and Condemn (reverse-coded), all 1–4; effects reported as a one-unit change in Mate Competition with 95% CIs. Additional checks: subsample of men (SI D.6) and East–West interactions (SI D.5.2).
