Abstract (first 500 chars): Advance Access Publication Date: 31 December 2021 PATIENCE, RISK-TAKING, AND HUMAN CAPITAL INVESTMENT ACROSS COUNTRIES∗ Eric A. Hanushek, Lavinia Kinne, Philipp Lergetporer and Ludger Woessmann Patience and risk-taking—two preference components that steer intertemporal decision-making—are fun- damental to human capital investment decisions. To understand how they contribute to international skill
Methodology Summary: - **Data and samples**: Student-level PISA data (math, science, reading for 15-year-olds; seven waves 2000, 2003, 2006, 2009, 2012, 2015, 2018) merged with country-level preference measures from the Global Preference Survey (GPS; Falk et al., 2018; ~1,000 respondents per country, surveyed 2012). The baseline cross-country sample covers the 49 countries in both PISA and GPS (1,992,276 students, 263 country-by-wave cells). The migrant sample uses waves 2003–2018 (2000 is dropped because it has no country-of-origin information): 80,398 migrant students from 58 origin countries in 48 residence countries (up to 145,506 under a wider migrant definition).
- **Preprocessing**: The GPS patience and risk-taking measures (each built from one qualitative item and one hypothetical-choice item, combined with validation weights) are z-standardised within the relevant analytical sample and then collapsed to country means, as are the other four GPS domains (positive reciprocity, negative reciprocity, altruism, trust). PISA scores are divided by 100, so the outcome is in (international) SD units. Missing control values are imputed and flagged with imputation dummies. Test-taking effort proxy: item non-response = share of unanswered questions in the student background questionnaire (individual level, plus the country mean or country-of-origin mean). Migrant = both parents born abroad. First-generation migrants are assigned their country of birth and second-generation migrants their father's country of origin.
- **Baseline specification (Eq. 1, Table 1)**: Student-level OLS of T_ict (PISA math score/100) on country-level Patience_c and Risk_c, entered separately (cols 1–2) and jointly (col 3, preferred). Controls B: student gender, age, migration status and imputation dummies, plus test-wave fixed effects μ_t. Extensions: adding the four other GPS preferences (col 4); separate natives and migrants subsamples (cols 5–6) with a test of the difference; adding individual item non-response (col 7) and country-mean item non-response (col 8). The coefficients are in SD of achievement per SD of the preference. The headline estimates are not otherwise standardised.
- **Weighting and inference**: Least squares weighted by students' sampling probabilities within countries, rescaled so each country gets equal weight. Robust standard errors are clustered at the country level (49 clusters) in the cross-country analysis. A country-level analysis (Figure 1) regresses country-average math scores pooled over waves on patience and/or risk-taking (bivariate fits and added-variable plots) and reports the R².
- **Migrant identification (Eq. 2, Table 2)**: OLS among migrant students of T_ioct on origin-country Patience_o and Risk_o with residence-country × wave fixed effects θ_c×μ_t (180 cells). Controls: gender, age, a dummy for OECD origin country, and imputation dummies. Standard errors are clustered by country of origin. Columns: each preference alone (1–2); both together (3, preferred); plus the four other origin-country GPS preferences (4); individual and origin-mean item non-response (5–7); split by language spoken at home, residence-country language vs other (8–9), with a test of the difference.
- **Selectivity robustness (Table 3)**: Adds continent-of-origin fixed effects; within-origin SDs of patience and risk-taking (from individual GPS data weighted by sampling probability); geographic distance between the most populous cities of the two countries in 1,000 km (Mayer–Zignago); interactions of each preference with the absolute residence–origin preference difference (all variables demeaned), on the subsample where that is available; and migrant schooling selectivity (the percentile of the origin-country attainment distribution from which the average migrant in each residence country comes), on the subsample where that is available. Online appendix robustness: OECD vs non-OECD countries, science and reading, the first PISA wave after the GPS, WVS and Hofstede measures, migrant generation and age at migration, and alternative migrant definitions.
- **Channels (Table 4)**: Country-level OLS (N = 49) with robust SEs of four aggregate proximate-input indices (family inputs, school inputs, institutional inputs, and a residual "productivity" term) on patience and risk-taking. The "upper bound" version builds the input aggregation without the preference controls, and the "lower bound" version builds it with them. This analysis is descriptive, not a mediation analysis.
- **Evaluation**: Coefficient signs, magnitudes and significance with clustered SEs; R² (student level, and country-level variance explained); tests of differences between subsamples. There is no train/test split or cross-validation.
