## Paper Information
Title: American Economic Review 2023, 113(2): 396–429
Abstract (first 500 chars): 396 Enabling or Limiting Cognitive Flexibility? Evidence of Demand for Moral Commitment† By Silvia Saccardo and Marta  Serra-Garcia* Moral behavior is more prevalent when individuals cannot easily distort their beliefs  self-servingly. Do individuals seek to limit or
Methodology Summary: - **Design / setting:** Online incentivized experiments (N = 9,323 advisors; Table 1) built on an advice game. An advisor recommends product A (urn with three $2 / two $0 balls, pays $2 w.p. 0.6) or product B (quality s ∈ {H, L}, Pr(H) = 0.5; H pays $2 w.p. 0.8, L w.p. 0.4) to an uninformed client. Advisors see one ball drawn from B as a quality signal and learn which product carries a $0.15 commission. By design, advisors with a low-quality B are paid a commission for recommending B and those with a high-quality B for recommending A, so about 70% face a conflict of interest (commission on B with a $0 signal, or commission on A with a $2 signal). The key outcome is a binary indicator for recommending the incentivized product, analysed mainly when there is a conflict of interest.
- **Treatments:** (i) NoChoice: random assignment to See Incentive First (N = 152) or Assess Quality First (N = 147). (ii) Choice: advisors choose the information order. Their choice is implemented with 50% probability, otherwise the order is set by a 50-50 draw, so the preferred order is implemented 75% of the time and the assigned order is random conditional on preference. Arms: Choice Free–Professionals (712; self-reported finance/insurance and legal workers, recruited on Prolific and CloudResearch and pooled), Choice Free (2,574), Incentive First Costly (1,562) and Quality First Costly (1,067), where the costly arms charge $0.05 to see the chosen piece first. Robustness arms: High Stakes 10-fold/100-fold, Replication, and Deterministic (preference implemented with certainty). (iii) Choice Stakes: commission of $0.01, $0.15 or $0.30, with seeing the incentive first always costly. (iv) Information Architect: third parties choose the order for advisors, with pay tied to the advisor (IA-Advisor) or to the client (IA-Client). The Choice experiment ran in three separately preregistered waves (aspredicted.org).
- **Estimation:** Treatment comparisons and regressions (linear probability-type) of recommending the incentivized product on information order (assigned and/or preferred). The key comparisons are: preference × assignment cells (preferred and assigned vs. preferred but not assigned); the commitment vs. flexibility gap among advisors assigned their preferred order; and Choice vs. NoChoice. Preference to see the incentive first is regressed on standardized "selfishness" (the moral-cost measure) and on take-up of blinding. Beliefs are compared by signal type (conflicting vs. aligned) and by order.
- **Controls:** All regressions control for wave-2 design variations: probabilistic (1-in-100, $15 commission, $0/$20 product payoffs) vs. standard payment, and whether the incentivized product was shown on the left or right of the screen. These variations are pooled.
- **Measures:** Beliefs that B is low quality, elicited (i) as one of ten 10-percentage-point intervals, paid $0.15 (or $15 in the 1/100 arms) for a correct range, and (ii) as an exact 0–100 number. Moral costs are measured with a 5-row multiple price list: a recommendation of Y (commission $0.15, $0 signal) vs. X paying $2 with probability 1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4 or 0. This is standardized and called "overall selfishness"; it is not collected for Professionals. In wave 3 there is also a blinding task (whether to avoid learning the incentive). Clients (N = 924) are recruited later and see only the recommendation.
- **Sample restrictions:** Following the preregistration, the main analyses keep only attentive advisors who gave consistent multiple-price-list responses and drop those who switched multiple times (results including them are in online Appendix C). Further exclusion criteria are listed in online Appendix B.
- **Inference / evaluation:** Tests of the differences in recommendation rates across orders and cells, in the share choosing each order (including the share willing to pay), and of the correlation of preferences with morality. The section on standard errors is not in the provided text; robust (heteroskedasticity-consistent) standard errors at the individual level would be expected for this individual-level randomization but are not stated. There is no train/test split or cross-validation.
