Methodology Summary: - **Design / data**: Natural field experiment near 30 randomly selected public elementary schools in Lyon, France, run on 30 days in May–June 2019. Total sample is 601 parents (301 in the "Alone" condition, 300 in "Child"). The design is 3×2 between-subject. The first dimension is scene, played by one of two trained actors (one male, one female): Violation (littering a banana-peel bag), Help (accidentally dropping a bag of folders and pens), or Violation + Help (VH). The second is the naturally occurring child presence: parents bringing or picking up a child ("Child") versus parents arriving to pick up or just having dropped off a child ("Alone"). Scenes ran in mixed order in the morning (~7:45–9:00) and afternoon (~16:15–18:00). Each school was visited on at most one day, and actors alternated randomly across schools. Pre-registered on AsPredicted (#24270).
- **Sample restrictions**: Only single parents walking alone or with one or more children were targeted. Parents were excluded if visibly in a rush, on the phone, pushing a stroller, riding a bike, walking a dog, holding something with both hands, or accompanying a disabled child. Targets who reported having no child at the school were dropped, as were parents who did not observe the violation. A follow-up survey (by RA2) recorded the relationship to the child. Survey statistics exclude parents who could not be reached. One observation lacks target gender and drops out of the controlled models.
- **Outcomes**: Punishment is a dummy equal to 1 if the parent verbally and explicitly confronts the actor about the littering. Helping is a dummy equal to 1 if the parent picks up at least one item or asks the child to do so; a child helping without any parental prompt does not count. Indirect punishment is the drop in helping in VH relative to Help.
- **Estimating equation**: Pooled OLS linear probability models (Table 2). The dependent variable is Punishment (cols 1–2) or Helping (cols 3–4). Regressors are Child, VH and Child × VH, all binary, with unstandardised coefficients. The Punishment models use only the Violation and VH scenes (~400 obs); the Helping models use only the Help and VH scenes (~400 obs). Child × VH tests Conjecture 2 (indirect punishment).
- **Controls (preferred cols 2 and 4)**: Male Target, Male Actor, Morning, Witness (another adult observing and able to intervene, as judged by RA1), Rich IRIS (school's IRIS-area median income above the city-wide median), Rain and Hot. Survey Response is not included. No fixed effects.
- **Inference**: Standard errors are clustered at the school level (30 clusters). Raw rate comparisons between conditions and scenes use χ² tests (1 df). Balance (Table 1) is checked with two-sided t-tests on equality of means.
- **Robustness / extensions**: Logit models instead of LPMs (App. A.4.4). Alternative sample restrictions and definitions, e.g. treating a parent who prompts the child to help as not helping (Table A9, App. A.4.5). A Child × Witness interaction (App. A.4.2). Exploratory heterogeneity by number, gender and age of children, actor and parent gender, and IRIS income (App. A.4.6). Timing-slot effects and their interaction with Child (App. A.4.7). Separate online vignette studies, using the Krupka–Weber norm-elicitation design and video-based ratings, check whether perceived norms differ with child presence (App. A.5–A.6).
