Abstract (first 500 chars): We examine the educative role played by parents in social norm transmission. Using
a field experiment, we study whether parents enforce and comply more with norms

Methodology Summary: - **Design**: Natural field experiment (pre-registered, AsPredicted #24270) with a 3×2 between-subject design, run near 30 randomly selected public elementary schools in Lyon on 30 days in May–June 2019 (N = 601 parents: 301 Alone, 300 Child). Scene treatments, played by one of two trained actors (male/female): "Violation" (actor throws a plastic bag with a banana peel to the side of the street), "Help" (actor seemingly accidentally drops the folders and pens from a bag), "Violation + Help" (both). Condition is the naturally occurring presence of a child: Child (parent bringing the child to school in the morning, 7:45–9:00, or just after pick-up in the afternoon, 16:15–18:00) vs. Alone (parent after drop-off or on the way to pick-up). About 100 observations per scene × condition cell.
- **Targeting / sample restrictions**: Only single adults walking alone or with one or more children. Excluded: parents visibly in a rush, on the phone, pushing a stroller, riding a bike, walking a dog, holding something in both hands, or accompanying disabled children. Also dropped: targets who did not observe the violation, and targets who said in the post-scene survey (RA2) that they had no child at the school. Each school was visited on at most one day. One observation is missing the target's gender, so the models with controls lose one observation (399/400).
- **Outcomes (binary)**: Punishment = 1 if the parent verbally and explicitly confronts the actor about the littering, else 0. Helping = 1 if the parent picks up at least one item or asks the child to pick one up, else 0; a child helping without the parent prompting does not count.
- **Estimation**: Pooled OLS linear probability models (Table 2). Punishment is estimated on the Violation and Violation + Help scenes only (Help scene dropped; ~400 obs). Helping is estimated on the Help and Violation + Help scenes only (Violation scene dropped; ~401 obs). Specification: Y = β0 + β1·Child + β2·VH + β3·Child×VH (+ controls) + ε, where VH is a dummy for the Violation + Help scene. Coefficients are in raw probability units, not standardised. Columns (1) and (3) have no controls; columns (2) and (4) are the full specification.
- **Controls (columns 2 and 4)**: Male Target (father), Male Actor, Morning, Witness (RA1 judged that another non-targeted adult was watching and able to intervene), Rich IRIS (school's IRIS-area median income is above the city median), Rain, Hot. Survey Response is not used as a control. There are no fixed effects.
- **Inference**: Standard errors clustered at the school level (30 clusters). Treatment comparisons also use χ² tests on rates: Child vs. Alone within a scene, and Violation vs. Violation + Help within a condition. Balance checks are two-sided t-tests of equal means (Table 1). Conjecture 2 (indirect punishment, meaning help withheld after a violation) is tested with the Child×VH coefficient in the Helping model. Conjecture 1 is tested with the Child coefficient in the Punishment model and Conjecture 3 with the Child coefficient in the Helping model.
- **Robustness and extensions**: Logit models instead of LPM. Child×Witness interactions, and Witness treated as a treatment within the Alone condition. Alternative sample restrictions and definitions (e.g., not counting a child prompted to help as helping). Heterogeneity by number, gender and age of children, actor and parent gender, and IRIS income (exploratory). Timing-slot effects. Two separate online norm-elicitation studies (Krupka–Weber vignette coordination game; a video study) check whether perceived norms change when a child is present.
