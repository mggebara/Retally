Methodology Summary: - **Design / data**: Pre-registered (AsPredicted #24270) natural field experiment with a 3 × 2 between-subject design, run on 30 days in May–June 2019 near 30 randomly selected public elementary schools in Lyon, France (each school visited at most one day). The scene is randomised: Violation (actor litters), Help (actor drops bag contents), or Violation + Help (VH). The child condition occurs naturally: Child (parent walking with child, bringing them to or picking them up from school) vs Alone (parent before pick-up or after drop-off). Mornings were 7:45–9:00 and afternoons 16:15–18:00. Two trained actors (one male, one female) were alternated randomly across schools. N = 601 parents (301 Alone, 300 Child), with about 100 per cell (Alone-VH has n = 101 for helping).
- **Outcomes (binary)**: *Punishment* = 1 if the parent verbally and explicitly confronts the actor about the littering, 0 otherwise. *Helping* = 1 if the parent picks up at least one item, or asks the child to, 0 otherwise. A child who helped with no prompting from the parent is not counted as helping.
- **Sample restrictions**: Targets were single parents walking alone or with one or more children. The team did not target parents visibly in a rush, on the phone, pushing a stroller, on a bike, walking a dog, holding something with both hands, or with a disabled child. Dropped from the sample: targets who did not see the violation, and targets who said in the survey that they had no child at the school. Punishment is analysed only on the Violation and VH scenes (N = 400), and Helping only on the Help and VH scenes (N = 401). One observation with missing target gender drops from the specifications with controls (N = 399 and 400).
- **Estimating equation**: Pooled OLS linear probability models (Table 2): Y_i = β0 + β1·Child_i + β2·VH_i + β3·(Child × VH)_i [+ X_i'γ] + ε_i, with Y = Punishment (cols 1–2) or Helping (cols 3–4). Coefficients are unstandardised probability changes. Child is the headline coefficient: 0.11** / 0.12** for Punishment and 0.21*** / 0.22*** for Helping. Child × VH tests Conjecture 2 (indirect punishment): −0.13*** / −0.12*** for Punishment and −0.06 / −0.07 (n.s.) for Helping.
- **Controls (preferred cols 2 and 4)**: Male Target, Male Actor, Morning, Witness (another adult able to intervene), Rich IRIS (school's IRIS-area median income above the city median), Rain, and Hot. These are all Table 1 variables except Survey Response. There are no fixed effects.
- **Inference**: Standard errors are clustered at the school level (30 clusters, df = 29). Raw comparisons between conditions use χ² tests (e.g., punishment in Violation 22% vs 11%, χ²(1) = 4.39, p = 0.036; helping in Help 47% vs 26%, χ²(1) = 9.82, p = 0.002; VH helping 31% vs 16%, χ²(1) = 6.26, p = 0.012). Balance (Table 1) is checked with two-sided t-tests: the Child condition has more Morning observations (+0.13***) and more Witness observations (+0.05*).
- **Robustness**: Logit models instead of LPM (App. A.4.4). Alternative sample restrictions and outcome definitions, e.g., child-prompted help coded as not helping (Table A9). A Child × Witness interaction, and Witness used as a pseudo-treatment within the Alone condition. Timing effects (A.4.7). Exploratory heterogeneity by the number, age and gender of children, actor and parent gender, and IRIS income (A.4.6). Online vignette studies using the Krupka–Weber norm-elicitation method (App. A.5–A.6).
- **Not applicable**: This is an experimental causal analysis, so there is no train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning.
