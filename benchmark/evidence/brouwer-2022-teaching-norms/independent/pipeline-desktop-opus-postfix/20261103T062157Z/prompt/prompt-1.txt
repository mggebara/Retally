Methodology Summary: - **Design / data**: Natural field experiment (pre-registered, AsPredicted #24270) near 30 randomly selected public elementary schools in Lyon, France, on 30 days in May–June 2019; N = 601 parents (301 Alone, 300 Child). It is a 3×2 between-subject design: scene (Violation, Help, Violation + Help [VH]) × condition (Child = parent accompanying a child to or from school; Alone = parent before pick-up or after drop-off). Two trained actors (one male, one female) were randomly alternated across schools. Each school was visited on at most one day. Scenes ran in mixed order in the morning (≈7:45–9:00) and afternoon (≈16:15–18:00). Cell sizes are about 100 each (Violation: 100/100; VH: 100/100; Help: 100 Alone / 101 Child).
- **Outcome variables (binary)**: *Punishment* = 1 if the parent verbally and explicitly confronts the actor about the littering, 0 otherwise. *Helping* = 1 if the parent picks up at least one dropped item (or asks the child to), 0 otherwise. A child helping without the parent prompting does not count. Indirect punishment is identified as lower helping in VH than in Help.
- **Sample restrictions**: The sample covers only single adults walking alone or with child(ren). Parents were not targeted if they were visibly in a rush, on the phone, pushing a stroller, on a bike, walking a dog, holding something with both hands, or accompanying a disabled child. Dropped: targets who did not observe the violation, and targets who said in the follow-up survey that they had no child at the school. The Punishment analysis uses only the Violation and VH scenes (N = 400). The Helping analysis uses only the Help and VH scenes (N = 401). One observation is missing target gender, so the models with controls have 399 and 400 observations.
- **Estimating equation (Table 2)**: Pooled OLS linear probability models: Y = β0 + β1·Child + β2·VH + β3·(Child × VH) [+ controls] + ε, with Y = Punishment (cols 1–2) or Helping (cols 3–4). Coefficients are unstandardised changes in probability. Columns 1 and 3 have no controls. Columns 2 and 4 (preferred) add Male Target, Male Actor, Morning, Witness (another non-targeted adult observing who could intervene), Rich IRIS ("Rich area": the school's IRIS-area median income is above the city median), Rain, and Hot. There are no fixed effects. Survey Response is not a control.
- **Standard errors**: Clustered at the school level (30 clusters, df = 29). The table reports R², F, and the number of clusters.
- **Headline estimates**: Punishment: Child = 0.11** (0.05) in col 1 and 0.12** (0.04) in col 2; Child × VH = −0.13*** / −0.12***; VH = −0.04; constant 0.11 / 0.14. Helping: Child = 0.21*** (0.05) in col 3 and 0.22*** (0.06) in col 4; VH = −0.10** in both; Child × VH = −0.06 / −0.07 (not significant); constant 0.26 / 0.36; R² = 0.04 / 0.07 / 0.06 / 0.13. Raw rates: punishment 0.22 Child vs 0.11 Alone in Violation and 0.05 vs 0.07 in VH; helping 0.47 vs 0.26 in Help and 0.31 vs 0.16 in VH.
- **Non-parametric tests and balance**: Conditions are compared with χ²(1) tests. Punishment in Violation, Child vs Alone: χ² = 4.39, p = 0.036. Helping in Help: χ² = 9.82, p = 0.002. Helping in VH: χ² = 6.26, p = 0.012. Drop in punishment from Violation to VH: Child χ² = 12.37, p < 0.001; Alone χ² = 0.98, p = 0.323. Balance (Table 1) uses two-sided t-tests of equal means. The Child condition has more Morning observations (diff −0.13***) and more Witness (−0.05*). Survey response is 47% overall (computed on 503 reachable parents).
- **Robustness**: Logit models instead of the LPM (Appendix A.4.4). Alternative sample restrictions and definitions, e.g. counting a parent prompting the child to help as not helping (Table A9). A Child × Witness interaction (A.4.2). Witness treated as a pseudo-treatment within the Alone condition. Exploratory heterogeneity by number, age, and gender of children, actor and parent gender, and IRIS income (A.4.6). Timing effects (A.4.7). There is no train/test split or cross-validation: this is inference from an experiment.
