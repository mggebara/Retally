Methodology Summary: - **Design / identification:** Natural experiment from the University of Cape Town's policy of randomly allocating first-year students to residences and, within 8 residences with double rooms where wardens stated they used random allocation, to roommates. One cohort: 2012 freshmen in double rooms; baseline survey in February 2012 (first week of the academic year) and follow-up in September 2012 (before final exams). Treatment `MixRoom` = 1 if the student was initially assigned a roommate of a different race; analysis uses the initial room assignment throughout, so estimates are intention-to-treat (no LATE).
- **Sample restrictions:** Of the 10 residences originally surveyed, 2 were dropped because they had very few double rooms and no white students in doubles (9 obs.). Working sample = 499 students with both baseline and follow-up data and non-missing Race IAT and Academic IAT (out of 621 with non-missing baseline IATs). Race groups: Black, White, Coloured, Indian/Asian/Other; main estimates are run on the full sample and separately on the white and black subsamples.
- **Outcomes / variable construction:** (i) Race IAT (positive vs negative attributes) and an author-designed Academic IAT (“First class (>75%)” vs “Third class (<60%)”), coded so that lower values mean more negative stereotypes towards black relative to white people; (ii) administrative academic outcomes: year-1 GPA standardised to mean 0, SD 1 within each year, number of exams passed (0–9), “Eligible to continue” dummy, and a summary performance index (first principal component of the three); second-year versions in Appendix Table A10; (iii) 15 attitudinal/behavioural measures grouped into friendship, explicit-attitudes and prosocial-behaviour indices, including a prisoner's dilemma game (strategy method, race shown through photos). UCT admission score = APS (best 6 high-school grades, max 600) divided by 1000. Household wealth = PCA index of durable-goods ownership; consumption = last month's spending on a listed set of items.
- **Estimating equation (eq. 2), OLS:** Y_ijkt = α·Y_ijk0 + β·MixRoom_ik0 + Race_i + γ·X_ik0 + δ·X_jk0 + φ·(RaceGrp_i × residence_k) + ε_ijkt, with the coefficient of interest on MixRoom (β in this notation). Y_ijk0 is the baseline value of the outcome (dropped when no baseline exists, e.g. GPA and the lab games). Race dummies: White, Coloured, Indian/Asian/Other; Black is the omitted group. Own controls X_i and the same controls for the roommate X_j: gender, UCT admission score, household wealth, monthly consumption, and a dummy for not being from South Africa. Fixed effects: RaceGrp (Black/White/residual) × residence in the full sample. For IAT outcomes the model is estimated only on the white and black subsamples, without race dummies and with residence FE only. Outcomes are in levels and coefficients are in the outcome's units (IAT score units, standardised GPA, number of exams, probability).
- **Additional specification details:** Academic-performance regressions add academic-program fixed effects. Prisoner's dilemma regressions add a dummy for knowing the matched player. Ordered logit is used for categorical (ordered) attitudinal outcomes. For controls other than the baseline IAT, missing values are replaced by the mean and a missing-value indicator dummy is added. Robustness checks: specifications without roommate controls (Tables A7, A9), effects split by the roommate's race (A8), interactions with same faculty or a shared course (A11), and a test of Race IAT vs Academic IAT effects using a Seemingly Unrelated Regression.
- **Inference:** Standard errors are clustered at the room level. Westfall–Young free step-down resampling p-values (FWER) with 10,000 replications, re-randomising mixed/non-mixed status and matching roommates within the same residence and gender while holding the number treated fixed. Families are defined within each table and subsample: Race IAT + Academic IAT (Table 3); GPA, exams passed, eligible to continue (Table 4); the three indices (Table 6). Anderson FDR q-values are used as a check.
- **Randomization / balance checks:** Baseline balance of means between mixed and non-mixed rooms (Table 1, full sample/white/black). Regressions of MixRoom on pre-treatment characteristics (Table 2 A–B), with 10,000-replication simulated within-dorm random assignment for comparison (Table A5). An undirected dyadic regression over all N(N−1) pairs, SameRoom_ij on |X_i − X_j| and (X_i + X_j) (eq. 1; Fafchamps–Gubert). Simulated random pairings (10,000 replications) to benchmark the share of mixed rooms. Placebo regressions of baseline outcomes on treatment (Table A6). Attrition regressions (Table A1).
