Title: Interaction, Stereotypes and Performance.
Methodology: - **Design / identification**: Natural experiment using the University of Cape Town policy of random allocation of first-year students to residences and (in 8 residences with double rooms where wardens stated they randomized) to same-gender roommates. Treatment `MixRoom` = 1 if at baseline the student was assigned a roommate of a different race; the initial assignment is always used, so estimates are intention-to-treat (19% changed roommate, but only 2 moved from mixed to non-mixed, none the reverse; no LATE reported). Randomization checked via balance tests (Table 1), regressions of MixRoom on baseline characteristics (Table 2, panels A–B), an undirected dyadic sorting regression SameRoom_ij = α + β|X_i − X_j| + γ(X_i + X_j) + ε_ij (Table 2, panel C), 10,000-replication simulated random pairings (Table A5), and placebo regressions on lagged outcomes (Table A6).

- **Data and sample**: One cohort of 2012 freshmen; baseline survey February 2012 (first week of the year) and follow-up September 2012 (before final exams). Of 621 baseline respondents with non-missing IAT scores, 499 were traced at follow-up (80%); working sample = students with baseline and follow-up data and non-missing Race and Academic IAT (499: 157 mixed, 342 same-race; 332 black, 117 white, 18 coloured, 32 Indian/Asian/other). Two residences (9 obs) with very few double rooms and no white students in doubles were dropped. Attrition checked in Table A1 (no differential attrition by MixRoom or IAT).

- **Outcomes and preprocessing**: (i) Race IAT and Academic IAT scores (coded so lower = more negative stereotypes towards black relative to white; IATs administered first in each session); (ii) administrative academic outcomes for year 1 (and year 2 in Table A10): GPA standardized to mean 0, SD 1 within each year; number of exams passed (0–9); "Eligible to continue" dummy; and a summary performance index = first principal component of the three; (iii) 15 attitudinal/behavioral outcomes aggregated into three indices (friendship, explicit attitudes, prosocial behaviour, incl. a prisoner's dilemma game). UCT admission score (APS, out of 600) rescaled by dividing by 1000. Household wealth = PCA index of durable-goods ownership per capita; consumption = last-month spending. Missing values of controls (other than the baseline IAT) are replaced with the mean, and missing-value indicator dummies are added.

- **Estimating equation (eq. 2), OLS, levels (no logs)**: Y_ijkt = α·Y_ijk0 + β·MixRoom_ik0 + Race_i + X_ik0 + X_jk0 + RaceGrp_i × δ_k + ε_ijkt, where Y_ijk0 is the baseline value of the outcome (lagged dependent variable; omitted when not available, e.g. GPA and lab games). Race_i = dummies for White, Coloured, Indian/Asian/Other (Black omitted). X_ik0 (own) and X_jk0 (roommate) controls: gender, UCT admission score, household wealth index, monthly consumption expenditure, non-South-African dummy. Fixed effects: race-group (Black, White, residual) × residence dummies in the full sample; residence dummies only in race subsamples. Academic-performance regressions add academic-program fixed effects; prisoner's dilemma regressions add a dummy for knowing the matched player. Coefficient of interest β.

Academic, attitudinal and behavioural outcomes (Tables 4, 6) estimated for full sample and white and black subsamples. Headline coefficients are in the raw units of the outcome (IAT score points; GPA in SD units because GPA itself is standardized; number of exams; percentage-point eligibility); the paper converts them to SD effects in the text (e.g. Race IAT effect for whites 0.316 = 0.63 SD; black GPA effect 0.257 ≈ 0.27 SD; exams passed +0.65 ≈ 0.23 SD; eligibility +15.2 pp; performance index +0.44 for blacks, +0.29 SD full sample).
