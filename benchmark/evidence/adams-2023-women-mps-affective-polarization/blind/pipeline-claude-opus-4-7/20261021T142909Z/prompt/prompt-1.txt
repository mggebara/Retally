Methodology Summary: ## Methodology Summary

- **Estimation method**: Ordinary least squares (OLS) regression at the directed party-dyad level, combining an original dataset on women's parliamentary representation in 125 parties across 20 Western democracies (1996–2017) with CSES survey data on partisan feeling-thermometer ratings.

- **Estimating equation**: Dependent variable is [party i's supporters' mean evaluation of out-party j (t)] on a 0–10 feeling thermometer. Key regressor is [out-party j's proportion of women MPs (t−1)], scaled 0–1 and lagged one year. Functional form is linear (levels, not log-log).

- **Headline coefficient units**: Unstandardized — coefficients are interpreted as the change in the 0–10 thermometer scale when moving the proportion of women MPs from 0 to 1. The [elite right–left distance i, j (t)] control, derived from CMP manifesto scores, is standardized.

- **Controls / fixed effects (preferred specification, Table 1 col. 2)**: [elite right–left distance i, j (t)], [i, j are coalition partners (t)] dummy, [i, j are opposition partners (t)] dummy, and country-year fixed effects.

- **Standard errors**: Robust standard errors clustered by election.

- **Sample restrictions**: Party dyads where both parties have at least 4 MPs in the year before the election survey (smaller parties excluded due to limited consequence and measurement issues with few partisan respondents). Each pair enters twice (i→j and j→i). Final sample: 1,842 directed dyads across 81 election-years.

- **Robustness / extensions**: Separate re-estimation on women vs. men respondents; models including smaller parties; stacked individual-level data with individual fixed effects; country (rather than country-year) fixed effects; controls for party family and electoral system proportionality; splits by time period, in-party women share, parity thresholds, and gender of out-party leader (Appendix S2–S11).
