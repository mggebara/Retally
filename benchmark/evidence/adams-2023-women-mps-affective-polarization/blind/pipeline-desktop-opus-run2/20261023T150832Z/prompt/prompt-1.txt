Methodology Summary: - **Data and unit of analysis**: An original party-level dataset on women's share of each party's parliamentary delegation (125 parties, 20 Western democracies, 1996–2017) is merged with CSES post-election surveys (81 election studies). The data are directed party dyads: each observation is party i's partisans' rating of out-party j in election survey year t. Each pair i, j appears twice, once in each direction (i rating j and j rating i). The estimation sample is 1,842 directed dyads (Table 1, columns 1–2).
- **Dependent variable**: [party i's supporters' evaluations of out-party j (t)] is the mean 0–10 CSES feeling-thermometer score (0 = strongly dislike, 10 = strongly like) that party i's partisans give out-party j. Partisans are respondents who say they feel "close" or "a little closer" to party i, from the two-step CSES party-closeness question.
- **Key regressor**: [out-party j's proportion of women MPs (t – 1)] is the share of j's MPs who are women, measured one year before the survey (e.g., 2012 data for the 2013 German election). It is a raw proportion from 0 (all men) to 1 (all women) and is not standardised, so its coefficient is the change in thermometer units for moving from 0 to 1.
- **Controls (full model, Table 1 col. 2)**:
  - [elite right–left distance i, j (t)]: the absolute distance between i's and j's Comparative Manifesto Project left–right scores (original scale −100 to +100) in the current election. This control is standardised, so its coefficient is per 1 SD.
  - Dummy [i, j are coalition partners (t)]: both parties govern together at the time of the survey.
  - Dummy [i, j are opposition partners (t)]: both parties are in opposition.
  - The omitted baseline is a pair with one governing party and one opposition party.
  - Column 1 is the bivariate model: the women-MPs share only, plus fixed effects.
- **Estimator, fixed effects and standard errors**: OLS with country-year (election) fixed effects, so identification comes from within-election comparisons of out-parties. (The Table 1 row is labelled "Country and year fixed effects.") Standard errors are robust and clustered by election (country-year survey).
- **Sample restrictions**: The sample includes only dyads where both parties i and j had at least 4 MPs in the year before the survey. Smaller parties are dropped, and Appendix S2 adds them back as a robustness check. The sample is limited to 20 Western publics and CSES election studies from 1996–2017.
- **Subgroup models**: The full model is re-estimated with dyad means built only from women partisans (col. 3, N = 1,836) and only from men partisans (col. 4, N = 1,833).
- **Robustness (appendix)**:
  - Out-party family controls (S3)
  - Early vs. late periods (S4)
  - In-party share of women MPs (S5)
  - Electoral-system proportionality (S6)
  - Nonlinearity above parity (S7)
  - Out-parties led by women vs. men (S8)
  - Country instead of country-year fixed effects, and other fixed-effects specifications (S9–S10)
  - A stacked individual-level dataset with individual fixed effects (S11)
  - Evaluation uses coefficient signs and significance from two-tailed tests (p < 0.05, p < 0.01), plus adjusted R².
