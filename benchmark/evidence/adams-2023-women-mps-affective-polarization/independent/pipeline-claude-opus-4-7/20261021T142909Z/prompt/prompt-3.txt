- **Estimation method**: Ordinary least squares (OLS) regression on a dataset of 1,842 directed party dyads across 20 Western democracies, 81 election-years (1996–2017), 125 parties, using CSES survey data merged with an original dataset on party-level women's parliamentary representation.

- **Estimating equation**: Dependent variable is [party i's supporters' mean 0–10 thermometer evaluation of out-party j in year t]. Key regressor is [out-party j's proportion of women MPs (t–1)], scaled 0–1 (lagged one year). Functional form is linear (levels on levels, not log-log). Coefficients are **unstandardised** — the headline +1.73 is interpreted as the thermometer change from moving j's share of women MPs from 0 to 1; a one-SD change (0.13 → 0.45) yields +0.55 thermometer units.

- **Controls (preferred specification, Table 1 col. 2)**: [elite right–left distance i,j (t)] (absolute CMP left–right distance, standardised); [i,j are coalition partners (t)] dummy; [i,j are opposition partners (t)] dummy (baseline = one governing + one opposition party).

- **Fixed effects**: Country-year fixed effects (identifying off within-country, within-election variation across out-parties). Robustness: country-only FE, and individual FE in a stacked individual-level dataset.

- **Standard errors**: Robust standard errors clustered at the **election** level.

- **Sample restrictions**: Dyads limited to parties with **at least 4 MPs each** in the year before the election survey (small parties excluded due to few partisan respondents and limited affective relevance); each pair enters twice (i→j and j→i).

- **Subgroup/robustness analyses**: Separate models for women vs. men partisans (cols. 3–4); controls for party family; electoral-system proportionality; early vs. late period split; in-party women MP share; nonlinearity past gender parity; split by out-party leader gender; inclusion of smaller parties; stacked individual-level data with individual FE.

- **Evaluation**: Statistical significance via two-tailed tests (p < 0.05, p < 0.01); model fit reported via adjusted R² (0.31 for the full model). No train/test split or cross-validation — this is an observational regression study, not predictive modeling.
