Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimation method:** Within-clinic randomized field experiment in 60 public community health clinics (CSComs) in Bamako, Kati and Kalaban Coro, Mali (Nov–Dec 2016), with 2,055 acutely ill patients. Each clinic was observed for 6 days over a two-week window. It had 2 Control days, 2 Doctor Voucher (DV) days, where providers got vouchers for free ACT (simple malaria) treatment and handed them out at their discretion, and 2 Patient Voucher (PV) days, where all acutely ill patients got a voucher at intake. Clinics were split into 3 geographic cohorts of 20, and within each cohort every clinic was randomly assigned to one of 20 intervention schedules (Figure 2). Days were unannounced. Clinics were reimbursed at retail price for redeemed vouchers, so providers' financial incentives stayed the same across arms. A cross-cut "Patient Information" video treatment and a clinic-level RDT-trust training (half of clinics) are analysed elsewhere; their interactions with PV/DV appear in Appendix Tables B3–B4.
- **Estimating equation (used throughout, OLS / linear probability model):** y_ict = β0 + βP·PV_ct + βD·DV_ct + γ_t + ε_ict. Here y_ict is the outcome for patient i at clinic c on day t. PV_ct and DV_ct are indicators for the two voucher arms, with Control days as the omitted category. γ_t are date fixed effects, included because the shares of PV/DV/Control days vary by date. No other controls appear in the baseline specification. Coefficients are in raw outcome units (percentage-point effects for binary outcomes), not standardised. Each table row is a separate regression.
- **Standard errors and tests:** Standard errors are clustered at the clinic level (the level at which rotation schedules were assigned; 60 clusters). Tables report an F-test p-value for H0: βP = βD (the key PV vs DV comparison) and a joint F-test p-value for βP = βD = 0.
- **Outcomes / test logic:** Outcomes come from the in-clinic survey and administrative data. They are: voucher redemption (administrative voucher data), whether any antimalarial was prescribed and purchased, simple (ACT) and severe malaria treatment, and treatment–illness match. Severe treatment is defined as artemisinin monotherapy or quinine given as an injectable, or recorded as tablets together with an ACT. The model's predictions map to tests as follows: (1) gatekeeping costs if voucher use is higher in PV than DV; (2) patient-driven demand if any-antimalarial prescription/purchase is higher in PV than DV; (3) doctor-driven demand if severe treatment is higher in DV than PV; (4) heterogeneity by predicted malaria risk (low-risk marginal patients point to patient-driven demand).
- **Malaria status / match:** A random subsample of 1,735 patients was selected for a home follow-up survey the next day (1,495 interviewed). Of these, 1,126 consented to a home RDT (CareStart HRP2 P. falciparum) and got a valid result. The match outcome is "appropriate" treatment: a malaria-positive patient purchased treatment, or a malaria-negative patient did not. It is estimated on the subsample with a valid home RDT. The home-survey sampling rule was up to 6 patients per clinic-day, with oversampling at busier clinics from 18 Nov until 60 patients per study day were sampled.
- **Predicted malaria risk (preprocessing):** Following Cohen, Dupas and Schaner (2015), the home RDT result is regressed on patient characteristics observed in the clinic survey (demographics, symptoms, illness duration, etc.). The fitted model gives every patient a predicted malaria probability (π), which is used to split patients by risk for the heterogeneity analysis (e.g. patients with the fewest malaria symptoms or lowest risk).
- **Sample definition:** Clinics that had closed, were more than 15 km from Bamako, or worked with an NGO offering subsidised care to mothers and children (21 clinics) were dropped, leaving 60 clinics. Patients are "acutely ill" consenting patients presenting with listed symptoms: fever, chills, sweating, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, poor appetite, headache, cough, weakness, fatigue or reduced consciousness. The Control group has 628 patient observations. Randomisation balance is checked with the same specification on baseline characteristics (Table 2). Selection into the home survey and RDT is checked in Appendix Tables B5–B6.
- **No ML train/test split or cross-validation:** Identification comes from the randomized within-clinic assignment of voucher conditions across days.
