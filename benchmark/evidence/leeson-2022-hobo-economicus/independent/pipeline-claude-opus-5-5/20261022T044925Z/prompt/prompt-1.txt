Methodology Summary: - **Design and data:** This is an observational field study with no train/test split or cross-validation. It has two components:
  - **Station-choice regressions:** 242 station-visits to 25 Metrorail stations plus the Georgetown intersection (Wisconsin Ave & M St), Oct 2016–Nov 2017, about 9 visits per station. Each visit counted panhandlers within a one-square-block area around the exits, giving 258 panhandler observations (218 unique). Street people were identified by appearance.
  - **Rate-of-return tests:** a 5-station downtown cluster (Farragut North, Farragut West, Gallery Pl-Chinatown, McPherson Sq, Metro Center), each visited 4 times simultaneously, 7:30–11:30 a.m., Feb–Apr 2019.

- **Regression variables:**
  - **Key regressor (opportunities):** WMATA monthly exits per station in the month of visit. Georgetown is assigned Foggy Bottom-GWU's exits. Summary values (mean 349, max 690.5) against a stated busiest-station figure of ~600,000 exits per month suggest the variable is in thousands of exits per month.
  - **Passerby friendliness:** from 93 visits and 701 passersby asked for directions to a local landmark over three train arrivals. Responses were coded on a 1–5 scale (ignored through shared a map); the variable is the station average of each passerby's friendliest response.
  - **Proximity dummies:** homeless service within a 10-minute walk; homeless shuttle stop within a 10-minute walk (from Google Maps and DC Data Catalog).

- **Estimating equation (Table 2):** OLS of the number of panhandlers per station-visit (levels, not logs) on number of passersby, passerby friendliness, homeless-shuttle-stop dummy and homeless-service dummy, with date fixed effects.
  - Standard errors are robust and clustered by Metro station.
  - Column (1) is the full sample (N=242, adj. R² 0.39); column (2) is the 5-station subsample (N=102, adj. R² 0.06).
  - Headline coefficients are unstandardised: passersby 0.005 (SE 0.001) and 0.006 (SE 0.002). The text adds standardised interpretations: a 1 SD increase in passersby is associated with a 0.53 SD (full sample) and 0.40 SD (subsample) increase in panhandlers.

- **Receipts measurement:**
  - Panhandlers were selected for practicality of discreet observation and watched covertly until they left. Observers recorded donations and minutes worked (67.6 hours in total, average 51 minutes per panhandler).
  - Panhandlers who received donations were offered $5 to count their take in front of observers; 82% accepted. The $5 is excluded from receipts, and in-kind gifts were monetised at market prices.
  - Outcomes are donations per hour (N=80 panhandlers, 76 unique) and dollars per hour (N=67, since some refused the count).

- **Tests of rate-of-return equalisation across the 5 stations (Tables 3–5):** each test is run separately for donations per hour (panel A) and dollars per hour (panel B), with equality not rejected if p-values are large.
  - **Variances (Table 3):** Brown-Forsythe test, chosen for skewed distributions; Levene's test is used as a robustness check. W = 0.541 (p=0.706) and 0.276 (p=0.893).
  - **Means (Table 4):** one-way ANOVA. F = 0.40 (p=0.809) and 0.15 (p=0.962).
  - **Medians (Table 5):** Kruskal-Wallis test, adjusted for ties. χ² = 2.506 (p=0.644) and 3.170 (p=0.530).

- **Robustness and power:**
  - Tables 3–5 are re-run with observed working minutes bounded below at 10 and above at mean + 3 SD (191.55 min) or mean + 2 SD (144.6 min), each bound applied separately or together. Results are qualitatively unchanged.
  - Power calculations for the ANOVA at α = 5% vary the assumed between/within variance ratio (1×, 0.74×, 1.35× the sample estimate). Power is 0.997, 0.978 and 1 for donations per hour, and 0.684, 0.535 and 0.826 for dollars per hour.
  - Collusion and passerby-diversion explanations are addressed with qualitative arguments.
