Methodology Summary: - **Data and samples**: There are two datasets. (i) Number of panhandlers: 242 station-visits to 26 "stations" (25 Metrorail stations plus the Wisconsin Ave/M St intersection in Georgetown) across 10 months in 2016–2017. Each visit counted the panhandlers within a one-square-block area around the station exits. The five-station subsample (Farragut North, Farragut West, Gallery Place-Chinatown, McPherson Square, Metro Center) has 102 station-visits. (ii) Panhandling receipts: 80 panhandler observations (76 unique) at the five subsample stations. Each station was visited 4 times, on the same dates, 7:30–11:30 a.m., in Feb–Apr 2019, for 67.6 hours of observed work in total.
- **Variable construction**: The dependent variable in Table 2 is the number of panhandlers at each station-visit. Regressors are:
  - Number of passersby: WMATA monthly exits at the station in the month of the visit, in thousands per month (mean 348.95 in the full sample). Georgetown is assigned the Foggy Bottom-GWU exits.
  - Passerby friendliness: the station average of each passerby's friendliest response on a 1–5 scale, from 701 passersby asked for directions over 93 visits.
  - Homeless shuttle stop: a dummy equal to 1 if the station is within a 10-minute walk of a stop.
  - Homeless service: a dummy equal to 1 if the station is within a 10-minute walk of a service.
  
  The receipts outcomes are donations per hour (N=80) and dollars per hour (N=67, because only 82% of approached panhandlers accepted the $5 offer to count their money). Each is computed as receipts divided by the minutes observed, times 60. In-kind donations are valued at market prices, and the $5 payment is excluded.
- **Specification (Table 2)**: The model is OLS in levels (linear, not logged): number of panhandlers_{s,t} = b1·passersby + b2·friendliness + b3·shuttle stop + b4·homeless service + date fixed effects + error. Column (1) uses the full sample (N=242, adj. R² 0.39) and column (2) the five-station subsample (N=102, adj. R² 0.06). Headline coefficients are unstandardised: passersby 0.005 (SE 0.001) and 0.006 (SE 0.002); shuttle stop 0.781 and 0.890. Standardised effects are reported only in the text: a 1 SD increase in passersby is associated with a 0.53 SD (full sample) and 0.40 SD (subsample) increase in panhandlers.
- **Standard errors**: Robust standard errors, clustered by Metro station (26 clusters in the full sample, 5 in the subsample).
- **Rate-of-return equality tests (Tables 3–5)**: These compare hourly receipts across the 5 stations. Panel A uses donations per hour and panel B uses dollars per hour.
  - Table 3: Brown-Forsythe test of equal variances (median-centred Levene). W = 0.541 (p = 0.706) in panel A and 0.276 (p = 0.893) in panel B. Results are robust to the mean-based Levene test.
  - Table 4: One-way ANOVA test of equal means. F = 0.40 (p = 0.809) with df 4/75, and F = 0.15 (p = 0.962) with df 4/62.
  - Table 5: Kruskal-Wallis test of equal medians, chi-squared adjusted for ties. 2.506 (p = 0.644) and 3.170 (p = 0.530).
- **Robustness**: Tables 3–5 are re-run with minutes observed bounded below at 10. The upper bound is set at the mean + 3 SD (191.55 min) in one version and at the mean + 2 SD (144.6 min) in another. Lower-only and upper-only bounds are also tried. Results are qualitatively unchanged.
- **Power analysis**: Power of the ANOVA at the 5% level, using the actual sample sizes, under three cases: between/within variances equal to the sample estimates; within variance +15% and between −15% (ratio ×0.74); and within −15% and between +15% (ratio ×1.35). Power is 0.997/0.978/1 for donations per hour and 0.684/0.535/0.826 for dollars per hour.
- **No ML elements**: There is no train/test split, no cross-validation and no hyperparameter tuning. Inference rests on clustered-SE significance tests (Table 2) and the p-values of the equality tests (Tables 3–5).
