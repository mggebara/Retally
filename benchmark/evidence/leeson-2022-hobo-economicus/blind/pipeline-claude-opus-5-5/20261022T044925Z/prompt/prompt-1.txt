Methodology Summary: - **Data collection, panhandler counts (full sample):** Over ten months in 2016–2017, the authors made 242 visits to 25 Metrorail stations plus the Wisconsin Ave./M St. intersection in Georgetown (26 "stations"), about 9 visits per station.
  - On each visit they counted panhandlers in a one-square-block area around the station exits. Anyone identified by appearance as a street person soliciting passersby was counted.
  - The unit of observation is the station-visit (N = 242).

- **Regressors and controls:**
  - *Number of passersby* is WMATA monthly exit counts for the station in the month of the visit. The scale appears to be thousands of exits. Georgetown is assigned Foggy Bottom-GWU's exits.
  - *Homeless service* and *Homeless shuttle stop* are dummies equal to 1 if the station is within a 10-minute walk, per Google Maps and DC Human Services data.
  - *Passerby friendliness* is the station average of passersby's friendliest response to a request for directions to a landmark. Responses were coded on a 1–5 ordinal scale. The data come from 93 visits, covering exiting adults across three train arrivals (15 minutes in Georgetown), for 701 passersby in total.

- **Regression specification (Table 2):**
  - Estimated by OLS.
  - The dependent variable is the number of panhandlers at a station-visit, in levels (not logs).
  - The key regressor is the number of passersby. Controls are passerby friendliness, homeless shuttle stop and homeless service.
  - All models include date fixed effects.
  - Standard errors are robust and clustered by Metro station.
  - The headline coefficients are unstandardised. Standardised magnitudes are mentioned only in the text.
  - Column (1) uses the full sample (242 obs.); column (2) uses the five-station subsample (102 station-visits).

- **Receipts subsample design:** Five neighbouring downtown stations were chosen so that information and travel costs are low: Farragut North, Farragut West, Gallery Place-Chinatown, McPherson Square and Metro Center. Each is within a 24-minute walk of the others and within 14 minutes of a homeless shuttle stop.
  - Each station was visited simultaneously four times, on the same dates, in February–April 2019, between 7:30 and 11:30 a.m.
  - Panhandlers were observed covertly, and the number of donations and minutes worked were recorded.
  - Those who received donations were offered $5 to count their take in front of the observers (82% accepted). The $5 is excluded from receipts.
  - In-kind donations were monetised at market prices.
  - This yielded 80 panhandler observations (76 unique) and 67.6 hours of observation.

- **Outcome measures (rate of return):** Donations per hour (N = 80) and dollars per hour (N = 67, those with counted money). The hourly measure accounts for time spent working. Legal costs are treated as zero, and travel costs as approximately equal across stations.

- **Equalisation tests (Tables 3–5):** Each test is applied separately to donations/hour (panel A) and dollars/hour (panel B).
  - Equality of variances across the 5 stations uses the Brown-Forsythe test, chosen because the distributions are skewed. Levene's test is used as a robustness check.
  - Equality of means uses one-way ANOVA (F-test).
  - Equality of medians uses the Kruskal-Wallis test, adjusted for ties.

- **Robustness checks:**
  - Observed minutes were bounded below at 10 and above at the mean plus 3 SD (191.55 minutes). A second version used mean plus 2 SD (144.6 minutes). Each bound was also applied on its own.
  - Power calculations for the ANOVA used a 5% significance level, the actual sample sizes and three variance assumptions:
    - within and between variances at their sample values;
    - within variance 15% larger and between variance 15% smaller;
    - within variance 15% smaller and between variance 15% larger.
  - Collusion and passerby-diversion explanations are discussed qualitatively.
