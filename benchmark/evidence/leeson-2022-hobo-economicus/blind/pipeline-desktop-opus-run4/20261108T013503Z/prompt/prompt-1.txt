Methodology Summary: - **Data and samples.** The paper uses two original field datasets from Washington, DC Metrorail. (i) Panhandler counts: 242 station-visits to 25 Metro stations plus the Wisconsin Ave/M St intersection in Georgetown, treated as a 26th "station". The visits ran over ten months in 2016–2017. Panhandlers were counted within a one-square-block area around the station exit(s), giving 258 panhandler observations (218 unique). The five-station subsample (Farragut North, Farragut West, Gallery Place-Chinatown, McPherson Square, Metro Center) has 102 station-visits. (ii) Panhandling receipts: in February–April 2019 the same five stations were each visited 4 times, on the same dates and simultaneously, between 7:30 and 11:30 a.m. This gives 80 panhandler observations (76 unique) and 67.6 hours of observed work. Dollar data exist for only 67 of the 80 because some panhandlers received nothing or declined to count, and 82% of those approached accepted.
- **Variable construction (Table 2).** The dependent variable is the number of panhandlers at each station-visit. The key regressor is "number of passersby": WMATA counts of people exiting the station in the month of the visit. Georgetown is assigned Foggy Bottom-GWU's exits. The controls are:
  - "Passerby friendliness": the station average of each passerby's friendliest response on a 1–5 scale (ignored, acknowledged but kept walking, stopped, gave directions, shared a map). It comes from requests for directions put to 701 passersby over 93 visits, three train arrivals per visit (15 minutes in Georgetown).
  - Homeless service: an indicator for being within a 10-minute walk of a shelter or soup kitchen (Google Maps).
  - Homeless shuttle stop: an indicator for being within a 10-minute walk of a UPO shuttle stop (Google Maps and DC Data Catalog).
- **Estimating equation (Table 2).** This is a linear OLS regression in levels, with no logs and no standardisation of the headline coefficients:
  - Number of panhandlers_{s,t} = β·Passersby_{s,month} + γ1·Friendliness + γ2·Shuttle stop + γ3·Homeless service + date fixed effects + ε.
  - Column (1) uses the full sample (N = 242 station-visits). Column (2) uses the five-station subsample (N = 102).
  - Robust standard errors are clustered by Metro station.
  - Adjusted R² is reported. The text separately interprets the passersby effect in standard-deviation units (a one-SD change in passersby against SDs of panhandlers), but the table coefficients are unstandardised.
- **Outcome measures for rate-of-return equalisation (Tables 3–5).** There are two measures for each panhandler, both per hour of observed work. The first is donations per hour: the number of donations divided by the minutes observed, scaled to an hour. The second is dollars per hour: the amount the panhandler counted in front of the researchers after leaving the station. In-kind donations are valued at market prices. The researchers' $5 payment for counting is excluded.
- **Tests of equality across the 5 stations.** Each test is run separately for donations per hour (panel A, N = 80) and dollars per hour (panel B, N = 67):
  - Table 3: the Brown-Forsythe test of equal variances, which uses deviations from the median. Levene's test, which uses deviations from the mean, is the robustness check.
  - Table 4: a one-way ANOVA F-test of equal means. Sums of squares, degrees of freedom and mean squares are reported, with station as the grouping factor.
  - Table 5: the Kruskal-Wallis rank test of equal medians, with a chi-squared statistic adjusted for ties. Station rank sums are reported.
  - p-values are reported for every test.
- **Robustness of the observation-time bounds (footnote 17).** Tables 3–5 are re-run with minutes observed bounded below at 10 minutes. The upper bound is set either at the mean plus 3 SD (191.55 minutes) or at the mean plus 2 SD (144.6 minutes). The analyses are also re-run with only the lower bound or only the upper bound.
- **Power analysis.** The power of the ANOVA equal-means test at the 5% level is computed with the actual group sample sizes for both receipt measures. It is computed under three assumptions about the within- and between-station variances: (1) both equal the sample estimates; (2) the within variance is 15% higher and the between variance 15% lower, so the between/within ratio is 0.74 times the sample ratio; (3) the within variance is 15% lower and the between variance 15% higher, so the ratio is 1.35 times the sample ratio.
- **No machine-learning set-up.** There is no train/test split, no cross-validation and no hyperparameters. Inference rests on clustered-SE significance tests in the regressions and on classical p-values in the equality tests. Figure 1 shows each station's receipt distribution on a cumulative probability scale, with means, 95% confidence intervals and medians.
