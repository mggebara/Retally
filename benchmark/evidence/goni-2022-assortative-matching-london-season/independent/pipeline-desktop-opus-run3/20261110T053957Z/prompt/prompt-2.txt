## Paper
Title: American Economic Journal: Applied Economics 2022, 14(3): 445–487
Methodology: - **Design / identification:** Quasi-experiment based on the interruption of the London Season (1861–1863; royal balls and court presentations cancelled during Queen Victoria's mourning). Because the decision to marry in 1861–1863 is endogenous, treatment is assigned by age: cohorts with different age-based risks of marrying during the interruption are compared. Data: Hollingsworth (2001) peerage genealogy, linked to Bateman (1883) family landholdings (acres) and Burke (1826) geo-referenced family seats.
- **Treatment variable:** Synthetic probability (in percent) of marrying during the interruption, eq. (1): T_t = p(t) + p(t+1) + p(t+2), where t is the woman's age in 1861 and p(t) is the percentage of women marrying at age t in "normal times". p(t) comes from a benchmark cohort of peers' daughters born 1815–1830, excluding those who married after 1861 or died before age 30. T peaks above 20% for women aged 19–22 in 1861. Its standard deviation is 7.3 pp. Robustness checks use alternative benchmark cohorts (Section IIIE).
- **Estimating equations:** Binary outcomes use a probit, eq. (2): Pr(y_it = 1 | X) = Φ(α + βT_t + X'δ). Outcomes are married a commoner (Table 2 col. 1), married an heir (col. 2), married down (col. 5) and never married (col. 6). Continuous outcomes use OLS, eq. (3): Y_it = A + BT_t + X'Δ + ε. Y is the difference between spouses' family-landholding rank percentiles in acres, either in absolute value (col. 3) or as husband − wife (col. 4). Coefficients are in levels: probit coefficients per 1 pp of T, and OLS coefficients in percentile ranks per 1 pp of T. Nothing is standardised. Headline Panel A estimates: 0.005 (0.002) for married a commoner; −0.004 (0.002) for married an heir; 0.524 (0.196) for the absolute difference; −0.516 (0.225) for husband − wife; 0.009 (0.003) for married down; 0.002 (0.002) for never married.
- **Controls:** Indicator for duke's/marquess's/earl's daughter (family title), indicator for English peerage (peerage of origin), and the woman's birth order excluding heirs. Panel B also adds the distance from the family seat to London, which restricts the sample to women with a recorded seat. There are no fixed effects.
- **Samples:** The baseline is peers' daughters aged 15–35 in 1861 who ever married (N = 644). It excludes second marriages, women married to foreigners and members of the royal family. The landholdings sample (cols. 3–5) keeps couples where Bateman lists both spouses' families (N = 324). Most of the lost observations are women who married landless commoners; the rest are families with fewer than 2,000 acres. The celibacy sample (col. 6) is N = 765 and includes never-married women but drops those who died before age 35. With the Panel B family-seat restriction the samples are N = 484 (baseline), 260 (landholdings) and 565 (celibacy).
- **Inference:** Standard errors are clustered by birth year (G = 21 clusters). The paper also reports p-values from the Cameron, Gelbach and Miller (2008) bootstrap-t procedure, to allow for few clusters: a wild cluster bootstrap for the OLS models and a pairs cluster bootstrap for the probits. Probit fit is reported as "percent correct", alongside the mean of the dependent variable.
- **Additional analyses:**
  - An IV model instruments marrying during 1861–1863 with T. It gives effects of +40% on marrying a commoner, −30% on marrying an heir, and husbands 44 percentile ranks poorer.
  - Placebo tests re-run the analysis on 40 pseudo-interruptions x ∈ {10,…,50} years earlier, with the outcome married a commoner (Fig. 6).
  - Nonparametric tests: chi-squared tests of association for sorting by title, and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests for sorting by landholdings.
  - Political-economy extensions use IV, instrumenting marrying a commoner with T. Outcomes are brothers'/family heads' election as MP, covering 27 general elections and 97 by-elections from 1776–1910, and school-board wealth tax rates. The tax rates average over 943 boards within 10 miles of 387 English family seats, for 1872–1878.

probit_me <- function(df, y, ctrls) {
  f <- as.formula(paste(y, "~ T +", paste(ctrls, collapse = " + ")))
  m <- glm(f, data = df, family = binomial(link = "probit"))
  b <- coef(m)
  X <- model.matrix(m)
  xb <- as.vector(X %*% b)
  me <- mean(dnorm(xb)) * b["T"]
  V <- tryCatch(sandwich::vcovCL(m, cluster = df$byear_cl[as.integer(rownames(X))], type = "HC0"),
                error = function(e) sandwich::vcovCL(m, cluster = ~byear_cl))
  # delta-method gradient of AME wrt b
  phi <- dnorm(xb)
  g <- colMeans(phi * (-xb) * X) * b["T"]
  g["T"] <- g["T"] + mean(phi)
  se <- sqrt(as.numeric(t(g) %*% V %*% g))
  pc <- mean((fitted(m) > 0.5) == (m$y == 1))
  list(me = unname(me), se = se, n = nobs(m), coef = unname(b["T"]), pc = pc)
}
