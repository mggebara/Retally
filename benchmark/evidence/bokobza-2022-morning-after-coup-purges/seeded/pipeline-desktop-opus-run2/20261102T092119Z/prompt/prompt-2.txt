## Paper
Title: General Rights
Methodology: - **Data and sample restrictions:** The panel combines the WhoGov cabinet dataset (Nyrup and Bramwell 2020; CIA "Chiefs of State and Cabinet Members" directory, coded each July, 177 countries, 1966–2016) with failed-coup data from Powell and Thyne (2011). The sample is limited to autocratic country-years, defined as a **lagged (t−1) Boix–Miller–Rosato democracy score = 0**. This gives 115 autocracies, 1967–2016, with up to **3,715 country-years** and 95 failed coup attempts in the country-level models. The individual-level models use **23,655 ministers / 95,751 minister-years**, and Figure 3 reports up to 90,020. Some models lose observations because control variables have missing coverage. Ministers moved to another post within the cabinet are not counted as purged. In the experience and H4 analyses, "junior ministers or other low-ranking post" are excluded.
- **Coup timing preprocessing:** The cabinet data are July snapshots, so a failed coup counts as 1 in year t only if it happened **before 1 July**. A failed coup on or after 1 July is assigned to year t+1. The paper says the results also hold with a conventional lagged independent variable.
- **Country-level specification (H1):** The model is two-way fixed-effects TSCS OLS: P_it = φ·C_it + β·X_it + α_i + λ_t + ε_it. The dependent variable P_it is the **cabinet replacement rate**, the year-to-year share of ministers purged (0–1), computed as 1 − retention_rate_adj_minister, which adjusts for cabinet size. The key regressor C_it is a failed-coup-attempt dummy. The model is linear-linear and the coefficient is in raw units: the change in the replacement rate in percentage points, not standardised. The headline estimate in the Base model with country FE is about +0.10, and the range across specifications is 0.08–0.11.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** All models include **country fixed effects plus a full set of year dummies**. Each model is also re-estimated with **leader fixed effects in place of country FE**. All controls are lagged one year:
  - **Base:** log GDP per capita and log population (PWT 9.0).
  - **Main (preferred):** Base plus military, party and monarchy dictatorship dummies (Anckar and Fredriksson 2019).
  - **Electoral controls:** Main plus NELDA national elections and years since the last election.
  - **Economic controls:** Main plus GDP per capita growth and Ross oil income.
  - **Instability controls:** Main plus UCDP/PRIO civil war onset, NAVCO nonviolent campaigns, COW military dispute and interstate war onsets, and CNTS strikes.
  - **Purges MAs:** Main plus 1-, 2- and 3-year moving averages of past replacement rates.
  - **All controls:** every control above.
- **Standard errors and inference:** Standard errors are **clustered by country**. Significance is judged with 95% confidence intervals (Figure 2; full tables in Appendix A).
- **Placebo tests:** The failed-coup dummy is replaced, one at a time, with civil war onset, nonviolent campaign, military dispute onset, interstate war onset and strikes. Each is estimated with both country FE and leader FE.
- **Individual-level specification (H2–H4):** These are linear probability models: F_ijt = φC + ζM + γ(C×M) + βX + α_i + λ_t + ε. F is 1 if the minister is purged, meaning absent from the cabinet the next year, and 0 if retained.
  - **Controls:** the Base controls, country and year FE, and a **cubic polynomial in the minister's years in office (t, t², t³)** (Carter and Signorino). The polynomial is dropped when experience is the moderator.
  - **Moderators M (one interaction at a time):**
    - experience: years in cabinet at the time of the coup;
    - party alignment: leader's party, other party, or unaffiliated;
    - strategic portfolio type, e.g. defence, security, foreign affairs;
    - a 5-tier importance ranking (Appendix Q).
  - **H4:** ministers are split into four loyalty × responsibility groups:
    - strong loyalty means same party as the leader and experience at or above the median for that leader-year;
    - high responsibility means top minister, deputy PM, VP, PM, or a President who is not the leader.
  - **Reporting:** the paper reports marginal effects of a failed coup for each minister type (Figures 3–4; Appendix K).
- **Robustness:**
  - Bjørnskov–Rode coup data and coup types (military, civilian, royal).
  - Alternative autocracy measures: DD/Cheibub et al. and Polity IV < 6.
  - Effects by month of the coup.
  - Regime-type interactions.
  - A personalism control.
  - Dropping full cabinet turnovers (replacement rate = 1).
  - Dropping a leader's first year.
  - Core-member and unadjusted replacement-rate variants.
  - Restricting to regimes with parties in government.

  x <- as.character(x)
  present <- unique(x[!is.na(x)])
  lv <- lv[lv %in% present]
  factor(x, levels = c(lv, sort(setdiff(present, lv))))
}

}
