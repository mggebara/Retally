Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample**: Failed coups come from Powell and Thyne (2011): illegal, overt attempts that did not "seize and hold power for at least seven days". There are 95 failed coup attempts in the analyses. Cabinet data come from WhoGov (Nyrup and Bramwell 2020), built from the CIA *Chiefs of State and Cabinet Members* directory and coded for July of each year. The sample is 115 autocracies, 1967–2016. Autocracy means a country-year with a **lagged (t−1) Boix–Miller–Rosato democracy score of 0**. Lagging the score keeps out coups in democracies that turned the regime autocratic in the same year. Robustness checks use lagged DD (Cheibub et al./Bjørnskov–Rode) and lagged Polity IV < 6 instead.
- **Coup timing recode**: Cabinets are recorded each July, so a failed coup before 1 July is coded 1 in that year. A failed coup on or after 1 July is coded 1 in the following year (e.g. May 2004 → 2004, Sept 2004 → 2005). A conventional lagged-IV version is a robustness check.
- **Country-level specification (H1)**: Time-series cross-sectional OLS, P_it = φ·FailedCoup_it + β·X_it + α_i + λ_t + ε_it.
  - The DV is the year-to-year cabinet replacement rate (0–1), defined as 1 − retention_rate_adj_minister, adjusted for cabinet size. The rate is in raw shares, not standardised, so φ is a percentage-point change.
  - The key regressor is a failed-coup dummy.
  - Every model has country fixed effects plus a full set of year dummies. Each model is also re-run with leader fixed effects in place of country fixed effects.
  - Up to 3,715 country-years. **Standard errors are clustered by country.**
- **Control sets (all lagged one year)**:
  - "Base": log GDP per capita and log population (Penn World Table 9.0).
  - "Main" (preferred): Base plus military-dictatorship, party-dictatorship and monarchy dummies (Anckar and Fredriksson 2019).
  - Extensions added to Main:
    - Electoral: NELDA national elections, years since last election.
    - Economic: GDP per capita growth, Ross oil income.
    - Instability: UCDP/PRIO civil war onset, NAVCO nonviolent campaigns, COW military dispute onset and interstate war onset, CNTS strikes.
    - "Purges MAs": 1-, 2- and 3-year moving averages of past replacement rates.
    - "All controls": all of the above together.
- **Placebo tests**: The failed-coup dummy is replaced in turn by civil war onset, nonviolent campaign, military dispute onset, interstate war onset and strikes. Each is run with country fixed effects and with leader fixed effects.
- **Individual-level specification (H2–H4)**: A linear probability model at the minister-year level, F_ijt = φC + ζM + γ(C×M) + βX + α_i + λ_t + ε.
  - The DV is 1 if the minister is purged, meaning absent from the dataset the next year. Moving to another post within the cabinet does not count as a purge.
  - Controls are the Base controls plus a cubic polynomial (t, t², t³) in the minister's years in office. The polynomial is dropped when experience is the moderator.
  - Country and year fixed effects.
  - Sample: 23,655 ministers and 95,751 minister-years (up to about 90,020 in the plotted models).
- **Moderators (M), each interacted with failed coup one at a time**:
  - Experience: years in cabinet. Junior and low-ranking posts are excluded here.
  - Partisan alignment: leader's party / other party / no party.
  - Strategic portfolio: e.g. defence, security/interior, foreign affairs.
  - Five-tier importance ranking, excluding the leader (Appendix Q).
  - For H4, four loyalty × responsibility groups:
    - "Strongly loyal" means same party as the leader and experience at or above the median for ministers under that leader in that year.
    - "Highly responsible" means top minister, deputy PM, VP, PM, or a president who is not the leader.
- **Evaluation and robustness**: Results are reported as coefficients or marginal effects of a failed coup for each minister type, with 95% CIs. Robustness checks:
  - Bjørnskov–Rode coup data, including military, civilian and royal coup types.
  - Interactions with regime type.
  - A personalism control.
  - Dropping replacement rates of 1.
  - Dropping a leader's first year.
  - Effects by month of the coup.
  - Alternative replacement-rate measures: core or all members, unadjusted.
