Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample:** Cabinet data come from WhoGov (Nyrup & Bramwell 2020), built from the CIA's Chiefs of State and Cabinet Members directory and coded for July of each year. Coup data come from Powell & Thyne (2011). A failed coup is one whose plotters did not seize and hold power for at least 7 days. The sample covers 115 autocracies from 1967 to 2016. It is restricted to country-years with a lagged (t−1) Boix–Miller–Rosato democracy score of 0. Analyses lose observations wherever covariates are missing. The country-level models use up to 3,715 country-years and 95 failed coups. The individual-level models use 23,655 ministers and 95,751 minister-years.
- **Country-level estimating equation (Eq. 1):** Time-series cross-sectional OLS, P_it = φC_it + βX_it + α_i + λ_t + ε_it.
  - The dependent variable P_it is the year-to-year cabinet replacement rate, scaled 0–1. It equals 1 − retention_rate_adj_minister, which adjusts for changes in cabinet size. The rate is in raw units, so coefficients are percentage-point changes; nothing is standardised.
  - The key regressor C_it is a failed-coup dummy. To line it up with the July cabinet coding, a failed coup before 1 July counts in year t, and one on or after 1 July counts in year t+1.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Every model has country fixed effects plus a full set of year dummies. Each model is also re-estimated with leader fixed effects in place of country fixed effects. All controls are lagged one year.
  - **Base:** log GDP per capita and log population, both from Penn World Table 9.0.
  - **Main (preferred):** Base plus dummies for military dictatorship, party dictatorship and monarchy (Anckar & Fredriksson 2019).
  - **Extensions added to Main:**
    - Electoral: NELDA national elections and years since the last election.
    - Economic: GDP per capita growth and oil income (Ross–Mahdavi).
    - Instability: UCDP/PRIO civil war onset, NAVCO nonviolent campaigns, COW military disputes and interstate war onsets, and CNTS strikes.
    - Purges MAs: 1-, 2- and 3-year moving averages of past replacement rates.
    - All controls: every control above together.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered by country in all models.
- **Placebo and robustness checks:**
  - Placebos swap the failed-coup dummy for civil war onset, nonviolent campaigns, military dispute onset, interstate war onset and strikes.
  - Alternative coup data and coup types come from Bjørnskov & Rode (2020).
  - Alternative autocracy definitions are lagged DD (Cheibub et al. / Bjørnskov–Rode) and lagged Polity IV below 6.
  - Other checks use a conventional lagged independent variable, drop cabinets with full turnover (replacement rate = 1), drop each leader's first year, add a personalism control, and interact the coup dummy with regime type.
- **Individual-level linear probability model:** F_ijt = φC + ζM + γ(C×M) + βX + α_i + λ_t + ε.
  - The outcome F is 1 if the minister is purged, meaning absent from the dataset the next year. Ministers reshuffled within the cabinet are not counted as purged.
  - Controls are the Base controls plus a cubic polynomial (t, t², t³) in the minister's years in office. The polynomial is dropped when experience is the moderator.
  - Each minister characteristic M is interacted with the failed-coup dummy one at a time. Reported quantities are the marginal effects of a failed coup by minister type.
- **Minister characteristics (M):**
  - Experience: years in cabinet at the time of the coup. Junior and low-ranking posts are excluded from these models.
  - Party: leader's party, another party, or unaffiliated.
  - Strategic portfolio: portfolio type, such as defense or foreign affairs, drawn from 42 portfolio types.
  - Importance: five ordered tiers of title and portfolio prestige, excluding the leader (Appendix Q).
  - H4 groups: four categories crossing high/low responsibility with strong/weak loyalty. Strong loyalty means the leader's party and experience at or above the median for ministers under that leader in that year. High responsibility means top minister, deputy PM, VP, PM, or a president who is not the leader.
  - Robustness: Appendix O restricts the sample to regimes with parties represented in government.
