Methodology Summary: - **Identification and estimating equation:** The paper uses a regression discontinuity (RD) design built on the 500 ha cumulative-landholding expropriation threshold in Decree 153 (1980). The specification (eq. 1) is
  y_po = α + γ·Above500_o + f(holdings_o) + ε_po, for o ∈ RS.
  - y_po is a 2007 outcome for plot p, whose pre-reform owner was o.
  - Above500_o = 1 if owner o's *cumulative* 1980 landholdings were ≥ 500 ha. This differs from the size of the individual plot.
  - f(·) is a polynomial in the former owner's total landholdings, which is the running variable.
  - No fixed effects or additional controls appear in the baseline equation.
  - γ is reported in outcome units. Only the geographic balance estimates (Fig. 4) are explicitly standardized (β).

- **Estimator, bandwidth and fuzzy RD:**
  - The baseline is local linear regression, estimated separately on each side of the cutoff, following Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik (2014).
  - The risk set RS is defined by the MSE-optimal bandwidth of Calonico et al. (2014b, 2017).
  - Compliance was imperfect: about 20% of above-threshold properties stayed as haciendas, and a few below-threshold properties were expropriated. The paper therefore also reports fuzzy RD/IV estimates, using Above500_o as the instrument for "reform cooperative."
  - Appendix J checks robustness to alternative polynomials and bandwidths. Appendix D.2 uses matching methods, and Appendix D.3 bounds results at the edges of the balance-test confidence intervals.

- **Standard errors:** Standard errors are clustered at the former-landholder level, because one owner's multiple plots share the same treatment status.

- **Data construction and matching (preprocessing):**
  - **Running variable:** 1980 property registry data come from two sources. ISTA records give total holdings for expropriated owners. Figueroa Aquino & Marroquín Mena (1991) cover all non-expropriated owners with more than 100 ha.
  - **Expropriation list:** MAG (1983) provides the list of expropriated properties.
  - **Outcome data:** The 2007–08 IV Census of Agriculture covers 94,168 producers. Properties were matched to census records by name, municipality, department and size in hectares.
  - **Match quality:** About 70% of pre-reform holdings were matched. The match probability shows no discontinuity at the threshold (Fig. D3) and is unrelated to geographic characteristics (Appendix C).

- **Worker sample:**
  - Worker data come from household surveys (EHPM, 2002–2013), using self-reported status as a cooperative member or hacienda laborer.
  - The sample is restricted to cantons with only one cooperative or large hacienda, because property names are not available in every survey year.
  - Assignment accuracy was validated with the 2008–2010 surveys, which include property names: 91% of individuals were assigned to the correct property.

- **Validity tests:**
  - **Sorting:** A McCrary (2008) density test bins the running variable and uses bin counts as the dependent variable in eq. (1) (Fig. 3). It finds no discontinuity.
  - **Covariate balance:** Eq. (1) is estimated on geographic covariates using local linear regression with the MSE-optimal bandwidth (Fig. 4). The covariates are land suitability, precipitation, elevation, and suitability for sugarcane, coffee, cotton, maize, beans, rice and sorghum. Discontinuities are small but imprecisely estimated.
  - **First stage (Fig. 5):** Expropriation is regressed on a local quadratic polynomial with a fixed 300 ha bandwidth, a uniform kernel and 25 ha bins, with SEs clustered by former owner. Expropriation probability jumps by about 75 percentage points at 500 ha.

- **Outcomes and evaluation:** The outcomes are tied to the model's predictions (Table 1):
  - share of land devoted to cash crops versus staple crops;
  - yields for cash crops (sugarcane, coffee) and for staple crops (maize, beans);
  - aggregate revenues and profits per hectare;
  - the interquartile range of worker earnings, as the equity measure.

  Section VII examines alternative mechanisms, including credit access and migration. There is no train/test or cross-validation split: inference relies on RD point estimates with clustered 95% confidence intervals.
