Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimator:** Sharp regression discontinuity (RD) design built on the 500 ha cumulative-landholding expropriation threshold in Decree 153 (El Salvador's 1980 land reform, phase I). Estimating equation (1): y_po = α + γ·Above500_o + f(holdings_o) + ε_po for o ∈ RS. The unit is plot/property p owned by pre-reform owner o. Above500_o = 1(cumulative landholdings of owner o ≥ 500 ha). The running variable is the former owner's total 1980 landholdings, not the size of the individual property. γ is the coefficient of interest (reduced-form effect of being above the threshold, i.e. cooperative vs. hacienda). Because compliance is imperfect, the paper also reports fuzzy RD (IV) estimates, instrumenting "reform cooperative_po" with Above500_o.
- **Polynomial, bandwidth, kernel:** The baseline follows Calonico, Cattaneo and Titiunik (2014a,b) and Calonico et al. (2017). It uses a local linear polynomial in landholdings, fitted separately on each side of the cutoff, on a "risk set" (RS) of owners inside the MSE-optimal bandwidth around 500 ha. Appendix J checks robustness to other polynomial orders and bandwidths. The first-stage plot (Fig. 5) uses a different setup: local quadratic polynomials on each side, a fixed 300 ha bandwidth, a uniform kernel, and 25 ha bins for the plotted means.
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the former-landholder (pre-reform owner) level, because one owner can hold several plots and treatment depends on that owner's total holdings.
- **Data and sample construction:** Treatment comes from the MAG (1983) list of expropriated properties plus ISTA records of former owners and their total 1980 holdings. Non-expropriated owners with more than 100 ha in cumulative holdings in 1980 come from Figueroa Aquino & Marroquín Mena (1991). Outcomes come from the 2007–08 IV Census of Agriculture (94,168 producers). Pre-reform properties are matched to census producers by name, municipality, department and size in hectares, and about 70% match. The census cooperative indicator separates cooperatives from haciendas. The paper checks that the match probability does not jump at the threshold (Fig. D3).
- **Outcomes (Table 1 mapping):** (1) Share of land devoted to cash crops (sugarcane, coffee) vs. staple crops (maize, beans). (2) Cash-crop yields. (3) Staple-crop yields. (4) Aggregate productivity, measured as revenues per hectare and profits per hectare. (5) Worker income equality, measured by the interquartile range of worker earnings, from the 2002–2013 household surveys (Encuesta de Hogares de Propósitos Múltiples). Survey workers are matched to cooperatives or haciendas only in cantons with a single cooperative or large hacienda. Matching accuracy was checked against the 2008–2010 surveys, which include property names.
- **Validity checks:** A McCrary (2008) density test, run by collapsing observations into landholding bins and using the bin count as the outcome in equation (1). Covariate balance is tested by estimating equation (1) with the same local linear, MSE-optimal setup on geographic characteristics: land suitability, precipitation, elevation, suitability for sugarcane, coffee and cotton, and suitability for maize, beans, rice and sorghum. These balance estimates are reported as standardized (β) coefficients in Fig. 4 only. Headline outcome estimates are in natural units and are not described as standardized. Appendices D.2 and D.3 add matching-method robustness and a sensitivity analysis using the bounds of the balance-test confidence intervals.
- **First stage:** Expropriation (an indicator that the property was expropriated) is regressed on Above500 to confirm the jump at 500 ha. Compliance is imperfect in both directions, which motivates the fuzzy RD scaling.
- **No ML components:** There is no train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameter tuning beyond the data-driven MSE-optimal RD bandwidth selection. The paper text provided was truncated in Section V, so details of the preferred results-table specification (e.g., any extra controls or fixed effects) are not available beyond equation (1), which contains no controls or fixed effects.
