## Paper Information
Title: UvA-DARE is a service provided by the library of the University of Amsterdam (https://dare.uva.nl)
Abstract (first 500 chars): Yellow vests, pessimistic beliefs, and carbon tax aversion Douenne, T.; Fabre, A. DOI 10.1257/pol.20200092 Publication date 2022
Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample:** An online survey of 3,002 French respondents run by Bilendi in February–March 2019. Quotas made the sample representative on gender, age (5 brackets), education (4), socioprofessional category (8), town size (5) and region (9), with quotas relaxed by 5–10%. Inattentive respondents and the quickest respondents were excluded. Observations are weighted to match population frequencies.
- **Policies and randomized treatments:** The main reform is a carbon tax increase of €50/tCO2 on housing and transport energy, with a lump-sum dividend of €110/year per adult. The survey contains several independent random treatments:
  - environmental information: a 2×2 split between climate-change and particulate-matter blocks;
  - a sectoral tax and dividend, assigned to either housing (+13% gas, +15% heating oil, €50 per adult) or transport (+€0.11/L gasoline, +€0.13/L diesel, €60 per adult);
  - a statement that the tax is effective, shown to half the sample;
  - after the initial questions, ½ get customized win/lose feedback only, ⅓ get progressivity information only, and ⅙ get both;
  - a targeted-dividend variant with eligibility cut-offs at the 20th, 30th, 40th or 50th income percentile (€780 / 1,140 / 1,430 / 1,670 per month; payments of €550 / 360 / 270 / 220 per year). Respondents whose income lies between two cut-offs are randomized between them; above the top 30% the second adult's income decides, and otherwise the variant is drawn at random.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Win/lose/unaffected category, plus the subjective net gain in interval brackets scaled per Eurostat consumption unit (c.u.).
  - Perceived effectiveness, perceived progressivity, and perceived own and aggregate price elasticity (5 brackets).
  - Approval: "Yes", "No" or "PNR" (prefer not to respond). "Approve" means Yes; "accept" means not No.
- **Objective net gains:** γ_h = N_h^a·D − ΔE_h^transport − ΔE_h^housing, using price elasticities of −0.4 (transport) and −0.2 (housing) and 80% consumer incidence.
  - Aggregates and distributions come from a matched BdF 2011 + ENTD 2008 file (over 10,000 households), with expenditures inflated using National Accounts data.
  - Each respondent's ΔE^transport is computed from their reported distance and fuel economy. ΔE^housing is predicted from a regression on household characteristics estimated on the housing survey (EL 2013).
  - The accuracy of the win/lose prediction is validated out of sample on the consumer survey (BdF), which is a different dataset from the one used to estimate it, and the prediction is made symmetric.
- **Bias analysis (Table 2):** The dependent variable is a "large bias" dummy, equal to 1 when the objective net gain minus the subjective net gain exceeds €110/year per c.u.
  - Estimated by a linear probability model (OLS) in col. 1 and by a logit reporting average marginal effects in col. 2. Col. 3 is OLS with initial approval of the tax added as a covariate.
  - Regressors: Yellow Vests attitude (omitted category: opposes), environmentalist, left-right position (omitted: extreme left), plus sociodemographic and political-leaning controls listed in the online Appendix.
  - N = 3,002, with conventional standard errors. Multiple testing uses Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control at 5%, applied to joint F-tests for each categorical variable and for the household-composition and income variable groups.
- **Belief updating (Table 3):** Covers the 1,968 respondents who received feedback. The measure is the share whose post-feedback win/lose belief matches the feedback, split by initial belief, with 95% binomial confidence intervals.
- **Causal effect of feedback on beliefs:** The feedback is a deterministic function of the estimated net gain, so it jumps from "lose" to "win" when estimated γ̂ crosses 0. The paper exploits this in a regression discontinuity design (RDD) around zero.
- **Causal effect of beliefs on support:** The randomized information treatments serve as instruments for binary belief variables (wins/does not lose; tax is effective) in instrumental-variables estimation of approval/acceptance. The random eligibility thresholds of the targeted dividend give a fuzzy RDD for the self-interest belief. The supplied text is cut off before the exact specifications.
