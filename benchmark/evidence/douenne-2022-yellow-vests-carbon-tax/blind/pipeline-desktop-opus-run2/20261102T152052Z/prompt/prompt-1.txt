## Paper Information
Title: UvA-DARE is a service provided by the library of the University of Amsterdam (https://dare.uva.nl)
Abstract (first 500 chars): Yellow vests, pessimistic beliefs, and carbon tax aversion Douenne, T.; Fabre, A. DOI 10.1257/pol.20200092 Publication date 2022
Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample:** An online survey of 3,002 French respondents, run by Bilendi in February–March 2019. Quotas on six sociodemographic variables (gender, age in 5 brackets, education in 4, socioprofessional category in 8, town size in 5, region in 9) were relaxed by 5–10%. Inattentive and very fast respondents were excluded. Observations are weighted to match population frequencies. The main policy studied ("tax and dividend") is a €50/tCO2 increase in the carbon tax on housing and transport energy, with the revenue paid back as €110/year to each adult (at most two adults per household). Gains and losses are always measured per consumption unit (Eurostat definition), and the answer intervals are scaled to each household's number of consumption units.
- **Objective net gains:** Computed as γ_h = N_h^a·D − ΔE_h^transport − ΔE_h^housing. Inputs come from Insee surveys: BdF 2011 matched to ENTD 2008 (over 10,000 households), and EL 2013, all inflated using National Accounts. Assumptions: price elasticities of −0.4 for transport and −0.2 for housing, and consumers bear 80% of the tax incidence. For each respondent, ΔE_transport is calculated directly from their reported distance driven and fuel economy. ΔE_housing comes from a regression on household characteristics fitted in EL 2013 and then predicted for our respondents. The win/lose prediction was validated out of sample on BdF, and the cut-off was made symmetric so it is right in 83.4% of cases for predicted winners and for predicted losers ("five cases out of six").
- **Randomised treatments:** (i) information on climate change and/or particulate matter (2×2 design); (ii) assignment to a housing or transport sectoral tax and dividend (€50/€60 per adult); (iii) the statement that "scientists agree a carbon tax would be effective" (half the sample); (iv) after initial perceptions, customized win/lose feedback (1/2), information on progressivity (1/3), or both (1/6); (v) a tax with a targeted dividend, paid only to adults below the 20th, 30th, 40th or 50th income percentile (€780/1,140/1,430/1,670 per month; €550/360/270/220 per year). Respondents whose income lies between two thresholds are randomised to one of those two, and the top 30% are assigned by the second adult's income or at random.
- **Outcomes:** Win/lose/unaffected category and the size of the subjective gain (in intervals), asked at the initial stage, after information, and for the targeted policy. Also perceived effectiveness, perceived progressivity, and own and aggregate perceived elasticities (expected consumption response to a 30% heating price rise or +€0.50/L fuel). "Approve" means answering "Yes"; "Accept" means not answering "No" (PNR counts as accepting).
- **Bias regressions (Table 2):** Outcome is a dummy for a large bias, |γ̂ − g| > €110/year per c.u. Subjective intervals are converted to numbers using the within-bin averages from Insee data. Specifications are OLS (col. 1), logit with average marginal effects (col. 2), and OLS adding initial approval of the tax (col. 3). Regressors are attitude toward the Yellow Vests (base: opposes), environmentalist, and left-right dummies (base: extreme left), plus sociodemographic and political controls. N = 3,002. False discovery rate is controlled at 5% (Benjamini–Hochberg) using joint F-tests for each categorical variable.
- **Belief updating (Table 3):** Among the 1,968 respondents who got feedback, the share whose updated belief matches the feedback is tabulated by feedback received and by initial belief, with 95% binomial confidence intervals. This is repeated for respondents with |γ̂| > €110 (Table D.4). The causal effect of feedback on beliefs is estimated with a sharp RDD at γ̂ = 0, since feedback switches from "lose" to "win" when estimated net gain crosses zero. Further regressions test for asymmetric updating and for heterogeneity by Yellow Vests attitude, initial approval and education, as evidence on motivated reasoning.
- **Causal effect of beliefs on support (Section IV):** IV/2SLS estimation of acceptance or approval (binary) on binary beliefs, using the random information treatments as instruments. (a) Self-interest (believing one does not lose): instruments are the feedback within the RDD subsample, plus a fuzzy RDD on the targeted dividend, where eligibility (income below the randomly assigned threshold) instruments the belief of not losing. (b) Environmental effectiveness: the "scientists agree" information is the instrument. (c) Progressivity: only non-causal (OLS) evidence. Controls are the sociodemographic and political set from the online Appendix, and observations carry survey weights.
