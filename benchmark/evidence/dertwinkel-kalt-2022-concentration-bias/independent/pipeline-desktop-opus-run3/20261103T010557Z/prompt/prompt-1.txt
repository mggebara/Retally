Abstract (first 500 chars): Many intertemporal trade-offs are unbalanced: while the advantages of options are concen-
trated in a few periods, the disadvantages are dispersed over numerous periods. We provide
novel experimental evidence for “concentration bias”, the tendency to overweight advantages
that are concentrated in time. Subjects commit to too much overtime work that is dispersed
over multiple days in exchange for a bonus that is concentrated in time: concentration bias
increases subjects’ willingness to work by 2
Methodology Summary: - **Design / identification (consumption experiment, preregistered, AEA RCT-4446 and RCT-4341):** A within-subject lab experiment run in oTree with subjects recruited via ORSEE (sessions of 27–32, up to 75 min). Each subject first does 8 *balanced* trade-offs in random order. In trade-off j, extra real-effort tasks on a single workday j are traded against a raise r_j in a restaurant voucher redeemable on one later "restaurant day" (t = 9). This gives indifference points x̃ = (x̃1,…,x̃8). Then comes one *unbalanced* trade-off: x̃_t + d extra tasks on all 8 workdays against the total raise R = Σr_j (voucher raised from €7.50 to €38.80, i.e. a €31.30 raise). The elicited indifference point d̃ is the nonparametric, subject-level absolute measure of concentration bias. Standard discounting predicts d̃ = 0; the focusing model (Kőszegi & Szeidl 2013) predicts d̃ > 0.
- **Parametrisation and elicitation:** Mandatory tasks e = (129,132,143,115,121,117,135,127). Raises r = (€2.90,2.70,3.10,2.70,3.20,2.90,3.10,3.20). Base voucher v1 = €7.50. Subjects pick 8 workdays from 20 business days and a restaurant day from 7 dates. The task is transcribing 6 numbers into letters, with 10 practice tasks first. Each trade-off has 126 binary choices (x ∈ {0,…,125} for balanced; d ∈ {−63,…,62} for unbalanced), resolved by an adaptive bisection procedure: the first choice is random, each later direct choice is the midpoint of the interval not yet covered, and there are at most about 9 direct choices. The indifference point is defined conservatively as the largest number of extra tasks accepted. Incentives follow Attema et al. (2016): 3 random subjects per session have a randomly chosen choice implemented (plus €100 lump sum). Everyone gets €10 plus earnings from the arithmetic task.
- **Sample restrictions / preprocessing:** Preregistered exclusion of any subject with at least one corner choice in the 8 balanced trade-offs (robustness check in Online Appendix A.4). Upper-corner choosers in the unbalanced trade-off (always picking overtime; 27% of Main-Treatment, since 73% are interior) have d̃ proxied three ways: (i) lower bound d̃ = 62; (ii) upper bound = their un-incentivised, manually stated indifference point; (iii) the midpoint of (i) and (ii). The midpoint is the preferred version. Main-Treatment N = 100.
- **Relative measure (headline outcome):** d̃_rel = (1/8) Σ_{t=1}^{8} d̃ / (e_t + x̃_t), i.e. extra tasks as a share of per-workday willingness to work (in proportion units, not standardised). Headline: d̃_rel = 0.224 (22.4%), using the midpoint d̃.
- **Estimation (Table 2):** For each version of d̃ (lower, midpoint, upper bound), an OLS regression of d̃ and of d̃_rel on a constant only (i.e. the sample mean). Main-Treatment d̃ = 31.640 (2.685) / 37.610 (3.575) / 43.580 (4.683); d̃_rel = 0.190 (0.016) / 0.224 (0.021) / 0.259 (0.027). A Tobit on the lower-bound d̃, right-censored at 62, gives 37.094 (3.658). All have N = 100 and **robust (heteroskedasticity-robust) SEs**; no clustering and no fixed effects. Mean x̃ = (39.2,34.9,33.6,41.5,41.8,42.2,35.9,38.3). 88% of subjects have d̃_rel > 0, which a test shows is significantly greater than 50%.
- **Between-condition comparisons (Table 3):** OLS of d̃_rel on a constant and a condition dummy, with N = 200 per column (100 per condition) and robust SEs. Mechanism-Treatment, where the raise is shown as a sum of parts: 0.075 (0.019); Main minus Mechanism = 0.149 (0.028). Donation-Treatment, where the benefit is a donation to LichtBlick Seniorenhilfe: 0.135 (0.021); difference from Donation-Control = 0.158 (0.022). Main-Control: −0.023 (0.008); Main-Treatment minus Main-Control = 0.247 (0.023). Mechanism-Control: −0.014 (0.010); difference = 0.090 (0.021). In the Control conditions, balanced trade-off j = 1, 4 or 8 is repeated in place of the unbalanced one, using the same d ∈ [−63, 62] deviation format. Within session, Treatment and Control are assigned at random by stratified pair matching: subjects are ranked by their mean balanced-choice tasks, and neighbouring pairs are split between the two conditions. Further comparisons: share with bias 70% vs 88% (p < 0.01); conditional magnitude 16% vs 26%.
- **Covariates / heterogeneity:** Raven matrices, the CRT, an incentivised arithmetic test and demographics are collected. They are used as controls and for preregistered heterogeneity analyses (Online Appendix A.3): cognitive skills show no correlation, while response time is negatively correlated with bias. There are robustness checks with an alternative relative measure (A.5) and the absolute measure (A.6).
- **Money experiment (Section 5 / Online Appendix C):** Convex time budget allocations à la Andreoni & Sprenger (2012), with a positive interest rate. Money-Main has n = 185 and Money-Mechanism n = 189. The outcome is the savings rate (share allocated to the later payoff). Balanced choices are compared with unbalanced ones, where the earlier or later payoff is dispersed over 2, 4 or 8 dates; all subjects face the same choices, with no individualisation. Results: −5.7 pp when the later payoff is dispersed and +6.8 pp when the earlier payoff is dispersed (both p < 0.01). The bias is 3.7 / 6.6 / 8.1 pp for 2 / 4 / 8 dates.
