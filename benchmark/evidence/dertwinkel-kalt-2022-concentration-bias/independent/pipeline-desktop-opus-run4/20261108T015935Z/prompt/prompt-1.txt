Abstract (first 500 chars): Many intertemporal trade-offs are unbalanced: while the advantages of options are concen-
trated in a few periods, the disadvantages are dispersed over numerous periods. We provide
novel experimental evidence for “concentration bias”, the tendency to overweight advantages
that are concentrated in time. Subjects commit to too much overtime work that is dispersed
over multiple days in exchange for a bonus that is concentrated in time: concentration bias
increases subjects’ willingness to work by 2
Methodology Summary: - **Design / identification (consumption experiment, preregistered, AEA RCT 4446 and 4341):** Within-subject lab experiment (oTree, ORSEE; sessions of 27–32 subjects, up to 75 min). Each subject first does 8 *balanced* trade-offs (random order): extra real-effort tasks x_j on a single workday j vs. a raise r_j in a restaurant voucher redeemable on a later "restaurant day" (t = 9). This yields individual indifference points x̃ = (x̃1,…,x̃8). They then face one *unbalanced* trade-off: x̃_t + d extra tasks on all 8 workdays vs. the full raise R = Σ r_j (€31.30, on top of v = €7.50). The indifference point d̃ is the nonparametric, subject-level measure of concentration bias (discounted utility predicts d̃ = 0; the focusing model of Kőszegi & Szeidl 2013 predicts d̃ > 0). Parameters: e = (129,132,143,115,121,117,135,127) mandatory tasks per workday; r = (€2.90, 2.70, 3.10, 2.70, 3.20, 2.90, 3.10, 3.20). Subjects pick 8 workdays out of 20 dates and the restaurant day out of 7 dates.
- **Elicitation:** Each trade-off has 126 binary choices (x ∈ {0,…,125} in the balanced trade-offs; d ∈ {−63,…,62} in the unbalanced one). Choices are made with an adaptive bisection procedure: the first value is drawn at random, each later value is the midpoint of the interval not yet covered, there are at most 9 direct choices per trade-off, and single switching is enforced. The indifference point is the largest number of tasks accepted. Every direct or indirect choice is equally likely to be paid. 3 subjects per session are drawn to carry out their work plan (voucher + €100 lump sum); everyone gets €10 plus earnings from the arithmetic task.
- **Sample restrictions / preprocessing:** As preregistered, subjects with any corner choice in the balanced trade-offs are excluded (Online Appendix A.4 shows this does not matter). Upper-corner choosers in the unbalanced trade-off (always chose overtime, so d ≥ 62; 27% of subjects) are coded three ways: lower bound d̃ = 62, upper bound = their unincentivised self-stated indifference point, and the midpoint of the two (the midpoint is the headline coding). Main-Treatment N = 100.
- **Outcome measures:** Absolute measure d̃ is in tasks per workday (not standardised). Relative measure d̃_rel = (1/8) Σ_{t=1}^{8} d̃ / (e_t + x̃_t), a proportional increase in willingness to work. Headline: d̃_rel = 0.224 (22.4%) with midpoint coding.
- **Estimation (Table 2):** Constant-only OLS regressions of d̃ (cols 1–3: lower bound 31.640, midpoint 37.610, upper bound 43.580) and of d̃_rel (0.190, 0.224, 0.259) estimate the sample means, N = 100. Col 4 is a Tobit of d̃ right-censored at the lower bound 62 (37.094). Robust (heteroskedasticity-consistent) standard errors, no clustering, no fixed effects or controls in the headline specification. Demographics and cognitive measures (Raven, CRT, incentivised arithmetic) are used only as controls or correlates in heterogeneity analyses (Online Appendix A.3). Mean x̃ = (39.2, 34.9, 33.6, 41.5, 41.8, 42.2, 35.9, 38.3). 88% of subjects have d̃_rel > 0, tested against 50%.
- **Between-condition comparisons (Table 3, OLS, robust SEs, N = 200 per column):** Within-session stratified randomisation into condition pairs: eligible subjects are ranked on their mean balanced-choice tasks, and neighbouring pairs are split between the two conditions. Results:
  - Mechanism-Treatment (raise framed in a dispersed way, as a sum of parts): d̃_rel = 0.075; Main − Mechanism = 0.149.
  - Donation-Treatment (a charity donation replaces the voucher): 0.135; difference vs. Donation-Control = 0.158.
  - Control conditions (in place of the unbalanced trade-off, balanced trade-off j = 1, 4 or 8 is repeated with d ∈ [−63, 62], a placebo test of preference stability): Main-Control −0.023 (Main − Main-Control = 0.247); Mechanism-Control −0.014 (difference 0.090).
- **Money experiment (secondary):** Convex time budget allocations in the style of Andreoni & Sprenger (2012) with a positive interest rate. Money-Main n = 185, Money-Mechanism n = 189. The outcome is the savings rate (share allocated to the later payoff). Within-subject comparison of balanced choices (both payoffs concentrated) with unbalanced choices (the earlier or the later payoff dispersed over 2, 4 or 8 dates), not individualised. Results: 5.7 p.p. less saving when the later payoff is dispersed and 6.8 p.p. more when the earlier payoff is dispersed. Bias is 3.7, 6.6 and 8.1 p.p. for 2, 4 and 8 dates.
- **Inference:** Significance is reported at p < 0.1, 0.05 and 0.01. All Treatment estimates are significantly greater than zero and than the Control placebos (p < 0.001).
