## Paper Information
Title: Funding by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) under
Abstract (first 500 chars): Many intertemporal trade-offs are unbalanced: while the advantages of options are concen-
trated in a few periods, the disadvantages are dispersed over numerous periods. We provide
novel experimental evidence for “concentration bias”, the tendency to overweight advantages
that are concentrated in time. Subjects commit to too much overtime work that is dispersed
over multiple days in exchange for a bonus that is concentrated in time: concentration bias
increases subjects’ willingness to work by 2
Methodology Summary: - **Design and identification (consumption experiment, Main-Treatment):** This is a preregistered within-subject lab experiment (AEA RCT registry 4446/4341). Each subject first completes 8 *balanced* trade-offs in random order. In trade-off j, extra real-effort tasks on workday j are exchanged for a raise r_j in a restaurant voucher redeemed on a later "restaurant day" (t = 9). These trade-offs elicit the indifference points x̃ = (x̃1,…,x̃8). Next, each subject faces one *unbalanced* trade-off that is individualised to them: x̃_t + d extra tasks on all 8 workdays in exchange for the summed raise R = r1+…+r8. The indifference point d̃ is the absolute measure of concentration bias. Discounted utility predicts d̃ = 0 and focusing predicts d̃ > 0. The relative measure is d̃_rel = (1/8) Σ_t d̃ / (e_t + x̃_t), using the midpoint proxy for corner subjects.
- **Parameters:** Mandatory tasks e = (129,132,143,115,121,117,135,127) per workday. Base voucher v1 = €7.50. Raises r = (€2.90,€2.70,€3.10,€2.70,€3.20,€2.90,€3.10,€3.20). Subjects choose their 8 workdays from 20 dates and the restaurant day from 7 dates. The real-effort task is transcribing 6 numbers into letters, with 10 practice tasks.
- **Elicitation:** Each trade-off covers 126 binary choices: x ∈ {0,…,125} in balanced trade-offs and d ∈ {−63,…,62} in the unbalanced one. Subjects make at most 9 of these choices directly. The first direct choice is random, and each later one is the midpoint of the interval not yet covered. All remaining choices are implied by monotonicity. The indifference point is the largest number of tasks the subject accepts. Incentives follow a random-choice payment rule: in each session of 27–32 subjects, 3 are randomly selected to carry out their work plan. They receive the voucher plus a €100 lump sum, and every subject receives a €10 show-up payment plus earnings from the arithmetic test.
- **Sample restrictions and corner handling:** As preregistered, subjects with at least one corner choice in any balanced trade-off are excluded, leaving N = 100 per condition. Subjects with an upper corner in the unbalanced trade-off (always choosing overtime) get three proxies for d̃: (i) a lower bound of 62, (ii) an upper bound equal to their unincentivised self-stated indifference point, and (iii) the midpoint of the two. The midpoint is the headline proxy.
- **Estimation (Table 2):** The mean of d̃ and the mean of d̃_rel are estimated by OLS of the measure on a constant, separately for the lower-bound, midpoint and upper-bound proxies (columns 1–3). Column 4 is a Tobit regression on the lower-bound measure that treats 62 as the upper censoring point. Robust (heteroskedasticity-consistent) standard errors are used, and the tests are against zero. The headline estimate is d̃_rel in the midpoint specification, Table 2 column 2, in raw (unstandardised) units: a proportion of per-workday tasks.
- **Between-condition comparisons (Table 3):** OLS on pooled pairs of conditions (N = 200 each) estimates the condition mean and the difference between conditions, with robust standard errors. The pairs are:
  - Mechanism-Treatment, where the voucher raise is framed as a sum of its parts, versus Main-Treatment;
  - Donation-Treatment, which uses a charitable donation instead of the voucher, versus Donation-Control;
  - Main-Treatment and Mechanism-Treatment versus their Control conditions, in which the final trade-off repeats balanced trade-off j = 1, 4 or 8 with d ∈ [−63, +62].

  Within each pair, assignment was randomised within session and stratified on the average number of balanced-choice tasks (neighbouring-rank pairs). Further tests are: the share of subjects with d̃_rel > 0 tested against 50%, and comparisons of the share with bias and of conditional magnitudes between conditions.
- **Covariates and heterogeneity:** Cognitive proxies (Raven matrices, CRT, an incentivised arithmetic test), demographics and response times are collected and used as controls or correlates in the preregistered heterogeneity regressions (Online Appendix A.3). Robustness checks use an alternative relative measure, the absolute measure, and the sample that includes corner choices (Appendices A.4–A.6).
- **Money experiment (secondary):** Convex time-budget allocations (Andreoni–Sprenger) between an earlier and a later payment at a positive interest rate. Conditions are Money-Main (n = 185) and Money-Mechanism (n = 189). The outcome is the savings rate, the share of the budget allocated to the later payoff. It is compared within subject between balanced choices (both payoffs concentrated) and unbalanced choices (the earlier or the later payoff dispersed over 2, 4 or 8 dates). The choices are identical across subjects and not individualised.
