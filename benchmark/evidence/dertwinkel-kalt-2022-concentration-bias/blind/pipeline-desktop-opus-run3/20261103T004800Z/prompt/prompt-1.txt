## Paper Information
Title: Funding by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) under
Abstract (first 500 chars): Many intertemporal trade-offs are unbalanced: while the advantages of options are concen-
trated in a few periods, the disadvantages are dispersed over numerous periods. We provide
novel experimental evidence for “concentration bias”, the tendency to overweight advantages
that are concentrated in time. Subjects commit to too much overtime work that is dispersed
over multiple days in exchange for a bonus that is concentrated in time: concentration bias
increases subjects’ willingness to work by 2
Methodology Summary: - **Design / identification (consumption experiment, Main-Treatment):** Within-subject lab experiment (oTree, ORSEE recruitment; sessions of 27–32 subjects, up to 75 min). Each subject first faces 8 *balanced* trade-offs (in random order), each trading extra real-effort tasks x_j on a single workday j ∈ {1,…,8} against a raise r_j in a restaurant voucher redeemable on restaurant day t = 9. The elicited indifference points x̃ = (x̃_1,…,x̃_8) are then plugged into one *unbalanced* trade-off, in which the subject chooses between the baseline plan and a plan with x̃_t + d extra tasks on **all** 8 workdays in exchange for the total raise R = r_1+…+r_8. The indifference point d̃ is the nonparametric, individual-level absolute measure of concentration bias (discounted utility predicts d̃ = 0; the focusing model predicts d̃ > 0). Subjects are not told that the balanced and unbalanced trade-offs are linked.
- **Parametrisation:** mandatory tasks e = (129, 132, 143, 115, 121, 117, 135, 127) per workday; base voucher v = v_1 = €7.50; raises r = (€2.90, €2.70, €3.10, €2.70, €3.20, €2.90, €3.10, €3.20), so R = €31.30 (v + R = €38.80). Subjects pick their 8 workdays from 20 business days in the four weeks after the session and the restaurant day from 7 dates. The real-effort task is transcribing a 6-digit sequence into letters with a mapping that is randomised for each task; 10 practice tasks are done first. Payment: €10 show-up fee plus earnings from an incentivised arithmetic test; 3 subjects per session are randomly chosen to carry out their payoff-relevant work plan and then receive the voucher plus €100.
- **Elicitation:** Each trade-off consists of 126 binary choices (balanced: x_t ∈ {0,…,125}; unbalanced: d ∈ {−63,…,62}). Subjects make up to 9 of these directly through an adaptive bisection procedure (random first value, then the midpoint of the remaining interval), and the rest are filled in by monotonicity. This is logically equivalent to a choice list and forces a single switch point. The indifference point is defined conservatively as the largest number of extra tasks the subject accepts. Each direct or indirect choice is equally likely to be paid.
- **Sample restrictions and handling of corner choices:** As preregistered (AEA RCT registry 4446 and 4341), subjects with at least one corner choice in the balanced trade-offs are excluded (the sample is n = 100 for Main-Treatment, and each pair of conditions has 200 observations). Subjects with an upper corner in the unbalanced trade-off (always choosing overtime) are asked, without incentives, to state d̃ manually. Their d̃ is proxied by (i) the lower bound, 62, (ii) the upper bound, i.e. the stated value, or (iii) the midpoint of the two. The midpoint is the headline proxy.
- **Outcome measures:** absolute d̃ (tasks per workday) and relative d̃_rel = (1/8) Σ_{t=1}^{8} d̃ / (e_t + x̃_t), the headline measure (using the midpoint proxy). The share of subjects with d̃_rel > 0 is also reported and tested against 50%.
- **Estimation and inference:** Mean d̃ and mean d̃_rel are estimated by OLS on a constant, with robust (heteroskedasticity-consistent) standard errors (Table 2, columns 1–3: lower bound, midpoint, upper bound). Column 4 adds a Tobit regression of d̃ on a constant using the lower bound, censored at the upper limit of 62. No fixed effects or controls are used in these headline estimates; demographics and cognitive measures (Raven matrices, CRT, arithmetic test) are used only in the heterogeneity and control regressions in the appendix.
- **Further conditions (between subjects, Table 3, OLS with robust SEs, 200 observations per pair):**
  - Mechanism-Treatment: same as Main-Treatment, but the voucher raise is shown as a sum of parts ("€7.50 + €2.90 + …") instead of as a total.
  - Donation-Treatment: a charitable donation replaces the voucher.
  - Control conditions (Main-, Mechanism-, Donation-Control): the final trade-off repeats balanced trade-off j = 1, 4 or 8, with a deviation from x̃_j in [−63, +62]. This acts as a placebo test of preference stability.

  Within each pair, subjects are randomised to a condition within session, stratified by their mean balanced-choice tasks: eligible subjects are ranked and neighbouring pairs are split between the two conditions. Mechanism and Main sessions alternated on the same dates. The differences reported are Main − Mechanism and Treatment − Control.
- **Money experiment (complementary):** Convex-budget allocations between an earlier and a later bank-transfer payoff (Andreoni–Sprenger style) with a positive interest rate, in Money-Main (n = 185) and Money-Mechanism (n = 189). The outcome is the savings rate (share allocated to the later payoff), compared within subject between balanced choices (both payoffs concentrated) and unbalanced choices (the earlier or the later payoff dispersed over 2, 4 or 8 dates). The choices are identical for all subjects (not individualised).
