Title: WAR, SOCIALISM, AND THE RISE OF FASCISM:
Abstract (first 500 chars): DARON ACEMOGLU GIUSEPPE DE FEO GIACOMO DE LUCA GIANLUCA RUSSO The recent ascent of right-wing populist movements in several countries has rekindled interest in understanding the causes of the rise of fascism in the in-
Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimator:** Cross-sectional municipality-level IV (2SLS). The endogenous regressor is the Socialist vote share in the 1919 national election. It is instrumented by the *Share of foot soldier casualties*: World War I foot-soldier deaths from the military Roll of Honor (529,028 deaths, digitised by ISTORECO) originating in the municipality, divided by the male population over age six in the 1911 census. Volunteers, special assault troops (Arditi), navy and air force are excluded because those units recruited selectively. First stage (eq. 1, Table I): Socialist vote share 1919_i = γ·Share of foot soldier casualties_i + X_i′β + ε_i, estimated by OLS. The second stage regresses fascist outcomes on instrumented 1919 socialist support. Linear in levels, no logs.
- **Outcomes (fascist activity):** (i) presence of a local Fascist Party branch in September 1921 (from prefect reports); (ii) Fascist violence 1920–22, the number of fascist violent episodes per 1,000 inhabitants (Franzinelli 2003); (iii) Fascist vote share in 1921; (iv) Fascist vote share in 1924. The 1921 fascist share gives fascist candidates their proportional share of National Bloc votes. A restricted no-imputation sample of 2,188 municipalities has candidate-level data. The rest are imputed from district, province or electoral-district candidate shares. Robustness outcomes: fascist killings, all political violence and non-fascist violence in 1920–22; 1920 municipal socialist control (dummy); 1921/1924 socialist shares. Later outcomes: Jewish deportation 1943–45 (a dummy, and deportees over 1911 Jewish population capped at 1); 1946–2018 vote shares, e.g. Christian Democrats.
- **Units:** The paper explicitly reports **standardised coefficients** in Table I "and all subsequent regressions". Both the left-hand-side and right-hand-side variables are standardised (z-scored) before estimation.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Every specification has province fixed effects, regiment fixed effects, and basic demographic controls. Regiment fixed effects are dummies for any death from the municipality in each regiment. Demographic controls are a quartic in log 1911 population and the share of population under age six in 1911. Controls are then added step by step (Table I cols 1–6):
  - (2) geographic: log area, elevation of the main centre, maximum elevation
  - (3) Socialist vote share in 1913
  - (4) military: veterans from classes 1874–95 and from classes 1896–1900 (per male population over six, measured at military-district level); assault-troop plus volunteer casualties per male population over six; a dummy for army-supplying production plants; a dummy for casualties in the highest-mortality battles (days with more than 1,000 deaths)
  - (5) agricultural: share of day labourers, share of sharecroppers, a dummy for a local landowner/agrarian association
  - (6) urban: industrial workers and industrial firms per male population (1911), 1911 literacy rate, share of entrepreneurs and rentiers (elites), share of bourgeoisie

  Column 6 is the full control set.
- **Standard errors:** Heteroskedasticity-robust and clustered at the administrative-district level (181 clusters). The first-stage F statistic is reported. Conley spatial standard errors are given as a robustness check (Online Appendix Table A2).
- **Sample:** 5,775 municipalities in 64 of 69 provinces (1921 boundaries, out of 8,355 municipalities in 1921), i.e. those with recovered 1919, 1921 and 1924 election data. Most missing areas are in Calabria and Sicily. Data from other periods are mapped onto 1921 municipalities. Census class shares (day labourers, sharecroppers, elites, bourgeoisie) come at agrarian-zone level (more than 700 zones) and are assigned to all municipalities in the zone. No train/test split or cross-validation is used.
- **Validity and mechanism checks:** Balance or placebo regressions of the instrument on pre-1919 political, economic and social covariates. Placebos on the 1919 Nationalist vote, WWI memorials by 1921 (a dummy and a count per 1,000 inhabitants), and volunteer or assault-troop casualties. Heterogeneity by prior socialist strength, landowner associations and elite share. Large donors to the Fascist Party in 1919–25 (dummy).
- **Alternative instruments:** (a) Excess mortality in 1918 relative to 1911–14, a Spanish-flu proxy, available for 207 urban municipalities. (b) Relative rainfall for December 1918–May 1919 against the 1915–79 average, from 427 weather stations. It is interpolated to municipalities by inverse-distance weighting with a 30 km cutoff and capped at 1, so only rainfall shortfalls are used.
