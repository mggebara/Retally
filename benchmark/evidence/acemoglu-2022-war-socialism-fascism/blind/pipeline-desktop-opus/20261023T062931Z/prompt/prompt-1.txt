Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimator:** Cross-sectional municipality-level IV design. The first stage (equation (1), Table I) is OLS of *Socialist vote share in 1919* on *Share of foot soldier casualties* plus covariates. The main results use two-stage least squares, with foot soldier casualties instrumenting the 1919 socialist vote share. The fascist outcomes in the second stage are local Fascist Party branches (September 1921), *Fascist violence in 1920–22* (episodes per 1,000 inhabitants, from Franzinelli 2003), and the *Fascist vote share in 1921* and *in 1924*. Robustness checks use fascist killings, all political violence, non-fascist violence, and the restricted (no-imputation) 2,188-municipality sample for the 1921 fascist share. Two alternative instruments are used: *Relative rainfall in winter–spring 1918–19* and *Excess mortality in 1918* (Spanish flu, 207 urban municipalities).
- **Key regressor / instrument construction:** The instrument is the count of WWI foot soldier deaths from the municipality in the military Roll of Honor, divided by the male population over age six in the 1911 census. Casualties among navy, air force, special assault troops (Arditi) and volunteers are excluded. All later-period data are mapped to 1921 municipal boundaries. The 1921 fascist vote share assigns National Bloc votes to fascists in proportion to fascist candidates' share of candidate votes. Where municipal candidate votes are missing, it is imputed from district, province or electoral-district data. Relative rainfall is measured as Dec 1918–May 1919 rainfall over the 1915–79 average at 427 stations. It is interpolated to municipalities by inverse distance (30 km cutoff) and capped at 1.
- **Units of the headline coefficients:** These are **standardized coefficients**. The paper explicitly standardizes both the left- and right-hand-side variables "in this and all subsequent regressions."
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Every specification includes province fixed effects, regiment fixed effects (dummies for any deaths from the municipality in each regiment), and demographic controls (a quartic in log 1911 population and the share of population under age six in 1911). Table I adds controls cumulatively across columns 1–6:
  - (2) geographic: log area, elevation of the main center, maximum elevation.
  - (3) socialist vote share in 1913.
  - (4) military: veterans from classes 1874–95 and 1896–1900, assault-troop and volunteer casualties over male population above age six, a dummy for army-supplying production plants, and a dummy for casualties in the highest-mortality battles (days with more than 1,000 deaths).
  - (5) agricultural: share of day laborers, share of sharecroppers, and a dummy for a local landowner/agrarian association.
  - (6) urban: industrial workers and industrial firms over male population in 1911, the 1911 literacy rate, the share of entrepreneurs and rentiers (elites), and the share of bourgeoisie.

  Column 6 is the full control set.
- **Standard errors:** Robust to heteroskedasticity and clustered at the administrative-district level (181 districts). Conley spatial-HAC standard errors are reported as a robustness check in Online Appendix Table A2. Instrument strength is reported with the first-stage F-statistic.
- **Sample:** 5,775 municipalities in 64 provinces (out of 8,355 municipalities in the 1921 census, excluding Julian Venetia and Trentino). These are the municipalities for which 1919, 1921 and 1924 election returns could be reconstructed. Coverage is missing mainly in parts of Calabria and Sicily. The 1921 fascist share uses a restricted sample of 2,188 municipalities without imputation. The Spanish flu analysis uses 207 urban municipalities.
- **Validation and mechanism tests:** Balance and falsification checks regress pre-war political, economic and social characteristics on the instrument. Placebo outcomes include the 1919 Nationalist vote share, WWI monuments by 1921, and volunteer and assault-troop casualties. Heterogeneity analyses split the sample by prior socialist strength, landowner associations and the elite share. Longer-run outcomes include Jewish deportations in 1943–45 (a dummy, and deportations over the 1911 Jewish population capped at 1) and postwar vote shares from 1946 to 2018.
- **Not applicable:** No machine-learning train/test split, cross-validation or hyperparameters are involved. Evaluation is by coefficient estimates with district-clustered inference.
