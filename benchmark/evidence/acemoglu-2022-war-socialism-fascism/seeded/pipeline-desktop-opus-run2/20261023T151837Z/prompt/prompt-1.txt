Title: WAR, SOCIALISM, AND THE RISE OF FASCISM:
Abstract (first 500 chars): DARON ACEMOGLU GIUSEPPE DE FEO GIACOMO DE LUCA GIANLUCA RUSSO The recent ascent of right-wing populist movements in several countries has rekindled interest in understanding the causes of the rise of fascism in the in-
Methodology Summary: - **Estimation method:** The paper uses cross-sectional OLS and instrumental variables at the municipality level. The first stage (equation (1), Table I) regresses Socialist vote share in 1919 on the Share of foot soldier casualties, plus covariates X_i. The main analysis is 2SLS. It instruments Socialist vote share 1919 with the Share of foot soldier casualties. The paper treats this as an exogenous "red scare" boost to socialist support. The second-stage outcomes are four measures of fascist activity:
  - presence of a local Fascist Party branch in September 1921;
  - Fascist violence in 1920–22, measured as violent episodes per 1,000 inhabitants (from Franzinelli 2003);
  - Fascist vote share in 1921;
  - Fascist vote share in 1924.
  Robustness checks use alternative violence measures (fascist killings, all political violence, non-fascist violence). Alternative instruments are Excess mortality in 1918 from the Spanish flu (207 urban municipalities) and Relative rainfall in winter–spring 1918–19, which is capped at 1. There are no ML train/test splits or cross-validation.
- **Key regressor and instrument:** The Share of foot soldier casualties is the count of WWI foot soldier deaths from the municipality in the Roll of Honor, divided by the male population over age six in the 1911 census. The variable is in levels (shares). It is not a log-log specification. Casualties among volunteers and special assault troops (Arditi) are excluded from the instrument and enter as a separate control.
- **Units of the coefficients:** The paper explicitly says that in this and all subsequent regressions it reports standardized coefficients, with both the left-hand-side and right-hand-side variables standardized. So the headline estimates are standardized (beta) coefficients. The headline first-stage coefficient is 0.12 (SE 0.02) in Table I, column (1). It falls to 0.10 (SE 0.01) in columns (3)–(6), with first-stage F-statistics of about 41.7–56.4. In column (1), zero foot soldier casualties would imply a socialist vote share lower by 6.5 percentage points.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Every specification includes:
  - province fixed effects;
  - regiment fixed effects (dummies for any deaths from the municipality in a given regiment);
  - basic demographic controls: a quartic in log municipality population and the share of the population under age six in 1911.

  The controls are added cumulatively across Table I columns (2)–(6):
  - (2) geographic controls: log area, elevation of the main center, maximum elevation;
  - (3) socialist vote share in 1913;
  - (4) military controls: veterans from the 1874–95 classes and from the 1896–1900 classes, and assault-troop plus volunteer casualties, each as a share of males over six; a dummy for army-supplying war production plants; and a dummy for any casualties in the highest-mortality battles (days with more than 1,000 deaths);
  - (5) agricultural controls: share of day laborers, share of sharecroppers, and a dummy for a local landowner/agrarian association;
  - (6) urban controls: industrial workers and industrial firms over male population in 1911, the 1911 literacy rate, the share of entrepreneurs and rentiers (elites), and the share of bourgeoisie.

  Column (6) is the full control set.
- **Standard errors:** Standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust and clustered at the administrative district level (181 clusters). Conley spatial standard errors are reported in Online Appendix Table A2.
- **Sample and preprocessing:**
  - The sample is the 5,775 municipalities (1921 boundaries, 64 provinces) with recovered 1919–24 election data. Areas without data, mainly parts of Calabria and Sicily, are dropped.
  - Data from other periods are mapped to the 1921 municipalities.
  - Census occupational shares come from about 700 agrarian zones and are assigned to every municipality in the zone.
  - Veterans are computed as draftees minus casualties at the military-district level.
  - The 1921 fascist vote share apportions National Bloc votes by the fascist candidates' share of candidate votes. It is observed directly for 2,188 municipalities (the restricted, no-imputation sample) and imputed for the rest from district, province or electoral-district data.
  - Jewish deportation ratios are capped at 1.
- **Further analyses and evaluation:**
  - Balance and falsification tests show the instrument is unrelated to pre-1919 characteristics, Nationalist Party votes in 1919, war memorials, and volunteer or assault-troop casualties.
  - Heterogeneity by prior socialist strength, landowner associations and elite share is used to test mechanisms.
  - Long-run outcomes are Jewish deportations in 1943–45 (an any-deportation dummy and the deported share of the 1911 Jewish population) and postwar (1946–2018) vote shares, for example for the Christian Democrats.
  - Fit is reported through R-squared, observation and cluster counts, and first-stage F-statistics.
