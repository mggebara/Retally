Methodology Summary: - **Design / estimation framework:** Field experiment with 1,248 members of a Midwest U.S. university-affiliated fitness facility, recruited in three waves (Oct 2015–Mar 2016). Each person did an online elicitation and then had gym attendance observed for four weeks, starting the Monday after the online component. Attendance is measured from gym swipe data. Reduced-form estimation uses the sufficient-statistic "behavior change premium" BCP(p,Δ) = [w(p+Δ) − w(p)]/Δ − [α̃(p+Δ) + α̃(p)]/2 (eq. 3), built from willingness to pay (WTP) w and forecasted attendance α̃ at adjacent piece-rate levels. The structural model (Section 7) is quasi-hyperbolic discounting with partial naivete in the style of O'Donoghue–Rabin (2001). It estimates actual present focus β, perceived present focus β̃ and the long-run benefit of attending b, with δ = 1, utility quasilinear in money and stochastic per-visit costs, and it is validated with out-of-sample tests. Commitment take-up is interpreted through an "imperfect perception" random-utility model, V̂ = V + σ(P)ε (logistic, two-stage Luce).
- **Randomized information treatment (50%, given before all elicitations):** In wave 1, participants saw a graph of their own weekly visits over the past 20 weeks. In waves 2–3 the treatment was enhanced: participants also entered their own estimate of their average weekly visits, and they were told that the prior wave overestimated attendance by 4 visits over 4 weeks.
- **Elicitations:** Participants gave a best-guess forecast of visits over the 4 weeks (0–28), a goal, and the probability of meeting the goal. For six piece rates ($1, $2, $3, $5, $7, $12 per day, one page each in random order), they gave a forecast of visits and a slider WTP. The slider ran from $0 to 30 × the rate, with a fill-in box when the slider was at its maximum. WTP was made incentive-compatible with BDM, and two comprehension checks were included. Forecasts were not incentivized. Commitment contracts were binary choices between $80 unconditional and $80 conditional on attendance. In waves 1–2 the thresholds were ≥8, ≥12, ≥16 ("more") and ≤7, ≤11, ≤15 ("fewer"). Wave 3 offered only ≥12 and ≤11, plus beliefs about the probability of meeting each threshold.
- **Random assignment of incentives (one question drawn per person):** BDM fixed payments were drawn from a mixture: U[$0,$7] with weight 0.99 and U[full slider range] with weight 0.01.
  - Wave 1: $0, $2 and $7 per visit, each with probability 0.33; the remaining 1% was a draw from all questions.
  - Wave 2: $0 33%, $2 33%, $5 16.5%, $7 16.5%.
  - Wave 3: $80 conditional on ≥12 visits 33%, $0 33%, $7 piece rate 33%.
- **Sample restrictions:** Participants had to be over 18 and gym members for the past 8 weeks. Waves had 350, 528 and 414 participants. 44 observations where the assignment could be endogenous are excluded throughout: 12 whose fixed-payment draw exceeded $7, and 32 whose WTP fell inside the $0–$7 range.
- **Outcomes / evaluation:**
  - Actual 4-week attendance by randomly assigned incentive, which gives treatment effects of piece rates and of the $80 ≥12 contract.
  - Forecast minus actual attendance (overestimation).
  - Mean BCP and how it varies with the information treatment and proxies for sophistication.
  - Take-up rates of "more" and "fewer" contracts, and the correlation between the two.
  - How take-up relates to BCP, proxies for sophistication and structural β, β̃, b, and the causal effect of the information treatment on take-up.
  - Welfare: consumer surplus under the long-run criterion and social efficiency of commitment contracts, compared with linear per-visit subsidies scaled to produce the same average change in attendance.
