Title: NBER WORKING PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design / sample:** Randomized field experiment with N = 1,248 members of a university-affiliated fitness facility in a Midwest U.S. city (aged 18+, members for the past 8 weeks). It ran in three waves between Oct 2015 and Mar 2016 (350, 528 and 414 enrolled). Participants completed an online elicitation, and then their gym attendance (card swipes) was observed for the 4 weeks starting the following Monday. Rewards were paid by mailed check after the 4 weeks.
- **Randomized information treatment (50% probability, assigned before any elicitation):** In wave 1 it was a graph of the participant's weekly visits over the past 20 weeks. In waves 2–3 it was enhanced: participants entered their own estimate of past weekly visits and were told that prior participants overestimated by about 4 visits over 4 weeks. The treatment is used to identify the causal effect of sophistication on beliefs, on the behavior change premium and on commitment take-up.
- **Elicitations:**
  - Forecast visits (0–28) and a goal for the next 4 weeks.
  - For six piece-rate incentives ($1, $2, $3, $5, $7 and $12 per visit, shown in random order): forecasted attendance and willingness to pay (WTP), elicited with an incentive-compatible BDM slider ($0 up to 30× the rate, with a fill-in box at the maximum).
  - Binary choices between $80 unconditional and $80 conditional on attending ≥8, ≥12 or ≥16 times ("more" contracts), or ≤7, ≤11 or ≤15 times ("less" contracts), in waves 1–2. Wave 3 offered only ≥12 and ≤11, plus the participant's believed probability of meeting each threshold.
- **Random assignment of actual incentives:** One question was drawn per person, and the BDM fixed payment came from a mixture: U[$0,$7] with weight 0.99 and U[full slider range] with weight 0.01.
  - Wave 1: $0, $2 and $7 per visit, each with probability 0.33.
  - Wave 2: $0 (33%), $2 (33%), $5 (16.5%) and $7 (16.5%).
  - Wave 3: $0, $7 per visit, and $80 for 12+ visits, each 33%.
  - In every wave the remaining 1% was spread over all other questions.
- **Sample restriction:** 44 observations where assignment could be endogenous are excluded throughout. These are 12 whose fixed-payment draw was above $7 and 32 whose WTP fell inside the $0–$7 range.
- **Reduced-form estimation:**
  - Behavior change premium: BCP(p,Δ) = [w(p+Δ) − w(p)]/Δ − [α̃(p+Δ) + α̃(p)]/2, built from adjacent incentive levels and expressed per $1 of incentive. Its headline mean is $1.78 per visit.
  - Effects of the randomized incentives on actual visits are estimated by comparing assigned groups. Headline: +3.51 visits for the $80/12+ contract.
  - Take-up rates of the "more" contracts (64%, 49% and 32%) and the "less" contracts (27–34%).
  - Correlation between "more" and "less" take-up, and the association of take-up with the BCP and other sophistication proxies.
  - Effect of the information treatment on forecasts, BCP and take-up.
- **Structural estimation (Section 7):** A quasi-hyperbolic (β–δ, with δ = 1) model with partial naivete (β̃), following O'Donoghue–Rabin (2001), with stochastic costs and quasilinear utility. It is fit to forecasted attendance, WTP and actual attendance at the randomized piece rates. Contract take-up is modelled with mean-zero stochastic valuation errors (logit, "imperfect perception").
  - Estimates: β = 0.55, β̃ = 0.84 (0.86 in control vs 0.78 under information), b = $9.66 per visit, internality (1−β)b = $4.39.
  - The model is validated with out-of-sample tests.
  - It is used to compute consumer surplus from the contracts under the long-run criterion: −$7.91, −$18.69 and −$10.51 per taker for 8+, 12+ and 16+. It also compares the social efficiency of the contracts with linear per-visit subsidies scaled to produce the same average attendance change.
