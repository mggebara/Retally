Methodology Summary: - **Design / sample**: Field experiment with members (age 18+, members for the prior 8 weeks) of a university-affiliated fitness facility in a large Midwest US city; three recruitment waves Oct 2015–Mar 2016 (Wave 1 = 350, Wave 2 = 528, Wave 3 = 414 participants; analysis N = 1,248). An online elicitation was followed by 4 weeks of attendance observed through gym swipe records. Outcome: number of gym visits (days, 0–28) over the 4-week incentive period starting the Monday after the online component.
- **Randomized information treatment (50% probability, assigned before any elicitation)**: Wave 1 showed a graph of the participant's own weekly visits over the past 20 weeks. Waves 2–3 used an enhanced version: participants also entered their estimate of average weekly past visits, and were told that prior-wave participants overestimated their attendance by 4 visits per 4 weeks (about 1 per week). Controls went straight to the elicitations.
- **Elicitations**: (i) best-guess visits, a goal and the probability of meeting it; (ii) for 6 piece-rate incentives ($1, $2, $3, $5, $7, $12 per visit, shown in random order), forecasted visits α̃(p) and WTP on a slider from $0 to 30×p (a fill-in box if the slider was maxed; 7.4% of responses), elicited with BDM; (iii) binary commitment choices of $80 unconditional vs. $80 conditional. Waves 1–2 offered "8+, 12+, 16+" ("more") and "≤7, ≤11, ≤15" ("fewer") contracts; Wave 3 offered only "12+" and "≤11", plus subjective probabilities of meeting each.
- **Random assignment of incentives**: BDM fixed payments came from a mixture distribution: U[$0,$7] with weight 0.99 and U[full slider range] with weight 0.01. Assignment probabilities: Wave 1 was 1/3 each for $0, $2 and $7 per visit (1% residual across all questions). Wave 2 was $0 33%, $2 33%, $5 16.5%, $7 16.5%. Wave 3 was 1/3 each for $0, $7 per visit, and $80 conditional on 12+ visits (given only to those who preferred it to $0). **Sample restriction**: 44 observations with endogenous incentive assignment are dropped throughout. These are 12 with a fixed-payment draw above $7 and 32 whose WTP fell inside the $0–$7 range.
- **Reduced-form estimators**: The behavior change premium is BCP(p,Δ) = [w(p+Δ) − w(p)]/Δ − [α̃(p+Δ) + α̃(p)]/2. It is computed per individual from WTP differences across adjacent incentive levels and forecasts, then averaged; it is in dollars per visit, per $1 of incentive (headline mean ≈ $1.78). Treatment effects of the randomized incentives on visits are estimated by comparing assigned-incentive groups (e.g., $80 for 12+ raises visits by 3.51). Information-treatment effects on forecasts, BCP and take-up compare treatment and control. Commitment take-up rates are reported (64% / 49% / 32% for 8+/12+/16+; 27–34% for "fewer" contracts), along with correlations between "more" and "fewer" take-up at each threshold and associations of take-up with BCP and sophistication proxies. The exact regression specifications, controls, fixed effects and SE clustering are not stated in the provided text (it is truncated at the Data section). Results are presumably regressions of visits or take-up on treatment indicators with wave controls and robust SEs, but that is unconfirmed.
- **Structural model**: Quasi-hyperbolic (β–δ) discounting with partial naivete (O'Donoghue–Rabin β̃), δ = 1, utility quasilinear in money, stochastic per-period attendance costs, and a long-run benefit b per visit. It is estimated from actual attendance under the randomized piece rates, forecasted attendance, and WTP moments, using Proposition 1: w′(p) = α̃(p) + (1−β̃)(b+p)α̃′(p). Estimates: β̂ = 0.55, β̃̂ = 0.84 (0.86 for control vs. 0.78 for information treatment), b̂ = $9.66 per visit, internality (1−β̂)b̂ = $4.39. The model is validated with out-of-sample tests.
- **Take-up noise model and welfare evaluation**: An imperfect-perception (random-utility / two-stage Luce, logistic) model V̂ = V + σ(P)ε captures mean-zero valuation errors in take-up. Welfare is evaluated under the long-run criterion: consumer surplus from 8+/12+/16+ contracts is −$7.91, −$18.69 and −$10.51 per taker. Social efficiency is compared against linear per-visit subsidies scaled to produce the same mean attendance change.
- **No ML-style train/test split or cross-validation**. Inference rests on randomized assignment, and structural validation uses out-of-sample prediction of behavior under incentive arms, including the commitment-contract arm.
