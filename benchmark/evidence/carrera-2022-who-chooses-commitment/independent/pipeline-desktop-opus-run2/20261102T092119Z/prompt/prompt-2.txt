## Paper
Title: NBER WORKING PAPER SERIES
Methodology: - **Design / sample:** Field experiment with N = 1,248 members (analysis sample) of a university-affiliated fitness facility in a large Midwest U.S. city. Enrollment was limited to adults (18+) who had held a membership for the past 8 weeks. There were three recruitment waves (Oct 2015 to Mar 2016; 350, 528 and 414 participants). Each participant did an online elicitation, followed by 4 weeks of attendance observed from gym swipe data, starting the Monday after the survey. No ML-style train/test split or cross-validation is used. The structural model is validated with out-of-sample tests.
- **Randomized information treatment (50% probability, before any elicitation):** In wave 1, participants saw a graph of their own weekly visits over the past 20 weeks. In waves 2 and 3, the treatment was enhanced: participants entered an estimate of their average weekly visits, and were told that the prior wave overestimated attendance by 4 visits per 4 weeks (1 visit per week). Its effects are estimated on forecasts, the behavior change premium (BCP) and commitment take-up.
- **Elicitations:** Participants gave a best-guess forecast of visits (0-28), a goal, and the probability of meeting the goal. For six piece-rate incentives ($1, $2, $3, $5, $7 and $12 per visit, in random order), they gave forecasted visits and WTP on a slider (range $0 to 30×rate, with fill-in-the-blank above the maximum). WTP was elicited with an incentive-compatible BDM mechanism. Fixed-payment draws came from a mixture: 0.99 weight on U[$0,$7] and 0.01 weight on U[full slider range]. Commitment contracts were binary choices between $80 unconditional and $80 conditional on ≥8, ≥12 or ≥16 visits ("more") or ≤7, ≤11 or ≤15 visits ("less"). Waves 1 and 2 had all 6 choices; wave 3 had only ≥12 and ≤11, plus beliefs about the probability of meeting each.
- **Random incentive assignment:** Wave 1: $0, $2 and $7 per visit, each with probability 0.33. Wave 2: $0 (33%), $2 (33%), $5 (16.5%), $7 (16.5%). Wave 3: $0 (33%), $7 per visit (33%), and $80 conditional on 12+ visits (33%, for those who chose it over $0). In every wave, the remaining 1% was a random draw from all questions. Incentive effects on visits are estimated by comparing randomized groups; for example, the $80/12+ contract raised attendance by 3.51 visits.
- **Sample restriction:** 44 observations are dropped throughout because their incentive assignment was endogenous to WTP: 12 with fixed-payment draws above $7, and 32 with WTP inside the $0-$7 range.
- **Key reduced-form statistic:** The behavior change premium is BCP(p,Δ) = [w(p+Δ) − w(p)]/Δ − [α̃(p+Δ) + α̃(p)]/2. It is computed from WTP differences and forecasts across adjacent incentive levels, in dollars per $1 of incentive. BCP > 0 implies β̃ < 1. The mean estimate is $1.78 per visit. The BCP and commitment take-up are related to the information treatment and to proxies for sophistication. Take-up rates are reported (64%, 49% and 32% for the 8+, 12+ and 16+ contracts; 27-34% for the "less" contracts), along with the correlation between take-up of "more" and "less" contracts at each threshold.
- **Structural estimation:** The model is quasi-hyperbolic (β-δ) discounting with partial naivete (O'Donoghue-Rabin β̃), δ = 1, quasilinear utility, stochastic per-period costs and a delayed benefit b. It is estimated from forecasted attendance, actual attendance and WTP under piece-rate incentives. Estimates: β̂ = 0.55, β̃̂ = 0.84 (0.86 in control vs 0.78 with information), b̂ = $9.66 per visit, internality (1−β)b = $4.39. Commitment take-up is modelled with mean-zero logistic "imperfect perception" valuation noise.
- **Welfare evaluation:** Consumer surplus under the long-run criterion for takers of the 8+, 12+ and 16+ contracts is −$7.91, −$18.69 and −$10.51 per person. The social efficiency of the contracts is compared with linear per-visit subsidies scaled to produce the same average increase in attendance.
