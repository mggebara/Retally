Title: Policy Deliberation and Voter Persuasion:
Methodology: - **Design / sample**: Field experiment in the May 13, 2013 Philippine party-list election with two party lists (Akbayan, feminist platform; Umalab Ka, urban-poor platform). Two-stage cluster sampling: 7 of 17 NCR municipalities and 6 of 90 Calabarzon municipalities (13 municipalities); in each, 3 barangays drawn at random, 1 assigned to treatment (2–3 town-hall meetings of ~40 participants, held Apr 21–May 9) and 2 to control ("business-as-usual" one-way campaign). Barangays within 1.5 km of each other were resampled. Municipalities were randomly assigned to the two party lists. Total 39 barangays (18 Akbayan, 21 Umalab Ka). Noncompliance: treatment was not implemented as assigned in Concepcion Dos (Marikina), Isla (Valenzuela) and San Roque (Louisiana); meetings were held in other barangays instead.

- **Barangay-level ITT (Eq. 1, Table 1)**: OLS of Y_jk = δ_k + β Z_jk + ε_jk, where Y is official COMELEC party-list turnout (% of registered voters) or the vote share of the treating party (% of total party-list votes), in percentage points (levels, not logs, not standardised). Z is the random assignment dummy and δ_k are municipality ("City") fixed effects. Columns: (1) turnout, N=39; (2) pooled vote share, N=39; (3) Akbayan vote share, N=18; (4) Umalab Ka vote share, N=21. Headline ITTs: −4.544, 1.096, 1.955, 0.360. Control means: 65.040, 2.397, 4.897, 0.253. R²: .389, .776, .453, .412.

- **CACE (Eq. 2)**: IV/2SLS of Y_jk = λ_k + α T_jk + υ_jk, where actual implementation T_jk is instrumented with assignment Z_jk and municipality FE are included. Compliers exclude the 3 noncomplying barangays. Table 1 CACEs: −5.908, 1.425, 2.606, 0.459.

- **Barangay-level inference**: nonparametric randomization/permutation test (Efron & Tibshirani 1994). Treatment assignment is permuted within municipality to build the null distribution of no effect, giving permutation p-values for the ITT (Table 1: turnout p=.404, pooled vote p=.048, Akbayan p=.087, Umalab Ka p=.141). No conventional SEs are used for the headline barangay estimates. Heterogeneity analysis: (a) treatment × number of registered voters with municipality FE (Fig. 2 left); (b) proportion of meeting attendees, instrumented with assignment, with municipality FE (Fig. 2 right).

- **Individual-level data**: postelection survey run by CPE two weeks after the election, using random-walk/quota sampling (intended 40 per barangay; every 5th household). There are 1,314 respondents. The estimation sample is 1,082 after dropping respondents without a valid turnout response. Municipalities Imus, Pateros and Santa Maria are excluded because they lack both treatment and control survey barangays. Multiple-imputation robustness check in SI Appendix E.

- **Individual-level specifications (Eqs. 3–5)**: linear probability models of turnout / vote for the treating party on assignment Z (ITT) with municipality FE, and IV versions instrumenting T with Z (CACE). SEs are clustered at the barangay level, the level of treatment assignment. Estimates use inverse probability weights to correct oversampling of meeting attendees (weight = ratio of self-reported survey attendees to actual attendees from attendance sheets). Unweighted results in SI Appendix F use permutation inference. Heterogeneity (Eq. 5): Z, X, and Z×X with municipality FE, where X is income (cut at 10,000 pesos), education (primary or less vs. high school or more) or gender. Run separately by party, with ITT and IV-CACE analogues. Robustness to adding pretreatment covariates is in SI Appendix D.

- **Mechanism outcomes (Fig. 5)**: Anderson (2008) approach. Each item is oriented so positive means more agreement, then standardised relative to the control-group mean and s.d. Items are combined into inverse-covariance-weighted summary indices: a gender-discrimination/sexism index from 6 items (Female Rep., Female Pol., Equality, Discrimination, Harassment) and a poverty index from 4 items (Poverty, GAP, CCT, Corruption). ITT and CACE are reported in s.d. units with city FE, barangay-clustered SEs and 95% CIs. Examples: Akbayan gender index ITT 0.45 s.d., CACE 0.67 s.d.; Umalab Ka poverty index ITT 0.13 s.d.

- **Balance / evaluation**: balance on pretreatment official statistics and on survey covariates is checked in SI Appendix B. There is no train/test split or cross-validation, since this is a randomized-experiment causal inference design. Metrics are ITT/CACE point estimates with permutation p-values (barangay level) or clustered-SE 95% CIs (individual level). Relative effects are also reported, e.g. pooled ITT 1.1 pp on a 2.4% base ≈ 46% increase.

anderson_index <- function(d, items) {
  items <- items[items %in% names(d)]
  X <- sapply(items, function(v) to_num_item(d[[v]]))
  X <- as.matrix(X)
  ctrl <- d$Z == 0 & !is.na(d$Z)
  Zs <- X
  for (j in seq_len(ncol(X))) {
    mu <- mean(X[ctrl, j], na.rm = TRUE)
    s <- sd(X[ctrl, j], na.rm = TRUE)
    Zs[, j] <- (X[, j] - mu) / s
  }
  keep <- apply(Zs, 2, function(z) any(is.finite(z)))
  Zs <- Zs[, keep, drop = FALSE]
  S <- cov(Zs, use = "pairwise.complete.obs")
  Sinv <- tryCatch(solve(S), error = function(e) MASS::ginv(S))
  wts <- rowSums(Sinv)
  idx <- apply(Zs, 1, function(z) {
    ok <- is.finite(z)
    if (!any(ok)) return(NA_real_)
    sum(wts[ok] * z[ok]) / sum(wts[ok])
  })
  mu <- mean(idx[ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
  s <- sd(idx[ctrl], na.rm = TRUE)
  (idx - mu) / s
}
