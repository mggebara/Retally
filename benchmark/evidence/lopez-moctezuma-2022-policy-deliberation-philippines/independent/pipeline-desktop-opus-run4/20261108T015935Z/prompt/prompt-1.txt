## Paper Information
Title: Policy Deliberation and Voter Persuasion:
Abstract (first 500 chars): In a randomized experiment in cooperation with two national parties competing in a congressional election
in the Philippines, we estimate the causal effect on voting behavior of a town-hall style campaign in which candidates
discuss their campaign platform with small groups of citizens. Keeping the parties’ platform fixed, we find that town-hall
meetings have a positive effect on parties’ vote shares compared to the status quo, in which voters play a passive role.

Methodology Summary: - **Design / sample**: Field experiment in the May 13, 2013 Philippine party-list election with two party lists, Akbayan (feminist platform) and Umalab Ka (pro-urban-poor platform). Two-stage cluster sampling picked 7 of 17 municipalities in NCR and 6 of 90 in Calabarzon (13 municipalities). Within each municipality, 3 barangays were drawn: 1 assigned to treatment (two or three town-hall meetings of about 40 people each, held April 21 to May 9) and 2 to control ("business-as-usual" one-way campaigning). A barangay was resampled whenever two picks were less than 1.5 km apart. Municipalities were then randomly assigned to a party list. This gives 39 barangays: 18 Akbayan and 21 Umalab Ka. There was non-compliance in 3 assigned-treatment barangays: Concepcion Dos (Marikina) and Isla (Valenzuela) were swapped with control units, and San Roque (Umalab Ka) was replaced by San Diego and San Antonio.
- **Barangay-level estimating equation (Eq. 1, ITT)**: Y_jk = δ_k + β·Z_jk + ε_jk, estimated by OLS. Z_jk is the original random-assignment dummy and δ_k are municipality (city) fixed effects. There are no other controls. Outcomes come from official COMELEC precinct returns aggregated to the barangay, in percentage points (levels, not logs, not standardised):
  - party-list turnout as a % of registered voters;
  - party's vote share as a % of total party-list votes, pooled ("Vote (overall)") and separately for Akbayan and Umalab Ka.
- **CACE (Eq. 2)**: IV/2SLS of Y_jk on the implemented-treatment dummy T_jk, with municipality FE and T instrumented by Z. Compliers exclude Concepcion Dos, Isla and San Roque.
- **Barangay-level inference**: nonparametric permutation (randomization-inference) test that re-permutes treatment within municipality and gives p-values for the ITT. Table 1 reports ITT, CACE, control mean, City FE, N and R². Headline results:
  - turnout: ITT −4.544 (p = .404), CACE −5.908;
  - pooled vote share: ITT 1.096 (p = .048), CACE 1.425; control mean 2.397; N = 39; R² = .776;
  - Akbayan: ITT 1.955 (p = .087), CACE 2.606; N = 18;
  - Umalab Ka: ITT 0.360 (p = .141), CACE 0.459; N = 21.
- **Barangay-level heterogeneity (Figure 2)**: (a) a linear interaction of assignment × number of registered voters, with municipality FE (marginal ITT on vote share); (b) vote share regressed on the proportion of meeting attendees (from attendance sheets), with municipality FE and town-hall assignment as the instrument for attendance.
- **Individual-level data and preprocessing**:
  - Post-election survey by CPE, run two weeks after the election using a random walk (every 5th household, starting at the town hall or meeting site) with a quota of 40 per barangay. 1,314 respondents.
  - Municipalities without both treatment and control samples (Imus, Pateros, Santa Maria) are dropped. Respondents without a valid turnout response are dropped, leaving an effective N = 1,082.
  - Inverse-probability weights correct for oversampling of meeting attendees. In treatment barangays the weight uses the ratio of self-reported attendees in the survey to actual attendees on the sign-in sheets.
  - Robustness checks: multiple imputation (SI App. E), unweighted results with permutation p-values (SI App. F), and added pretreatment covariates (SI App. D).
- **Individual-level specifications**: linear probability models with municipality (city) FE and standard errors clustered at the barangay level (the level of assignment).
  - Eq. 3 (ITT): Y_ijk = δ_k + β·Z_jk + ε.
  - Eq. 4 (CACE): IV with T_jk instrumented by Z_jk.
  - Outcomes are binary turnout and a binary vote for the campaigning party (pooled, Akbayan, Umalab Ka), shown in Figure 3 and SI Table C.2.
  - Eq. 5 (heterogeneity): Y = γ_k + β1·Z + β2·X + β3·(Z×X) + ξ. X is income (cut at 10,000 pesos), education (primary or less vs high school or more) or female gender. A matching IV version gives the differential CACE (Figure 4).
- **Mechanisms (Figure 5, SI Table D.3)**:
  - Attitude outcomes are oriented so that positive means more agreement, then standardised by the control-group mean and s.d.
  - They are combined into Anderson (2008) inverse-covariance-weighted summary indices. The gender index uses 6 items (Female Rep., Female Pol., Equality, Discrimination, Harassment). The poverty index uses 4 items (Poverty, GAP, CCT, Corruption).
  - Indices are estimated separately for Akbayan and Umalab Ka municipalities, by ITT and by CACE, with city FE, barangay-clustered SEs and 95% CIs.
  - Results are in s.d. units: Akbayan gender index ITT ≈ 0.45 and CACE ≈ 0.67; Umalab Ka poverty index ITT ≈ 0.13.
