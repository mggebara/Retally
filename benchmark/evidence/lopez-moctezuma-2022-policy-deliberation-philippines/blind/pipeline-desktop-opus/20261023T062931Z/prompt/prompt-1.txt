Title: Policy Deliberation and Voter Persuasion:
Abstract (first 500 chars): In a randomized experiment in cooperation with two national parties competing in a congressional election
in the Philippines, we estimate the causal effect on voting behavior of a town-hall style campaign in which candidates
discuss their campaign platform with small groups of citizens. Keeping the parties’ platform fixed, we find that town-hall

Methodology Summary: - **Design / sample**: Clustered randomized field experiment around the May 13, 2013 Philippine party-list election, with two party lists (Akbayan, feminist platform; Umalab Ka, urban-poor platform). Two-stage cluster sampling: 7 of 17 NCR municipalities and 6 of 90 Calabarzon municipalities (13 in total). In each municipality 3 barangays were drawn (1 treatment, 2 control), and any barangay within 1.5 km of another selected one was replaced. Municipalities were then randomly assigned to a party list. The barangay sample is 39 (18 Akbayan, 21 Umalab Ka). Treatment was 2–3 town-hall meetings (~40 participants each, 90–120 min) held April 21–May 9. Controls got the "business-as-usual" campaign with no town-hall meetings. There was noncompliance in Marikina, Valenzuela and Louisiana: the assigned treatment barangays Concepcion Dos, Isla and San Roque were not treated.
- **Barangay-level estimation (Table 1)**: OLS ITT, Y_jk = δ_k + β Z_jk + ε_jk, with municipality (city) fixed effects and no other controls. Z is the random assignment dummy. The outcomes come from official COMELEC precinct results, in percentage points: party-list turnout (% of registered voters), and vote share (% of total party-list votes) pooled ("overall", N=39) and separately for Akbayan (N=18) and Umalab Ka (N=21). The CACE comes from IV/2SLS, Y_jk = λ_k + α T_jk + υ_jk, where actual implementation T is instrumented with assignment Z, plus municipality FE. Headline coefficients are unstandardised.
- **Barangay-level inference**: nonparametric randomization/permutation tests. The null distribution of no effect is built from within-municipality permutations of treatment assignment, and permutation p-values are reported (no conventional SEs).
- **Barangay heterogeneity (Figure 2)**: (a) linear interaction of assignment with the number of registered voters, plus municipality FE (marginal ITT on pooled vote share). (b) Vote share regressed on the proportion of town-hall attendees (from attendance sheets), with assignment as the instrument, plus municipality FE.
- **Individual-level data**: postelection survey run by CPE two weeks after the election. It used a random walk from the barangay town hall or the meeting location, took every fifth household, and had a quota of 40 per barangay, for 1,314 respondents. Imus, Pateros and Santa Maria are excluded because they lack both treatment and control surveys. The effective sample is 1,082 after dropping respondents with no valid turnout answer. Results are reweighted by inverse probability weights to correct for oversampling of meeting attendees: in treatment barangays the weights use the ratio of self-reported survey attendees to actual attendees from attendance sheets.
- **Individual-level specifications**: linear probability models. ITT: Y_ijk = δ_k + β Z_jk + ε_ijk, with municipality FE. CACE: 2SLS with T_jk instrumented by Z_jk. Standard errors are clustered at the barangay level (the assignment level). The outcomes are self-reported turnout and vote for the party list, pooled and by party. Heterogeneity (Eq. 5) interacts Z with a pretreatment X: income (cut at 10,000 pesos), education (primary or less vs. high school or more), or female gender. It includes municipality FE, barangay-clustered SEs and a matching IV version for the differential CACE. Robustness checks: adding pretreatment covariates (SI App. D), multiple imputation (SI App. E), and unweighted estimates with two-stage cluster permutation p-values (SI App. F).
- **Attitude mechanisms (Figure 5)**: follows Anderson (2008). Each item is oriented so that positive means more agreement, then standardized by the control-group mean and s.d. Items are combined into inverse-covariance-weighted summary indices: a gender-discrimination index from 6 items (Female Rep., Female Pol., Equality, Discrimination, Harassment) and a poverty index from 4 items (Poverty, GAP, CCT, Corruption). ITT and CACE are estimated separately for Akbayan and Umalab Ka municipalities, with city FE and barangay-clustered SEs, and effects are reported in s.d. units. Balance on pretreatment official statistics and survey covariates is checked in SI Appendix B.
