Methodology Summary: - **Data and panel construction:** Monthly Current Population Survey (CPS) interviews from January 1994 to September 2016. Consecutive months are matched with the Madrian and Lefgren (1999) procedure: link on administrative IDs, then confirm the match with sex, race and age. The sample starts in 1994 because earlier questionnaires do not identify job-to-job moves. May–August 1995 are dropped because the longitudinal link ID is missing. Month pairs that span the eight-month break between the 4th and 5th interview months are also dropped. The sample keeps civilians over age 16 with non-missing age and education, about 16.9 million person-month observations (summary statistics in Table B.1). Every regression is weighted by the average of the two months' CPS sampling weights.
- **Key variables:** The outcome is D^hired, an indicator scaled to equal 100 if the person is hired in month t. Being hired means either (a) non-employed in t−1 and employed in t, or (b) employed in both months and reporting a change of firm since last month. Moves into self-employment do not count as hires. Potential experience (PE) = age − years of education − 6. "Young" means PE ≤ 10 years and "experienced" means PE > 10. The main figures use one-year PE bins. The business-cycle measure is the state monthly unemployment rate for the 50 states plus DC (51 per month).
- **Main estimating equation (Eq. 1):** A linear probability model, D^hired_ikst = α_s + δ_t + Σ_k (β_k·D^PE_k + γ_k·D^PE_k × StateUnempRate_st) + ε. It includes state fixed effects (α_s), month-year fixed effects (δ_t) and a main effect for each PE group. The state unemployment rate enters only through its interactions with every PE group; its own main effect is left out. Coefficients are in levels (percentage points of hiring per percentage point of unemployment) and are not standardised. The coefficient of interest is γ_k, and the null hypothesis is that γ_k is equal across PE groups.
- **Preferred specification and controls:** Table 1, Panel B, Column 4. It uses two PE groups (≤10 and >10 years) with state, month-year and non-parametric demographic fixed effects. The demographic effects are the full interaction of gender × race (white/non-white) × Hispanic × education (less than high school, high school, some college, BA, MA, professional degree, PhD). Columns 1–3 add the controls in steps: none, then state FE, then demographic FE. Panel A uses the pooled unemployment rate without the PE interactions. The preferred sample does not condition on labour-market status in t−1 and covers all working-age people. Figure 2 and Table 2 split the sample by status in t−1: employed, unemployed, or not in the labour force (NILF). Table 9 and Figure 3 split it by four-year college degree versus none.
- **Inference:** Standard errors are clustered by state (51 clusters) in all specifications. The paper tests whether γ differs between young and experienced workers.
- **Composition and other flows:** Eq. 2 regresses the potential experience of new hires on the state unemployment rate. It includes state, month-year and demographic fixed effects, and adds detailed occupation and industry fixed effects (508 occupations and 261 industries, crosswalked to consistent 2002 Census codes). It is weighted and clustered by state (Table 5; Tables B.4 and B.5 by major group). Table 6 regresses average PE at the state×month-year cell level, and the ratio of hires' average PE to the population's average PE, for all people and separately for employed, unemployed and NILF. Tables 3 and 4 use the same framework for other flows: exits, employment to unemployment (split into quits and layoffs or ended temporary jobs), job-to-job moves, and transitions between unemployment and NILF. Figure 1 applies an HP filter to annualised hiring rates.
- **Labour-supply checks:** Table 7 measures search intensity as the number of search methods an unemployed person reports (following Shimer 2004), with and without demographic controls. Table 8 re-estimates the Table 2 specifications on restricted samples: unemployed people available to start within a week, and NILF people not in school.
- **Wage test (Table 10):** The outcome is the non-allocated log weekly wage of new hires, deflated to 1994 dollars. Wages come only from outgoing-rotation months 4 and 8. The model includes state, month-year, demographic, and detailed industry and occupation fixed effects, with standard errors clustered by state. Panel A pools all hires and Panel B splits them into young and experienced. Column 1 uses all hires, and Columns 2–4 use hires from employment, unemployment and NILF. A separate stylised theoretical model (Stole–Zwiebel bargaining, endogenous firm size, a per-worker fixed cost k) generates the prediction being tested.
