Methodology Summary: - **Data and sample construction**: The paper builds a matched month-to-month panel from CPS monthly interviews, January 1994 to September 2016. Individuals are linked across consecutive months with the Madrian and Lefgren (1999) procedure: administrative IDs are matched and the match is confirmed on sex, race and age. The sample keeps only civilians over 16 with non-missing age and education, about 16.9 million observations. It excludes May–August 1995, when the longitudinal link ID is missing, and month pairs that span the 8-month break between the 4th and 5th months in sample. The sample starts in 1994 because firm-to-firm mobility cannot be seen before then.
- **Key variables**: The outcome D_hired equals 100 if the person is hired in month t and 0 otherwise. A hire is either (a) non-employed at t−1 and employed at t, or (b) employed at t−1 and reporting a change of employer at t. A move into self-employment does not count as a hire. Potential experience is age − years of education − 6. The business-cycle measure is the monthly state unemployment rate for 51 states (DC included), in percentage points. "Young" means 10 or fewer years of potential experience and "experienced" means more than 10.
- **Main estimating equation (Eq. 1)**: This is a linear probability model, D_hired_ikst = α_s + δ_t + Σ_k [β_k·D^PE_k + γ_k·D^PE_k × StateUnempRate_st] + ε. There is no separate main effect of the unemployment rate; instead every experience group gets its own interaction, and the γ_k are the coefficients of interest. The groups are either one-year potential-experience bins (Figure 2, with wider bins at high experience) or two groups, ≤10 vs >10 years (Tables 1 and 2). The null hypothesis is that all γ_k are equal.
- **Fixed effects and controls**: Table 1 builds up across columns: (1) no fixed effects, (2) state FE, (3) plus demographic FE, (4) plus month-year FE. Column (4), with state, demographic and month-year FE, is the preferred specification. The demographic FE are non-parametric: indicators for every combination of gender × race (white/non-white) × Hispanic × education. Education has 7 levels: less than high school, high school, some college, BA, MA, professional degree, PhD. The preferred sample includes everyone of working age, whatever their labor-force status at t−1. Other specifications restrict to people who were employed, unemployed or NILF at t−1 (Figure 2, Table 2). Table 9 and Figure 3 split the sample by college degree.
- **Inference and weighting**: Standard errors are clustered by state. Every regression is weighted by the average of the CPS sampling weights across the two linked months. Figure 1 also shows descriptive time series, with an HP filter applied to annualized hiring rates.
- **Other flow regressions**: The same framework is used for other flows. Table 3 covers exits from employment, E→U, job-to-job moves, and U↔NILF flows. Table 4 splits separations into voluntary quits and involuntary separations (layoffs and temporary jobs that ended).
- **Composition test (Eq. 2)**: The outcome is the potential experience of new hires, regressed on the state unemployment rate with state, month-year and demographic FE. A second version adds detailed occupation and industry FE: 508 occupations and 261 industries, crosswalked to consistent 2002 Census codes. These regressions are weighted and clustered by state (Table 5; Tables B.4 and B.5 by major group).
- **Labor-supply tests**:
  - Table 6 uses state-by-month-year cells. It regresses three outcomes on the unemployment rate: the average potential experience of the working-age population, the average potential experience of hires, and the ratio of the two. It does this for everyone and separately for employed, unemployed and NILF.
  - Table 7 regresses search intensity (the number of search methods used, following Shimer 2004) on the unemployment rate for unemployed people only, with and without demographic FE.
  - Table 8 re-runs Table 2 with availability restrictions: unemployed people who say they could start work within a week, and NILF people who are not in school.
- **Wage test (Table 10)**: The outcome is non-allocated log weekly wages of new hires, deflated to 1994 prices, taken from the 4th and 8th months in sample (the outgoing-rotation months). The model includes state, month-year, detailed occupation, detailed industry and demographic FE, with standard errors clustered by state. Panel A pools all hires; Panel B splits young (≤10 years) and experienced. Column 1 uses all hires; columns 2–4 use hires from employment, unemployment and NILF.
