## Paper
Title: Why Don’t Firms Hire Young Workers During
Methodology: - **Data and sample:** Matched monthly CPS panel, January 1994 to September 2016. Consecutive months are linked with the Madrian and Lefgren (1999) method: administrative IDs, with matches confirmed on sex, race and age. The sample keeps civilians over 16 with non-missing age and education. Excluded: May–August 1995 (no longitudinal link ID) and month pairs that span the 8-month sampling break between months-in-sample 4 and 5. The result is about 16.9 million person-month observations (Table B.1). There is no train/test split or cross-validation; this is observational panel regression.
- **Variables:** The outcome D^hired is 100 if the worker is hired in month t, otherwise 0, so coefficients are in percentage points. A hire is either non-employed at t−1 and employed at t, or employed at both and reporting a change of firm since last month. Moves into self-employment are not counted as hires. The key regressor is the monthly state unemployment rate (51 units including DC), in percentage points. Potential experience = age − years of education − 6. It enters as one-year bins (Figure 2) or as two groups: young (≤10 years) and experienced (>10 years) (Tables 1, 2).
- **Main specification (Eq. 1):** A linear probability model, D^hired_ikst = α_s + δ_t + Σ_k [β_k D^PE_k + γ_k (D^PE_k × StateUnempRate_st)] + ε. The main effect of the unemployment rate is omitted and every experience-group interaction is included. γ_k is the cyclical hiring response for group k. The null hypothesis is equal γ across groups. Coefficients are unstandardised percentage points of hiring per one percentage point of state unemployment.
- **Fixed effects and controls:** Table 1 adds fixed effects step by step. Column (1) has none. Column (2) adds state fixed effects. Column (3) adds non-parametric demographic fixed effects: full interactions of gender × race (white/non-white) × Hispanic × education in 7 categories (below high school, high school, some college, BA, MA, professional degree, PhD). Column (4), the preferred specification, adds month-year fixed effects as well. That gives young γ ≈ −0.25 (no-controls Column (1): −0.34 on a mean of 7.7%). Experienced γ turns small but positive and significant (no-controls Column (1): −0.04 on a mean of 2.5%). Panel A pools all workers (−0.1 pp on a 3.2% base, no fixed effects).
- **Inference and weights:** Standard errors are clustered by state. Every regression is weighted by the average of the CPS sampling weights across the two matched months.
- **Sample variants and robustness:** The preferred sample is all working-age individuals, whatever their status at t−1. Table 2 / Figure 2 split it by origin at t−1 (employed, unemployed, NILF). Table 3 covers other flows: exits from employment, employment-to-unemployment, job-to-job, and unemployment↔NILF. Table 4 splits separations into voluntary (quits) and involuntary. Table 9 / Figure 3 compare those with and without a college degree. Table 8 restricts to the available unemployed (sample about 14% smaller) and to NILF people not in school (about 20% smaller). Table 7 covers search intensity: the number of search methods used, following Shimer (2004), unemployed only.
- **Job-composition test (Eq. 2, Table 5):** Among new hires, potential experience is regressed on the state unemployment rate. Fixed effects are state, month-year and demographics, then 508 occupation and 261 industry codes (crosswalked to consistent 2002 Census codes). The estimate is about +1.5 months of experience per pp of unemployment and barely moves when occupation and industry fixed effects are added. SEs are clustered by state and weighted. Table 6 runs state-month-year cell regressions on average potential experience (population, hires, and the ratio of hires to population) by labor-force status.
- **Wage test (Table 10):** Outgoing-rotation months (MIS 4 and 8) only, new hires only. The outcome is non-allocated log weekly wages deflated to 1994 prices. Fixed effects are state, month-year, detailed industry, occupation and demographics, with SEs clustered by state. Panel A pools all hires: about −1.7% per pp of unemployment (from employment −0.8%, from unemployment −1.7%, from NILF −2.4%). Panel B splits young and experienced: −2.6% for young versus −1.2% for experienced. Figure 1 shows the descriptive series with an HP filter on annualized hiring rates.
