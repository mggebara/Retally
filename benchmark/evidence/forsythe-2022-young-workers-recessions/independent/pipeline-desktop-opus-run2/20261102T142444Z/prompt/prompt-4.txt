Methodology: - **Data and sample construction:** Monthly Current Population Survey (CPS) interviews from January 1994 to September 2016, linked into adjacent-month pairs with the Madrian and Lefgren (1999) method (administrative IDs, with matches confirmed on sex, race and age). The sample is civilians over 16 with non-missing age and education, about 16.9 million observations. Dropped: May–August 1995 (no longitudinal link ID) and month pairs that span the 8-month sampling break between months 4 and 5 in sample. The sample starts in 1994 because the earlier questionnaire could not identify employer-to-employer moves. Summary statistics are in Table B.1.
- **Outcome and key variables:** D^hired is an indicator scaled to **100** (so coefficients are in percentage points) for being hired in month t. A hire is either (a) non-employed at t−1 and employed at t, or (b) employed at t−1 and reporting a change of employer at t. A new job classified as self-employment does not count as a hire. Potential experience (PE) = age − years of education − 6. "Young" means PE ≤ 10 years and "experienced" means PE > 10. The cyclical measure is the state monthly unemployment rate (51 units, including DC), in percentage points. Coefficients are unstandardised.
- **Main estimating equation (Eq. 1), a linear probability model estimated by weighted OLS:** D^hired_ikst = α_s + δ_t + Σ_k (β_k·D^PE_k + γ_k·D^PE_k × StateUnempRate_st) + ε_ikst. It includes state FE (α_s), month-year FE (δ_t), PE-group main effects, and PE-group × state-unemployment interactions. The main effect of the unemployment rate is omitted, so every group gets its own slope γ_k. The null hypothesis is that the γ_k are equal across groups. Table 1 Panel B / Table 2 use two groups (≤10 vs >10 years). Figure 2 uses one-year PE bins (with 5-year bins at higher PE). Table 1 Panel A pools everyone into a single slope.
- **Controls and fixed effects in the preferred specification (Table 1, Col. 4):** state FE, month-year FE and non-parametric demographic FE. The demographic FE are full interactions of gender × race (white/non-white) × Hispanic × education (7 categories: less than high school, high school, some college, BA, MA, professional, PhD). Columns 1–3 add the FE in steps: none; then state; then state + demographic. The preferred sample does not condition on labor-force status at t−1. Other specifications split by origin state at t−1 (employed, unemployed, NILF; Table 2 and Figure 2 panels) and by college degree (Table 9, Figure 3).
- **Inference and weights:** standard errors are clustered by state (51 clusters). All regressions are weighted by the average of the CPS sampling weights across the two linked months. There is no train/test split or cross-validation, since this is a regression-based causal/descriptive design. The evaluation is coefficient estimates and significance tests of differences across experience groups. Headline result: young hiring rate −0.34 pp per 1 pp of unemployment with no FE (mean 7.7%) and about −0.25 pp in the preferred Col. 4. The experienced coefficient is small and turns positive and significant once month-year FE are included. A 5 pp increase in unemployment lowers young hiring by 1.7 pp, versus at most 0.18 pp for experienced workers (mean 2.6%). Figure 1 also shows HP-filtered annual hiring rates as a descriptive check.
- **Other flows and labor-supply tests (same Eq. 1 structure):** Table 3 covers exits and flows (E→U, E→E, U↔NILF). Table 4 splits voluntary (quit) from involuntary separations. Table 7 covers search intensity, measured as the number of search methods (Shimer 2004 measure), for the unemployed only, with and without demographic FE. Table 8 is availability: it reruns Table 2 restricted to unemployed workers available to start next week (sample about 14% smaller) and to NILF workers not in school (about 20% smaller).
- **Composition test (Eq. 2, Tables 5–6):** the dependent variable is the PE (years) of new hires, regressed on the state unemployment rate with state, month-year and demographic FE. Column 2 adds occupation FE (508) and industry FE (261), crosswalked to consistent 2002 Census codes. SEs are clustered by state and the regressions are weighted. Headline: about +1.5 months of PE per pp of unemployment, little changed by the occupation/industry FE. Table 6 regresses cell-level average PE (state × month-year) for the population, for hires, and for the hire/population ratio, overall and by employed/unemployed/NILF.
- **Wage test (Table 10):** new hires only, from outgoing-rotation months 4 and 8 (about one-third of the sample). Controls are state, month-year, demographic, detailed industry and detailed occupation FE. SEs are clustered by state. Columns: all hires, then hires from E, U and NILF. Headline: −1.7% per pp overall; young −2.6% versus experienced −1.2%.
