Title: Why Don’t Firms Hire Young Workers During
Methodology: - **Data and sample:** Matched monthly CPS panel, January 1994 to September 2016. Consecutive months are linked using the Madrian & Lefgren (1999) procedure: administrative IDs, with matches confirmed on sex, race and age. The sample keeps civilians over 16 with non-missing age and education, about 16.9M observations. Dropped: May–Aug 1995 (no longitudinal link ID) and month pairs that span the 8-month sampling break between interview months 4 and 5. Pre-1994 data are excluded because firm-to-firm mobility cannot be observed. Summary statistics are in Table B.1.
- **Key variables:**
  - Potential experience (PE) = age − years of education − 6. "Young" means PE ≤ 10; "experienced" means PE > 10.
  - Outcome D^hired = 100 if the person is hired in month t, otherwise 0. A hire is either (a) non-employed at t−1 and employed at t, or (b) employed at t−1 and reporting a change of employer at t. New jobs that are self-employment do not count as hires.
  - The cyclical measure is the monthly state unemployment rate for 51 units (including DC). It enters in percentage points.
- **Main estimating equation (Eq. 1):** A linear probability model, in levels, of D^hired_ikst on state FE α_s, month-year FE δ_t, PE-group indicators D^PE_k, and the interactions D^PE_k × State Unemp. Rate_st. The main effect of the unemployment rate is left out, so each group k gets its own γ_k: the change in the hiring rate (in pp) per 1 pp of state unemployment. The null hypothesis is that the γ_k are equal across groups.
  - Coefficients are unstandardised.
  - The main figures (Fig. 2) use one-year PE bins.
  - Table 1 Panel B and Table 2 use two groups (≤10 and >10).
- **Fixed effects and controls:**
  - Table 1 adds controls column by column: (1) none; (2) state FE; (3) plus demographic FE; (4) plus month-year FE, the preferred specification.
  - The demographic FE are non-parametric cells formed by fully interacting gender × race (white/non-white) × Hispanic × education (7 categories: <HS, HS, some college, BA, MA, professional, PhD).
  - In the preferred sample, hires are not restricted by labor-market status at t−1, so the base is all working-age people. Tables 2 and 8 and Fig. 2 also split by origin: employed, unemployed, NILF.
  - Headline result (Table 1, col. 4): about −0.25 pp per pp of unemployment for young workers, a small positive coefficient for experienced workers. Mean hiring rates are 7.7% (young) and 2.5–2.6% (experienced).
- **Inference and weighting:** Standard errors are clustered by state (51 clusters). All regressions are weighted by the average of the CPS sampling weights across the two matched months.
- **Composition and labor-supply checks:**
  - Eq. 2 regresses the potential experience of new hires on the state unemployment rate with state, month-year and demographic FE, adding 508 occupation and 261 industry FE (2002 Census codes, using Census crosswalks) in Table 5. Tables B.4 and B.5 split this by major occupation and by industry.
  - Table 6: state×month cell averages of PE for hires, for the working-age population, and the ratio of the two. Each is shown overall and by employed/unemployed/NILF.
  - Table 7: search intensity among the unemployed, measured as the number of search methods used (Shimer 2004).
  - Table 8 re-runs Table 2 on two restricted samples: unemployed people available to start work (about 14% of the sample dropped) and NILF people not in school (about 20% dropped).
  - Tables 3–4 cover other flows: exits, E→U, E→E, U↔NILF, and voluntary vs involuntary separations.
  - Table 9 and Fig. 3 split the sample by college degree.
- **Wage test (Table 10):** Log real weekly wages of new hires, non-allocated only and deflated to 1994 dollars, from outgoing-rotation months 4 and 8 only (about 2/3 of the sample lost).
  - The regressors are the state unemployment rate alone (Panel A) or interacted with the young/experienced split (Panel B).
  - Controls are state FE, month-year FE, detailed industry and occupation FE, and demographic FE. Standard errors are clustered by state.
  - Columns: (1) all hires, (2) hires from employment, (3) from unemployment, (4) from NILF.
  - Estimates: −1.7% overall; −2.6% for young vs −1.2% for experienced.
- **No ML protocol:** There are no train/test splits, cross-validation or hyperparameters. Figure 1 HP-filters annualized hiring rates for descriptive purposes only. Section 4 is a theoretical model (Stole–Zwiebel bargaining, endogenous firm size). It is not estimated.
