## 1. Summary

We built a benchmark that asks a language-model system to reproduce the headline numbers of published economics and political-science papers, scored against the papers' printed values and against the values that human replicators at the Institute for Replication (I4R) obtained.  Each paper is attempted in three modes: *seeded* (the authors' replication scripts are provided), *independent* (scripts withheld, target values shown) and *blind* (scripts and target values withheld).  Two systems sat the benchmark: a fixed pipeline that writes one R script and refines it, and a Claude Code agent with its own loop.  On twenty-four evaluation papers (290 pinned claims) the agent reproduced 289, 280 and 264 claims within tolerance of the paper's printed value in seeded, independent and blind mode; the pipeline, run on the same model family, reproduced 275, 251 and 179.  Those are first samples, scored, like every count in this report, with sample-size claims at a 5 % band (Section 3; ROADMAP 3.7.4).  Second samples of both systems exist on all twenty-four papers: the agent's second reproduced 278, 283 and 263, its seeded count 11 lower because one cell (Holman) crashed after its error repairs ran out, and the pipeline's second 277, 238 and 182; the pipeline's third reproduced 266, 233 and 193.  The twenty-fourth paper (Molina-Garzón et al., AJPS 2022, 12 claims, ROADMAP 3.9) was the first admitted under a looser corpus rule; the agent reproduced all twelve claims in every sample and mode, and the pipeline missed the same six claims in three of its nine cells (Section 4.8).  On the twenty-three papers measured before it (278 claims) the first samples are 277, 268 and 252 for the agent and 263, 239 and 173 for the pipeline.  On the seventeen papers measured first (201 claims) the agent reproduced 200, 195 and 182, then 189, 195 and 180, and the pipeline 194, 172 and 121, then 195, 164 and 124.  On the seven papers where an older model was also run, moving the pipeline from `claude-opus-4-7` to Opus 5.5 raised its blind count from 29 to 52 of 79, against the agent's 75.  The finding: with the authors' code in hand both systems are near the ceiling, and the loss when the code and the targets are withheld is mostly a property of the harness and of what the papers' methods sections leave unstated.  Over the twenty-four papers the first-sample gap is 14 claims seeded, 29 independent and 85 blind (14, 29 and 79 on the twenty-three); on the six papers of ROADMAP 3.7 it is 8, 6 and 18, the seeded figure driven by one pipeline cell (Section 4.3), and on the twenty-fourth 0, 0 and 6.  The agent also has a third sample in independent and blind mode, 277 and 262.  Pairing each of the agent's three samples with each of the pipeline's three, the gap over all twenty-four papers is 26 to 50 claims in independent mode and 69 to 85 in blind mode (26 to 45 and 69 to 79 on the twenty-three, 20 to 31 and 51 to 61 on the seventeen measured first, 11 to 19 and 35 to 51 on the thirteen); in seeded mode, with two agent samples against three pipeline samples, it is 1 to 23 claims on both the twenty-four and the twenty-three (−6 to 17 on the seventeen): 12 to 23 with the agent's first seeded sample and 1 to 12 with its second, whose Holman cell crashed; the top end is the pipeline's third seeded sample, whose Forsythe cell printed no results (ROADMAP 3.7.4), and Douenne seeded, the inverted pipeline cell of Section 4.3, accounts for more than half of the 12 to 14 that the first two pipeline samples give against the agent's first.  A memorization probe, asking the same model for each number from memory with no data, landed 44 of the 278 claims of the twenty-three papers it was run on inside the blind tolerance, none by stated recall, so memory does not explain the agent's blind result (it was not run on the twenty-fourth).  The limitations: on all twenty-four papers the pipeline on Opus 5.5 has three samples per cell in every mode and the agent three in independent and blind mode and two seeded, and the older pipeline one; all twenty-four papers predate the models' training cutoffs; two or three samples show how much a cell moves but do not give a standard error, so none of these differences carries a measured uncertainty.

## 2. Introduction

A replication package is a set of data files and the scripts that turn them into the paper's tables.  Re-running the scripts and checking that the printed numbers come out is *computational reproduction*.  It confirms that the package is complete and that the code does what the tables say, but the specification (which sample, which controls, which estimator, which standard errors) travels inside the scripts.  A replicator who rebuilds the analysis from the paper's text and the raw data, without the authors' code, performs a different test: whether the paper describes its own analysis well enough to be redone.  We call that *independent reproduction*.  A third variant withholds the printed results as well, so that the replicator cannot steer toward the known answer.  We call that *blind reproduction*.

The three are routinely reported under one word.  "The model reproduced the paper" can mean that it translated a Stata do-file into R, that it rebuilt the regression from the methods section while knowing the target coefficient, or that it derived the coefficient without ever seeing it.  These are different claims about the model and different claims about the paper.  This benchmark keeps them apart: every result row records its mode, and no number in this report pools two modes.

The ground truth comes from the Institute for Replication (I4R), which organizes replication games in which teams of economists and political scientists reproduce recently published papers and file reports.  I4R's first meta-database covers 110 replication reports on 109 papers from leading economics and political-science journals, with estimate-level rows that record both the paper's coefficient and the replicator's (`benchmark/corpus/SOURCE.md`).  It is the only source we know of that gives, for each paper, a human replicator's number to set beside the published one.  That matters for two of our papers, where the replicators found that the published numbers are wrong.

Two systems were evaluated.  The *pipeline* is a fixed program: it extracts the paper's text and a methodology summary, writes one R script with a language model, runs it in a sandbox, repairs it when it crashes (an *error refinement*), and, when the target values are shown, tells the model which claims are off and by how much and asks for a corrected script (a *claim refinement*), with at most three of each.  The *agent* is a Claude Code session given the same task text, the same data mount and the same sandbox, and left to run its own loop: it can inspect the data, run exploratory scripts, read the whole paper and try alternatives.  Both are scored by the same harness from the numbers their final script prints.  The question the comparison answers is how much of what looks like a model's reproduction ability is the loop around it.

### 3.2 Pinned claims

A *pinned claim* is one published number that the benchmark scores: a coefficient, a standard error, a test statistic or a sample size, stored with its printed value, its standard error where the paper gives one, a location ("Table 1, column 2") and a short description of what it is.  The curation rule (`benchmark/README.md`, "Curating headline claims") admits a number only if it is in the abstract or a main results table, the paper's conclusions rest on it, and it is stated numerically; the target is eight to twelve claims per paper.  The evaluation papers carry 290 pinned claims (154 on the thirteen measured first, 201 on the first seventeen, 278 on the first twenty-three), the dev papers 35.  One exception to the main-table rule is recorded: Bokobza's main text has only coefficient plots, so its claims come from the online-appendix tables that print them.

Each claim also has a *context* string, written in the paper's own words, which every mode shows to the reproducer.  Two curation rules came out of leaks found during the project.  Contexts may not name package variables, file names or commands (ROADMAP 1.7, where the first contexts had been written from the package), and no context may state the value of any pinned claim of its entry, which a catalog test now enforces (ROADMAP 3.4, PR #69).

### 3.3 Three modes

The modes differ only in what the reproducer is shown.

| Mode | Authors' scripts | Script-free data copy | Claim values and SEs | Claim refinement loop | What a pass measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| seeded | shown, as ground truth | no (full package) | shown | on | translating the authors' code |
| independent | withheld | yes | shown | on | rebuilding the analysis from the text, knowing the targets |
| blind | withheld | yes | withheld (also redacted from the paper text) | off | deriving the numbers without seeing them |

In independent and blind mode the data directory is a *script-free copy*: scripts are removed by file name, and logs, transcripts, code saved under other names and generated regression tables are removed by content (`benchmark/README.md`, "The script-free copy").  Blind mode additionally redacts every pinned value and standard error from the paper text, including percentage forms such as "3.5 points" for 0.035.  A blind pass is therefore the only count free of *target-guided search*, by which we mean changing the analysis until the printed number appears.

### 3.4 Tolerance and scoring

A claim *passes* when the reproduced value is within tolerance of the paper's printed value.  We report these as counts of claims, not as rates.  Seeded mode uses a strict relative band per claim type (2 % for coefficients, 5 % for standard errors and for counts such as observations, clusters and respondents, 1 % for other quantities such as F statistics; `benchmark/README.md` and `src/retally/validation/comparator.py`), because translating the authors' code should land on their numbers.  Counts had the 1 % band until 2026-11-03, when 62 sample-size claims were relabelled `count` and every stored results file was rescored offline from its stored values; first-scored counts were left as recorded, because the first-scored value is not stored (ROADMAP 3.7.4).  Independent and blind mode accept a value within one standard error of the printed value when the claim pins a standard error, never narrower than the relative band, and use the relative band otherwise; rebuilding a specification from prose is not expected to hit the fourth decimal.  In every mode the band is never narrower than half a unit in the printed value's last decimal place, so a reproduction that rounds to the printed number always passes.  This floor matters for papers that print coefficients to one significant digit.

The wider independent band is a real asymmetry.  A pass count in independent mode is not comparable with one in seeded mode on closeness, so we report median relative error and the share of claims within one, two and five times their tolerance beside every count (Section 4.6).

Scoring belongs to the harness, not the reproducer.  The reproducer returns raw `name = value` pairs printed by its final script; the harness matches them to the pinned claims and applies the mode's tolerance.  Three verdicts are recorded per paper and never collapsed into one: `retally_ran` (the reproducer finished), `agrees_with_paper` (every pinned claim within tolerance of the printed value) and `agrees_with_i4r` (every claim for which I4R recorded a value, and for which the reproducer printed a value, within tolerance of the *replicator's* value).  A reproduction that matches the paper but not the replicators is the case the benchmark exists to surface.

Each row also records a *first-scored* count: the claims passed by the first execution that printed a parsable result, after any crash repairs and before any claim-targeted feedback.  The difference between first scored and final is what target-guided refinement added.

### 3.5 The `Reproducer` protocol and the two reproducers

The harness talks to a reproducer through one method, `reproduce(paper_source, language, dataset_reference, claims, reproduction_mode)`, which returns a `Reproduction`: the raw outputs keyed by claim name, the generated code, its output and exit code, declared deviations and per-stage flags, and no verdicts (`benchmark/reproducer.py`).  The pipeline is the reference implementation.  The agent is plugged in through a file-system reproducer: a rendering step writes a task directory (the pipeline generator's own claims block, dataset listing and mode instructions, the paper text, and the mode's data mount), a Claude Code subagent does the task and writes its scripts, outputs and a manifest, and the harness scores the files (ROADMAP 3.3).  The harness never drives the agent and never calls the API for it.

The agent's isolation is partly by instruction.  Its task directory holds only the mode's mount, and the sandbox mounts nothing else, but the session runs inside a checkout of this repository, where the catalog (with pinned values) and the download cache (with the authors' scripts) are readable.  Each agent had to list every file it read outside its mount; across the runs reported here the lists were empty or named only the task's own files (ROADMAP 3.3).

### 3.6 Models

Three model configurations appear, and the report never pools them.  The pipeline's original rows ran on `claude-opus-4-7` with no extended thinking (ROADMAP 3.5).  The pipeline was then moved to `claude-opus-5-5` through the API, with effort `high` for code generation and refinement and `medium` for claim extraction (`src/retally/llm.py`); credit allowed four of the seven earlier evaluation papers.  The agent rows, and the oracle rows below, ran on the Claude desktop app's Opus model at its default effort; the agents reported it as Opus 5.5.

### 3.7 The LLM oracle

The *oracle* runs the pipeline with every Claude call answered out of band rather than by the API (`src/retally/llm_oracle.py`, ROADMAP 3.5).  The pipeline's requests are built exactly as for a live run; each unanswered one is written to disk, a Claude Code subagent told to use no tools and read nothing but the prompt writes the answer, the answer is installed, and the run resumes.  It guarantees that the prompts, the loop and the scoring are the pipeline's own, that oracle answers live in a separate cache and can never be served to a real API run, and that oracle rows carry their own label.  It does not guarantee that the answerer thinks at the effort the request names (the subagents run at the desktop default), and it does not enforce isolation: two of 88 answering subagents reported running a harmless shell command on their own answer file, and none reported reading anything outside the prompt (ROADMAP 3.5).  The oracle let us run the remaining nine evaluation papers, and later the four of ROADMAP 3.6 and the six of ROADMAP 3.7, through the pipeline at no API cost.

### 3.8 Sandbox and cost accounting

Every script, from either reproducer, runs in the same Docker image (`retally-sandbox-r`, 47 R packages plus base; ROADMAP 1.8) with no network, a read-only file system and the pipeline executor's limits of 300 seconds, 4 GB and one CPU (ROADMAP 3.3).  Pipeline rows record the tokens each API call used, by model, and an estimated cost from one price table (`benchmark/pricing.py`).  Agent and oracle rows use plan usage, which the results files do not price.

### 4.1 The pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7`

On the seven evaluation papers measured first, the pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7` reproduced 55 of 79 claims seeded, 47 independent and 29 blind.  Its first-scored counts were 46, 27 and 24 (first-scored counts were not rescored when the count band widened, ROADMAP 3.7.4).  Seeded was not a ceiling.  Grier failed in every mode, including seeded, because the model estimated a conditional logit instead of the paper's logit with fixed-effect dummies, and Arias passed only its four counts seeded because the model re-estimated the conjoint effects with a linear model instead of translating the package's `cjoint::amce` call (ROADMAP 3.2).  Montero is the clearest single case.  I4R found duplicated rows behind the paper's Table 5 earnings results.  Seeded, the first script matched neither the paper nor the replicators; one claim-refinement round, told the gaps to the printed values, then reproduced the paper's printed numbers exactly.  Target-guided search converged on numbers the replicators had shown to be wrong (ROADMAP 3.2).

### 4.2 The agent on twenty-four papers

Over all twenty-four evaluation papers the agent reproduced 289, 280 and 264 of 290 claims seeded, independent and blind, with first-scored counts of 279, 264 and 255.  On the twenty-three papers of ROADMAP 3.2–3.7 it reproduced 277, 268 and 252 of 278 (first scored 267, 252 and 243), and on the twenty-fourth, Molina-Garzón (ROADMAP 3.9), all twelve in every mode, first scored.  On the seventeen papers of ROADMAP 3.2–3.6 it reproduced 200, 195 and 182 of 201 (first scored 194, 192 and 173), on the thirteen measured first 153, 149 and 136 of 154, on the six added last 77, 73 and 70 of 77, and on the seven papers above 78, 78 and 75 of 79.  Its misses are few and concentrated: in independent mode ten claims on four papers, in blind mode twenty-six on eight (Section 5).  On the four papers of ROADMAP 3.6 the two misses are both Forsythe's; on the six of ROADMAP 3.7 ten of its eleven misses are Labandeira's, its lowest independent and blind shares on any paper (8 and 6 of 12), and the agent matched every claim of Mokyr, Hollyer, Hanushek and Dancygier in every mode (ROADMAP 3.7).  Two caveats belong next to these numbers.  First, the agent's first execution is usually a data-inspection run that prints nothing, so its *first-attempt* count is meaningless; first scored is the comparable figure.  Second, its error and claim refinement counts are its own bookkeeping, and ROADMAP 3.3 records two re-runs steered by numbers stated in claim contexts that were not labeled as claim refinements, so those counts are a lower bound.

Over the seventeen papers of ROADMAP 3.2–3.6 it is 6, 25 and 62, over the twenty-three of ROADMAP 3.2–3.7, first samples only, 14, 29 and 79, on the paper of ROADMAP 3.9 0, 0 and 6, and over all twenty-four 14, 29 and 85.

This decomposition should not be leaned on hard, for three reasons.  The four API papers were chosen, when credit allowed only four, by the size of the agent-over-pipeline gap per dollar (ROADMAP 3.5); they lean toward papers where the original pipeline did badly.  The route split shows the consequence: almost all of the seven-paper harness effect comes from the API route (10 claims independent, 23 blind), while on the three oracle-route papers it is one claim in each of those modes.  Second, the two halves of "pipeline on Opus 5.5" are not the same answerer: the API rows run at named effort levels, the oracle rows at the desktop default.  Third, the decomposition uses the first pipeline sample only, and the agent and the 4.7 pipeline have one sample per cell.  The second samples (Section 4.8) put the seventeen-paper harness gap at 25 to 33 claims in independent mode and 57 to 62 in blind mode, but they move single pipeline cells by up to six claims, which is larger than the one-claim harness effects on the oracle route.  None of these differences has a standard error, and we make no claim of statistical significance for any of them.

The cell the benchmark was designed to find, a reproduction that agrees with the replicators and not with the paper, did not occur in a substantive form.[^i4r-cell]  The table does show rows in that column, four on the thirteen earlier papers, a fifth in the seventeen-paper rows and a sixth in the twenty-four-paper rows, but each is an artifact of how the I4R verdict is defined: it is computed over the claims that have an I4R value *and* a printed reproduction.  The agent's three Grier rows each miss one claim for which I4R recorded no value, and the Manekin blind row on `claude-opus-4-7` and the oracle pipeline's Bokobza and Molina-Garzón blind rows printed no value for the claims they missed.  On Molina-Garzón "agrees with I4R" means less than elsewhere: its seven paired rows are all robustness variants (controls removed; OLS on a proportion in place of the Poisson count model), so the entry records the paper's own printed value under the team's blanket "same output" verdict, and an I4R match there is a match to the paper on those seven claims (ROADMAP 3.9).  The seeded Forsythe rows of both systems show the reverse, paper ✓ and I4R ✗, as expected: I4R rebuilt Forsythe's panel from IPUMS and reports a different sample, so its numbers are not the package's.

The papers I4R rated not reproducible tell the more useful story.  On Montero, every configuration in seeded mode reproduced the paper's printed numbers, which the replicators showed are produced by duplicated rows; the seeded agent did so on its first script by following the do-file's "replicating original results" block (ROADMAP 3.3).  Seeded reproduction faithfully reproduces a package's error.  Independent and blind agents built a paper-faithful specification that matched neither the paper (except inside the wide band) nor the replicators.  Holman shows the same pattern: seeded runs follow the do-file, which estimates random effects and a linear model where the paper says fixed effects and a logit, and reproduce the printed numbers that I4R's report (DP41) corrected; the blind agent did what the paper says and reproduced neither (ROADMAP 3.4).  Douenne, the third paper I4R rated not reproducible, repeats the seeded half of the pattern: the seeded agent matched all thirteen printed values and only one of the eight values the replicators recomputed after correcting the treatment coding (ROADMAP 3.7).  Here independent mode agrees with the paper too, inside the one-standard-error band, for both systems.  Hollyer shows the band's other face: the seeded rows of both systems match the paper and miss four of the replicators' values, including the two extremist-party rows where the paper's text and the authors' code disagree, while every independent row, and the agent's blind row, passes those two claims against both the paper and the replicators, because one standard error is wide enough to cover the gap between them (ROADMAP 3.7).

## 5. Failure taxonomy

We classified every claim that missed in independent or blind mode on the evaluation papers, for the pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7` (seven papers), the pipeline on Opus 5.5 (twenty-four papers: API rows where they exist, oracle rows otherwise) and the agent (twenty-four papers), first samples only. A claim is classified by the root cause ROADMAP gives for its row; where ROADMAP gives none, it is unclassified.

**Data-file resolution.**  The reproducer read the wrong file.  On Montero blind the pipeline on Opus 5.5 built its regression on 130 rows instead of 4,770 because it never found the right data file without the scripts (ROADMAP 3.5).  On Grier the package ships three datasets that differ only in the contribution window and never says which is which; ROADMAP 3.5 attributes the pipeline's independent run, on 172 and 94 observations instead of 186 and 106, to these files, while the agent resolved the ambiguity by trying both (ROADMAP 3.4–3.5).  We keep that classification because it is ROADMAP's, but it is weaker than the Montero case.  In all three pipeline samples the three contribution-window claims carry one identical value, so the pipeline never estimated the windows separately, and in the second sample the independent run reached 186 and 106 observations and still missed every coefficient (appendix table "Grier and Holman, claim by claim").  If the data file is the cause, it is in the form "one file used for all six models", not the mapping between the two window files that the agent faced.  The six Molina-Garzón blind misses are counted here too, as the right file read through the wrong columns: the prompt's data inventory truncated the network file's header before the tie-count columns, and the script built its counts from the player-type dummies instead (Section 4.8; ROADMAP 3.9).  Every miss in this category belongs to the pipeline.

**Specification mismatch.**  The reproducer estimated a different model from the one the paper states.  The pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7` produced every Adams blind coefficient with the wrong sign (ROADMAP 3.2).  The agent's six specification misses are all Gagliarducci blind: it used the dataset's percent-opposite variable, which is missing for bills with no cosponsors, where the paper divides by cosponsors plus one, so Panel B lost bills and its coefficients moved (ROADMAP 3.4).  The oracle pipeline's twelve on Mokyr blind are variable construction: per-capita densities built on a different population column, so the coefficients came out about 10,000 times the printed values (ROADMAP 3.7).

*Holman is not counted here, and why.*  ROADMAP 3.5 describes the oracle pipeline's Holman independent and blind runs as fitting "random-effects / linear probability models where the paper uses fixed effects and logit".  That cannot explain a miss.  Random effects and a linear model are what the authors' do-file estimates (ROADMAP 3.4), and that specification is what reproduces the printed Table 2 in seeded mode, for the pipeline and the agent alike.  The results files point to the sample instead.  In each pipeline row that misses Table 2, the two Table 2 sample sizes are wrong too: 145,012 and 86,517 against 143,499 and 97,155 in the first independent sample, and 239,919 and 1,481,392 in blind mode in both samples, where two of the "May best PM" coefficients come out numerically zero.  When the second sample's independent run landed on the printed sample sizes, all eight Table 2 claims passed, inside the band rather than exactly (appendix table "Grier and Holman, claim by claim").  We therefore count the Table 2 sample sizes as sample restriction and leave the Holman coefficients unclassified.  The open question is which estimator the pipeline's scripts used, and whether the coefficient misses follow from the sample alone.  ROADMAP's aside that this is "the agent's miss in 3.4, worse here" suggests the sentence meant that the pipeline, like the blind agent, followed the paper's fixed effects and logit; if so, the coefficients belong with the agent's under under-specified methods section.  The results files cannot show it; the generated scripts, under the gitignored `benchmark/results/artifacts/holman-2022-theresa-may-rally/{independent,blind}/pipeline/` directories, would.

**Sample restriction.**  The model was right and the sample was not.  The oracle pipeline ran Altmann blind on 17,840 observations instead of 12,160 by taking the wrong subset of treatment arms, and Acemoglu blind on 178 and 171 clusters instead of 181 and 175 (ROADMAP 3.5).  On Leeson blind the pipeline on Opus 5.5 got the five-station subsample wrong (98 visits against 102), and with it the two dollars-per-hour tests (ROADMAP 3.5); the run on `claude-opus-4-7` also missed both visit counts (ROADMAP 3.2).  The oracle pipeline's Holman Table 2 sample sizes, in independent and blind mode, are counted here for the reason given above.  So are three of the ROADMAP 3.6 cells: the oracle pipeline's Carrera independent run kept 122 participants the paper excludes (1,248 against 1,126), its Carrera blind run filtered the sample down to nothing, and Forsythe's full-sample count drifted in three independent or blind cells with the handling of the rows that carry no state code from mid-2014 (ROADMAP 3.6).  On Labandeira the oracle pipeline built its own estimation sample in both modes: 21,317 observations independent and 12,349 blind against 20,872, and the blind coefficients on consumption are counted with that sample (ROADMAP 3.7).

**SE or cluster choice.**  No evaluation-paper miss in independent or blind mode was attributed to the standard-error or clustering choice.  The category is not empty in the project: on the dev paper Dincecco, two standard-error claims failed because the generated code used HC1 where Stata's `ivreg2, r` reports HC0 (ROADMAP 1.7–1.8).

**Tolerance artifact.**  One miss.  The agent's independent Grier run gives −0.000565 for a coefficient the paper prints as −0.0005; the authors' own script gives the same value, so the printed digit is itself off by 12.9 % against a 10 % band (ROADMAP 3.3).  The opposite artifact, passes the band lets through, is more consequential and is discussed in Section 4.6.

**Extraction error.**  No usable value reached the scorer: the script crashed, printed `NA`, or printed results under other names.  All of these are pipeline misses.  The pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7` printed nothing on Grier independent after three error repairs and `NA` for all six Study 1 coefficients on Manekin blind (ROADMAP 3.2); the oracle pipeline printed `NA` for Bokobza's seven individual-level coefficients in blind mode although its log shows the models estimated (ROADMAP 3.6).

**Under-specified methods section.**  The paper's text does not determine the number, or states a rule the authors' code does not follow, so a reproducer that follows the text misses and the seeded run, following the code, does not.  This is the category the benchmark exists to measure, and it accounts for 24 of the agent's 39 misses.  López-Moctezuma's four attitude-index effects fail in independent and blind mode for both harnesses because the paper does not state the attendance weights or the item orientations (ROADMAP 3.4).  Adams states that both parties need at least four MPs; the authors filtered on one party only, and the blind agent, applying the stated rule twice, got 1,535 dyads against 1,842 both times (ROADMAP 3.3–3.4).  Montero's paper states a five-member rule its code never applied (ROADMAP 3.2), and Holman's states fixed effects and a logit where its code uses random effects and a linear model (ROADMAP 3.4).  Grier's deflator base year is unstated; two agent runs chose different years, which rescales every contribution coefficient by 8 % (ROADMAP 3.3).  Labandeira adds two cases (ROADMAP 3.7).  Its paper says every Table 2 column has quarterly dummies, and the authors' instrumental-variable columns have none, so a text-following column 2 misses in both agent rows.  And its paper does not say the price regressor is a real (deflated) price: the blind agent, and the blind pipeline, built price as expenditure over quantity, which makes every expenditure coefficient identical to its consumption counterpart, and missed the expenditure columns.

**Unclassified.**  Seventy-nine misses, seventy-two of them the pipeline's, have no cause in ROADMAP that the results files bear out.  Two of the agent's seven are the same Forsythe claim, the out-of-the-labour-force interaction in Table 2, which lands near −0.59 against −0.49 in every first-sample independent and blind cell of both systems and on the printed value in both seeded cells (ROADMAP 3.6); the agent's second independent sample then landed on −0.500 with the exact full-sample count (ROADMAP 3.7), so the claim follows from a sample choice the paper leaves open rather than from something only the do-file contains.  Four more are Labandeira coefficients beyond the two gaps above, and one is a Douenne blind coefficient whose controls the agent rebuilt from variable names.  The pipeline's include the Arias independent conjoint effects on `claude-opus-4-7`, the Montero independent misses on `claude-opus-4-7`, the oracle pipeline's Holman coefficients (above) and several single-claim misses on the oracle rows.

The Grier blind coefficients are the clearest case.  The row on `claude-opus-4-7`, the API row on `claude-opus-5-5` and the second oracle sample print the same nine coefficients to every printed digit (appendix table "Grier and Holman, claim by claim"), so three separately generated analyses agree in data, sample, estimator and deflator.  Neither cause in ROADMAP fits them.  The estimator account in ROADMAP 3.2, a conditional logit in place of the paper's logit with fixed-effect dummies, is quoted with the seeded row's numbers (−0.00009, N 860 and 612); the blind row prints −0.0001877 and reports N 842 and 604, and ROADMAP gives no blind-specific cause.  The window-file account in ROADMAP 3.5, read as the mapping between the two robustness-window files, could at most explain the four Table 3 claims: the five Table 1 claims come from the main file and miss by as much.  Within each blind row, moreover, the three window claims carry one identical value, so the blind scripts did not tell the window files apart.  What the results files do show is a sample difference: the two rows on Opus 5.5 estimate on 172 and 96 observations against 186 and 106, and the row on `claude-opus-4-7`, whose coefficients are identical, reports all rows instead of its estimation sample.  Whether the file read, that sample, the coding of non-votes (which the entry's notes record as absent from the paper's text) or the estimator produces these coefficients is a question for the generated scripts, which are under the gitignored `benchmark/results/artifacts/grier-2023-sugar-roll-call-voting/blind/pipeline/` directory (runs `20261021T142909Z`, `20261022T043524Z` and `20261023T150832Z`) and are not in the repository.  Until someone reads them the category stays unclassified.  The pipeline's third oracle sample (ROADMAP 3.7.4) prints slightly different coefficients (−0.0001969 against −0.0001877 for the district contribution coefficient) on 186 and 106 observations, passing both counts and still missing every coefficient, with the three window claims again carrying one value.

**Not a miss: the sandbox's input limit.**  One failure mode cost no claims but shaped how two papers were reproduced.  The sandbox gives every script 300 seconds against a read-only mount, and the agents measured that mount at roughly 5 to 15 MB/s.  Hanushek's Table 1 file is 2.47 GB as `.dta` and 2.97 GB as `.csv`.  In every first-sample agent cell at least one exploratory full read timed out, and each of those agents then estimated Table 1 on a row sample and recorded it as a declared deviation: 98.6 % of rows in seeded mode, about 20 % in independent and 50 % in blind.  All three still passed every Table 1 claim.  The pipeline's scripts, under the same limit, read all 1,992,276 rows in every mode, with column-selected reads of the `.dta` (`haven::read_dta`) or the `.csv` (`data.table::fread`), the same calls that had timed out in the agents' exploration, and its seeded run reproduced Table 1 exactly; in the agent's second sample all three Hanushek cells also read every row with a column-selected `fread`, the independent and blind ones in two to three minutes and the seeded one in 152 seconds (ROADMAP 3.7.2).  The files do not say why the first-sample agents' reads timed out.  What the record supports is an I/O constraint, not a hard wall, that column-selected reads clear and that the first-sample agents worked around by sampling, all recorded in the evidence.  Forsythe's 2.17 GB file met the same constraint (ROADMAP 3.6), and every agent there read a column subset; the second seeded sample read all of it in 90 to 135 seconds.  During the agent batch of ROADMAP 3.7 the Docker daemon also went down twice.  The first time it wedged, and the coordinator cleared it by quitting and reopening Docker Desktop.  The second time the Hanushek seeded cell's agent restarted Docker Desktop itself (`open -a Docker`), as its hand-back reported, which is outside the isolation Section 3.5 describes.  The hung runs never started R, were re-run and are not counted as attempts (ROADMAP 3.7).

Memory alone puts 44 of 278 claims inside the blind tolerance, about one in six, and the answering model rated none of its 150 answers as recalled.  On the thirteen earlier papers the count is 27 of 154; the four papers of ROADMAP 3.6 add 10 and the six of ROADMAP 3.7 add 7.  The hits are weak evidence of memorization.  Thirty-seven are guesses, all rated *inferred*, inside one-standard-error bands that are mostly wide, though three of Forsythe's four are close (−0.35 against −0.335, 0.05 against 0.0496, −0.12 against −0.119), as are two of Douenne's three (0.52 against 0.534, 0.42 against 0.416).  Four are counts inside their band: Gagliarducci's sponsor-terms count within 1 % (4,700 answered against 4,746), and, since the count band widened to 5 % (ROADMAP 3.7.4), Grier's 110 against 106, Hanushek's 1,900,000 against 1,992,276 and Labandeira's 20,000 against 20,872, all round-number answers.  The remaining three are a leak of a kind the catalog's context test did not cover.  The Leeson Brown–Forsythe and ANOVA statistics were not stated in their claim contexts, but each context gave the test's p-value and, for the ANOVAs, its degrees of freedom.  The answering agent said it back-computed the statistics from those.  Those contexts were in every Leeson prompt in this report, blind mode included, for the pipeline and the agent alike.  They have since been reworded, and a test now forbids p-values, degrees of freedom and t-statistics in the contexts of any entry that pins a test statistic, but every Leeson row here was produced with the old text (ROADMAP 3.5, Next (d)).  Paper by paper, the probe does not track the agent: it answered nothing on Montero and López-Moctezuma, hit nothing on Holman and only a rounded count on Grier, papers where the agent's blind counts are between 6 and 10, on the four papers of ROADMAP 3.6 it hit 2 to 4 claims where the agent's blind counts are 11 to 12, and on the six of ROADMAP 3.7 it hit 0 to 3 where the agent's are 6 to 13; the one Douenne claim the blind agent missed is one the probe landed inside its band.  So memory cannot account for the agent's blind result.  The probe does not show that the papers were unseen, because a model can have read a paper without retaining its coefficients, and the answering model is the same desktop Opus that produced the agent rows.  Every hit is listed in the appendix.

**The oracle is not the API.**  Oracle answers come from desktop subagents at default effort, not at the effort the pipeline's request names; isolation is by instruction; and the extraction prompts truncate the paper text by design, which several answering agents noted (ROADMAP 3.5).  We present oracle rows beside the API rows, labeled, and never pool them in a table.  The "pipeline on Opus 5.5" aggregate in Sections 4.3–4.4 combines the two routes by paper, and its caveats are stated there.

**Few runs per cell.**  The pipeline on Opus 5.5 has three samples per cell on all twenty-four papers; the agent has three in independent and blind mode and two seeded on all twenty-four (more in five blind cells); the pipeline on `claude-opus-4-7` has one on the evaluation papers.  The twenty-fourth paper's six samples were all run on one day, after the frozen samples on the other twenty-three, through the same frozen pipeline and oracle protocol.  Two or three samples bound nothing.  The post-fix pipeline has two samples, on the twenty-three earlier papers and in independent and blind mode only: one in which the sample-size check never fired (ROADMAP 3.8.1) and one in which it fired (ROADMAP 3.8.3).  Because its fixes were chosen after seeing these results, both are post hoc and neither is pooled with the frozen samples or with the other.  See Section 4.8.

**Scope.**  Every reproduction is written in R.  Stata originals are included only because their claims can be scored without running Stata.  The corpus is twenty-four evaluation papers (290 claims), selected by availability of open data, open PDFs and an I4R verdict, not at random; eight of the twenty-four are from APSR or AJPS, and most methods are regressions on observational or experimental data.

**Tolerance choices.**  The seeded band is strict; the independent and blind band is one standard error and can pass values a quarter or more away from the printed number when the estimate is imprecise.  Changing either moves the counts.

**Seeded mode reproduces the package's mistakes.**  Montero seeded passes every claim on every configuration, and the numbers it reproduces are wrong (Section 4.7).  A seeded pass certifies the package, not the paper's result.

## 7. Using the benchmark with another agent

Any system that implements the `Reproducer` protocol can be scored on the same catalog without changing the harness.  It receives the paper source, the target language, a reference to the dataset, the pinned claims and the mode, and returns raw outputs keyed by claim name plus its code and logs.  The harness, not the agent, applies the tolerance and records the three verdicts, so no reproducer can grade its own exam or narrow the claim set.  An adapter must honor the mode by what is on disk (the script-free copy in independent and blind mode, not an instruction alone), run its code in the same network-less sandbox image, and raise rather than return junk when it cannot produce a result (`benchmark/README.md`, "Plugging in another agent").  A reproducer is registered by name in `benchmark/runner.py` or passed as `module:factory` on the command line.

The agent in this report used the file-system route: render a task per cell, let the agent work, score the files it leaves.  That route needs no code changes for a new agent, only a session that follows `task.md`.  The I4R meta-database the catalog draws on is MIT-licensed (`benchmark/corpus/SOURCE.md`).  The repository itself carries no license file, so the license of the catalog, the pinned claims and the harness is currently unstated.

## 8. Next steps

Four pieces of work would most change what this report can claim.  A full variance pass, three runs per paper per mode on a frozen and tagged harness, would let the gaps carry uncertainty.  Part of it exists: the pipeline on Opus 5.5 has three samples on all twenty-four papers in every mode, and the agent three in independent and blind mode and two seeded (Section 4.8).  What is missing is a third agent sample in seeded mode, and an API-route second sample for the four papers whose two pipeline samples came by different routes.  Corpus growth to the 20–30 papers the roadmap targets, with the open-data and open-PDF funnel recorded, would make the per-category counts in Section 5 mean something; the corpus is at twenty-four, and the third and fourth shortlist passes found the open pool exhausted down to six paired rows: the next growth needs the 33 openICPSR packages placed by hand under a user's login, which is the user's decision (Section 3.1.1).  A small set of post-cutoff replications, if I4R publishes them in machine-readable form, would give the contamination control that the memorization probe (Section 6) cannot (ROADMAP Phase 5 treats it as a bonus that expires with the next model release).  The probe itself should be rerun after the Leeson context rewording and on every new entry; it has not been run on Molina-Garzón, so its tables stay at twenty-three papers.  And the pipeline freeze question needs an answer: the two Opus 5.5 pipeline misses that are harness rather than model (Grier's data file, with the reservations of Section 5, and Montero blind's) are concrete targets if tuning is unfrozen, but tuning on evaluation papers would end their use as evaluation papers.

> **Editor's note:** Venue.  Options in ROADMAP Phase 5: an arXiv or SocArXiv preprint, the I4R discussion-paper series (I4R has run AI replication games and is the natural audience), or a methods journal.  The register of this draft fits the first two.
>
> *Recommended resolution:* Post a preprint first, because it fixes the date and invites the corrections a new benchmark needs, and decide between the I4R series and a journal after the agent's second samples and the related-work paragraph are in.  The venue is the user's decision.
